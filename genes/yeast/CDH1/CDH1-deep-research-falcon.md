# Functional annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* CDH1/HCT1 (UniProt P53197)

## Executive conclusion

The target is correctly identified as budding-yeast **Cdh1/Hct1**, encoded by **YGL003C** in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c. It is **not** mammalian CDH1/E-cadherin. Yeast Cdh1 is a 566-residue, nonenzymatic member of the Cdc20/Fizzy WD-repeat family and is the late-mitotic/G1 coactivator and substrate receptor of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome, APC/C. Its primary function is to recruit selected proteins to APC/C and stimulate their polyubiquitylation, leading mainly to 26S-proteasome degradation. Thus, no chemical reaction or small-molecule substrate should be assigned to Cdh1 itself: catalytic ubiquitin transfer is performed by the APC/C–E2 machinery, whereas Cdh1 supplies activation, timing, localization and substrate specificity. (ostapenko2018apcccdh1mediateddegradation pages 1-6, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2, hockner2016dualcontrolby pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

The supplied identifiers are internally consistent: **CDH1**, synonym **HCT1**, ordered locus **YGL003C**, UniProt **P53197**, in *S. cerevisiae* strain S288c. Yeast literature frequently uses “Hct1/Cdh1” and explicitly describes this protein as an APC/C substrate-specific activator. The reported C-terminal WD40 architecture, regulatory N-terminal region, Cdc20/Fizzy family membership and APC/C function all match the supplied domain annotation. (ostapenko2018apcccdh1mediateddegradation pages 1-6, yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 10-10, hockner2016dualcontrolby pages 1-2)

The symbol is nevertheless highly ambiguous across organisms. In human biomedical literature, **CDH1** normally denotes cadherin-1/E-cadherin, a cell-adhesion protein unrelated to yeast Cdh1/Hct1. Mammalian E-cadherin findings were therefore excluded. Likewise, results concerning mammalian APC/C coactivator FZR1/CDH1 are useful only as comparative APC/C biology and are not treated here as direct evidence for YGL003C.

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 APC/C coactivator and substrate receptor

Cdh1 is a noncatalytic adaptor/coactivator for APC/C, a multisubunit RING-family E3 ubiquitin ligase. It forms **APC/C–Cdh1**, recruits appropriately marked proteins to the ligase, and promotes their ubiquitylation and subsequent proteasomal destruction. A 2012 yeast study described APC/C as a 13-subunit complex that employs Cdc20 or Cdh1 as interchangeable but temporally distinct substrate-binding activators. (ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

Cdh1 attaches to the APC/C through two conserved elements: an N-terminal **C-box** and the invariant C-terminal **Ile-Arg, or IR, tail**. These make contacts with Apc1 and TPR-lobe components including Cdc23 and Cdc27. Deletion of Cdh1’s final two residues generated Cdh1-ΔIR, which failed to bind Cdc23 and could not promote APC/C substrate degradation, providing direct functional evidence for the docking role of the IR tail. (ostapenko2018apcccdh1mediateddegradation pages 28-32, ostapenko2018apcccdh1mediateddegradation pages 1-6)

Cdh1 also induces or stabilizes a catalytically competent APC/C conformation. It should therefore be annotated as both an **APC/C activator** and a **substrate-recognition factor**, rather than merely as a passive binding protein.

### 2.2 Domain architecture and substrate selection

The C-terminal WD40-repeat region forms the characteristic β-propeller substrate-recognition domain, while the less structured N-terminal domain contains APC/C-docking, localization and regulatory phosphorylation elements. Domain dissection demonstrated that the N-terminal domain is sufficient for regulated nuclear localization, whereas the C-terminal WD40 propeller participates in substrate binding. This agrees with the supplied InterPro assignments IPR033010, IPR015943, IPR056150, IPR019775 and IPR036322. (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 10-10, hockner2016dualcontrolby pages 1-2)

Canonical substrates contain short degrons, principally:

- **D box:** typically RxxLxxxxN;
- **KEN box:** a KEN sequence embedded in an appropriate context;
- **ABBA motif** and related activator-binding elements in some substrates.

These motifs are necessary but often not sufficient: degron accessibility, multiplicity, affinity, substrate phosphorylation, partner binding and nuclear localization determine whether a protein is degraded in vivo. Mutation of D or KEN motifs can disrupt Cdh1 binding and stabilize substrates; Tos4, for example, depends on its C-terminal KEN box and D boxes for recognition. (ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 1-6, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2, yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 10-10)

Minimal-degron experiments further showed that an effective yeast APC/C–Cdh1 reporter can comprise a D box or KEN box together with a nuclear-localization signal. This demonstrates that subcellular access is a key component of apparent substrate specificity, not merely an incidental property. (ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

## 3. Biological processes and pathways

### 3.1 Mitotic exit and G1 maintenance

Cdh1’s central pathway role is to sustain APC/C-mediated proteolysis after the earlier APC/C–Cdc20 phase. APC/C–Cdh1 becomes active in late mitosis, removes mitotic regulators and remains active through G1. This keeps B-type cyclin–Cdk activity low, makes mitotic exit effectively irreversible, supports spindle dismantling and prevents premature accumulation of S- and M-phase proteins. (ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 1-2)

The canonical substrate is the mitotic cyclin **Clb2**. Biochemical and genetic work showed that Hct1 recognizes Clb2 and other APC/C substrates directly. Clb2 destruction lowers Cdc28/Cdk1 activity, reinforcing Cdh1 activation in a positive-feedback transition into G1. Clb5 can also be degraded when APC/C–Cdh1 is experimentally activated. (ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 33-36, yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 10-10, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 11-14)

Cdh1 also promotes spindle disassembly through turnover of **Cin8, Kip1, Ase1 and Fin1**. These substrates connect the molecular ubiquitin-recognition function to the physical reversal of mitotic-spindle architecture. (ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

### 3.2 Resetting transcription and checkpoint regulators

APC/C–Cdh1 degrades the transcriptional repressors **Nrm1** and **Yhp1** during G1, helping reset periodic transcription for the next cell cycle. Stabilization of these repressors causes target-gene misexpression and reduced cellular fitness. (ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

A combination of cell-cycle expression/stability profiling and Cdh1 two-hybrid screening identified **Tos4, Pdr3, Fir1, Mps1 and Ybr138C** as candidate APC/C substrates. The evidence is not equally definitive for every candidate, so these should not all be treated as equivalent to Clb2. Nonetheless, stabilized Mps1 delayed division after mild spindle perturbation, and elevated Ybr138C reduced fitness, supporting biological consequences from failed turnover. (ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

### 3.3 Meiosis and nutrient-responsive regulation

A major 2024 development connects Cdh1 turnover to the glucose-response pathway during entry into sporulation. In a July 2024 preprint, glucose limitation activated **Snf1**, whose upstream kinase in this context was Elm1. Snf1 physically associated with Cdh1 and phosphorylated **Ser200** in vitro. Cdh1 was then degraded by APC/C–Cdh1 itself through a **trans** mechanism: one Cdh1 molecule acted as activator while recruiting another as substrate. Recognition did not require the standard D-box, KEN-box or ABBA interfaces, indicating a noncanonical Cdh1–Cdh1 recognition mechanism. (ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 1-6, ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 13-17, ostapenko2018apcccdh1mediateddegradation pages 13-17)

The phospho-null **Cdh1-S200A** protein remained stable for at least 24 hours in sporulation medium but retained the ability to promote degradation of Clb2 and Cdc5; the phenotype therefore reflects defective regulated turnover rather than a generally inactive or misfolded coactivator. The phosphomimetic S200D protein was highly unstable, including in the absence of SNF1, and its degradation remained APC/C-dependent. (ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 33-36, ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 13-17)

The meiotic consequences were substantial: S200A cells formed about **fourfold more dyads** than wild type, and approximately **20% of S200A tetrads had only three viable spores**, whereas all 100 wild-type tetrads tested produced four viable spores. S200D cells failed to form tetrads under the reported conditions and failed to resume growth after return to rich medium. These results support a model in which Cdh1 removal preserves cell-cycle and cohesion regulators between meiosis I and II. Because this work was a preprint as of July 2024, its mechanistic and quantitative conclusions warrant independent replication despite the internally strong genetic and biochemical evidence. [Ostapenko & Solomon, posted July 2024, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.07.01.601619]. (ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 33-36, ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 13-17)

## 4. Subcellular localization

Cdh1 performs its best-established function principally in the **nucleus**. Compartment-targeted degron reporters indicated that APC/C–Cdh1 activity is strongest in the nucleoplasm and was not detectably active toward comparable cytoplasmic reporters. This nuclear restriction explains why adding an NLS can convert an otherwise inadequate D-box or KEN-box reporter into an efficient in-vivo substrate. (ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2)

Localization is regulated rather than constitutive. Cdh1’s N-terminal region contains a bipartite NLS, and Cdk1-dependent phosphorylation promotes its export through the karyopherin **Msn5**. The best-supported functional annotation is therefore **nuclear/nucleoplasmic APC/C coactivator**, with regulated nucleocytoplasmic shuttling. It should not be annotated as a mitochondrial-resident protein solely because altered Cdh1 activity can have downstream metabolic or mitochondrial phenotypes. (ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 1-2, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 11-14, hockner2016dualcontrolby pages 1-2)

## 5. Regulation of Cdh1 activity

### 5.1 Cdk1 phosphorylation and Cdc14 dephosphorylation

Cdh1 is controlled by multisite phosphorylation. A detailed 2016 study separated two functional classes among nine N-terminal Cdk1 sites:

- **Sites 1–3** disable the bipartite NLS and control nuclear localization without directly impairing APC/C binding.
- **Sites 4–9** primarily inhibit association with the APC/C core without controlling regulated localization.

Thus, Cdk1 blocks Cdh1 twice—by excluding it from the nucleus and by preventing productive core binding. (hockner2016dualcontrolby pages 1-2)

Other mapping schemes describe 11 consensus CDK sites, with seven in the N-terminal region and four in or near the WD40 domain. These numbering differences reflect experimental constructs and grouping conventions rather than a contradiction in the core conclusion that multisite phosphorylation provides redundant inhibition. At mitotic exit, **Cdc14 phosphatase** reverses inhibitory phosphorylation, permitting nuclear APC/C binding and Cdh1 activation. (ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 1-2)

### 5.2 Acm1 pseudosubstrate inhibition

During S phase and mitosis, **Acm1** acts as a stoichiometric pseudosubstrate inhibitor of APC/C–Cdh1. Acm1 engages Cdh1’s substrate-binding machinery but is protected from ordinary substrate processing, thereby competing with genuine targets. This mechanism operates alongside phosphorylation and Msn5-dependent export. Genetic interaction experiments showed that ACM1 became essential in incompletely phosphorylatable CDH1 backgrounds and that deleting ACM1 with severe Cdh1 phosphosite mutants caused inviability, demonstrating physiologically important redundancy among inhibitory systems. (ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 14-15, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 11-14)

### 5.3 Temporal precision

A live-cell fluorescent APC/C–Cdh1 biosensor found that Cdh1 inactivation occurred approximately **12 minutes after Whi5 nuclear exit**, a marker of Start, with little measured cell-to-cell timing variation. Reducing available Cdh1 phosphorylation sites increased timing variability and caused stochastic defects in budding, nuclear division and APC/C–Cdh1 activity. In one defective phosphosite background, 62% of arrested cells underwent at least one, often abnormal, nuclear division. These data support an expert interpretation in which redundant phosphorylation, Acm1 and nuclear export provide robustness rather than mere mechanistic duplication. [Ondracka et al., published July 2016, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0159166]. (ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 14-15, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 1-2, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 11-14)

## 6. Experimental strength and current applications

The functional assignment rests on convergent evidence:

1. **Biochemical binding and ubiquitination assays** establish substrate recognition and APC/C activation.
2. **IR-tail deletion and phosphorylation-site mutants** separate APC/C docking, localization and regulatory functions.
3. **Protein-stability and degron mutagenesis** establish D-box/KEN-box dependence.
4. **Compartment-targeted reporters** locate active APC/C–Cdh1 primarily in the nucleus.
5. **Live-cell biosensors** resolve activation/inactivation timing.
6. **Genetic and sporulation assays** connect molecular regulation to chromosome segregation and viability. (ostapenko2018apcccdh1mediateddegradation pages 28-32, ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 13-17, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 1-2, hockner2016dualcontrolby pages 1-2)

In current practice, Cdh1 is primarily a **research-system component**, not an industrially deployed yeast enzyme or transporter. APC/C–Cdh1 degron reporters are useful for measuring cell-cycle state, testing degron sufficiency, and studying proteolytic timing. Phosphosite mutants, Acm1 perturbations and localization reporters are used to dissect robustness at Start and mitotic exit. Candidate degrons can potentially be used in synthetic biology to impose late-mitotic/G1-specific protein destruction, although successful design must incorporate compartment targeting and degron context rather than relying on a short motif alone. The Snf1–Ser200 result additionally suggests an experimental route for coupling nutrient state to programmed proteolysis during sporulation, but it is not yet a validated real-world biotechnology implementation. (ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 13-17, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 1-2)

## 7. Recent-research assessment and unresolved questions

Direct 2023–2024 literature on this exact S288c protein is limited compared with the mature foundational literature. The clearest recent mechanistic advance is the 2024 Snf1–Ser200 study described above. A 2023 report proposed a role for Cdh1 in mitochondrial metabolic remodeling, but the retrievable evidence was insufficient to assign a direct mitochondrial substrate or localization; it should therefore be regarded as a broader systems-level phenotype rather than a revision of Cdh1’s primary molecular annotation. (ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 13-17, leite2023crossregulationofmitochondrial pages 163-167)

Important open questions include the molecular interface responsible for degron-independent Cdh1 self-recognition during sporulation, the full physiological substrate spectrum in different nutrient states, and how substrate affinity, localization and multisite degrons quantitatively determine degradation order. The most defensible current expert view is that Cdh1 is a regulated nuclear substrate receptor whose apparently broad phenotypes emerge from temporally coordinated destruction of multiple cell-cycle regulators, not from independent enzymatic activity.

## Final recommended annotation

**Cdh1/Hct1 (P53197; YGL003C) is a nuclear, WD40-repeat Cdc20/Fizzy-family coactivator and substrate-recognition subunit of the *S. cerevisiae* APC/C E3 ubiquitin ligase. Through C-box/IR-tail docking to APC/C and recognition of D-box-, KEN-box- and context-dependent degrons, it promotes polyubiquitylation and proteasomal destruction of mitotic cyclins, spindle factors, transcriptional repressors and checkpoint regulators during late mitosis and G1. Its activity is inhibited by Cdk1-dependent phosphorylation, Msn5-mediated nuclear export and the Acm1 pseudosubstrate, and activated at mitotic exit by Cdc14-dependent dephosphorylation. Recent evidence further implicates Snf1 phosphorylation of Ser200 in APC/C-dependent Cdh1 turnover during sporulation and faithful meiotic chromosome segregation.** (ostapenko2024apccdh1mediateddegradationof pages 1-6, ostapenko2012identificationofanaphase pages 1-2, ondracka2016anapcccdh1biosensor pages 1-2, hockner2016dualcontrolby pages 1-2)
