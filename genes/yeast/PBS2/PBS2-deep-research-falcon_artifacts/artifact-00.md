| Identity and domain architecture | Pbs2 is the *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* HOG-pathway MAP kinase kinase (MAP2K), combining an N-terminal regulatory/scaffolding region with a C-terminal protein-kinase domain. This matches PBS2/YJL128C and UniProt P08018. | N-terminal docking regions; C-terminal kinase domain | Sequence/domain annotation supported by interaction mapping and mutagenesis | Tatebayashi *et al.*, July 2003 (pqac-00000001) |
| Catalytic reaction and substrate specificity | Activated Pbs2 transfers phosphate from ATP to both regulatory residues of its direct MAPK substrate Hog1, thereby activating Hog1 during osmotic stress. | Hog1 Thr174 and Tyr176 | Phosphosite analysis, pathway genetics, and biochemical assays | Tatebayashi & Saito, April 2023 (pqac-00000003) |
| Differential upstream activation | The SHO1-branch MAP3K Ste11 phosphorylates Pbs2 primarily at Thr518, whereas the SLN1-branch MAP3Ks Ssk2/Ssk22 can phosphorylate both Ser514 and Thr518. Diphosphorylated Pbs2 is intrinsically competent to activate Hog1; Thr518-monophosphorylated Pbs2 requires osmostress-enhanced Pbs2–Hog1 reactivity. | Pbs2 Ser514 and Thr518; Ste11; Ssk2; Ssk22 | Phospho-specific immunoblotting, mutant reconstruction, and pathway genetics | Tatebayashi *et al.*, February 2020; Tatebayashi & Saito, April 2023 (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) |
| Scaffolding and docking | Pbs2 is both an enzyme and a pathway scaffold: separate N-terminal regions dock Ssk2/Ssk22, Sho1, and Hog1, increasing signaling efficiency and insulating the HOG cascade from other MAPK pathways. | Ssk2/Ssk22 docking region; Sho1-binding proline-rich motif; HBD-1; Hog1 CD and PBD-2 regions | Interaction assays, docking-site mutations, domain transfer, and osmosensitivity phenotypes | Tatebayashi *et al.*, July 2003; Murakami *et al.*, April 2008 (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000006) |
| Cellular localization | Wild-type Pbs2 is predominantly cytoplasmic and normally excluded from the nucleus. Osmostress transiently recruits Pbs2 to Sho1-associated polarized cortical sites at bud tips and mother–bud necks; recruitment depends on Sho1 and Cdc42. An N-terminal nuclear-export signal maintains cytoplasmic enrichment. | Sho1 SH3 domain; Pbs2 Pro96 region; Cdc42; N-terminal nuclear-export signal; Pbs2(K389M) reporter | GFP microscopy, deletion/mutant analysis, and localization scoring after osmotic shock | Reiser *et al.*, September 2000; Saito & Tatebayashi, September 2004 (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010) |
| PP2C-mediated downregulation | Ptc1–Ptc4 reverse activating phosphorylation of Pbs2. Ptc1 predominantly controls Thr518, whereas all four phosphatases act redundantly on Ser514; Nbp2 helps recruit Ptc1 to Pbs2. | Ptc1, Ptc2, Ptc3, Ptc4; Nbp2; Pbs2 Ser514 and Thr518 | Phos-tag electrophoresis, phospho-specific antibodies, immunoprecipitation, and combinatorial phosphatase deletions | Tatebayashi & Saito, April 2023 (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000005) |
| Hog1-to-Pbs2 positive feedback | Activated Hog1 phosphorylates Pbs2 at Ser248. Modeling and mutant validation support an affinity/catalytic feedback mechanism that can shift Hog1 phosphorylation from distributive toward processive behavior, improving response sensitivity and robustness. | Pbs2 Ser248; Pbs2-S248A; Pbs2-S248E; Hog1 | ODE modeling, in-vivo phosphosignaling measurements, in-vitro kinase assays, and binding analysis | Mosbacher *et al.*, May 2023 (pqac-00000016) |
| Glycerol-assimilation application | Disrupting PBS2 increased glycerol assimilation, consistent with reduced HOG-pathway output improving glycerol use under the tested conditions. The engineering effect is context-dependent and may trade metabolic performance against stress tolerance. | PBS2 deletion; HOG1 deletion; SSK2/SSK22/STE11 triple disruption; STL1; RIM15; CYB2 | Adaptive laboratory evolution, genome resequencing, targeted gene disruption, growth assays, and lactic-acid production demonstration | Sone *et al.*, July 2023 (pqac-00000017) |
| Telomerase-associated oxidative stress | In telomerase-deficient yeast, Hog1 activation and control of elevated reactive oxygen species require the canonical upstream MAP2K Pbs2. The response is separable from Mec1 checkpoint signaling, although effects of Hog1 loss on senescence are complicated by pre-existing telomere and viability defects. | Pbs2; Hog1 Thr174/Tyr176; Mec1; reactive oxygen species | Gene deletion, phospho-Hog1 analysis, ROS measurements, and replicative-senescence experiments | Zeinoun *et al.*, October 2023 preprint; peer-reviewed June 2024 report (pqac-00000018) |
