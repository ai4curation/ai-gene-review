Gene Description

Pet191 is a 108-residue twin CX9C protein required for cytochrome c oxidase maturation in yeast, and the ortholog of human COA5. It resides in the mitochondrial intermembrane space associated with the inner membrane, where its motif cysteines are present in disulfide linkages within a large oligomeric complex; the motif cysteines are functionally important. Pet191 is unusual among twin CX9C proteins in that its import is independent of the MIA40 pathway. It has been proposed to stabilize an early cytochrome c oxidase assembly intermediate until displaced by copper metallochaperone action, but no molecular function has been demonstrated.

Core Functions

Twin CX9C assembly factor for cytochrome c oxidase, residing in the mitochondrial intermembrane space in association with the inner membrane as part of a large oligomeric complex in which its motif cysteines are disulfide linked. Required for cytochrome c oxidase maturation but not a subunit of the mature enzyme. Unlike other twin CX9C proteins of this pathway, its import does not require the MIA40 relay. Its molecular activity is undetermined.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Pet191 import is independent of the MIA40 pathway, unlike every other twin CX9C protein in this pathway. What is the import route, and does the difference reflect a distinct functional role?

Suggested experts: Winge lab, Herrmann lab

Q: What is the large oligomeric complex in which Pet191 resides? Its composition has never been determined, and it is the most obvious route to a molecular function for this protein.

Suggested experts: Winge lab, Barrientos lab

Q: Human COA5 is the ortholog and is disease-associated. Is the yeast evidence sufficient to support annotation transfer, given the divergent import route?

Suggested experts: GO Consortium PAINT curators

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine the composition of the Pet191 complex by blue-native PAGE followed by mass spectrometry, in wild-type and in strains blocked at defined steps of complex IV assembly.

Hypothesis: The Pet191-containing oligomeric complex is an early cytochrome c oxidase assembly intermediate.

Experiment: Screen import mutants for failure to accumulate Pet191 in mitochondria, and test in vitro import of Pet191 into mitochondria depleted of candidate receptors.
