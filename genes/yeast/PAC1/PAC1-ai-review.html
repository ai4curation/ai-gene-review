Gene Description

Pac1 is the Saccharomyces cerevisiae ortholog of LIS1 (Aspergillus NudF), a dimeric WD40 beta-propeller protein with an N-terminal LisH dimerization domain that binds the motor domain of cytoplasmic dynein (Dyn1). In budding yeast dynein's principal role is to pull the mitotic spindle into the bud neck: dynein is carried to the plus ends of astral microtubules, off-loaded to cortical Num1 receptors in the bud, and then walks toward the spindle pole body so that microtubules slide along the bud cortex. Pac1 localizes to the plus ends of cytoplasmic microtubules (with help from Bik1/CLIP-170 and the NudE homolog Ndl1) and is required for dynein's plus-end accumulation and hence for microtubule sliding, spindle movement into the neck and nuclear segregation; pac1 mutants are synthetically lethal with loss of the kinesin-5 Cin8 and sick with loss of the parallel Kar9 pathway. Biochemically, Pac1 engages the dynein motor at the interface between the AAA+ ring and stalk, prolonging microtubule attachment, and it stabilizes an uninhibited (open) conformation of dynein, coordinating dynein's plus-end localization with its later activation at the cortex.

PAC1 (P39946/YOR269W) in Saccharomyces cerevisiae: Functional Annotation Research Report

Executive summary

The requested protein is correctly identified as Pac1 from Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288c, encoded by PAC1/YOR269W and represented by UniProt P39946. It is the budding-yeast homolog of LIS1/NudF and belongs to the conserved WD-repeat LIS1 family. The literature-supported architecture—an N-terminal dimerization/coiled-coil region followed by seven WD40 repeats forming β-propeller domains—matches the supplied domain annotations. This report excludes unrelated genes named PAC1 in other organisms. (lee2003theroleof pages 8-9, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 1-2)

Pac1 is not an enzyme, transporter, or structural microtubule component. Its primary function is that of a non-catalytic, dimeric regulator of cytoplasmic dynein. It binds directly to the Dyn1 motor domain, promotes an open or activation-competent dynein conformation, supports delivery and retention of dynein at cytoplasmic-microtubule plus ends, and regulates the transition from a targeted motor complex to an active cortical force generator. This pathway positions the mitotic spindle and moves the nucleus through the mother–bud neck. (lee2003theroleof pages 1-1, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 16-17, karasmanis2023lis1relievescytoplasmic pages 1-2)

Identity verification and nomenclature

The identity criteria are internally consistent:

  • Organism: budding yeast, S. cerevisiae.
  • Gene/protein: PAC1/Pac1, the yeast homolog of human LIS1 and fungal NudF.
  • Family: conserved LIS1/nudF WD-repeat dynein regulators.
  • Architecture: N-terminal dimerization region and seven C-terminal WD40 repeats, yielding paired β-propeller interaction modules.
  • Functional match: direct regulation of cytoplasmic dynein during nuclear migration and spindle positioning. (lee2003theroleof pages 8-9, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 1-2)

Thus, the supplied PAC1–P39946–YOR269W assignment is biologically coherent. Reports about mammalian PAC1 receptors, phosphatases, or unrelated PAC1 genes were not treated as evidence for this protein.

Primary molecular function

Pac1 binds directly to the motor domain of the dynein heavy chain Dyn1. Structural work identifies two principal interfaces: a ring site near AAA3/AAA4 in dynein’s AAA+ motor ring and a stalk site near the stalk emerging from AAA4. Pac1 dimerization and the integrity of both interfaces are important for dynein localization and function in yeast. A 3.1-Å cryo-EM structure resolved these contacts at high resolution. (gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 16-17)

Pac1 should therefore be annotated as a dynein-activating and targeting cofactor. It does not supply ATPase activity; ATP hydrolysis and force production reside in Dyn1. Pac1 instead controls dynein’s conformational ensemble and mechanochemistry. Its binding at the motor ring is sterically incompatible with dynein’s compact autoinhibited “Phi” state, favoring an open state that can proceed toward active assemblies. However, Pac1 has additional effects beyond simply breaking Phi autoinhibition, because mutations at its ring and stalk interfaces continue to affect cortical targeting and spindle positioning even in some dynein backgrounds with weakened Phi autoinhibition. (gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 16-17, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 11-13)

Structure-guided endogenous mutations strengthen this assignment. Pac1 E253A/H254A at the ring-associated interface substantially reduced dynein foci at spindle-pole bodies, microtubule plus ends, and the cortex. Pac1 S248Q at the stalk-associated interface produced a partial separation-of-function phenotype that preserved plus-end localization better than subsequent cortical localization. Mutations F185D/I189D/R494A affecting the Pac1–Pac1 interface also impaired localization or function, supporting an important role for Pac1 dimerization. (gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 11-13)

Cellular localization and pathway

Pac1 acts principally in the cytoplasm at three connected sites: cytoplasmic-microtubule plus ends, spindle-pole-body-associated microtubules, and the bud cortex during dynein offloading or activation.

1. Assembly and delivery to microtubule plus ends

Pac1 and Dyn1 form a complex before or during recruitment to plus ends decorated by Bik1, the yeast CLIP-170 homolog. Pac1 and Dyn1 depend reciprocally on one another and on Bik1 for efficient plus-end localization, whereas Bik1 localization is comparatively independent of Pac1 and Dyn1. Quantitative imaging and purification suggested approximately coordinated, near-1:1 loading of Pac1 and Dyn1; increasing Bik1 recruited roughly equal additional amounts of each. Kinesin Kip2 contributes to transport toward plus ends, although cytosolic recruitment also occurs. (markus2011quantitativeanalysisof pages 1-2, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 16-17)

Pac1 is necessary for productive Dyn1 accumulation at distal cytoplasmic-microtubule tips. Early imaging found that Pac1 tip localization could occur independently of Dyn1, whereas subsequent quantitative work showed reciprocal dependence under other assay conditions; the combined evidence supports a cooperative targeting system rather than Pac1 functioning merely as a passive dynein cargo. (lee2003theroleof pages 8-9, markus2011quantitativeanalysisof pages 1-2, lee2003theroleof pages 1-1)

2. Cortical encounter, offloading, and activation

Plus ends probe the bud cortex. At Num1-containing cortical sites, dynein and associated factors are transferred or “offloaded” from the microtubule tip to the cortex. Dynactin acts downstream of initial Pac1-dependent tip targeting, contributing to cortical capture, anchoring, activation, and force production. Pac1 and Dyn1 accumulate at plus ends when Num1 or dynactin is absent, supporting this ordering. (lee2003theroleof pages 8-9, lee2003theroleof pages 1-1)

Pac1 must then relinquish dynein for sustained active movement. Recent structural work indicates that dynein’s transition into a microtubule-bound conformation lowers Pac1 affinity. Microtubule binding remodels the AAA5-loop, AAA4-helix, and AAA3-loop region and distorts the Pac1-binding geometry by approximately 3.2 Å. This provides an allosteric mechanism for Pac1 release before sustained cortical or cargo-associated transport. The precise temporal sequence among microtubule binding, Num1 engagement, dynactin assembly, and complete Pac1 dissociation remains an active mechanistic question rather than a fully resolved linear pathway. (ton2022microtubulebindinginducedallostery pages 14-15)

3. Force production and spindle positioning

Once anchored at cortical Num1 sites and associated with dynactin, dynein pulls on cytoplasmic microtubules through lateral sliding and end-capture/shrinkage mechanisms. These forces draw the spindle and nucleus toward and through the mother–bud neck. Pac1 is thus upstream of force production but essential for placing dynein into the correct spatial and conformational state. (lee2003theroleof pages 8-9, lee2003theroleof pages 1-1)

Biological process and loss-of-function evidence

The best-defined biological role is the dynein-dependent phase of mitotic nuclear migration and spindle positioning. Cells lacking functional Pac1 lose productive microtubule sliding along the bud cortex and exhibit defective spindle movement and nuclear segregation. In one quantitative analysis, approximately 83% of an elongating spindle could remain on the mother-cell side. Some mutant microtubules became laterally plastered against the bud cortex but failed to slide and detached after roughly 10 seconds. (lee2003theroleof pages 1-1, lee2003theroleof pages 2-3)

These defects are not explained by a general collapse of microtubule dynamics. Reported growth rates were similar in pac1 and wild type—4.92 versus 4.67 µm min⁻¹—and shrinkage rates were 5.59 versus 4.91 µm min⁻¹, with no major qualitative change in catastrophe or rescue. Pac1 therefore regulates productive dynein targeting and force generation rather than serving as a general controller of microtubule polymerization. (lee2003theroleof pages 2-3)

Recent developments, 2023–2024

Molecular-wedge model of dynein activation — 2023

Karasmanis and colleagues visualized an activation intermediate termed Chi, containing two yeast dynein motor domains and two Lis1/Pac1 dimers. The Pac1 dimers are wedged between the motor domains, preventing or resolving the autoinhibited Phi arrangement. Chi-contact mutations impaired Pac1-dependent dynein regulation in S. cerevisiae, while complementary human reconstitution showed that equivalent contacts support assembly of active dynein–dynactin–adaptor complexes. This study supplies strong direct yeast evidence for the wedge mechanism, but claims about complete cargo-adaptor complex assembly derive partly from the human system. Published online 24 August 2023 in Nature Structural & Molecular Biology; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01069-6. (karasmanis2023lis1relievescytoplasmic pages 1-2)

Microtubule-binding-induced Pac1 release — 2023

Ton and colleagues showed that dynein’s microtubule-bound conformational state has lower Pac1/LIS1 affinity than its microtubule-unbound state. The work links motor-domain allostery to release of Pac1 before processive transport and explains why Pac1 can be required for targeting and activation yet absent from the final motile complex. The yeast evidence includes motor-domain conformational mutants, Pac1 binding assays, structural comparison, and cellular dynein-focus assays; the approximately 3.2-Å deformation of the binding site provides a structural basis. Peer-reviewed version: Nature Structural & Molecular Biology 30, 1365–1379; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01010-x. (ton2022microtubulebindinginducedallostery pages 14-15)

Force-dependent dynein mechanochemistry — 2024

Kusakci and colleagues used purified budding-yeast dynein with Pac1/Lis1 in single-molecule and optical-trapping experiments. Pac1 slowed movement through its interaction with the AAA+ ring rather than acting as a physical roadblock or simple microtubule tether. It prolonged stalls and reduced the normal directional asymmetry of force-induced detachment without measurably increasing dynein’s force generation. Mutating stalk contacts partly restored detachment asymmetry but did not restore velocity, separating the ring-mediated slowing effect from the stalk-mediated resistance to detachment. Experiments further indicated that one bound regulator slows dynein and increases run time, with two producing stronger effects. Published in 2024 in Nature Chemical Biology 20, 521–529; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-023-01464-6. (kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3, kusakci2023lis1bindingregulates pages 7-9, kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 22-23)

Together, these recent studies reconcile apparently opposing descriptions of LIS1-family proteins as dynein activators and motility inhibitors. Pac1 is a positive regulator at the assembly and targeting stages, but persistent occupancy during movement can slow the motor and stabilize microtubule attachment. Timely dissociation is therefore part of normal activation. (ton2022microtubulebindinginducedallostery pages 14-15, kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3, karasmanis2023lis1relievescytoplasmic pages 1-2)

Current applications and broader significance

Pac1 has no direct industrial or clinical application in budding yeast. Its principal real-world use is as an experimentally tractable model for conserved LIS1–dynein regulation. Yeast permits endogenous mutagenesis, live-cell imaging of spindle positioning, purification of unusually stable dynein, cryo-EM, and single-molecule force measurements. These approaches have defined interfaces and conformational intermediates that are difficult to isolate in mammalian cells. (gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3, karasmanis2023lis1relievescytoplasmic pages 1-2)

The translational relevance is indirect but substantial: human LIS1 insufficiency causes lissencephaly, and the dynein-binding surfaces and activation principles are strongly conserved. Nevertheless, yeast Pac1 phenotypes should not be equated directly with neuronal migration or human disease, and findings from human dynein–dynactin–adaptor reconstitution should not automatically be assigned to the simpler yeast cortical pathway. (gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, karasmanis2023lis1relievescytoplasmic pages 1-2)

Evidence assessment and remaining uncertainties

The core annotation is supported by convergent genetics, live-cell imaging, biochemistry, cryo-EM, structure-guided mutation, single-molecule imaging, and optical trapping. The strongest conclusions are that Pac1 directly binds dynein, is required for plus-end targeting and cortical delivery, and enables dynein-dependent spindle positioning. (lee2003theroleof pages 1-1, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 1-2, gillies2022structuralbasisfor pages 16-17)

Three issues remain less certain. First, the exact contribution of direct Pac1–microtubule binding versus recruitment through Dyn1, Bik1, or another plus-end factor remains incompletely resolved. Second, the order and kinetics of Pac1 release relative to Num1 and dynactin engagement have not been fully measured in living cells. Third, Pac1’s effects vary by state: it opens or targets dynein before activation but slows motility and detachment when it remains motor-bound. These are state-dependent functions, not contradictory annotations. (lee2003theroleof pages 8-9, ton2022microtubulebindinginducedallostery pages 14-15, kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3)

Recommended functional annotation

Pac1 is a dimeric WD40-repeat LIS1-family regulator of cytoplasmic dynein. It binds the Dyn1 AAA+ motor ring and stalk, promotes an open activation-competent motor state, and enables dynein recruitment to cytoplasmic-microtubule plus ends through a Bik1/Kip2-associated targeting pathway. Following cortical encounter, Pac1 dissociation permits Num1/dynactin-associated dynein to generate microtubule-sliding and pulling forces required for mitotic spindle positioning and nuclear segregation across the mother–bud neck. Pac1 has no known catalytic reaction or transported substrate.
