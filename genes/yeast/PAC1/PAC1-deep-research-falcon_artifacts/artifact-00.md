| Identity and architecture | PAC1 encodes budding-yeast Pac1, the conserved LIS1/nudF-family dynein regulator matching P39946/YOR269W. It has an N-terminal dimerization/coiled-coil region and seven C-terminal WD40 repeats forming β-propellers. | *S. cerevisiae* S288c cytoplasm; Pac1 homodimer; Dyn1 dynein | Yeast genetics, domain analysis, and structural biology (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000004) | Direct yeast evidence supports the identity and seven-WD-repeat architecture. Same-symbol genes in other organisms are not interchangeable with this target. |
| Primary molecular function | Pac1 is a nonenzymatic dynein cofactor, not an enzyme or transporter. It directly binds the Dyn1 motor domain, regulates dynein mechanochemistry, favors an activation-competent state, and enables correct motor targeting. | Dyn1 AAA+ ring and stalk; Pac1–Pac1 interface | Biochemistry, structure-guided mutagenesis, live-cell imaging, and yeast genetics (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000006) | Ring-, stalk-, and dimer-interface mutations impair cortical dynein delivery and spindle positioning. Pac1 is an established direct motor-domain-binding dynein regulator. |
| Plus-end targeting | Pac1 and Dyn1 are coordinately recruited to cytoplasmic-microtubule plus ends. Pac1 is required for Dyn1 tip localization; Bik1/CLIP-170 and kinesin Kip2 contribute to delivery, while cytosolic recruitment can also occur. | Microtubule plus ends; Dyn1, Bik1, Kip2 | Endogenous fluorescence imaging, co-purification, dosage experiments, and genetics (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000006) | Pac1 and Dyn1 were inferred to load at approximately 1:1 stoichiometry. Increasing Bik1 recruits roughly equal amounts of both, and Pac1 overexpression increases Dyn1 plus-end targeting. |
| Cortical offloading and force generation | Plus-end-associated dynein encounters cortical Num1 sites, where dynein–dynactin is captured or offloaded and activated as Pac1 is released. Active cortical dynein drives lateral microtubule sliding or end-capture/shrinkage forces. | Bud cortex; Num1, dynactin/Arp1, cytoplasmic microtubules | Live-cell imaging and yeast loss-of-function genetics (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002) | Pac1 and Dyn1 accumulate at plus ends when Num1 or dynactin is absent, supporting a later role for these proteins in offloading or activation. The exact order of Pac1 release and Num1 engagement remains a dynamic mechanistic model. |
| Spindle positioning and nuclear segregation | Pac1-dependent cortical dynein pulls spindle-pole-body-associated microtubules, moving the nucleus and anaphase spindle through the mother–bud neck. Pac1 loss abolishes productive cortical sliding and disrupts spindle positioning and nuclear segregation. | Bud cortex, cytoplasmic microtubules, spindle pole bodies, mother–bud neck | Time-lapse microscopy and deletion-mutant phenotyping (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003) | About 83% of spindle length could remain on the mother side in mutants; cortex-associated microtubules detached after approximately 10 s instead of sliding. Growth rates were similar in mutant and wild type, 4.92 versus 4.67 µm min⁻¹. |
| 2022 dynein–Pac1 structure | A high-resolution yeast complex established two conserved Pac1 contacts: a ring site near AAA3/AAA4 and a stalk site emerging from AAA4. Pac1 dimerization and both interfaces contribute to in-vivo dynein function. | Dyn1 AAA+ ring and stalk; paired Pac1 β-propellers | Cryo-EM and structure-guided endogenous yeast mutations (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000015) | The structure was resolved at 3.1 Å. Pac1 E253A/H254A strongly reduced dynein foci; S248Q was a partial separation-of-function mutant; F185D/I189D/R494A altered the Pac1 dimer interface. Some activation conclusions rely partly on human reconstitution. |
| 2023 Chi molecular-wedge complex | Two Pac1/Lis1 dimers wedge between two yeast dynein motor domains in the Chi intermediate, opposing the autoinhibited Phi arrangement and promoting progression toward active dynein complexes. | Between paired Dyn1 motor domains; Chi contacts | Yeast cryo-EM, structure-guided *S. cerevisiae* assays, and human reconstitution (pqac-00000014) | The observed complex contains two motor domains and two Pac1/Lis1 dimers. Chi contacts were required for Pac1 regulation in yeast; effects on dynein–dynactin–adaptor assembly were tested mainly with human proteins. |
| Microtubule-triggered Pac1 release | Dynein’s transition into its microtubule-bound conformation lowers Pac1 affinity, providing an allosteric mechanism for regulator release before sustained cortical or cargo-associated transport. | Dyn1 ring–stalk pathway; microtubule; subsequently Num1-associated dynein | Cryo-EM, conformational mutants, chromatography, and yeast cellular-focus assays (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000012) | Microtubule binding remodels the AAA5-loop, AAA4-helix, and AAA3-loop interface and distorts the Pac1-binding site by approximately 3.2 Å. Temporal coupling to Num1 in living yeast remains partly model-based. |
| 2024 force and motility mechanism | With purified yeast dynein, Pac1/Lis1 slows movement through its AAA+ ring interaction and prolongs stalls. Stalk binding reduces force-induced detachment by interfering with stalk-registry changes but does not measurably increase force generation. | Dyn1 AAA+ ring, stalk, and microtubule under assisting or hindering load | Single-molecule fluorescence and optical trapping (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) | One regulator slows dynein and increases run time; two enhance both effects. Stalk-interface mutations partly restore detachment asymmetry but not velocity. These are direct purified yeast-motor results, not whole-cell measurements. |
