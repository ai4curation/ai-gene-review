Gene Description

Met5 (Ecm17) is the beta (hemoprotein) subunit of the assimilatory NADPH-sulfite reductase of Saccharomyces cerevisiae (EC 1.8.1.2). Two Met5 and two Met10 subunits form a cytoplasmic alpha2beta2 hemoflavoprotein that reduces sulfite to hydrogen sulfide with six electrons from NADPH, the final reductive step of sulfate assimilation; the sulfide is then incorporated into homocysteine and from there into methionine, cysteine and glutathione. Met5 holds the catalytic siroheme-[4Fe-4S] centre (C-terminal nitrite/sulfite reductase ferredoxin-like and 4Fe-4S domains), which needs siroheme made by Met1 and Met8. It also has a flavodoxin-like FMN-binding domain, which in bacterial sulfite reductases sits on the flavoprotein subunit. met5 mutants lack sulfite reductase activity, require organic sulfur, and in wine yeast give much lower H2S release during fermentation. Assignment of FMN to the beta subunit is supported by flavodoxin-domain architecture and holoenzyme cofactor content, rather than a direct isolated-subunit FMN assay.
