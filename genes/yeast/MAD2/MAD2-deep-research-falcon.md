# Functional annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* MAD2 (UniProt P40958)

## Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified as the budding-yeast mitotic spindle-checkpoint protein Mad2: gene **MAD2**, systematic locus **YJL030W**, in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. Primary literature mapped MAD2 to YJL030W and described a 196-amino-acid protein, matching the supplied UniProt P40958 identity. No conflicting same-symbol protein was used in this report. Mad2 belongs to the Mad2/HORMA-domain family, consistent with the supplied InterPro and Pfam assignments. (chen1999thespindlecheckpoint pages 1-3, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3)

Mad2 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. It is a conformationally regulated protein–protein interaction module and checkpoint adapter. Its principal function is to convert the presence of unattached or improperly attached kinetochores into inhibition of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome activated by Cdc20 (APC/C–Cdc20). This delays securin/Pds1 and mitotic-cyclin destruction, anaphase onset, and mitotic exit until chromosome–spindle attachment defects are corrected. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 1-2, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3)

## 1. Identity and domain verification

The gene symbol is not ambiguous in the specified context. Chen and colleagues cloned budding-yeast MAD2, explicitly associated it with **YJL030W**, and reported that it encodes a **196-residue** protein whose abundance remains relatively constant through the cell cycle. Their allelic and deletion analyses directly established its spindle-checkpoint function in *S. cerevisiae*. The supplied strain designation—ATCC 204508/S288c—and UniProt accession P40958 therefore identify the intended budding-yeast protein rather than mammalian MAD2L1 or a fungal protein from another species. (chen1999thespindlecheckpoint pages 1-3)

The domain assignments also align with current understanding. Mad2 is almost entirely a **HORMA-domain molecular switch**. HORMA proteins use a mobile C-terminal “safety belt” to capture short closure motifs in binding partners. Mad2 adopts at least two native folded states: open Mad2 (O-Mad2), which is relatively inactive toward Cdc20, and closed Mad2 (C-Mad2), which topologically captures a Mad2-interacting motif in Mad1 or Cdc20. A 2023 structural synthesis identifies conversion of O-Mad2 to C-Mad2, coupled to Cdc20 engagement, as the central activation step in mitotic-checkpoint-complex formation. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 13-15)

This structural model is experimentally validated in budding yeast. Mutations at the yeast O-Mad2/C-Mad2 interaction surface failed to restore checkpoint function in a *mad2* deletion strain. Importantly, the mutant proteins retained Mad1 binding but were severely defective in binding Cdc20 in vivo, separating Mad1 association from productive checkpoint-signal propagation. (nezi2006accumulationofmad2–cdc20 pages 1-2, nezi2006accumulationofmad2–cdc20 pages 8-9)

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Mad1-bound receptor and conformational template

Mad2 forms a tight complex with Mad1. In direct *S. cerevisiae* experiments, gel filtration and co-immunoprecipitation showed that this association is stable, independent of cell-cycle stage and other known checkpoint proteins, and includes the resolved phosphorylated forms of Mad1. Mutations that disrupt this complex impair checkpoint signaling and Mad1 hyperphosphorylation. (chen1999thespindlecheckpoint pages 1-3)

In the accepted template model, Mad1-bound C-Mad2 provides a docking surface for soluble O-Mad2. This O–C interaction helps overcome the otherwise unfavorable kinetic barrier to Mad2 closure around the Cdc20 Mad2-interacting motif. Yeast interface mutants that bind Mad1 normally but fail to bind Cdc20 or support the checkpoint provide especially strong organism-specific evidence for this mechanism. (nezi2006accumulationofmad2–cdc20 pages 1-2, nezi2006accumulationofmad2–cdc20 pages 8-9)

### 2.2 Cdc20 capture and mitotic checkpoint complex formation

Once converted to the closed state, Mad2 binds Cdc20. C-Mad2–Cdc20 associates with Mad3 and Bub3 to produce the yeast mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC; Mad2–Mad3–Bub3–Cdc20). Yeast biochemical work detected both a smaller MCC and a more abundant Mad2–Cdc20 complex during mitosis. Mad2 and Mad3 are required not only to establish but also to maintain checkpoint arrest. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 1-2, poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 11-12)

The principal inhibited target is **Cdc20**, the substrate-recognition activator of APC/C. MCC binding suppresses APC/C ubiquitin-ligase activity, preventing degradation of securin/Pds1 and mitotic cyclins. Securin retention prevents separase-dependent sister-chromatid separation, thereby delaying anaphase. Thus Mad2 has no catalytic reaction or small-molecule substrate; its relevant molecular specificity is for closure-motif-bearing protein ligands, principally **Mad1 and Cdc20**, in different functional contexts. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 1-2, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3)

An important mechanistic qualification is that yeast Mad2-containing complexes can assemble away from kinetochores, but complexes formed without functional kinetochores are not sufficient for full APC/C inhibition. Kinetochores therefore act less as the only physical assembly site than as regulated catalytic/signaling platforms that confer productive checkpoint activity. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 11-12)

## 3. Pathway context

The canonical pathway can be summarized as follows:

1. An unattached or incorrectly attached kinetochore activates the spindle assembly checkpoint.
2. Mps1-dependent phosphorylation organizes a kinetochore scaffold containing Bub proteins and Mad1–C-Mad2.
3. Mad1-bound C-Mad2 recruits O-Mad2 and promotes its conversion into C-Mad2 while Cdc20 is positioned for capture.
4. C-Mad2–Cdc20 joins Mad3–Bub3 to form MCC.
5. MCC inhibits APC/C–Cdc20, stabilizing Pds1/securin and mitotic cyclins and delaying anaphase.
6. Correct bipolar attachment silences checkpoint signaling, permitting APC/C activation and chromosome segregation. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 1-3, fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 13-15)

This makes Mad2 a signal-transduction adapter at the core of the **spindle assembly checkpoint**, chromosome-segregation, metaphase-to-anaphase, and APC/C regulatory pathways. Foundational yeast genetics showed that defects in microtubule polymerization, spindle-pole-body duplication, motors, and kinetochore components activate the MAD-dependent checkpoint, illustrating that Mad2 responds to diverse spindle lesions through a shared attachment-surveillance pathway.

## 4. Cellular localization

Because budding yeast undergoes closed mitosis, Mad2 functions within the intact nucleus rather than entering after nuclear-envelope breakdown. During much of an unperturbed cell cycle, Mad1 and Mad2 reside predominantly at nuclear pore complexes (NPCs), associated with a Nup53/Nup157/Nup170-containing nucleoporin subcomplex. Mad2’s NPC association depends on Mad1. (iouk2002theyeastnuclear pages 1-2)

Checkpoint activation changes this localization. After nocodazole-induced spindle disruption, Mad2-GFP was released from NPCs and accumulated at kinetochores, whereas Mad1 largely remained NPC-associated in the cited experiment. Among arrested cells displaying both Mad2-GFP and the kinetochore marker Mtw1-CFP, **94% showed overlapping foci**; Mtw1 and Mad2 foci were detectable in 75% and 50% of cells, respectively. Mad2 was not detectably localized to spindle-pole bodies under those conditions. (iouk2002theyeastnuclear pages 3-4)

These observations support a dynamic nuclear pool: NPC-associated Mad1–Mad2 may act as a reservoir or regulatory scaffold, while checkpoint activation mobilizes Mad2 to kinetochores where productive Cdc20-inhibitory signaling is generated. The nuclear-pore association may also connect checkpoint regulation to nuclear transport, although that is secondary to Mad2’s core chromosome-attachment surveillance function. (iouk2002theyeastnuclear pages 1-2, iouk2002theyeastnuclear pages 3-4)

## 5. Biological processes and phenotypic evidence

### Mitosis and genome stability

The primary phenotype of MAD2 deficiency is failure to delay anaphase when spindle assembly or kinetochore attachment is defective. Cells then segregate chromosomes without a functional spindle, causing severe chromosome mis-segregation and rapid loss of viability under spindle stress. Mad2 is therefore conditionally crucial rather than simply a constitutive structural component of the spindle. (chen1999thespindlecheckpoint pages 1-3)

### Meiosis I

A 2024 *Current Biology* study showed that budding-yeast Mad2 also has functions distinguishable from Mad3 during meiosis I. Both proteins contribute to checkpoint delay, but Mad2 is specifically important for homolog biorientation and for orderly progression from prophase exit to metaphase I. Chromosome-V homolog nondisjunction was approximately **5% in *mad3Δ***, **10–15% in *mad2Δ***, and **~20% in the double mutant**, demonstrating additive, partly independent functions. Loss of Mad1 or Mad2 also allowed Cdc14 release before kinetochore-cluster splitting in a subset of cells, indicating defective ordering of metaphase and anaphase events. This study used SK1-derived meiotic strains, so the functions are attributable to the *S. cerevisiae* ortholog but the quantitative values should not be treated as S288c-strain measurements. Published 19 August 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.07.025. (mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 1-3, mukherjee2024distinctrolesof pages 8-10)

### Persistent DNA damage

A second 2024 study identified a context-dependent role for Mad2 in sustaining very prolonged arrest after irreparable DNA double-strand breaks. One DSB normally produced a pre-anaphase arrest of approximately **12–15 hours**, followed by adaptation. Two persistent DSBs generated arrest lasting **more than 24 hours**. Ddc2, Rad9, and Rad53 were initially required, but became dispensable after about 15 hours; continued arrest instead required Mad1, Mad2, and Bub2. Depleting Mad1 or Mad2 caused re-entry beginning around 15 hours even while Rad53 remained hyperphosphorylated. The authors interpret this as a handoff from the DNA-damage checkpoint to spindle-checkpoint machinery, possibly because centromere-proximal breaks perturb kinetochore attachment. Published 10 December 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.94334. (zhou2024prolongedcellcycle pages 1-2, zhou2024prolongedcellcycle pages 18-20)

This is an extension of Mad2’s attachment-surveillance function, not evidence that Mad2 directly detects or repairs DNA.

## 6. Recent developments and experimental applications

### Engineered karyotypes

A 2024 chromosome-fusion study used Mad2-dependent checkpoint behavior to probe the mechanical limits of the yeast spindle. Strains with fewer than five chromosomes—and therefore fewer than five centromere/kinetochore attachment units—showed spindle defects, checkpoint activation, and prolonged metaphase. These strains had a **5–10-minute increase in doubling time**, while three-chromosome cells required about **8 additional minutes** to traverse mitosis. The proposed explanation is that too few kinetochore microtubules cannot adequately oppose outward spindle forces, producing attachment or tension defects that activate the checkpoint. This demonstrates a real-world research application of MAD2 perturbation as a sensor of spindle mechanics and a tool in synthetic-karyotype biology. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.10.25.563899. (helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrains pages 11-14, helsen2024spindlearchitectureconstrains pages 1-4)

### Genetic and chemical screening

MAD2 deletion or checkpoint-dependent arrest is widely useful in yeast genetics for determining whether a growth defect or drug response derives from spindle/kinetochore stress. The system also supports antifungal-target discovery upstream of Mad2: overexpressed Mps1 can activate the Mad1–Mad2 checkpoint and arrest budding yeast, providing a whole-cell assay architecture for testing fungal Mps1 orthologs and inhibitors. Mad2 itself is best regarded as a pathway readout and interaction switch rather than an enzyme amenable to conventional catalytic assays.

### Reassessment of indirect genome-instability phenotypes

Recent work also cautions against assigning every genetic phenotype of SAC mutants directly to Mad2. A 2024 study revisited claims that the spindle checkpoint promotes gross chromosomal rearrangements. It found that the apparent effect arose from synthetic lethality between checkpoint disruption and loss of the kinesin gene CIN8 in the selection assay; after adding an extra CIN8 copy, the evidence did not support a direct SAC role in promoting GCR formation. This is an important expert-level annotation principle: phenotypes should be attributed to MAD2 only after excluding assay-specific genetic interactions. Preprint posted 13 April 2024; DOI: https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.11.589040. (yao2024revisitingtherole pages 1-5, yao2024revisitingtherole pages 14-18)

## 7. Expert interpretation and confidence

The strongest functional annotation is:

> **Mad2 is a nuclear HORMA-domain spindle-checkpoint adapter that, through Mad1-catalyzed conformational conversion and Cdc20 capture, promotes MCC formation and inhibits APC/C–Cdc20 until kinetochores achieve correct spindle attachment.**

Confidence is **high** because the conclusion is supported by convergent budding-yeast genetics, co-immunoprecipitation, biochemical complex analysis, live-cell localization, conformational-interface mutants, and conserved structural studies. The interaction with Mad1, engagement of Cdc20, formation of Mad2-containing inhibitory complexes, and NPC-to-kinetochore redistribution are directly demonstrated in *S. cerevisiae*. (poddar2005twocomplexesof pages 1-2, nezi2006accumulationofmad2–cdc20 pages 1-2, chen1999thespindlecheckpoint pages 1-3, iouk2002theyeastnuclear pages 3-4)

The exact molecular choreography of kinetochore-catalyzed MCC assembly continues to be refined. Current structural opinion emphasizes not merely local concentration of components but coordinated repositioning of Mad2 and the Cdc20 interaction motif by a phosphorylated Bub1–Mad1 scaffold. Some details—including how much Cdc20 autoinhibition contributes and the precise relative contributions of kinetochore-bound versus soluble complex assembly—remain unresolved. (fischer2023kinetochorecatalyzedmccformation pages 13-15)

## 8. Recommended database-style annotation

- **Molecular function:** conformationally regulated checkpoint adapter; Mad1- and Cdc20-binding HORMA-domain protein; component of the mitotic checkpoint complex.
- **Primary biological process:** spindle assembly checkpoint signaling and negative regulation of the metaphase-to-anaphase transition.
- **Direct pathway output:** inhibition of APC/C–Cdc20, stabilization of securin/Pds1 and mitotic cyclins, and delayed chromosome separation.
- **Primary site of action:** nucleus; nuclear pore-associated pool during much of the cell cycle and kinetochore-enriched pool following checkpoint activation.
- **Additional supported roles:** homolog biorientation and event ordering in meiosis I; maintenance of prolonged arrest after particular persistent DNA-damage configurations.
- **Not supported as a primary function:** enzymatic catalysis, transport, direct microtubule binding, or direct DNA repair.
