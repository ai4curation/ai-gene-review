MRX20 / YFR045W (P43617): does this SLC25 carrier transport citrate?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-mrx20-carrier-substrate-class Target: Saccharomyces cerevisiae S288C (NCBITaxon:559292), UniProt P43617, gene directory genes/yeast/MRX20, source genes/yeast/MRX20/MRX20-ai-review.yaml, focus type free_text. Verdict: Partially supported. The carrier class is supported, the citrate assignment is over-annotated.

Summary

The seed hypothesis has two parts, and the evidence splits them cleanly. MRX20 is a structurally competent mitochondrial carrier whose substrate site has the chemistry of a carboxylic-acid anion carrier: that part is supported. MRX20 transports citrate: that part has no support of any kind, and one quantitative structural signal argues against it.

A geometric scan of the substrate cavity across 30 AlphaFold SLC25 models, validated by recovering the mutagenesis-defined contact residues of AAC2 and CTP1, places MRX20's three Robinson and Kunji contact points at K69 (point I, helix H2), T190-R193-Q194-x-x-N197 (point II, H4) and R281 / K284 (point III, H6). That is the CTP1 arrangement (K83; R181-Q182-x-x-N185; R276/R279) and the SFC1 arrangement (K85; R184-Q185-x-x-N188; R282/R285) residue for residue, with one Lys-for-Arg swap at point III. The matrix salt-bridge network is complete and three-fold (E30-K124 2.81 Å, E121-K243 2.64 Å, D240-K33 4.23 Å), so the m-gate that seals the translocation pathway is intact and the protein is not a degenerate fold-only family member. The cavity carries 4 basic and 1 acidic residue, net +3, the same balance as CTP1.

The citrate assignment fails on a single discriminating position and on provenance. Eight residues C-terminal to the contact point II anchor, all three tricarboxylate carriers in the panel carry arginine (CTP1 R189, YHM2 R189, human SLC25A1 R198), the five dicarboxylate carriers carry an amide (SFC1 N192, DIC1 Q185, SLC25A10 Q178, SLC25A11 Q198, OAC1 Q208) and the three oxodicarboxylate carriers carry tyrosine (ODC1 Y194, ODC2 Y190, SLC25A21 Y186). MRX20 has Gln201, the dicarboxylate residue (Fisher exact one-sided p = 0.0059; permutation p = 0.0066 over 20,000 permutations; pLDDT 89; selected in 27 of 27 cavity-scan parameter settings). CTP1 R189 is one of two arginines shown by site-directed mutagenesis to be required for citrate transport PMID: 10702279. A leave-one-out nearest-neighbour classifier built on the four-residue contact point II motif is correct in 9 of 11 held-out carboxylate carriers (majority baseline 0.45, binomial one-sided p = 0.0160), so the motif carries real class information. Applied to MRX20 it excludes the oxodicarboxylate class outright (scores 1, 0, 0) and ranks CTP1 and YHM2 at 17 barely above SFC1 at 16, which is not a separation that can carry a citrate call.

On the annotation side, every citrate term on P43617 is IBA only. GO:0071913 and GO:0006843 come from GO_REF:0000033 (PAINT), propagated from the SLC25A1 orthology group plus yeast CTP1 (SGD:S000000495). The PANTHER subfamily that supplies the inference, PTHR45788, contains exactly three reviewed yeast proteins: CTP1 (citrate, tricarboxylate), SFC1 (succinate/fumarate, dicarboxylate) and MRX20. A family that disagrees with itself about substrate class cannot transfer one. SGD separately records GO:0003674 with evidence code ND, in direct conflict with the GO_Central citrate transporter activity. No experimental evidence on MRX20 bears on substrate at all: the BioID, AP-MS, Puf3 and deletion-survey data all constrain where the protein is, which is the mitochondrial inner membrane.

Key findings

F002: MRX20 has an intact carrier fold and a complete three-fold matrix salt-bridge network

The first question is whether MRX20 is a working transporter at all, because its only experimental association is with mitoribosome complexes, which would be consistent with a carrier-fold protein repurposed as a membrane scaffold. The structural answer is that the transport machinery is intact.

A regex scan of all 34 S. cerevisiae PF00153 proteins for the mitochondrial carrier signature P-x-[DE]-x-x-[KR] finds three copies in MRX20: positions 28 (PFEYLK), 119 (PFENIK) and 238 (PIDVVK). CTP1 has three at 32, 129 and 234; SFC1 has three at 30, 131 and 234. In the AlphaFold model AF-P43617-F1-v4, all three inter-repeat matrix salt bridges are formed, measured as the minimum carboxylate-oxygen to guanidinium or amine-nitrogen distance: E30-K124 at 2.81 Å, E121-K243 at 2.64 Å and D240-K33 at 4.23 Å. The CTP1 equivalents are 2.66, 2.68 and 3.60 Å; the SFC1 equivalents are 3.74, 3.17 and 4.78 Å. Helix-axis analysis recovers six transmembrane helices spanning about 45 Å.

The salt-bridge network is the m-gate that seals the translocation pathway on the matrix side, and its perturbation by bound substrate is the proposed trigger for the translocation event PMID: 16759636. MRX20 maintains it to the same standard as two carriers with assayed transport function. That is an argument from conservation rather than from activity, so it supports plausibility of transport, not demonstration of it.

F003: The three contact points carry carboxylate-carrier chemistry

The decisive analysis the seed hypothesis asked for is a comparison of contact points I, II and III. The published Robinson and Kunji contact point residue list could not be retrieved programmatically (see Limitations), so the contact points were defined geometrically from structure and then validated against residues known from mutagenesis.

The scan selects residues whose distal side-chain atom lies 4 to 16 Å above the matrix salt-bridge network centroid, within 9 Å of the helix-bundle axis, and radially inward of its own alpha-carbon. The membrane normal is taken from the sign-aligned SVD directions of the six longest transmembrane helices, and the origin from the salt-bridge network centroid. Two controls establish that the definition returns functionally real residues. On yeast AAC2/PET9 the scan returns E45, R96, Y203, T238, S242, S245, N291 and R294, where R96, Y203 and R294 correspond to bovine AAC1 R79, Y186 and R279, the known nucleotide contact points. On CTP1 it returns R181, R276 and R279, three residues shown by mutagenesis to be required for citrate transport PMID: 10702279.

It argues against nucleotide, cationic amino acid, metal and cofactor substrates, all of which show different cavity compositions in the same scan (PET9 +1 with Tyr at point II, FLX1 +2 with no carboxylate-type point II, ORT1 with Trp at the +8 position).

F004: MRX20 lacks the arginine that marks every tricarboxylate carrier at contact point II +8

Using the geometrically defined contact point II anchor as the reference, the residue eight positions C-terminal lines the cavity in 17 of the 18 carriers examined, judged by its side-chain tip being closer to the bundle axis than its own alpha-carbon. That position separates the three substrate classes almost perfectly.

Of the 14 non-tricarboxylate carriers, only the phosphate carrier MIR1 (K187) carries a basic residue at this position, giving a Fisher exact one-sided p = 0.0059 for the association between a basic +8 residue and tricarboxylate specificity. The mechanistic reading is direct: CTP1 R181 and R189 are both required for citrate transport, positive charge at both positions is essential and the chemistry of the guanidinium group is not, since Lys or chemically restored positive charge supports function PMID: 10702279. Those two arginines are the extra positive charge that a tricarboxylate needs over a dicarboxylate. MRX20 keeps the first (R193, the point II anchor) and lacks the second.

F008: The Gln201 call survives confidence, parameter and permutation checks

A single-position structural argument from a predicted model needs sensitivity analysis before a curator can use it.

AlphaFold pLDDT at the MRX20 contact point II residues R193, Q194, N197 and Q201 is 92, 92, 92 and 89, above the model mean of 81, so the discriminating residue sits in a confidently modelled helix. Sweeping the cavity scan over 27 parameter combinations (z lower bound 2, 4 or 6 Å; z upper bound 14, 16 or 18 Å; radial cut-off 8, 9 or 10 Å) selects the MRX20 anchor R193 in 27 of 27 settings, and selects the CTP1 R181, YHM2 R181, DIC1 R177, ODC1 R186, ODC2 R182, SLC25A21 R178 and GGC1 R180 anchors in 27 of 27 as well. The weakest anchors in the panel are CRC1 (9 of 27) and MIR1 (12 of 27), both of which are non-carboxylate carriers and peripheral to the argument. A permutation test over the 16 carriers of known substrate, asking how often all three tricarboxylate carriers carry Arg or Lys at +8 by chance, gives p = 0.0066 over 20,000 permutations, matching the Fisher p = 0.0059.

The limit on this result is panel size. With three tricarboxylate carriers available, the rule is supported but not strongly powered, and no single position can establish a substrate on its own.

F010: A leave-one-out classifier assigns the class and refuses the citrate call

To test how much substrate-class information the contact point actually carries, each carrier was encoded as the four-residue motif at the H4 anchor, +1, +4 and +8, with similarity scored as the sum of BLOSUM62 values. AAC2 was excluded because its anchor failed the inward-facing test (anchor z = -4.3 Å), so the scan found no genuine cavity anchor for it, leaving 16 carriers of known substrate plus MRX20.

Leave-one-out 1-NN over the 11 carboxylate carriers, with the full 16-carrier panel as the reference set, is correct 9 of 11 times against a majority-class baseline of 0.45 (binomial one-sided p = 0.0160). The two errors are SFC1 RQNN called tricarboxylate and OAC1 RTGQ called nucleotide.

F001 and F005: The propagation source is a family that spans two substrate classes, and no close ortholog exists

The InterPro API (entry/all/protein/uniprot/P43617) returns PANTHER PTHR45788 "SUCCINATE/FUMARATE MITOCHONDRIAL TRANSPORTER-RELATED", integrated into InterPro IPR049563 "Tricarboxylate transport protein-like". The family contains 8111 proteins across 8795 taxa in 6 subfamilies. Querying reviewed S. cerevisiae S288C proteins in PTHR45788 returns exactly three: P33303 (SFC1, succinate/fumarate), P38152 (CTP1, citrate) and P43617 (MRX20). The family name and the InterPro name already disagree about substrate class, and so do its two characterised yeast members.

Sequence distance makes the transfer worse. Pairwise global alignment of MRX20 against all 33 other yeast PF00153 carriers puts CTP1 top at 33.8% identity, against a within-family median of 23.1% (94.8th percentile), with every other carrier at or below 28%. Yeast carrier pairs that genuinely share a substrate sit far higher: ODC1/ODC2 at 64.7%, AAC1/AAC3 at 74.6%, MRS3/MRS4 at 73.4%.

UniRef50_P43617 contains 17 members, all Saccharomyces (S288C, YJM789, RM11-1a, AWRI1631, S. paradoxus, S. mikatae, S. kudriavzevii, S. arboricola, S. uvarum, S. eubayanus, S. pastorianus and cerevisiae × kudriavzevii hybrids). No ortholog outside the genus reaches 50% identity, so the "fungal subfamily ortholog" comparison the seed hypothesis called for could not be run beyond a genus whose members are 85 to 99% identical, which carries no information about which residues are under specificity constraint. One member, A0A6C1DQS1_SACPS, is labelled "Mitochondrial dicarboxylate transporter" while the rest are "YFR045W-like protein" or "Uncharacterized protein". That label is unreviewed database carry-over, and it propagates a different substrate class from the one GOA propagates.

F007: The experimental record places MRX20 at the inner membrane and says nothing about substrate

SGD/BioGRID returns 114 interaction records for MRX20 with 101 unique partners. Of those, 82 are negative genetic and 16 are physical. The physical set is inner-membrane gene-expression machinery: BioID proximity labelling scores MRX20 as proximal to CBP3, MBA1, MRP20, MRPL4, MRPL15, MSS51 and OXA1 at log2 fold change ≥ 1.5 and p ≤ 0.05 PMID: 32877643, the study that gave the gene its name. Affinity capture MS recovers COX20 PMID: 27550809, TIM23 PMID: 25124039, HSC82 PMID: 37295430 and MRA1 PMID: 38508182. Puf3 binds the MRX20 3' UTR PMID: 23409723, the signature of nuclear-encoded mitochondrial mRNAs.

Deletion phenotypes come entirely from large-scale surveys and are mild: viable, decreased competitive fitness, decreased resistance to valinomycin PMID: 27711131 and nickel sulfate PMID: 19917080, decreased rate of proline utilization as nitrogen source PMID: 24721214 and abnormal vacuolar morphology in sodium chloride PMID: 23383298. No classical phenotype is curated. Mean protein abundance at SGD is 710 molecules per cell.

This upgrades the CC call from ISS to experimental support. It constrains the substrate not at all. No carbon-source growth defect of the kind a citrate carrier deletion would be expected to show appears anywhere in the record.

F009: The reference 309-residue translation carries a 24-residue N-terminal extension the rest of the genus lacks

UniProt P43617 is 309 residues beginning MANQNSDLYKQITAGSVAAVFQTT-M25-TYPFEYLK. Global alignment against four Saccharomyces orthologs (J8PP08 S. arboricola, J8TXW4 S. kudriavzevii, H0GFS1 cerevisiae × kudriavzevii, A0A6C1DQS1 S. pastorianus), all 285 residues, shows every one starting at MTYPFEYLK, exactly the residue aligned to S288C Met25, at 85.6 to 99.3% identity over the aligned region. Belenkiy et al. resequenced YFR045W and reported 285 residues PMID: 10930523.

The reference translation most likely uses an upstream in-frame ATG the rest of the genus does not use. The carrier domain, the three signature motifs and all contact points lie downstream, so the substrate inference does not depend on which start is correct. Any residue numbering in future mutagenesis must be stated explicitly against P43617.

Mechanistic model

The immediate molecular function under test is alternating access transport: a six-helix carrier in the mitochondrial inner membrane binds a small anion at a single central site at the midpoint of the membrane, and substrate binding perturbs the matrix salt-bridge network to trigger the conformational change PMID: 16759636. Reconstitution kinetics on human SLC25A1, SLC25A10 and SLC25A11 show these carriers are monomers with a single binding site operating by a ping-pong mechanism PMID: 38937634, which is what licenses reading specificity off one cavity.

For MRX20, what the data establish is the machinery and the charge chemistry of the site, not the cargo.

Everything else on the MRX20 record is positional or downstream. The BioID proximity to OXA1, MBA1, MSS51, CBP3 and four mitoribosomal proteins, the COX20 and TIM23 co-purifications and the Puf3 3' UTR binding all say where the protein is. The deletion phenotypes (competitive fitness, valinomycin and nickel resistance, proline utilization rate, vacuole morphology in NaCl) are pleiotropic survey readouts from which no substrate can be read. An abundance of 710 molecules per cell is consistent with a working carrier and says nothing about cargo.

The MIOREX association deserves separate handling. MIOREX complexes are expressosome-like assemblies of mitoribosomes with factors for every step of post-transcriptional gene expression, distributed throughout the mitochondrial network PMID: 25683707, and the BioID network places newly synthesized proteins at ribosomal tunnel exit factors that mediate membrane insertion and cofactor acquisition PMID: 32877643. An inner-membrane carrier sitting near membrane-bound translation sites is a neighbourhood observation. It does not make MRX20 a gene-expression factor, and the intact m-gate argues that the transport machinery is under selection.

Evidence base

The contact point framework. Robinson and Kunji identified a common substrate binding site by combining amino acid conservation, distance and chemical constraints with modelling on the ADP/ATP translocase structure, stating that it "enables the substrate specificity of uncharacterized mitochondrial carriers to be predicted" PMID: 16469842. The companion review adds that "The main contact points in the site are related by threefold symmetry like the common structural fold" and that the translocation trigger "is likely to be the substrate-induced perturbation of the salt bridge network at the bottom of the cavity" PMID: 16759636. These two papers justify the whole approach. Their specific residue tables could not be retrieved (see Limitations), which is why the contact points here are geometric and validated against mutagenesis rather than copied.

The citrate determinant. The CTP1 mutagenesis study is the single most load-bearing primary reference. Replacement of either Arg181 or Arg189 with Cys abolishes function, while replacement with Lys or chemical restoration of positive charge supports it, leading the authors to conclude that "the polar face of alpha-helical transmembrane domain IV, within which Arg(181), Arg(189), and Cys(192) are located, constitutes an essential portion of the citrate translocation pathway" PMID: 10702279. That paper both validates the scan (it returns R181 for CTP1) and supplies the mechanistic reason the +8 position matters. MRX20 has Gln there.

The dicarboxylate comparator. The yeast DIC1 identification paper gives the substrate profile of the alternative class: reconstituted DIC1 supports malonate uptake with internal malonate, malate, succinate and phosphate, "whereas other anions tested did not", with Km 1.55 mM and Vmax 3.0 µmol/min/mg PMID: 9020177. This defines the panel any MRX20 transport assay should use.

The transport mechanism. The monomer and single-site result for SLC25A1, SLC25A10 and SLC25A11 supports reading specificity off a single cavity, since these carriers "are monomers, except for dimeric SLC25A13, and operate with a ping-pong kinetic mechanism" PMID: 38937634. Electrostatic analysis of AAC further indicates that a strong positive lumen potential "is likely a common attribute among the entire family of mitochondrial carriers" PMID: 18621725, which is consistent with the net +3 cavity charge measured for MRX20 but also means that a positive cavity alone is weak evidence of anion specificity, since it is family-wide.

The annotation source. 10930523 is the ISS reference behind both SGD terms. Its own description of YFR045W is a resequencing and consensus-residue analysis: the four resequenced genes "have 545, 285, 902, and 502 residues, respectively", and "We do now find these five consensus residues also in the new sequences of YNL083W and YFR045W" PMID: 10930523. That supports family membership and nothing about substrate.

The localization source. The BioID study describes "a comprehensive network of factors involved in biogenesis of mitochondrial encoded proteins", in which "newly synthesized proteins are directly handed over to ribosomal tunnel exit-bound factors that mediate membrane insertion, co-factor acquisition, or their mounting into OXPHOS complexes" PMID: 32877643. MRX20 appears in that network, which is how it got its name.

Two papers in the literature set bear on the question only obliquely. The SLC25A20 carnitine/acylcarnitine review PMID: 41875964 supports the general single-binding-centred-gated-pore model but concerns a different substrate class. The citrate transporter inhibitor study PMID: 20686672 describes two citrate binding sites within the CTP and a homology model of the unrelated plasma membrane transporter, which is orientation rather than evidence for MRX20.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

No transport assay for MRX20 exists in any organism. Checked: PubMed searches on MRX20, YFR045W and carrier substrate terms; SGD GO and phenotype records; BioGRID. Every substrate statement in this report, including the recommended generalized term, is inference from a predicted structure. Resolved by proteoliposome reconstitution.

The Robinson and Kunji contact point residue table could not be read programmatically. Europe PMC fullTextXML for 16469842 returned HTTP 500; the PMC article pages for PMC1413793 and PMC2582046 returned short stubs rather than article bodies. The contact points used here were therefore defined geometrically from structure, not copied from the paper. The indirect validation that was possible: on AAC2 the scan returns R96, Y203 and R294, corresponding to bovine AAC1 R79, Y186 and R279; on CTP1 it returns R181, R276 and R279, the three mutagenesis-defined residues. A curator should check the published table against provenance/cavity_residues_panel.tsv.

Only three tricarboxylate carriers with a known substrate and a structure were available. The +8 rule rests on n = 3, so Fisher p = 0.0059 reflects a small but complete panel rather than a sampled one. Adding plant and trypanosome tricarboxylate carriers with assayed substrates would test it.

No fungal ortholog outside Saccharomyces was analysed. UniRef50_P43617 has 17 members, all Saccharomyces sensu stricto at 85 to 99% identity, so the subfamily contact point comparison the seed hypothesis specified could not be run in a way that carries selection information. A profile search across Saccharomycotina would test whether Gln201 is fixed.

The structures are AlphaFold models. Mean pLDDT for AF-P43617-F1-v4 is 81, with 89 to 92 at the contact point II residues, so the specific region is well modelled. Side-chain rotamers in a predicted model are still less reliable than in an experimental structure, and the whole panel shares any systematic bias AlphaFold has toward the ADP/ATP carrier training examples.

Whether the MIOREX association is functional. If MRX20 has a direct role in mitochondrial gene expression, the carrier annotation is a different kind of error from an over-specific substrate. Testing whether an mrx20 deletion affects mitochondrial translation would separate these.

The classifier cannot separate citrate from a C4 dicarboxylate. Its one carboxylate error is SFC1 called tricarboxylate, on exactly the RQNR-versus-RQNN axis where MRX20's RQNQ sits. This is the honest ceiling of the method, and it is why the recommended replacement term is at the organic anion level.

Discriminating tests and proposed follow-up

  1. Proteoliposome transport assay with a focused substrate panel. Express MRX20 in E. coli, purify from inclusion bodies and reconstitute, following the procedure that worked for yeast DIC1 and CTP1 PMID: 9020177. Test citrate, isocitrate, cis-aconitate, malate, succinate, fumarate, malonate, 2-oxoglutarate, oxaloacetate and phosphate by exchange with internal substrate. This settles the question. The structural prediction is uptake of a C4 dicarboxylate with weak or absent citrate transport.
  2. Reciprocal swap at the +8 position. If Gln201 is the determinant, MRX20 Q201R should confer or improve citrate transport in the assay above, and CTP1 R189Q should reduce it. This tests the inference directly instead of relying on panel statistics, and it is the cheapest way to falsify the central claim of this report.
  3. Targeted metabolomics on isolated mrx20 mitochondria. Measure matrix and cytosolic pools of citrate, malate, succinate, fumarate and 2-oxoglutarate from wild type and mrx20 on fermentable and non-fermentable carbon. A specific pool shift narrows the class without a reconstitution.
  4. Synthetic genetic array against the carrier set. Cross mrx20 with ctp1, sfc1, dic1, odc1 odc2, oac1 and yhm2. A redundancy partner localizes the function. The existing record already contains 82 negative genetic interactions for MRX20, so the raw SGA data may carry this signal already.
  5. Deeper ortholog sampling. Build a Saccharomycotina-wide profile alignment for PTHR45788:SF5 and test whether Gln at +8 is fixed. Fixation under selection strengthens the dicarboxylate call; variability weakens the single-residue argument.
  6. Read the Robinson and Kunji contact point table by hand and recompute the panel with the published residue assignments, confirming or correcting the geometric definition used here.
