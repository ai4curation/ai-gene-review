| Aspect | Evidence-based annotation | Representative evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | **CDC53/YDL132W; UniProt Q12018** from *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* S288c encodes Cdc53, also called yeast Cul1/Cullin-A. The supplied accession, organism, cullin-domain annotation, and SCF description are concordant with literature identifying Cdc53 as the essential yeast CRL1/SCF cullin; no similarly named protein from another organism is being annotated. | Reviews identify yCul1 as the vital yeast cullin that forms SCF complexes, while primary yeast studies identify Cdc53 as their common scaffold (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000012). |
| Primary molecular role | **Noncatalytic cullin scaffold.** Cdc53 does not itself recognize substrates or catalyze an enzyme reaction; it spatially organizes substrate-recognition and ubiquitin-transfer modules within an E3 ubiquitin ligase. Cysteine-less Cdc53 remained functional and Rub1-modified, arguing against a catalytic ubiquitin-thioester intermediate on Cdc53 (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000009). | In-vivo interaction mapping and reconstituted Sic1 ubiquitylation established Cdc53 as a shared structural platform rather than a substrate receptor (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000005). |
| SCF architecture | The N-terminal region binds **Skp1**, which couples interchangeable **F-box substrate receptors** to Cdc53; the C-terminal cullin region binds **Hrt1/Rbx1**, whose RING domain recruits ubiquitin-charged **Cdc34**. Thus the canonical complex is F-box protein–Skp1–Cdc53–Hrt1/Rbx1–Cdc34~Ub (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000012). | Cdc53 associates in vivo with Cdc34, Skp1, Cdc4, Grr1, and Met30. Cdc34–Cdc53 and Skp1/F-box–Cdc53 interactions are separable, demonstrating modular assembly (pqac-00000003). |
| Chemistry and substrate specificity | SCF complexes promote transfer of ubiquitin from the E2 Cdc34 to lysines on recruited proteins, often building proteasome-targeting chains. **Cdc53 has no intrinsic substrate specificity**: specificity resides principally in the F-box receptor and its degron recognition, frequently after substrate phosphorylation (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005). | Purified Cdc34–Cdc53–Skp1–Cdc4 ubiquitylates phosphorylated Sic1 in vitro. Distinct receptor requirements for Sic1, Cln2, and methionine-pathway control demonstrate that these are not direct Cdc53-specific substrates (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000009). |
| Rub1 regulation | Cdc53 is covalently modified on a lysine by **Rub1**, the yeast NEDD8 ortholog. Rub1 is activated by Ula1–Uba3, transferred through Ubc12, and conjugated with Hrt1/Rbx1 and Dcn1 assistance; cullin modification favors an active CRL configuration and is reversed by the COP9 signalosome (pqac-00000012). | Hrt1 serves both pathways: it engages Ubc12 during Rub1 conjugation and Cdc34 during ubiquitination. The modification is regulatory rather than a Cdc53 catalytic intermediate (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000012). |
| Localization | Cdc53 is an intracellular, soluble SCF-core protein rather than a membrane, secreted, or organellar structural protein. The gathered evidence does **not** establish one exclusive steady-state compartment for Cdc53; its functional site is best described as context- and receptor-dependent, including nuclear cell-cycle, chromatin, and replication functions and cytosolic proteostasis functions. | Nuclear action is supported by Sic1/kinetochore/meiotic and replisome pathways, whereas SCF–Das1 acts in protein quality control. These pathway locations should not be mistaken for proof that all Cdc53 is constitutively localized there (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015). |
| SCF–Cdc4 | Cdc4 recognizes phosphorylated cell-cycle and chromosome-associated substrates. Established outcomes include Sic1 destruction to permit G1/S progression, regulation of other cell-cycle proteins, Cse4 and Ame1 homeostasis, and control of synaptonemal-complex assembly during meiosis (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000007). | Loss of Cdc53 prevents Sic1 degradation and causes G1 arrest; meiosis-specific Cdc53 depletion disrupts synaptonemal-complex formation, with a conditional *cdc4* mutant producing a similar phenotype (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000007). |
| SCF–Grr1 | Grr1 directs Cdc53-containing SCF activity toward G1 cyclins and metabolic or signaling proteins; Cln2 is a well-supported receptor-specific substrate. | Cln2 was stabilized in *grr1Δ* but not in conditional *cdc4* or *met30* mutants, demonstrating receptor-defined specificity within the shared Cdc53 core (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000009). |
| SCF–Met30 | Met30 connects sulfur-metabolite and heavy-metal signals to transcription and cell-cycle control. Its principal regulator is Met4: SCF–Met30 ubiquitylation can inhibit Met4 without necessarily degrading it. Cadmium induces Met30 autoubiquitylation, Cdc48/p97–Shp1 recruitment, removal of Met30 from the Cdc53 core, and activation of stress programs (pqac-00000008). | Genetic evidence shows that Cdc53, Cdc34, Skp1, and Met30 are jointly required for methionine-dependent repression, while cadmium-responsive complex disassembly provides a real-time regulatory implementation of Cdc53-core remodeling (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000008). |
| SCF–Dia2 | At DNA-replication termination, SCF–Dia2 ubiquitylates Mcm7 in the CMG helicase, enabling Cdc48-mediated replisome extraction. Dia2 binds Mcm3/Mcm5; at active forks this interface is occluded by the excluded DNA strand, preventing premature helicase unloading and fork collapse (pqac-00000015). | Cryo-EM structures of yeast termination assemblies at **3.2–4.0 Å** resolution contained Hrt1–Cdc53–Skp1–Dia2 and showed how DNA gates ligase access to CMG (pqac-00000015). |
| SCF–Das1 and 2023 advance | A 2023 unbiased yeast C-degron survey implicated SCF–Das1 in removing orphan protein-complex subunits. Das1 is sequence-selective but unusually promiscuous, allowing one Cdc53-based ligase to recognize diverse exposed C termini and contribute to proteostasis (pqac-00000013). | The study reported **over 5,000** potential C-degrons; SCF–Das1 accounted for approximately **40%** of detected degrons. In the 12-residue random-peptide library, **4,726** sequences met the putative-degron threshold, and **3.6%** of cells expressed unstable variants versus **0.2%** and **0.4%** for 4- and 8-residue libraries (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000014). |
| Current interpretation and application | Cdc53 is best annotated as an **essential, reusable ubiquitination platform** whose biological range arises from receptor exchange and regulated assembly—not as an enzyme with one substrate. Yeast Cdc53 systems provide experimentally tractable models for degron discovery, phosphorylation-coupled degradation, replication termination, stress-responsive ligase remodeling, cullin neddylation, and targeted-protein-degradation design (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013). | A 2021 authoritative review describes CRLs as dynamic modular platforms and a major conceptual basis for small molecules that redirect ubiquitination, while recent yeast studies demonstrate receptor-specific degron mapping and structurally gated substrate access (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015). |
