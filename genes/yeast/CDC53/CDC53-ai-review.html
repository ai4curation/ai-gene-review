Gene Description

Cdc53 is the cullin scaffold of the Saccharomyces cerevisiae SCF (Skp1-Cdc53/Cul1-F-box protein) family of cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligases and the orthologue of human CUL1. It is an elongated, non-catalytic protein: its N-terminal region (residues 9-280) binds Skp1, which in turn carries one of the interchangeable F-box substrate receptors (Cdc4, Grr1, Met30, Dia2, Mdm30, Das1, Saf1, Ufo1, Hrt3 and others), while its C-terminal cullin-homology region binds the RING-H2 protein Hrt1/Rbx1 and, together with Hrt1, recruits ubiquitin-charged E2 enzymes (chiefly Cdc34, also Ubc4) so that the receptor-bound substrate and the E2 thioester are held in a productive geometry. Cdc53 forms no essential ubiquitin thioester (a cysteine-less mutant is fully functional); the Cdc53-Hrt1 heterodimer is the minimal ubiquitin ligase module that activates Cdc34, and the F-box protein defines which substrate is ubiquitinated. Its activity is switched on by covalent attachment of the ubiquitin-like protein Rub1 (yeast NEDD8) to a conserved C-terminal lysine, promoted by the Ubc12 E2 and the Dcn1 co-E3 and reversed by the COP9 signalosome; the unmodified cullin is sequestered by the CAND1-like exchange factor Lag2, which occupies the Skp1-binding site and is displaced by Skp1-F-box modules, and the rubylated cullin is recognised by the Cdc48/p97 cofactor Ubx5. Through its receptors, Cdc53-based SCF ligases build proteasome-targeting ubiquitin chains that drive the G1/S transition (SCF(Cdc4) destruction of the Clb-Cdk inhibitor Sic1 and of Cdc6, Far1 and Gcn4; SCF(Grr1) destruction of the G1 cyclins Cln1/Cln2 and of Gic2), repress methionine biosynthesis genes and couple sulfur and cadmium signals to transcription (SCF(Met30) ubiquitination of Met4), regulate DNA replication, replisome disassembly, S-phase checkpoint recovery and Sir4-dependent silencing (SCF(Dia2)), promote mitochondrial outer-membrane fusion through Fzo1 turnover (SCF(Mdm30)), relieve GAL1 repression by Mig2 (SCF(Das1)) and clear C-degron-bearing orphan proteins (SCF(Das1)). CDC53 is essential; cdc53 temperature-sensitive mutants arrest at G1/S with stabilised Sic1 and elongated multibudded morphology indistinguishable from cdc4 and cdc34 mutants. Cdc53 is found in both the nucleus and the cytoplasm, and the site of action of each SCF is set by its F-box receptor.

Functional annotation report: Saccharomyces cerevisiae CDC53

Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified. CDC53/YDL132W, UniProt Q12018, in Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain S288c is the protein historically called Cdc53, Cullin-A, or yeast Cul1. The name, organism, cullin-family classification, and Cullin/Cullin-homology domain annotations supplied in the query all agree with literature describing Cdc53 as the essential cullin scaffold of Skp1–Cullin–F-box (SCF/CRL1) ubiquitin ligases. No similarly named protein from another species was used in this annotation. Modern literature often calls the orthologous protein Cul1, but in budding yeast the relevant protein is Cdc53 (patton1998cdc53isa pages 1-2, sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5).

The most precise primary-function annotation is:

Cdc53 is an essential, nonenzymatic cullin scaffold that assembles modular SCF E3 ubiquitin ligases. It connects a Skp1–F-box substrate-recognition module to the Hrt1/Rbx1–Cdc34 ubiquitin-transfer module, thereby positioning receptor-bound substrates for ubiquitylation.

Cdc53 therefore has neither a single catalytic reaction nor intrinsic specificity for one substrate. Substrate specificity resides primarily in interchangeable F-box receptors such as Cdc4, Grr1, Met30, Dia2, and Das1. Effects attributed genetically to “Cdc53” usually represent loss of several SCF ligases simultaneously and must not be interpreted as direct binding of every affected protein to Cdc53 (patton1998cdc53isa pages 8-9, harper2021cullinringubiquitinligase pages 18-20, patton1998cdc53isa pages 1-2).

Identity and domain verification

The experimentally established architecture is consistent with the supplied InterPro annotations—Cullin, Cullin-like_AB, Cullin_CS, and Cullin-homology domains. Cullins are elongated molecular scaffolds: the N-terminal portion organizes the Skp1–F-box receptor arm, whereas the C-terminal cullin region binds the RING protein Hrt1/Rbx1. Foundational interaction mapping showed that Cdc53 associates in vivo with Cdc34, Skp1, and multiple F-box proteins, including Cdc4, Grr1, and Met30. Cdc34–Cdc53 binding and Skp1/F-box–Cdc53 assembly are separable, directly supporting a modular scaffold rather than an enzyme-active-site model (patton1998cdc53isa pages 8-9, patton1998cdc53isa pages 1-2).

A useful caution is that historical diagrams sometimes show Cdc34 binding Cdc53 directly, whereas current CRL chemistry emphasizes recruitment of ubiquitin-charged Cdc34 by the Hrt1/Rbx1 RING domain attached to Cdc53. These descriptions are compatible at the complex level: Cdc53 organizes the catalytic Cdc34–Hrt1 module but does not itself perform ubiquitin transfer (mathur2015skp1independentfunction pages 19-20, harper2021cullinringubiquitinligase pages 18-20, sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5).

Molecular mechanism

In a canonical yeast SCF complex, an F-box protein binds Skp1 and recognizes a substrate degron. Skp1 couples that receptor to the N-terminal region of Cdc53. At the opposite end, Cdc53 binds Hrt1/Rbx1, whose RING domain recruits Cdc34 carrying activated ubiquitin through a thioester bond. This architecture brings Cdc34~ubiquitin close to lysines on the receptor-bound substrate, promoting ubiquitin transfer and, after repeated cycles, chain formation. Many—but not all—products are subsequently degraded by the 26S proteasome (harper2021cullinringubiquitinligase pages 18-20, sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5).

Direct reconstitution showed that Cdc34, Cdc53, Skp1, and Cdc4 can ubiquitylate phosphorylated Sic1 in vitro. Genetic specificity is equally informative: Sic1 degradation requires Cdc4, Cln2 degradation primarily requires Grr1, and methionine-pathway repression requires Met30. Thus, these are receptor-defined SCF substrates, not substrates selected directly by Cdc53 (patton1998cdc53isa pages 7-8, patton2001e3ubiquitinprotein pages 88-93, patton1998cdc53isa pages 1-2).

Cdc53 is not a HECT-like catalytic ligase and does not form an obligatory ubiquitin thioester. Mutation of all six Cdc53 cysteines did not eliminate function or Rub1 modification, arguing against catalytic transfer through a Cdc53 cysteine. Its role is structural, conformational, and regulatory (patton1998cdc53isa pages 7-8).

Rub1/NEDD8 regulation

Cdc53 activity is regulated by covalent attachment of Rub1, the yeast NEDD8 ortholog, to a cullin lysine. Rub1 is activated by Ula1–Uba3, passed to Ubc12, and transferred to cullins with assistance from Hrt1/Rbx1 and Dcn1. Hrt1 therefore participates in two related reactions: it engages Ubc12 for cullin Rub1 modification and Cdc34 for substrate ubiquitylation. Cullin modification favors an active CRL configuration; the COP9 signalosome reverses the modification, and Lag2/CAND1-family exchange mechanisms participate in CRL remodeling (sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5).

A 2023 review catalogued 18 budding-yeast F-box-motif proteins, including ten confirmed SCF receptors and five additional likely receptors. This illustrates why Cdc53 should be viewed as a reusable ligase platform rather than a one-pathway cell-cycle factor (sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5).

Biological pathways

Cell-cycle progression: SCF–Cdc4 and SCF–Grr1

SCF–Cdc4 recognizes phosphorylated substrates, classically the CDK inhibitor Sic1. Sic1 destruction releases inhibition of S-phase CDK and permits the G1/S transition; impaired Cdc53 function stabilizes Sic1 and causes G1 arrest. Cdc4 also participates in regulation of additional phosphorylated cell-cycle and chromosome-associated proteins (harper2021cullinringubiquitinligase pages 18-20, patton1998cdc53isa pages 1-2).

SCF–Grr1 is distinct despite sharing Cdc53, Skp1, Hrt1, and Cdc34. Grr1 targets G1 cyclins such as Cln2 and participates in nutrient-responsive regulation. Cln2 was stabilized in grr1 mutants but not equivalently in cdc4 or met30 mutants, providing clear experimental evidence that F-box identity—not Cdc53 alone—determines pathway specificity (patton1998cdc53isa pages 7-8).

Sulfur metabolism and heavy-metal signaling: SCF–Met30

SCF–Met30 couples sulfur-metabolite availability and environmental stress to transcription and cell division. A central target is the transcription factor Met4. Importantly, Met4 ubiquitylation can serve a nonproteolytic inhibitory function, demonstrating that Cdc53-based SCF activity does not invariably cause substrate destruction. Genetic experiments showed that Met30, Cdc53, Cdc34, and Skp1 are jointly required for methionine-dependent transcriptional repression (patton1998cdc53isa pages 7-8, lauinger2020cdc48cofactorshp1 pages 1-2).

Cadmium causes Met30 autoubiquitylation, recruitment of Cdc48/p97 and its Shp1/p47 cofactor, and active removal of Met30 from the Cdc53-containing core. The resulting SCF–Met30 inactivation reduces modification of Met4 and Met32 and activates stress-responsive transcription and cell-cycle arrest. This is a real-world example of signal-controlled disassembly of a Cdc53 complex rather than simple regulation of substrate binding (lauinger2020cdc48cofactorshp1 pages 1-2).

A 2024 study advanced this mechanism by showing that the Met30 F-box itself acts as a cadmium sensor: conserved cysteines bind cadmium with a reported KD of approximately 8 μM, inducing a conformational change, autoubiquitylation, and Cdc48/p97-mediated complex disassembly. This expands the accepted function of an F-box beyond Skp1 binding to direct environmental sensing [Lauinger et al., Nature Communications, May 2024, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48184-6].

DNA-replication termination: SCF–Dia2

SCF–Dia2 ubiquitylates Mcm7 within the CMG replicative helicase after replication termination. Ubiquitylated CMG is then extracted by Cdc48, allowing replisome disassembly. Cryo-EM analysis used a complete yeast SCF–Dia2 containing Hrt1–Cdc53–Skp1–Dia2 and resolved termination assemblies at average resolutions of 3.2–4.0 Å. Dia2 contacts Mcm3 and Mcm5; at an active replication fork, the excluded DNA strand blocks this interface, preventing premature Mcm7 ubiquitylation and catastrophic fork collapse. Loss of that DNA obstruction after termination licenses SCF–Dia2 access (jenkynbedford2021aconservedmechanism pages 1-2).

This work provides unusually precise structural evidence for where and when a Cdc53 ligase acts: on terminated chromatin-bound replisomes, with substrate access gated by DNA topology rather than by a change in Cdc53 itself [Jenkyn-Bedford et al., Nature, published October 26, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04145-3] (jenkynbedford2021aconservedmechanism pages 1-2).

Protein quality control: SCF–Das1

A major 2023 development was discovery of a broad C-terminal degron pathway centered on SCF–Das1. An unbiased budding-yeast screen identified more than 5,000 potential C-degrons; approximately 40% were targeted through the single F-box receptor Das1. Das1 is sequence-selective but unusually permissive, recognizing multiple C-terminal motifs and directing degradation of orphan protein-complex subunits (kong2023orphanqualitycontrol pages 1-2).

In one 12-residue random-peptide library, 4,726 sequences met the putative-degron threshold. The percentage of cells expressing unstable variants was 3.6% for 12-residue peptides, compared with 0.2% and 0.4% for 4- and 8-residue libraries, respectively. These results establish a quantitatively broad Cdc53-dependent proteostasis role, but the C-terminal sequence recognition belongs to Das1, not Cdc53 [Kong et al., Nature Communications 14:8363; received August 11 and accepted November 30, 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44096-z] (kong2023orphanqualitycontrol pages 1-2, kong2023orphanqualitycontrol pages 2-3).

Chromosome and meiotic organization

Cdc53-containing SCFs also regulate chromosome-associated proteins. SCF–Cdc4 and SCF–Met30 cooperate in controlling Cse4/CENP-A homeostasis, helping prevent ectopic Cse4 accumulation and chromosome instability. In meiosis, conditional depletion of Cdc53 disrupted synaptonemal-complex formation and progression through meiosis I; a conditional cdc4 mutant produced similar defects. Suppression and synthetic phenotypes involving Pch2 indicate that SCF–Cdc4-dependent ubiquitylation collaborates with this AAA+ ATPase in synaptonemal-complex assembly and recombination control (zhu2021scfcdc4ubiquitinligaseregulates pages 1-2).

Cellular localization

Cdc53 is an intracellular, soluble SCF-core protein, not a secreted protein, transmembrane transporter, or organelle-resident structural component. The available evidence does not justify assigning it to one exclusive compartment. Its site of action changes with the assembled receptor and substrate:

  • SCF–Cdc4 acts on nuclear cell-cycle, kinetochore, and meiotic substrates.
  • SCF–Dia2 acts at chromatin-bound replisomes at DNA-replication termination.
  • SCF–Met30 regulates intracellular stress and transcriptional programs.
  • SCF–Das1 contributes to proteostasis; Das1 has been reported as cytosolic.

Accordingly, the most defensible annotation is cytosolic and nuclear/context-dependent, with receptor- and substrate-driven recruitment to functional sites. Evidence for action at a nuclear substrate should not be converted into a claim that all cellular Cdc53 is constitutively nuclear. Likewise, a receptor's localization is not necessarily identical to that of every Cdc53 complex (zhu2021scfcdc4ubiquitinligaseregulates pages 1-2, kong2023orphanqualitycontrol pages 1-2, jenkynbedford2021aconservedmechanism pages 1-2).

Applications and broader significance

Cdc53 is principally a research model rather than a direct industrial product. Its major applications are mechanistic:

  1. Cell-cycle and degron research. The Sic1–SCF–Cdc4 system is a foundational model for phosphorylation-dependent degradation and multisite phosphodegron recognition.
  2. Targeted protein degradation. The modular SCF principle helped establish the concept that interchangeable receptors can redirect a common ubiquitination core. Authoritative analysis identifies CRLs as a major framework for molecular glues and PROTAC-like strategies that induce degradation of chosen proteins (harper2021cullinringubiquitinligase pages 18-20).
  3. Proteostasis screens. SCF–Das1 demonstrates how peptide libraries, fluorescent timers, machine learning, mutagenesis, and genetic screens can define degron rules at proteome scale (kong2023orphanqualitycontrol pages 1-2, kong2023orphanqualitycontrol pages 2-3).
  4. Replication and genome-stability models. Structural work on SCF–Dia2 provides a model for substrate access controlled by nucleic-acid geometry and for regulated replisome unloading (jenkynbedford2021aconservedmechanism pages 1-2).
  5. Environmental sensing. SCF–Met30 shows that an F-box receptor can directly sense a toxic metal and initiate active disassembly of its ligase, connecting environmental chemistry to transcription and cell-cycle control (lauinger2020cdc48cofactorshp1 pages 1-2).

Expert interpretation and remaining uncertainties

The consensus interpretation is that Cdc53 is best understood as a dynamic molecular chassis. Its essentiality reflects the combined requirement for numerous SCF ligases, not necessarily a unique Cdc53-specific substrate. Statements such as “Cdc53 degrades Sic1” are convenient shorthand but mechanistically incomplete: SCF–Cdc4 recruits phosphorylated Sic1, while Cdc53 provides the geometry that couples this receptor to the Hrt1–Cdc34 ubiquitin-transfer machinery (patton1998cdc53isa pages 8-9, harper2021cullinringubiquitinligase pages 18-20, patton1998cdc53isa pages 1-2).

Several boundaries remain important. First, the complete in-vivo substrate repertoire of many yeast F-box proteins remains unresolved. Second, physical association or stabilization in a cdc53 mutant does not by itself establish direct ubiquitylation. Third, localization is likely heterogeneous and complex-specific; current evidence is stronger for pathway-specific sites of action than for one global Cdc53 localization. Finally, Rub1 modification and receptor exchange regulate CRL conformational state, but the quantitative contribution of each regulatory cycle differs by SCF complex and physiological condition (harper2021cullinringubiquitinligase pages 18-20, sengupta2023theubiquitinlikeproteins pages 4-5).

Overall functional annotation

CDC53 encodes the essential Cul1-family scaffold of budding-yeast SCF ubiquitin ligases. Cdc53 binds the Skp1–F-box substrate-recognition arm at its N-terminal region and the Hrt1/Rbx1–Cdc34 ubiquitin-transfer arm at its C-terminal region. It enables receptor-selected proteins to be ubiquitylated but has no intrinsic enzymatic reaction or universal substrate specificity of its own. Through different F-box receptors, Cdc53 participates in G1/S control, nutrient and heavy-metal signaling, sulfur metabolism, replication termination, chromosome and meiotic organization, and protein quality control. Its activity is regulated by Rub1/NEDD8 modification and by receptor-specific assembly, localization, and signal-induced remodeling.
