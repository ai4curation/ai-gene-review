# Functional annotation report: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* **SSK1**

## Executive summary

The requested protein identity is verified. **SSK1/YLR006C, UniProt Q07084**, in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* strain S288c is the cytoplasmic response regulator of the **Sln1–Ypd1–Ssk1 phosphorelay**, not an identically named protein from *Candida* or another fungus. Its C-terminal CheY-like receiver domain contains the phospho-acceptor **Asp554**, matching the supplied Response_reg/PF00072, Sig_transdc_resp-reg_receiver, and CheY-like annotations. Literature concerning other fungal SSK1 homologs was excluded from the protein-specific conclusions below. (saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5, posas1996yeasthog1map pages 1-2, branscum2015structuralandmutagenesis pages 89-94)

Ssk1 is **not an enzyme, transporter, or structural protein**. Its primary function is that of a phosphorylation-controlled signaling adaptor or molecular switch. Under normal osmotic conditions, Ssk1 is phosphorylated and inactive. Hyperosmotic stress interrupts phosphate delivery, producing unphosphorylated Ssk1, which binds and activates the MAP kinase kinase kinases Ssk2 and Ssk22. These activate Pbs2 and then Hog1, initiating osmoadaptation. Ssk1 therefore couples a fungal His–Asp phosphorelay to a eukaryotic MAPK cascade. (posas1998activationofthe pages 1-2, posas1996yeasthog1map pages 1-2, nadal2022thehogpathway pages 3-4)

## 1. Identity, nomenclature, and domain interpretation

The evidence is internally consistent with the supplied identity:

- **Organism:** budding yeast, *S. cerevisiae*.
- **Gene/locus:** **SSK1/YLR006C**.
- **Protein:** Ssk1 response regulator, UniProt **Q07084**.
- **Protein family/domain:** fungal Ssk1-family response regulator containing a bacterial-like, CheY-fold receiver domain.
- **Regulatory residue:** **Asp554**, which receives phosphate from Ypd1.

The receiver domain has the expected response-regulator α/β topology. Structural work on the isolated Ssk1 receiver region identified Asp554 at the active site, a flexible approximately 20-residue loop, and a disordered approximately 50-residue C-terminal tail. Ypd1-contact substitutions E510G, N512T, I514T, and I518T disrupted interaction in vivo, supporting a specific phosphotransfer interface rather than nonspecific association. (branscum2015structuralandmutagenesis pages 89-94)

This domain agreement is important because SSK1 homologs occur in other fungi and may control additional stress or virulence phenotypes. Those homologs are evolutionarily informative but are not interchangeable evidence for Q07084. The conclusions here derive from experiments explicitly performed in *S. cerevisiae*.

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Phosphorelay-controlled switch

At normal osmolarity and turgor, the transmembrane hybrid histidine kinase Sln1 is active. Phosphate flows through the sequence:

**Sln1-His576 → Sln1-Asp1144 → Ypd1-His64 → Ssk1-Asp554.**

Phosphorylated Ssk1, conventionally denoted Ssk1-P, is inactive toward the downstream MAP3Ks. Increased extracellular osmolarity reduces turgor and inhibits Sln1 kinase activity. Continued phosphoaspartate hydrolysis without replenishment then increases unphosphorylated Ssk1, or Ssk1-OH, which is the active signaling species. Ssk1 consequently exhibits an unusual **dephosphorylation-activates** response-regulator logic. (saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5, posas1996yeasthog1map pages 1-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 1-2)

Biochemical kinetics support this model. Ypd1 transfers phosphate to Ssk1 at approximately **160 s⁻¹**, compared with approximately **1.4 s⁻¹** for the alternative response regulator Skn7. This rapid transfer helps keep Ssk1 phosphorylated under nonstress conditions. In vitro, Ssk1-P had an approximately **13-minute** dephosphorylation half-life without Ypd1 but approximately **42 hours** in the presence of Ypd1, indicating that Ypd1 both transfers phosphate and strongly stabilizes Ssk1-P. (saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 11-12)

### 2.2 Activation of Ssk2/Ssk22

Unphosphorylated Ssk1 directly binds the N-terminal regulatory region of **Ssk2**; pathway genetics and established annotation extend this activation to the paralogous MAP3K **Ssk22**. Biochemical reconstitution showed that Ssk1 promotes rapid Ssk2 autophosphorylation. The favored mechanism has two stages: Ssk1 binding relieves N-terminal autoinhibition and initiates latent kinase activity; Ssk2 then autophosphorylates and remains catalytically active without continued dependence on Ssk1. Thr1460 in the Ssk2 activation loop is important for this process. (posas1998activationofthe pages 1-2)

Ssk1 itself does not catalyze phosphorylation of Ssk2. It is better annotated as a **phosphorylation-state-dependent MAP3K activator/adaptor** whose molecular specificity is for the regulatory regions of Ssk2/Ssk22.

Ssk1 also appears to buffer basal signaling. It is predominantly dimeric: Ssk1-OH homodimers efficiently activate Ssk2, whereas phosphorylated Ssk1 can associate with and inhibit Ssk1-OH. This provides a mechanism for preventing small amounts of spontaneously dephosphorylated Ssk1 from activating Hog1 in unstressed cells. Mutations that prevent Ssk1 phosphorylation cause constitutive pathway activation, while overexpressed wild-type Ssk1 or an Ssk2-binding-defective variant can inhibit constitutively active Ssk1 mutants. (horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 1-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 11-12, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 2-2)

## 3. Pathway position and biological process

Ssk1 functions specifically in the **Sln1 branch** of the high-osmolarity glycerol pathway:

**Sln1 → Ypd1 → Ssk1 → Ssk2/Ssk22 → Pbs2 → Hog1.**

The separate Sho1 branch uses membrane-associated Hkr1/Msb2–Sho1/Opy2 machinery and Ste11, rather than Ssk1, before converging on Pbs2. Thus, Ssk1 is neither a component of the Sho1 branch nor a general downstream MAPK protein. (nadal2022thehogpathway pages 3-4, tatebayashi2023twoactivatingphosphorylation pages 1-2)

Foundational epistasis established this ordering. Loss of SLN1 or YPD1 causes excessive HOG signaling, while deletion of SSK1 or downstream SSK2, PBS2, or HOG1 suppresses relevant constitutive-activation phenotypes. Conversely, activated N-terminally truncated Ssk2/Ssk22 bypass upstream control and are toxic because they drive the cascade constitutively. (posas1996yeasthog1map pages 1-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 2-2)

Activated Hog1 controls the wider adaptive program, including glycerol synthesis and retention, transcriptional and translational reprogramming, and temporary cell-cycle arrest. These are downstream consequences of Ssk1 signaling rather than direct biochemical activities of Ssk1 itself. The authoritative 2022 review retains Ssk1's established annotation as the Sln1-branch response regulator upstream of Ssk2/Ssk22. (nadal2022thehogpathway pages 3-4)

## 4. Cellular localization

The best-supported site of Ssk1 action is the **cytoplasm**. Localization studies distinguished cytoplasmic Ssk1 from the more nuclear response regulator Skn7 and proposed that Ypd1 shuttles between nucleus and cytoplasm to phosphorylate the two targets in their respective compartments. No stress-dependent relocalization of Ssk1 was detected. Its cytoplasmic assignment also agrees with its physical and functional interactions with Ypd1 and Ssk2/Ssk22. (lu2003saccharomycescerevisiaehistidine pages 1-3)

Ssk1 should therefore not be annotated as a plasma-membrane osmosensor. Sln1 performs transmembrane sensing; Ssk1 receives the intracellular phosphorelay state and activates the cytoplasmic MAPK module.

## 5. Signal termination and protein turnover

Ssk1 abundance is regulated as well as its phosphorylation state. Unphosphorylated, signaling-active Ssk1 is preferentially degraded through a **Ubc7/Qri8-dependent ubiquitin–proteasome pathway**. Loss of UBC7 impairs Ssk1 degradation and delays Hog1 dephosphorylation, indicating that Ssk1 turnover contributes to pathway shutoff after osmoadaptation. (sato2003phosphorelayregulateddegradationof pages 1-2)

This creates layered negative regulation: rapid phosphorylation maintains inactivity before stress; phosphorylated Ssk1 inhibits residual active Ssk1; and proteasomal turnover removes unphosphorylated Ssk1 during adaptation. The Sln1–Ypd1–Ssk1 network is also robust to fluctuations in component abundance, consistent with the need to avoid inappropriate Hog1 activation under normal growth conditions.

## 6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

No 2023–2024 study retrieved here overturns or materially revises the core molecular annotation of Ssk1. Recent work instead uses the established SSK1 branch to resolve downstream chemistry and branch-specific signaling behavior.

### 6.1 Differential Pbs2 phosphorylation—2023

Tatebayashi and Saito, published **April 2023**, showed that Ssk2/Ssk22 can phosphorylate both **Ser514 and Thr518** in the activation loop of Pbs2, whereas the Sho1-branch MAP3K Ste11 phosphorylates only Thr518. Ptc1–Ptc4 then differentially dephosphorylate Pbs2, with Ptc1 preferentially acting on Thr518 and using Nbp2 for recruitment. This refines the biochemical consequences of signaling through the Ssk1-controlled branch but does not change Ssk1's placement or direct function. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2023.104569. (tatebayashi2023twoactivatingphosphorylation pages 1-2)

### 6.2 Branch-specific amplitude, duration, and cross-talk—2024

Zhang and colleagues, published **October 2024**, compared SSK1 and SHO1 deletion strains using MAPK phosphorylation, Hog1 nuclear trafficking, reporter assays, and transcriptomics. Their results indicate complementary branch effects on Hog1 activation amplitude and duration and show that loss of Sln1-branch signaling can release Sho1/Kss1-dependent cross-talk toward the mating pathway. Experiments included CRE-LacZ responses over **0–550 mM KCl**, RNA sequencing at **350 mM KCl**, and Hog1-mRuby2 nuclear imaging. The study used SSK1 deletion as a branch-selective perturbation; it did not identify a new catalytic activity or localization for Ssk1. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e24-06-0270. (zhang2024sharedandredundant pages 1-2, zhang2024sharedandredundant pages 4-5)

These modern data caution against describing the Sln1/Ssk1 and Sho1 branches as merely interchangeable. They converge on Pbs2-Hog1 but differ in downstream phosphorylation potential, signal kinetics, transcriptional output, and cross-pathway insulation.

## 7. Applications and real-world relevance

Ssk1 itself is primarily a research-system component rather than a currently deployed industrial reagent or therapeutic target. Its established applications are nevertheless substantial:

1. **Quantitative signaling model.** The Sln1–Ypd1–Ssk1 relay is used to study how phosphotransfer kinetics, protein abundance, dimerization, and degradation suppress signaling noise while retaining rapid stress responsiveness. Modern HOG research applies microfluidics, live-cell imaging, single-cell analysis, and mathematical modeling to this system. (saito2012responsetohyperosmotic pages 4-5, nadal2022thehogpathway pages 3-4)
2. **Branch-selective genetic perturbation.** Deletion of SSK1 provides a practical means of disabling the Sln1 branch while retaining the Sho1 branch, enabling analysis of MAPK amplitude, duration, nuclear trafficking, transcription, and cross-talk. (zhang2024sharedandredundant pages 1-2, zhang2024sharedandredundant pages 4-5)
3. **Fungal stress-signaling framework.** Because fungi broadly employ two-component/HOG systems, the *S. cerevisiae* relay supplies a mechanistic reference for studying osmotic adaptation in yeasts relevant to fermentation, biotechnology, spoilage, and fungal pathogenesis. Extrapolation to homologs must remain organism-specific because pathway wiring and phenotypes differ among fungi.
4. **Synthetic- and systems-biology test bed.** Its defined input-output structure—sensor phosphorelay, phosphorylation-controlled adaptor, MAP3K cascade, and measurable Hog1 nuclear response—makes the pathway useful for engineering and analyzing signal processing. These are research applications; the retrieved evidence does not establish a commercial Ssk1-based implementation.

## 8. Evidence-weighted annotation

A concise functional annotation supported by the literature is:

> **Ssk1 is a cytoplasmic, CheY-like response regulator in the *S. cerevisiae* Sln1–Ypd1 osmosensing phosphorelay. At normal osmolarity, phosphorylation of Asp554 keeps Ssk1 inactive. Hyperosmotic inhibition of Sln1 causes unphosphorylated Ssk1 to accumulate and bind the regulatory regions of Ssk2/Ssk22, promoting MAP3K activation and signaling through Pbs2 to Hog1. Phosphorylated-Ssk1-mediated inhibition and ubiquitin–proteasome-dependent turnover constrain basal and prolonged signaling.**

Confidence is **high** for identity, Asp554 phosphorylation, phosphorelay direction, cytoplasmic action, direct Ssk2 activation, and downstream Pbs2-Hog1 signaling because these are supported by convergent genetics, biochemistry, mutagenesis, structural evidence, localization studies, and authoritative reviews. The strongest qualification concerns treating Ssk2 and Ssk22 as completely equivalent: they are commonly described as redundant in osmostress signaling, but stimulus-specific work and recent branch analyses indicate that apparent redundancy should not be interpreted as identical behavior in every context. (posas1998activationofthe pages 1-2, horie2008phosphorylatedssk1prevents pages 11-12, nadal2022thehogpathway pages 3-4, tatebayashi2023twoactivatingphosphorylation pages 1-2)
