Gene Description

IadD (Vapar_1493) is the 437-amino-acid catalytic alpha subunit of the heterohexameric (alpha3beta3) indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) Rieske non-heme iron aromatic-ring-hydroxylating dioxygenase complex (IadD/IadE) in Variovorax paradoxus S110. Together with the structural beta subunit IadE and the FMN/[2Fe-2S]-containing NADH-dependent reductase IadC, IadD catalyzes the initial aerobic oxygenation of the plant hormone indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) into 2-hydroxyindole-3-acetic acid, which tautomerizes to biologically inactive 2-oxindole-3-acetic acid (oxIAA) and is further degraded via dioxindole-3-acetic acid and isatin to anthranilic acid. Structurally, each IadD monomer comprises an N-terminal Rieske domain (residues 44-160) that coordinates a [2Fe-2S] cluster via Cys85, His87, Cys106, and His109, and a C-terminal catalytic domain (residues 1-43 and 161-437) that coordinates a mononuclear ferrous iron center via the 2-His-1-carboxylate facial triad His216, His221, and Asp377 adjacent to the IAA substrate-binding pocket. In the rhizosphere, IAA catabolism by the IadD/IadE-IadC dioxygenase system enables Variovorax to buffer root-zone auxin levels and prevent auxin-induced root growth inhibition in host plants.

Core Functions

Catalytic alpha subunit of the Rieske non-heme iron aromatic-ring-hydroxylating dioxygenase complex (IadD/IadE) that catalyzes NADH/IadC-dependent oxygenation of indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) to 2-oxindole-3-acetic acid (oxIAA) in the first step of bacterial auxin catabolism.

Literature & Structural Evidence

  1. Discovery of the Variovorax iad Auxin-Degradation Locus (PMID:32999461):
  2. Finkel et al. (2020) Nature 587:103–108 demonstrated that a single bacterial genus, Variovorax, is necessary and sufficient within a 185-member synthetic root community to revert severe root-growth inhibition (RGI) induced by auxin-producing commensals or exogenous indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) [PMID:32999461, "Here we show that a single bacterial genus, Variovorax, which is taxonomically and metabolically diverse, is able to maintain the developmental programme of the root within a complex community context."].

  3. Comparative genomics and RNA-seq under IAA induction identified hotspot 33, a conserved syntenic gene cluster present across all 26 RGI-reverting Variovorax genomes that degrades auxin and is required in vivo to maintain stereotypic root growth [PMID:32999461, "Guided by bulk and single-strain inoculations and comparative genomics, we identified a single auxin-degradation operon that is conserved in all available genomes of Variovorax and is necessary and sufficient for the reversion of root growth inhibition."].

  4. Genetic Dissection and Metabolomic Pathway (PMID:36266335):

  5. Conway et al. (2022) Nature Microbiology 7:1817–1832 genetically dissected the Variovorax iad operon (iadR-iadA-iadB-iadC-iadD-iadE-...) in Variovorax sp. CL14 and Variovorax paradoxus S110.
  6. Heterologous expression in E. coli established that iadD and iadE (iadDE, encoding the large and small subunits of the aromatic-ring-hydroxylating dioxygenase) are the minimal gene set sufficient for IAA conversion, and inclusion of the ferredoxin reductase iadC (iadCDE) boosts IAA degradation efficiency in vitro and is required in vivo in Variovorax to achieve the rapid IAA degradation rate needed to revert Arthrobacter CL28-induced RGI [PMID:36266335, "Incorporation of IadC (Ferredoxin reductase) to IadD/IadE enhanced transformation of IAA to oxIAA in vitro, although IadC was not essential for the whole-cell degradation in E. coli."].

  7. Metabolomic profiling showed that Variovorax degrades IAA aerobically via 2-oxindole-3-acetic acid (oxIAA) and dioxindole-3-acetic acid (DOAA) to isatin and anthranilic acid PMID:36266335.

  8. Crystal/Cryo-EM Structures and Biochemical Mechanism (PMID:37459330):

  9. Ma et al. (2023) PLoS Biology 21:e3002189 solved the high-resolution crystal structures of apo IadD/IadE (PDB 7YLS) and IAA-bound IadD/IadE (PDB 8H2T) from Variovorax paradoxus S110 (C5CSP6 / C5CSP7).
  10. IadD (large $\alpha$ subunit) and IadE (small $\beta$ subunit) assemble into a mushroom-shaped heterohexamer ($\alpha_3\beta_3$) where a trimer of IadD forms the catalytic cap and a trimer of IadE forms the structural stem [PMID:37459330, "IadD/IadE exhibits a mushroom-shaped quaternary structure handling a 3-fold symmetry axis, with the trimeric α subunits (IadD) functioning as the catalytic 'cap' strategy and the β subunits (IadE) forming the 'stem' base"].
  11. Each IadD monomer contains:
    • N-terminal Rieske [2Fe-2S] domain (residues 44–160): Coordinates the [2Fe-2S] cluster via Cys85, His87, Cys106, and His109.
    • C-terminal catalytic non-heme mononuclear iron domain (residues 1–43 and 161–437): Coordinates the active-site $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ion via His216, His221, and Asp377 (2-His-1-carboxylate facial triad).
  12. Due to the 43.3 Å intrasuubunit distance vs. 11.9 Å intersubunit distance between the Rieske [2Fe-2S] cluster and the mononuclear iron center, electrons are transferred across adjacent IadD subunits via the bridging conserved Asp213 residue (His109 -> Asp213 -> His216).
  13. Isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) confirmed direct IAA binding to IadD ($K_d = 17.0 \pm 1.9\text{ }\mu\text{M}$ for IadD/IadE; $24.1 \pm 3.0\text{ }\mu\text{M}$ for IadD alone), and site-directed mutagenesis of active-site residues (H216A, H221A, F251A, H311A, R329A, Y362A) abolished or severely reduced IAA conversion to oxIAA PMID:37459330.
  14. In vivo, IadD/IadE shows high substrate specificity for IAA and sluggish/negligible activity toward 2,4-D, IBA, or IPA, explaining why Variovorax selectively buffers IAA in the rhizosphere without degrading synthetic auxins such as 2,4-D PMID:37459330.
