Gene Description

UreC (Vapar_4243) is the 572-amino-acid catalytic alpha subunit of cytoplasmic nickel-dependent urease (EC 3.5.1.5) in the plant-growth-promoting endophyte Variovorax paradoxus S110. Together with the structural subunits UreA (gamma) and UreB (beta), UreC assembles into a trimer of heterotrimers ((UreABC)3) whose active site coordinates a dinuclear Ni(2+) metallocenter bridged by a post-translationally carboxylated lysine residue (Lys222) and a hydroxide ion, ligated by His139, His141, His251, His277, and Asp365, with His325 serving as the general acid proton donor. Following chaperone-assisted metallocenter maturation by UreD, UreF, UreG, and the nickel metallochaperone UreE, UreC catalyzes the hydrolysis of urea into ammonia and carbamate (which spontaneously decomposes to a second ammonia molecule and carbonic acid/bicarbonate), supplying assimilable nitrogen and elevating local pH in the rhizosphere where carbonate generation can also promote microbially induced metal-carbonate biomineralization.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: To what extent does urease-catalyzed carbonate production and local pH elevation by Variovorax paradoxus S110 drive microbially induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) of toxic divalent heavy metals (e.g., Cd2+, Ni2+, Pb2+) and lanthanides in contaminated rhizosphere soils?

Suggested experts: Hausinger RP, Han JI, Leadbetter JR

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Compare urea-supplemented cultures of wild-type Variovorax paradoxus S110 and a ΔureC (Vapar_4243) mutant for pH elevation, dissolved carbonate production, and extracellular precipitation of CdCO3, NiCO3, and lanthanide carbonates by ICP-MS and X-ray diffraction.

Hypothesis: UreC-dependent urea hydrolysis raises extracellular pH and carbonate ion activity sufficiently to precipitate divalent heavy metals and rare earth elements out of solution, protecting host roots from metal toxicity while supplying ammonium nitrogen.

2. Pathway Context & Module Linkage

  • Existing Reusable Module: modules/urease_biogenesis_urea_hydrolysis.yaml (MODULE:urease_biogenesis_urea_hydrolysis).
  • Mechanism: UreC (alpha) combines with UreA (gamma) and UreB (beta) to form the $(\text{UreABC})_3$ apoenzyme (GO:0035550 urease complex), which undergoes $\text{CO}_2$-dependent Lys222 carbamylation and GTP-dependent $\text{Ni}^{2+}$ insertion catalyzed by the UreDFG–UreE metallochaperone machinery [PMID:20046957, "The active site of urease contains two nickel atoms bridged by a carboxylated lysine and a solvent molecule, as shown for the K. aerogenes enzyme (Fig. 2).19 In addition, Ni1 is coordinated by two His residues plus a terminal water molecule while Ni2 is coordinated by two His, an Asp, and a terminal solvent."].
  • Rhizosphere & Biomineralization Significance: In Variovorax paradoxus S110, urease-catalyzed urea hydrolysis ($\text{urea} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{H}^+ \rightarrow \text{HCO}_3^- + 2\text{NH}_4^+$) both supplies bioavailable ammonium nitrogen to host plants and generates carbonate/hydroxide alkalinity that drives microbially induced carbonate precipitation (MICP) of toxic divalent heavy metals ($\text{Cd}^{2+}, \text{Ni}^{2+}, \text{Pb}^{2+}$) and rare earth elements, requiring intracellular $\text{Ni}^{2+}$ homeostasis coordinated with CzcA/CnrA RND efflux systems (czcA3, cnrA).

    precipitation (MICP) of toxic divalent heavy metals (e.g., Cd2+, Ni2+, Pb2+)
    and lanthanides in contaminated rhizosphere soils?
  experts:
  - Hausinger RP
  - Han JI
  - Leadbetter JR
suggested_experiments:
- description: >-
    Compare urea-supplemented cultures of wild-type Variovorax paradoxus S110 and
    a ΔureC (Vapar_4243) mutant for pH elevation, dissolved carbonate production,
    and extracellular precipitation of CdCO3, NiCO3, and lanthanide carbonates by
    ICP-MS and X-ray diffraction.
  hypothesis: >-
    UreC-dependent urea hydrolysis raises extracellular pH and carbonate ion
    activity sufficiently to precipitate divalent heavy metals and rare earth
    elements out of solution, protecting host roots from metal toxicity while
    supplying ammonium nitrogen.
