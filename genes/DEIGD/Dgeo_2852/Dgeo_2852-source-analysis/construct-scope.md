# Dgeo_2852 construct and assay scope

PMID:38622146 explicitly identifies DgKdpD as UniProt Q1J2K3, the 365-residue Dgeo_2852 protein from Deinococcus geothermalis DSM 11300. The official frozen UniProt record supplies the taxon and sequence; the paper does not report a native DSM 11300 pump assay.

Supplementary Table 1 (PDF page 8), visually checked against the downloaded source:

> D. geothermalis KdpD, 3C cleavage site, His10 tag

The matching plasmid is pBXC3H-kdpD with superscript Dg; the experiment column says “In vitro phospho-transfer”. Unlike the separately listed E. coli constructs, no Dgeo substitution is specified. Table 2 identifies the Dgeo-specific forward and reverse cloning primers. The methods describe expression in E. coli TK2281 and on-column 3C cleavage; the exact residual cleavage scar is not established here. This is not a claim that we sequenced the expression plasmid.

The measured substrate is E. coli KdpFABC carrying KdpB D307N, which suppresses catalytic-aspartate phosphorylation. The paper's S162 controls and mechanistic series assign the regulatory phosphorylation to serine. Figure 5 measures DgKdpD-dependent phosphorylation with 1 mM cyclic di-AMP or 400 mM KCl. Cyclic di-AMP stimulates this assay; KCl does not. This does not measure native D. geothermalis pump transport, its physiological cyclic-di-AMP concentration, or direct ligand occupancy of DgKdpD. Conserved regulatory-site chemistry is supported; native signaling circuitry remains an inference.

The short KdpD_N/Usp architecture lacks the transmitter HisKA/HATPase domains of canonical histidine kinases and lacks an integral-membrane segment. Soluble recombinant purification supports a cytosolic interpretation together with the architecture, but is not native localization. Peripheral association with a membrane pump remains possible.
