Gene Description

AcrIIA2 is a 123-residue acidic protein of the Listeria phage LP-101, an orthologue of the AcrIIA2 inhibitors encoded by Listeria monocytogenes prophages, which neutralise the host type II-A CRISPR-Cas9 system and thereby allow phage and prophage DNA to persist in a Cas9-competent cell. It works as a DNA mimic. Highly acidic surface residues let it bind guide-RNA-loaded Cas9 at the position the target duplex would occupy, contacting the protospacer-adjacent-motif recognition residues and so preventing the enzyme from detecting double-stranded DNA. Inhibition is stoichiometric and competitive with target DNA, requires the guide RNA to be loaded before the binding surface exists, and extends to Cas9 orthologues sharing only about half their sequence, because the occluded surface is conserved. The binding motif used is distinct from that of the unrelated Listeria inhibitor AcrIIA4, so the two proteins represent independent evolutionary arrivals at the same strategy against the same enzyme surface.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity

Definition: A molecular function inhibitor activity in which a protein presents a surface whose shape and charge distribution imitate those of a nucleic acid, and thereby binds competitively to the nucleic-acid-binding site of a target protein or protein-containing complex, occluding that site so that the target cannot engage its genuine nucleic acid ligand. The inhibitor is not itself a nucleic acid, and because the mechanism is competitive occupancy rather than catalysis, inhibition is reversible and requires the inhibitor at or above the concentration of its target.

Justification: AcrIIA2 is the case that shows the term has to be defined by mechanism and not by family, because AcrIIA2 and AcrIIA4 occlude the same Cas9 surface by the same strategy while sharing no sequence relationship and using, in the structural paper's own words, distinct binding motifs. No existing term expresses what they have in common. GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein complex binding, asserted separately on this entry, records what is bound but not that binding is inhibitory or competitive. GO:0005515 protein binding is uninformative. GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity and the generic GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity say the target is inhibited but cannot separate a mimic from a catalytic-domain blocker such as AcrIIC1 or from an enzymatic inhibitor such as AcrVA1, and that separation is what predicts whether substrate excess rescues the target. GO:0140489 molecular template activity concerns templating polymer synthesis and is unrelated. Three unrelated families curated in this round would use the term at once, namely AcrIIA2 here, AcrIIA4 (UniProtKB:A0A247D711) and the type I-F inhibitor AcrF2 (UniProtKB:Q6TM72).

Parent term: molecular function inhibitor activity

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: AcrIIA2 and AcrIIA4 occlude the same Cas9 surface with different binding motifs and no sequence relationship. Does the Cas9 PAM-interacting domain show signatures of selection driven by both, or has it been unable to escape because the mimicked feature, the phosphate backbone, cannot be abandoned?

Suggested experts: Yanli Wang, Joseph Bondy-Denomy

Q: Do the nine substitutions separating the LP-101 orthologue from the assayed prophage protein alter potency against Lmo Cas9, and is the Listeria phage-versus-prophage split reflected in two functionally distinct AcrIIA2 subfamilies?

Suggested experts: Yanli Wang

Q: Why does the apparent effect of AcrIIA2 on Cas9 cleavage exceed its effect on DNA binding, if the mechanism is occlusion of the PAM-recognition site upstream of both?

Suggested experts: Joseph Bondy-Denomy

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express LP101_024 and AEO04363.1 side by side, then measure Cas9 inhibition on three readouts at matched protein concentrations, namely isothermal titration calorimetry against sgRNA-loaded SpyCas9, in vitro plasmid cleavage, and plasmid interference rescue in a Cas9-competent host. Equivalence would retrospectively justify the ISS annotations here and let them be upgraded; divergence would mean the module's exemplar must be swapped for the assayed sequence.

Hypothesis: The LP-101 orthologue is functionally equivalent to the assayed Listeria prophage AcrIIA2, so the nine substitutions are neutral with respect to Cas9 inhibition.

Type: side-by-side orthologue potency comparison

Experiment: Titrate AcrIIA2 against sgRNA-loaded dCas9 across a range of target duplex concentrations and fit the inhibition surface to competitive and non-competitive models. A mimic should lose apparent potency as duplex concentration rises; a failure of that prediction would mean the acidic surface binds at the same place as DNA without being outcompeted by it, which is a different mechanism and would need different wording in the proposed molecular function.

Hypothesis: AcrIIA2 inhibition is competitive with target DNA, which is the operational test of the mimicry claim.

Type: competitive binding isotherm analysis

Experiment: Delete LP101_024 from phage LP-101 and compare plaquing efficiency on a Listeria monocytogenes host with an intact type II-A system against an isogenic cas9 deletion, with complementation in trans as the control. This is the necessity-and-sufficiency test that grounded the original anti-CRISPR assignments, and it has not been done for a free Listeria phage; it would also convert these ISS annotations into experimental ones on this very accession.

Hypothesis: acrIIA2 is required for LP-101 to replicate on a Cas9-competent Listeria host.

Type: phage gene deletion and plaquing assay

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: No function has been measured on this accession. Every functional claim for A0A059T5F6 is transferred from the 92.7 percent identical Listeria monocytogenes prophage protein AEO04363.1, and GOA holds no annotations for it at all.

OPEN CURATIONBIOLOGY

What is known: The AcrIIA2 family's mechanism is firmly established by a crystal structure of the inhibited complex and by in vivo inhibition of two Cas9 orthologues. What is unmeasured is whether this particular phage orthologue, with its nine substitutions, retains full potency, and whether the LP-101 phage actually depends on it.

Significance: This accession is the gene-product-level exemplar for the AcrIIA2 convergence case in modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml, and the module cites the structural paper as evidence for it. A reader following the accession reaches an unreviewed entry whose protein name is a ProtNLM machine prediction, with no experiment on the sequence itself. This is the same accession-level slippage the module already records as an open gap for the AcrIII-1 exemplar.

What would resolve it: Either assay the LP-101 protein directly, by expressing LP101_024 and testing inhibition of Lmo Cas9 and SpyCas9 and deleting it from the phage, or replace the module's gene-product selector with the assayed prophage sequence and keep LP101_024 as a family member.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "AcrIIA2- (AEO04363.1), AcrIIA3- (CBY03209.1) and AcrIIA4- (AEO04689.1) homologous protein sequences were acquired by BLASTp searches" — PMID:28041849

Gap: Whether AcrIIA2 inhibits only target licensing, as pure PAM-site occlusion predicts, or additionally interferes with the cleavage step, is undetermined.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: The structure establishes occupancy of the PAM-recognition residues and competition with the target duplex. The ambiguity is about a possible second, post-licensing effect.

Significance: Pure occlusion of the PAM-reading surface should affect DNA binding and DNA cleavage equally, because nothing downstream can happen without licensing. The genetics showed a larger effect on cleavage than on dCas9 binding, which if real would mean AcrIIA2 belongs partly in the module's nuclease-neutralisation part as well as in its recognition-blockade part, and would move it off the pure AcrIIA4 analogy.

What would resolve it: Compare AcrIIA2 inhibition of dCas9 target binding and of wild-type Cas9 cleavage side by side with purified components and matched stoichiometries, rather than in a heterologous reporter system.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "The enhanced efficacy of acrIIA2 in the cleavage-based Cas9 assay relative to the dCas9 based assay suggests that it may inhibit both binding and cleavage to some degree" — PMID:28041849

Identity, and an important caveat about which protein was assayed

Unreviewed TrEMBL entry, 123 aa, RecName: Full=Anti-CRISPR protein AcrIIA2 {ECO:0008006|Google:ProtNLM} — i.e. the name is a machine prediction (ProtNLM), not a
curator assertion. PE 4: Predicted, no PDB, no GO annotations in GOA at all. The only
non-electronic support for the AcrIIA2 assignment is NCBIfam NF033945 AcrIIA2_fam and
CDD cd22261 AcrIIA2, both HMM family hits. Seeded taxon was wrong
(NCBITaxon:10663); UniProt gives OX NCBI_TaxID=1458856 for Listeria phage LP-101, and
the review has been corrected to that.

The published AcrIIA2 work was done on a different, closely related sequence. Rauch et
al. name their reference sequence explicitly
PMID:28041849.
NCBI efetch of AEO04363.1 returns gp29 [Listeria monocytogenes J0161], 123 aa:

Pairwise comparison against A0A059T5F6 (same length, no gaps): 114/123 identical =
92.7%, differing only at positions 3, 5, 8, 11, 39, 44, 56, 81, 112. All nine are
conservative or surface substitutions; none is in a position the structural paper
identifies as a Cas9 contact. So this is a genuine orthologue of the characterised
protein, encoded by a free Listeria phage rather than by a L. monocytogenes prophage.

Consequence for evidence codes. The functional claims for this accession are
transferred by sequence similarity, not demonstrated on it. Every annotation proposed here
therefore carries ISS, with AEO04363.1 named as the source. The alternative — writing
IDA because the family is well characterised — would assert an experiment that was not
done on this sequence, and would be exactly the kind of accession-level slippage that
modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml records as an open gap for the AcrIII-1 exemplar.

Mechanism: the module's DNA-mimicry assignment holds

The module (annoton acriia2_dna_mimic) places AcrIIA2 in the
cas9_dna_mimicry_variant as "the convergence case", i.e. a second, sequence-unrelated
Listeria inhibitor that attacks the same Cas9 surface as AcrIIA4. The assignment is
correct, and the structural paper states it in almost those words.

Liu et al. solved the AcrIIA2–SpyCas9–sgRNA complex at 3.3 Å
PMID:30606466,
identified the occluded residues
PMID:30606466,
and drew the mimicry conclusion together with the explicit statement that the binding
motif differs from AcrIIA4's
PMID:30606466.
That last clause is the whole point of the module's convergence claim: same enzyme, same
surface, same mimicry strategy, different binding motif and no sequence relationship.
Cached record is abstract-only, so these are abstract quotes and are marked as such.

AcrIIA2's guide dependence is also on record
PMID:28448066,
as is the in vivo inhibition of both Lmo Cas9 and SpyCas9
PMID:28448066,
PMID:28041849.

One mechanistic subtlety from the genetics is worth keeping: AcrIIA2 was more effective in
the cleavage assay than in the dCas9 binding assay, which the authors read as partial
inhibition of binding plus inhibition of cleavage
PMID:28041849.
Pure PAM-site occlusion predicts equal effects on binding and cleavage, so this is a hint
that AcrIIA2 may be doing something at the catalytic step as well. It is a single
qualitative observation in a heterologous system and is recorded as a knowledge gap rather
than annotated.

Breadth across orthologues is attributed to a conserved target surface
PMID:28041849.

GOA state and the curation gap

GOA holds zero annotations for A0A059T5F6. Combined with an unreviewed entry whose
protein name is a ProtNLM prediction, this is a concrete instance of the second knowledge
gap recorded in modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml ("Most characterised Acr proteins
have no reviewed UniProt entry, and several carry uninformative names"). Here the name is
not uninformative but is machine-generated, which is arguably worse: it reads like a
curator assertion and is not one. The family HMM hits (NF033945, cd22261) do support the
assignment, and the 92.7% identity to the assayed protein supports it independently.

Ontology gap

Same gap as AcrIIA4 and AcrF2: no GO molecular-function term for mimicry-based competitive
inhibition. The proposed term nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity
is restated here with AcrIIA2-specific supporting text, since the three reviews should be
able to cite one proposal independently.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: nucleic acid mimicry-based competitive inhibitor activity
  proposed_definition: A molecular function inhibitor activity in which a protein
    presents a surface whose shape and charge distribution imitate those of a nucleic
    acid, and thereby binds competitively to the nucleic-acid-binding site of a target
    protein or protein-containing complex, occluding that site so that the target
    cannot engage its genuine nucleic acid ligand. The inhibitor is not itself a nucleic
    acid, and because the mechanism is competitive occupancy rather than catalysis,
    inhibition is reversible and requires the inhibitor at or above the concentration
    of its target.
  justification: AcrIIA2 is the case that shows the term has to be defined by mechanism
    and not by family, because AcrIIA2 and AcrIIA4 occlude the same Cas9 surface by
    the same strategy while sharing no sequence relationship and using, in the structural
    paper's own words, distinct binding motifs. No existing term expresses what they
    have in common. GO:0043021 ribonucleoprotein complex binding, asserted separately
    on this entry, records what is bound but not that binding is inhibitory or competitive.
    GO:0005515 protein binding is uninformative. GO:0140721 nuclease inhibitor activity
    and the generic GO:0140678 molecular function inhibitor activity say the target
    is inhibited but cannot separate a mimic from a catalytic-domain blocker such
    as AcrIIC1 or from an enzymatic inhibitor such as AcrVA1, and that separation
    is what predicts whether substrate excess rescues the target. GO:0140489 molecular
    template activity concerns templating polymer synthesis and is unrelated. Three
    unrelated families curated in this round would use the term at once, namely AcrIIA2
    here, AcrIIA4 (UniProtKB:A0A247D711) and the type I-F inhibitor AcrF2 (UniProtKB:Q6TM72).
