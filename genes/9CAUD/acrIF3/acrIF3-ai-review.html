Gene Description

AcrF3 is a 139-residue homodimeric protein of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa phage JBD5 that disables the host type I-F CRISPR-Cas system by sequestering its effector nuclease. Unlike inhibitors that block target recognition, AcrF3 leaves the crRNA-guided Csy surveillance complex free to find and bind its target; what it prevents is the arrival of the enzyme that would destroy that target. The dimer clamps the Cas2/3 helicase-nuclease, locking it in an ADP-bound state, occluding the entrance to the DNA-binding tunnel of its helicase domain, and masking the linker region and C-terminal domain through which the Csy complex recruits it, so Cas2/3 can neither be delivered to the R-loop nor act on DNA. Because recruitment is blocked, the host also loses the ability to acquire new spacers by priming, so the phage escapes both immediate interference and the updating of CRISPR memory against it. A side consequence of leaving a target-bound but nuclease-free surveillance complex in place is that the inhibited system behaves as a sequence-specific transcriptional repressor. The mechanism is available only against class 1 CRISPR systems, in which target recognition and target destruction reside in separate proteins.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does AcrF3 sequestration of Cas2/3 leave the host with a functional sequence-specific DNA-binding module that has physiological consequences of its own, beyond the reported transcriptional repression, for instance interference with replication at self-targeting loci?

Suggested experts: Alan R. Davidson, Karen L. Maxwell

Q: Is the Ca(2+) seen at the AcrF3 N-terminus and surface in PDB 5GNF conserved across AcrF3-family members, or is it unique to that crystal form, which would settle the GO:0046872 question definitively?

Suggested experts: Yanli Wang

Q: Cas3 sequestration has no counterpart against class 2 systems, where recognition and cleavage sit in one protein. Is that why class 2 inhibitors converged instead on domain blockade, as AcrIIC1 does, and guide destruction, as AcrVA1 does?

Suggested experts: Joseph Bondy-Denomy

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure AcrF3 dimerisation by size-exclusion chromatography with inline light scattering, and AcrF3-Cas2/3 affinity by isothermal titration calorimetry, in buffers with saturating Ca(2+), with no added divalent cation, and with EDTA, then test inhibition of Cas2/3-dependent plasmid degradation under the same three conditions. Calcium-independence across all three readouts would support removing the keyword outright rather than only marking the annotation over-annotated.

Hypothesis: Calcium is not required for AcrF3 function, so the GO:0046872 annotation reflects crystallisation conditions rather than biology.

Type: metal-dependence titration with paired binding and activity readouts

Experiment: Reconstitute the licensed interference intermediate by assembling Csy complex on a matched protospacer duplex and recruiting purified Cas2/3, then add AcrF3 either before or after recruitment and score target degradation and complex composition by native gel and mass photometry. An order-of-addition effect is the prediction; its absence would mean AcrF3 is an active disassembly factor rather than a sequestering agent, which would change the molecular function assigned here.

Hypothesis: AcrF3 can only intercept free Cas2/3 and cannot displace an already recruited nuclease.

Type: order-of-addition reconstitution assay

Experiment: Challenge Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14 carrying an intact type I-F system with a partially mismatched protospacer plasmid while inducing either AcrF3 or the Csy-blocking gp30, then deep sequence the CRISPR arrays for newly acquired spacers. The mechanisms predict different outcomes, since a Csy blocker prevents the initial recognition that priming starts from whereas AcrF3 permits recognition but removes the nuclease, so comparing them tests whether priming requires Cas2/3 activity or only target engagement.

Hypothesis: AcrF3 blocks primed spacer acquisition in vivo, and does so to a greater extent than inhibitors that act on the surveillance complex.

Type: CRISPR array deep sequencing with paired inhibitor comparison

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: The protein is unreviewed in UniProt and named only "Phage protein", a machine-generated placeholder, despite three deposited structures of this very sequence and a mechanism established twice independently. The single GO annotation it carries describes a crystallisation artefact.

OPEN CURATION

What is known: The mechanism itself is not in doubt. What is missing is any curated record of it on the accession, so the only machine-readable function for this protein is metal ion binding.

Significance: This is the concrete case behind the curation gap recorded in modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml. InterPro already carries a mechanism-bearing family name, IPR049085 "Cas3 inhibitor AcrF3-like", which is why the module grounds the family in InterPro rather than in the member record. A reviewer who follows the accession reaches an entry that does not say what the protein does and does say something misleading about what it binds.

What would resolve it: Nominate L7P7R7 for Swiss-Prot review with a FUNCTION line citing PMID:26416740, PMID:27455460 and PMID:27585537, and drop KW-0479 and KW-0106 or requalify them so that the keyword-to-GO pipeline stops emitting GO:0046872.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "Here we report the crystal structure of the anti-CRISPR protein AcrF3 in complex with Pseudomonas aeruginosa Cas3 (PaCas3)." — PMID:27455460

Gap: It is undetermined whether AcrF3 can extract Cas2/3 that is already engaged with a licensed R-loop, or whether it can only intercept free Cas2/3 before recruitment.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: That AcrF3 prevents recruitment, and which Cas3 surfaces it masks, are both established structurally. The ambiguity concerns the order of events, not the interaction.

Significance: The two possibilities differ in what they demand of the phage. Intercepting free Cas2/3 requires only that AcrF3 reach inhibitory concentration before the first target is licensed, which is a race the phage can win by expressing early. Displacing an engaged nuclease would make the inhibitor effective at any point in the infection, and would also explain how a phage rescues itself after a delay.

What would resolve it: Assemble Csy complex on a target duplex, recruit Cas2/3, then add AcrF3 and follow dissociation and DNA degradation, comparing against addition of AcrF3 before recruitment.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "The third anti-CRISPR protein operates by binding to the Cas3 helicase-nuclease and preventing its recruitment to the DNA-bound CRISPR-Cas complex." — PMID:26416740

Mechanism: the module's Cas3-sequestration assignment holds

The module (annoton acrif3_cas3_sequestration) asserts GO:0140311 protein sequestering activity, with the target being host type I-F Cas3. The assignment holds and is well
supported by two independent structures.

Wang X et al.
PMID:27455460,
PMID:27455460.

Wang J et al. independently
PMID:27585537,
and add a consequence the first paper did not report — inhibition also blocks primed
spacer acquisition
PMID:27585537.
That second point is biologically significant: AcrF3 does not merely spare the current
infection, it prevents the host from updating its CRISPR memory against the phage.

Bondy-Denomy et al. established the mechanism class biochemically before either structure
PMID:26416740,
and noted the striking downstream effect that the inhibited system is converted into a
DNA-binding-only module
PMID:26416740.

GO:0140311 protein sequestering activity is defined as "Binding to a protein to prevent
it from interacting with other partners or to inhibit its localization to the area of the
cell or complex where it is active." Both clauses apply literally: AcrF3 masks the linker
and CTD that Cascade uses to recruit Cas3, so Cas3 cannot interact with its partner and
cannot localise to the R-loop where it is active. This is the one anti-CRISPR mechanism
in the module for which GO already has a precise molecular-function term, and no new
term is proposed here.

Note that in type I-F Cas2 and Cas3 are a single fusion protein
PMID:27585537,
so the sequestered entity is Cas2/3; the term is unaffected.

GOA state: one annotation, and it is an over-annotation

GOA holds exactly one row: enables GO:0046872 metal ion binding, IEA from
GO_REF:0000043, keyword KW-0479 (Metal-binding). The keyword chain is traceable and
the chain is the problem:

PDB 5GNF → UniProt FT BINDING records at residues 2, 2, 3, 65, 66, 70 for three Ca(2+)
ions, all with ECO:0007829|PDB:5GNF (i.e. automatic annotation from PDB ligand records)
→ keywords KW-0106 Calcium and KW-0479 Metal-binding, both with the same PDB-derived
evidence → GO:0046872.

Reasons to treat this as an over-annotation rather than a function:

  1. The paper that produced 5GNF never ascribes any role to calcium. Searching
    PMID:27585537 for "calcium", "Ca2+" or "ion" returns no statement about a metal
    requirement, metal dependence, or a metal site; the mechanism it reports is entirely
    protein-protein (dimerisation plus masking of the Cas3 HD, Linker and CTD domains).
  2. The sites look crystallographic. Two of the three Ca(2+) are coordinated by
    residues 2 and 3, i.e. the extreme N-terminus, and the third by the surface stretch
    65/66/70. Surface and terminal Ca(2+) at a 1.50 Å resolution are the classic signature
    of ions picked up from the crystallisation liquor.
  3. The independent structure disagrees. The 2.60 Å complex 5B7I (PMID:27455460)
    contains no calcium, and its abstract describes the nucleotide state of Cas3 (ADP) as
    the functionally relevant ligand, not a metal on AcrF3.
  4. The mechanism needs no metal. Sequestration by steric masking is not a chemistry
    that requires a cofactor.

Action: MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED rather than REMOVE. The Ca(2+) density is a real
observation in a real deposited structure, so saying "metal ion binding is false" would
overstate the case; what is unsupported is the inference that metal binding is a function
of this protein. This is an IEA keyword mapping, not an experimental annotation, so
challenging it does not run against the rule about deferring to curators who read full
texts — and the relevant full text is cached and has been read.

Not asserted

No Aca repressor is assigned to the JBD5 locus in the evidence reviewed here, so the
module's part 4 is not annotated for this gene.
