# jumble (Salpingoeca rosetta; UniProt F2TWH0; PTSG_00436; GenBank EGD72416) - curation notes

**Note:** automated deep research (e.g. `just deep-research ... --provider perplexity`) was
unavailable in this environment (no provider API keys). There is therefore no
`jumble-deep-research-*.md` file. These notes were compiled manually from the cached
publications in `publications/` and from direct database queries (UniProt, InterPro API).

## Identity

- UniProtKB F2TWH0 (TrEMBL, unreviewed, PE 4 "Predicted"), 467 aa, ORF name PTSG_00436,
  described in UniProt only as "Uncharacterized protein".
- UniProt features: one predicted N-terminal helical transmembrane segment (residues 12-30,
  Phobius), two disordered regions (73-125, 368-389). No domain, family or GO annotation in
  UniProt; GOA has zero annotations.
- InterPro API (queried 2026-09-30): `https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/api/protein/uniprot/F2TWH0`
  returns `"entries":0`, `"domain_architectures":0`; the `entry/.../protein/uniprot/F2TWH0`
  endpoints return HTTP 204 (no content). So no InterPro/Pfam/PANTHER member-database
  signature is called on this protein. This agrees with the primary paper:
  [PMID:30556809 "Although Interpro (Finn et al., 2017) and Pfam (Finn et al., 2016) did not reveal any known protein domains in Jumble, the NCBI Conserved Domain Search (Marchler-Bauer et al., 2017) predicted a glycosyltransferase domain with low confidence (E-value 3.87−03)."]
- Topology: a single N-terminal TM segment followed by a large C-terminal region is the
  classic type II membrane topology of Golgi glycosyltransferases, but that is my inference
  from the UniProt/Phobius feature, not something tested.

## Primary paper: Wetzel et al. 2018 eLife (PMID:30556809)

### Mutant phenotype (demonstrated)
- Jumble is an EMS-induced mutant from the Levin et al. 2014 screen
  [PMID:30556809 "Seafoam, Soapsuds, and Couscous (previously named Branched) were generated through X-ray mutagenesis and Jumble was generated by EMS mutagenesis as documented in Levin et al."]
  (listed as "Mutant class E Jumble" in Table 1 of PMID:25299189).
- Class C phenotype: amorphous clumps, no rosettes, with and without RIFs
  [PMID:30556809 "For this study, we focused on four Class C mutants — Seafoam, Soapsuds, Jumble, and Couscous (previously named Branched in Levin et al., 2014) — that form amorphous, tightly packed clumps of cells, both in the presence and absence of RIFs, but never develop into rosettes (Table 1; Figure 1A,B)."]
- Clumps re-form rapidly after shear; clumping not dependent on cell division; mutant cells
  adhere to wild-type cells
  [PMID:30556809 "Within 30 min after disruption by shear force, cell clumps as large as 75, 55, 32, and 23 cells formed in Couscous, Soapsuds, Seafoam, and Jumble mutant cultures, respectively."]
  [PMID:30556809 "Cell aggregation was not strain-specific, as unlabeled Jumble and Couscous mutant cells adhered to wild type cells identified by their expression of cytoplasmic mWasabi (Figure 1—figure supplement 4)."]

### Mapping (demonstrated)
- Bulk segregant analysis + backcross; single SNV (T->C) causing L305P
  [PMID:30556809 "Given the tight linkage of both traits with the SNV and the absence of any detectable neighboring sequence variants, we infer that the single point mutation at genome position 1:1,919,681 causes both the clumping and rosette defect phenotypes in Jumble mutants."]
  [PMID:30556809 "The jumble gene contains a single exon and is predicted to encode a 467 amino acid protein containing a single transmembrane domain."]

### Rescue (demonstrated, partial penetrance)
- [PMID:30556809 "Transfection of Jumble mutant cells with wild type jumble-mTFP followed by puromycin selection and the addition of RIFs yielded cultures in which 9.33 ± 5.07% of cells were in rosettes (Figure 2C)."]
- [PMID:30556809 "Importantly, we did not detect any rosettes when we transfected Jumble cells with mTFP alone, jumblelw1-mTFP, or mTFP-jumblelw1."]

### Predicted enzymatic family (predicted only)
- Remote structural homology only (HHpred/Phyre2) to polypeptide GalNAc-transferases:
  [PMID:30556809 "Moreover, two different algorithms that use predicted secondary and tertiary structures to identify potential homologs, HHphred (Zimmermann et al., 2018) and Phyre2 (Kelley et al., 2015), predict that Jumble is related to well-annotated glycosyltransferases (HHphred: E-value 7.5−19 to polypeptide N-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 4; Phyre2: Confidence 94.5% to human polypeptide n-acetylgalactosaminyltransferase 2) (Figure 2—figure supplement 2B)."]
- No biochemical assay of any glycosyltransferase activity; no donor or acceptor substrate
  identified [PMID:30556809 "While we have not uncovered the target(s) of the predicted glycotransferases or the exact nature of the interplay between the two phenotypes"].
- Homologs in other choanoflagellates and fungi, none in animals
  [PMID:30556809 "BLAST searches uncovered unannotated jumble homologs in nine other choanoflagellates (Figure 2—figure supplement 2A) and in fungi (Figure 2—figure supplement 3), but none in animals."]

### Localization (demonstrated for tagged, overexpressed protein; organelle identity inferred)
- Jumble-mWasabi under the efl promoter in wild-type cells
  [PMID:30556809 "Jumble-mWasabi protein localized to the apical pole of the cell body near the base of the flagellum."]
  [PMID:30556809 "Based on comparisons with transmission electron micrographs of S."] ...
  "rosetta and other choanoflagellates, Jumble-mWasabi localization corresponds to the location of the Golgi apparatus, for which there is not yet a fluorescent marker in S."
- Caveat: no Golgi marker exists in S. rosetta; assignment is by position relative to EM
  ultrastructure. Construct is overexpressed and tagged. Still, the authors state the
  conclusion directly [PMID:30556809 "Consistent with its role as a glycosyltransferase, Jumble localized to the Golgi apparatus, but Couscous appeared to localize in cytoplasmic puncta and to the cell membrane."]
- The L305P mutant protein is retained in the ER
  [PMID:30556809 "In contrast, Jumblelw1-mWasabi, was distributed in a tubular pattern throughout the cell and co-localized with an endoplasmic reticulum (ER) marker (Figure 2F,H; Figure 2—figure supplement 4B) (Booth et al., 2018)."]

### Glycosylation phenotype (demonstrated; mechanism ambiguous)
- 21/22 lectins unchanged; jacalin basal staining lost
  [PMID:30556809 "In contrast with wild type cells, the basal patch of jacalin staining was absent or significantly diminished in Couscous and Jumble mutants, both in the presence and absence of RIFs (Figure 4C–F)."]
- Restored by complementation
  [PMID:30556809 "Transformation of Jumble cells with mTFP-jumble not only rescued rosette development (Figure 2C,D), but also restored the wild type glycosylation pattern, as revealed by jacalin staining in the center of complemented rosettes (Figure 4—figure supplement 2)."]
- BUT jacalin Western blots of whole-cell lysates showed no clear difference in banding
  between wild type and Jumble ("No clear differences in banding pattern were observed among the S. rosetta strains").
  The authors themselves leave open whether the defect is in glycosylation or in trafficking
  [PMID:30556809 "The loss of basal jacalin staining in Jumble and Couscous mutants indicated that jumblelw1 and couscouslw1 either disrupt proper trafficking of sugar-modified molecules to the basal pole of cells or alter the glycosylation events themselves."]

### Rosetteless in the mutant (demonstrated)
- [PMID:30556809 "In both Jumble and Couscous cells, Rosetteless protein properly localized to the basal pole, but its expression did not increase nor was it secreted upon treatment with RIFs, as normally occurs in wild type cells (Figure 4—figure supplement 3)."]

### Models (speculative, from Discussion)
- Regulatory glycosylation of adhesion molecules (e.g. cadherins), or a protective sugar
  layer masking sticky surfaces; possible glycosylation of Rosetteless or its partners.

## Other literature
- PMID:31896587 (Larson et al. 2020, biophysics of rosettes) summarizes jumble as an ECM
  regulator: "a C-type lectin called rosetteless (18) and 2 predicted glycosyltransferases called jumble and couscous (19)".
- PMID:41037400 (Combredet, Ansel & Brunet 2025, Cell Rep; abstract only in cache): CRISPR
  knockout of jumble (with rosetteless and couscous) as a positive control
  ["We inactivated three known S. rosetta multicellular developmental regulators (rosetteless, couscous, and jumble)"].
  The KO phenotype details are not in the abstract; I have not seen the full text.
- PMID:25299189 (Levin et al. 2014): original isolation of the Jumble mutant (Table 1).

## Annotation decisions (reasoning)

- **CC Golgi apparatus (GO:0005794), IDA, PMID:30556809 - propose NEW.** Direct imaging of
  the fusion protein, organelle assigned by position; authors explicitly conclude Golgi.
  Caveat noted.
- **MF glycosyltransferase activity (GO:0016757) - NOT proposed as NEW.** Evidence is remote
  structural similarity only (HHpred/Phyre2), low-confidence CDD, no InterPro signature, no
  biochemistry. Captured in core_functions as a hedged prediction instead. A specific
  activity (e.g. polypeptide GalNAc-transferase) is certainly not justified.
- **BP glycosylation - NOT proposed.** GO:0070085 glycosylation and GO:0006486 protein
  glycosylation are obsolete (verified QuickGO; 0006486 replaced_by GO:0009101 glycoprotein
  biosynthetic process). An IMP to GO:0009101 was considered, but (i) the glycosylated
  molecule(s) are unknown (not shown to be glycoprotein), (ii) jacalin Western blots of
  lysates showed no difference, so the defect may be spatial (trafficking/secretion) rather
  than loss of a glycan, and (iii) the authors themselves leave both possibilities open.
  Without the MF, participation cannot be established; left as a question.
- **BP rosette development / negative regulation of cell-cell adhesion (GO:0022408) - NOT
  proposed.** Evidence is necessity (mutant/rescue), with mechanism unknown; the protein is
  in the Golgi, not at the adhesion interface. Fails the participation test on current data.
  There is also no GO term for choanoflagellate rosette (colony) development.
