Gene Description

Msed_1700 encodes the aspartate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase (ASD, EC 1.2.1.11) of the thermoacidophilic crenarchaeon Metallosphaera sedula. It is a 348-residue member of the archaeal/fungal branch of the ASD family. ASD catalyses the NADPH-dependent reductive dephosphorylation of 4-phospho-L-aspartate to L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde, the branch-point intermediate for homoserine, threonine and methionine synthesis. The reaction goes through a covalent acyl-thioester intermediate on a catalytic cysteine (Cys150), with His242 as acid/base. The gene lies next to the genes for aspartokinase (Msed_1699) and threonine synthase (Msed_1698), and it carries the archaeal/fungal ASD conserved-domain models. It is about 69% identical to the asd gene product of Sulfolobus tokodaii but only about 39% identical to its M. sedula paralog Msed_0709. Msed_0709 evolved from an ancestral ASD into the malonyl-CoA/succinyl-CoA reductase of the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate CO2-fixation cycle. By analogy with archaeal homologs, the enzyme is expected to be a cytoplasmic homotetramer (a dimer of dimers) that may accept NADH as well as NADPH. The protein has not been characterised experimentally, and its function is inferred from genomic context, sequence signatures and homology.

Core Functions

Aspartate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase. Catalyses the NADPH-dependent reductive dephosphorylation of 4-phospho-L-aspartate (from aspartokinase Msed_1699) to L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde. This is the branch-point step that feeds homoserine, threonine (with threonine synthase Msed_1698) and methionine synthesis, and it uses an acyl-thioester intermediate on Cys150. The function is inferred from genomic context, orthology and signatures; no experimental data exist for this protein.

Msed_1700 (A4YHF3): curation notes

Metallosphaera sedula DSM 5348. UniProt A4YHF3 is an unreviewed TrEMBL entry, 348 aa.
UniProt (via ARBA00067001) names it "malonyl CoA reductase (malonate semialdehyde-forming),
EC 1.2.1.75". This review concludes that it is the aspartate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase
(ASD, EC 1.2.1.11) of M. sedula. ARCHAEA project gene, reviewed alongside the MCR
paralog Msed_0709.
