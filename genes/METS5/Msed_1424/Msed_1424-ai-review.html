Gene Description

Msed_1424 encodes the NADPH-dependent succinic semialdehyde reductase (SSR) of the thermoacidophilic crenarchaeon Metallosphaera sedula. It reduces succinate semialdehyde to 4-hydroxybutyrate in the second, acetyl-CoA-regenerating half of the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate autotrophic CO2-fixation cycle of the Sulfolobales. The enzyme was purified from autotrophically grown cells, identified by peptide mass fingerprinting, and characterized as a recombinant protein. It is a homodimeric medium-chain, zinc-containing alcohol dehydrogenase with about two zinc ions per subunit, one at a catalytic site and one at a structural site. It strongly prefers NADPH over NADH, is specific for succinate semialdehyde (it does not reduce malonic semialdehyde or simple aliphatic aldehydes), and its activity is detectable only in autotrophically grown cells. The gene lies two genes downstream of the acryloyl-CoA reductase gene. The preceding step, reduction of succinyl-CoA to succinate semialdehyde, is catalysed by a different enzyme, the malonyl-CoA/succinyl-CoA reductase Msed_0709. Close homologs act as succinic semialdehyde reductases in other autotrophic Sulfolobales and in Thermoproteus.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

succinate-semialdehyde reductase (NADPH) activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: 4-hydroxybutanoate + NADP+ = succinate semialdehyde + NADPH + H+.

Justification: This reaction is RHEA:26381 (EC 1.1.1.n11), which has no GO term. GO:0047577 4-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase activity covers only the NAD+ reaction (RHEA:23948). The NADPH-dependent enzyme catalyses the 4-hydroxybutyrate-forming step of the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate and dicarboxylate/4-hydroxybutyrate cycles (M. sedula Msed_1424, T. neutrophilus Tneu_0419). An NAD(P)-agnostic grouping term could be considered as a common parent with GO:0047577.

Parent term: alcohol dehydrogenase (NADP+) activity

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:19684143 : Succinic semialdehyde reductase (EC 1.1.1.2) (Fig. 1 ) strongly prefers NADPH as the electron donor and does not act on malonic semialdehyde.

carbon fixation by 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate cycle

Definition: An autotrophic carbon dioxide fixation pathway in which acetyl-CoA is carboxylated twice by acetyl-CoA/propionyl-CoA carboxylase, and the products are reductively converted via 3-hydroxypropionate and succinyl-CoA to 4-hydroxybutyrate. 4-Hydroxybutyrate is then converted via 4-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydratase into two molecules of acetyl-CoA. The pathway occurs in autotrophic Sulfolobales and in ammonia-oxidizing Thaumarchaeota.

Justification: GO:0043427 carbon fixation by 3-hydroxypropionate cycle is defined as the Chloroflexus cycle that releases glyoxylate via malyl-CoA cleavage. The archaeal cycle shares only the first half with it and regenerates acetyl-CoA via 4-hydroxybutyrate, the branch to which Msed_1424 belongs. The same term is proposed in the Msed_0709 review.

Parent term: carbon fixation

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:18079405 : This intermediate was reduced to 4-hydroxybutyrate and converted into two acetyl-CoA molecules via 4-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydratase.

Bottom line: UniProt's name and EC are wrong

UniProt (Swiss-Prot, entry v101) names A4YGN0 "Succinate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase
(acetylating)", EC 1.2.1.76 (RHEA:26450, succinate semialdehyde + NADP+ + CoA = succinyl-CoA
+ NADPH), with ECO:0000269 citing Berg et al. 2007 (PMID:18079405). Its FUNCTION text reads
"Catalyzes the reduction of succinate semialdehyde to succinyl-CoA", which is chemically
incoherent (going from the aldehyde to the thioester is an oxidation).

The primary literature says something different. Msed_1424 is the succinic semialdehyde
reductase (SSR), which reduces succinate semialdehyde with NADPH to 4-hydroxybutyrate
(a Zn-dependent medium-chain alcohol dehydrogenase). The succinyl-CoA reductase step
(EC 1.2.1.76) belongs to Msed_0709, the promiscuous malonyl-CoA/succinyl-CoA reductase
(see genes/METS5/Msed_0709/). So the two genes do not conflict; UniProt has given Msed_1424
the EC of the step just upstream of the one it catalyses.

Where did EC 1.2.1.76 come from?

Unverified. UniProt cites Berg et al. 2007 (PMID:18079405), which predates the gene
identification in Kockelkorn & Fuchs 2009; only the abstract is cached, and I could not read the
Science supplement. Possibly the 2007 paper's supplement listed Msed_1424 as a candidate for
the succinyl-CoA/succinate semialdehyde step, and the Swiss-Prot entry (integrated 2011) was
not updated after PMID:19684143. Whatever the origin, the 2009 and 2011 papers identify the
protein by purification and recombinant expression as SSR, and the succinyl-CoA reductase as
Msed_0709.

Annotation decisions

  • GO:0008270 zinc ion binding (IEA, IPR002328) — ACCEPT: 1.9 Zn/monomer measured; the
    catalytic and structural sites are conserved.
  • GO:0016491 oxidoreductase activity (IEA) — MODIFY to GO:0008106 alcohol dehydrogenase
    (NADP+) activity (EC 1.1.1.2, the EC the authors assign; 4-hydroxybutanoate is a primary
    alcohol and succinate semialdehyde an aldehyde). No GO term exists for the exact reaction
    (RHEA:26381); a new term "succinate-semialdehyde reductase (NADPH) activity" is proposed.
  • NOT proposed: "succinyl-CoA reductase (NADP+) activity" (the Msed_0709 proposed term). It
    is the wrong activity for this gene.
  • NEW GO:0015977 carbon fixation (IDA, PMID:19684143), consistent with Msed_0709; the enzyme
    catalyses a cycle step.

Pattern for the catalogue

A UniProt/Swiss-Prot name and EC (and Rhea catalytic activity) assigned to the wrong step of a
pathway: Msed_1424 carries EC 1.2.1.76 (succinyl-CoA reductase) but is the downstream
succinate semialdehyde reductase. No GO row derives from it yet, but a Rhea-to-GO mapping for
RHEA:26450 (for example a new succinyl-CoA reductase term) would put the wrong activity on it.
The InterPro family (MDR zinc ADH) already contradicts the name.
