Gene Description

Msed_0709 encodes the NADPH-dependent malonyl-CoA/succinyl-CoA reductase (MCR) of the thermoacidophilic crenarchaeon Metallosphaera sedula, a soluble homotetrameric enzyme of the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate autotrophic CO2-fixation cycle of the Sulfolobales. It reduces malonyl-CoA, the product of acetyl-CoA carboxylation, to malonate semialdehyde with release of CoA (EC 1.2.1.75), and with similar efficiency reduces succinyl-CoA to succinate semialdehyde (EC 1.2.1.76) in the second half of the cycle that regenerates acetyl-CoA. The activity is NADPH-specific, thiol-dependent (a catalytic cysteine forms an acyl-enzyme thioester) and about 10-fold induced under autotrophic growth. MCR is a paralog of aspartate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase (about 40% identity) that arose by gene duplication and neofunctionalization; structures of the Sulfolobus tokodaii ortholog show a remodelled substrate pocket and a single bispecific site that is occupied in turn by NADP+ and CoA. It is unrelated to the bifunctional malonyl-CoA reductase of Chloroflexus, and has been used to engineer CO2-dependent 3-hydroxypropionate production in Pyrococcus furiosus.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

malonyl-CoA reductase (NADP+) activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: malonyl-CoA + NADPH + H+ = 3-oxopropanoate (malonate semialdehyde) + CoA + NADP+.

Justification: This is EC 1.2.1.75 (RHEA:26446), and GO has no term for it; ec2go has no mapping for EC 1.2.1.75. GO:0018478 (EC 1.2.1.18) is a different, decarboxylating reaction. The activity is the key reductive step of the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate cycle in Sulfolobales. Any new term should also cover the unrelated Chloroflexus enzyme's first half-reaction.

Parent term: oxidoreductase activity, acting on the aldehyde or oxo group of donors, NAD or NADP as acceptor

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:17041055 : The enzyme catalyzes the following reaction: malonyl-CoA + NADPH + H+ --> malonate-semialdehyde + CoA + NADP+.

succinyl-CoA reductase (NADP+) activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: succinyl-CoA + NADPH + H+ = succinate semialdehyde + CoA + NADP+.

Justification: This is EC 1.2.1.76, succinate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase (acylating), and GO has no term for it. Existing succinate-semialdehyde dehydrogenase terms (GO:0009013, GO:0036243) describe the non-acylating reaction that forms succinate. The activity is required in the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate and dicarboxylate/4-hydroxybutyrate cycles.

Parent term: oxidoreductase activity, acting on the aldehyde or oxo group of donors, NAD or NADP as acceptor

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:19684143 : Succinyl-CoA reduction to succinic semialdehyde is catalyzed by malonyl-CoA/succinyl-CoA reductase, a promiscuous NADPH-dependent enzyme that is a paralogue of aspartate semialdehyde dehydrogenase.

carbon fixation by 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate cycle

Definition: An autotrophic carbon dioxide fixation pathway in which acetyl-CoA is carboxylated twice by acetyl-CoA/propionyl-CoA carboxylase, and the products are reductively converted via 3-hydroxypropionate and succinyl-CoA to 4-hydroxybutyrate. 4-Hydroxybutyrate is then converted via 4-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydratase into two molecules of acetyl-CoA. The pathway occurs in autotrophic Sulfolobales and in ammonia-oxidizing Thaumarchaeota.

Justification: GO:0043427 carbon fixation by 3-hydroxypropionate cycle is defined as the Chloroflexus cycle that releases glyoxylate via malyl-CoA cleavage. The archaeal 3HP/4HB cycle shares only the first half and regenerates acetyl-CoA via 4-hydroxybutyrate, so its enzymes currently have no specific process term.

Parent term: carbon fixation

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:18079405 : This intermediate was reduced to 4-hydroxybutyrate and converted into two acetyl-CoA molecules via 4-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydratase.

Core Functions

NADPH-dependent reduction of malonyl-CoA to malonate semialdehyde with release of CoA (EC 1.2.1.75), the first reductive step after acetyl-CoA carboxylation in the 3-hydroxypropionate/4-hydroxybutyrate CO2-fixation cycle. It proceeds through a catalytic-cysteine acyl-enzyme intermediate.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Construct a Msed_0709 deletion, for example with a Sulfolobales genetic system, and compare growth on CO2/H2 with growth on yeast extract or peptides. Test complementation with Msed_0709 and with the ASD paralog Msed_1700.

Hypothesis: Msed_0709 is required for autotrophic but not heterotrophic growth of Metallosphaera sedula.

Experiment: Purify recombinant Msed_0709 and Msed_1700. Assay both with malonyl-CoA, succinyl-CoA and L-aspartyl-4-phosphate (forward direction), and with aspartate semialdehyde (reverse direction), and determine kcat/Km for each pair.

Hypothesis: The MCR and ASD paralogs have non-overlapping substrate specificities.

returned two proteins: A4YEN2 (Msed_0709, this gene) and A4YHF3 (Msed_1700, unreviewed).
Msed_1700 neighbours are Msed_1698 threonine synthase and Msed_1699 aspartokinase, i.e. the
asd context described in PMID:23325803. So Msed_1700 is the genuine ASD of M. sedula.

Correction (2026-10-15, from the Msed_1700 review): an earlier version of these notes said
that Msed_1700 carries NCBIfam TIGR00978 (asd_EA) and PRK08664 and that A4YEN2 lacks them.
That was wrong: the A4YEN2 UniProt record (Msed_0709-uniprot.txt) also lists TIGR00978 and
NF006416 (PRK08664.1). The paralogs are separated by CDD models instead: Msed_1700 matches the
archaeal/fungal ASD models cd18130 and cd02315, Msed_0709 the MCR models cd23940 and cd24150
(genes/METS5/Msed_1700/Msed_1700-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md).

Both paralogs fall in PANTHER PTHR46718:SF1 and share identical InterPro signatures, so they
receive identical GO sets (ASD activity, threonine biosynthesis, NAD binding, NADP binding,
dimerization). Conversely, ARBA (ARBA00067001) names the true ASD Msed_1700 "malonyl CoA
reductase (malonate semialdehyde-forming), EC 1.2.1.75". The neofunctionalized paralog and
the ancestral enzyme are not separated by either pipeline — a paralog over-annotation in both
directions. (Not cited as a supporting_text source because it is a live database query.)
