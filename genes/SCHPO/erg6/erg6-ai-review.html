Gene Description

erg6 (SPBC16E9.05) encodes the sterol 24-C-methyltransferase of Schizosaccharomyces pombe, a SAM-dependent methyltransferase of the Erg6/SMT family. It transfers a methyl group from S-adenosyl-L-methionine to C-24 of zymosterol to form fecosterol (EC 2.1.1.41), adding the C-28 methyl group that distinguishes ergosterol from cholesterol. Sterol analyses of Schizosaccharomyces suggest that C-24 methylation can also occur on lanosterol, giving eburicol, so erg6 may act at two points of the pathway. erg6 deletion mutants are viable but make no ergosterol, accumulate lanosterol, have abnormal morphology, and are sensitive to cycloheximide and staurosporine; erg6 is induced under anaerobiosis via the SREBP Sre1. The enzyme is associated with the ER.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does S. pombe Erg6 methylate lanosterol to eburicol in vitro, as suggested by eburicol accumulation under azole treatment in Schizosaccharomyces? GO has no lanosterol 24-C-methyltransferase term (GO:0003838 is defined on zymosterol only).

Q: Is the nuclear YFP signal reported for Erg6 in the ORFeome screen a nuclear-envelope/ER signal, and does Erg6 also reside on lipid droplets in fission yeast as in budding yeast?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express S. pombe Erg6 and measure SAM-dependent methylation of zymosterol and lanosterol (kinetics, product identification by GC-MS).

Hypothesis: S. pombe Erg6 accepts both zymosterol and lanosterol as methyl acceptors.
