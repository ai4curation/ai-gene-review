# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe kms1* (UniProt P87245)

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified as ***Schizosaccharomyces pombe kms1***, ORF **SPAC3A11.05c**, encoding Kms1 (“karyogamy meiotic segregation protein 1”). The gene-specific literature concerns the same fission-yeast protein: anti-Kms1 antibodies label defined spindle-pole-body (SPB) structures in wild type but not a *kms1* null strain, while genetic studies use *kms1-1* or *kms1Δ* in *S. pombe*. No findings about a different same-symbol gene or organism are included here. Direct literature-to-accession mapping was not available in the retrieved articles, so the P87245/SPAC3A11.05c equivalence ultimately rests on the UniProt identity supplied in the question; nevertheless, the description, organism, locus symbol, localization, and phenotypes all agree with the literature target. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 2-3, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 8-9)

**Primary functional annotation:** Kms1 is not an enzyme or transporter. It is best understood as a **KASH/Nesprin-like, membrane-associated structural adaptor in the Sad1–Kms1 LINC complex**. At the nuclear envelope/SPB, it transmits cytoskeletal force to nuclear structures. Its dominant meiotic role is to maintain SPB integrity and connect telomere-associated chromosome ends to dynein–microtubule machinery, thereby enabling karyogamy, telomere-bouquet organization, horsetail nuclear motion, homolog alignment and recombination, and faithful chromosome segregation. It also has a separable vegetative/S–G2 function in organizing persistent DNA breaks at nuclear-envelope LINC sites and influencing repair-pathway choice. (hiraoka2020phaseseparationdrives pages 1-3, laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 25-27, swartz2014arolefor pages 5-7)

## 1. Identity, protein class, and domain interpretation

### Identity verification

Niwa and colleagues directly localized Kms1p with a polyclonal antibody. Signal was observed at the leading edges of fusing haploid nuclei, at the tip of the meiotic horsetail nucleus, at meiosis-I and meiosis-II spindle poles, and at the vegetative SPB; specific staining disappeared in a *kms1* null strain. This is strong experimental verification that the genetically defined *kms1* locus encodes the SPB-associated protein described in the supplied UniProt record. [Niwa et al., July 2000, *EMBO Journal*, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/19.14.3831]. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 2-3)

### Structural rather than catalytic function

Kms1 has no demonstrated catalytic reaction, substrate specificity, or transporter substrate. Current literature instead classifies it as a KASH/Nesprin-like component of a LINC—linker of nucleoskeleton and cytoskeleton—complex. LINC complexes pair an inner-nuclear-membrane SUN protein with a cytoplasmic-side KASH protein to convey mechanical force across the nuclear envelope. In *S. pombe*, the relevant SUN partner is Sad1 and the KASH partner is Kms1. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 1-2, kim2022howandwhy pages 2-4, laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 25-27)

A yeast two-hybrid experiment using Kms1 residues 201–607 recovered Sad1 residues 263–514; subsequent mapping implicated the **C-terminal hydrophobic region of Kms1** in Sad1 association. Together with its membrane/SPB localization, this supports the LINC/KASH interpretation, although the retrieved papers did not provide a complete experimentally determined membrane topology or high-resolution Kms1 structure. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 8-9)

### EF-hand annotation caveat

The supplied InterPro call, **EF-hand domain pair (IPR011992)**, was not independently corroborated by the retrieved Kms1 literature. None of the examined studies demonstrated calcium binding, mapped functional EF-hand residues, or interpreted Kms1 as a calcium-regulated enzyme. This does not prove that the computational annotation is false, but it should be treated as **unvalidated for functional annotation**. The experimentally supported model is a C-terminally hydrophobic KASH/LINC structural protein, not a characterized EF-hand enzyme. (laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 25-27, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 8-9)

## 2. Cellular localization

Kms1 functions principally at the **nuclear envelope and spindle pole body**, the fungal centrosome equivalent. Its localization is dynamically relevant to several cell states:

- **Mating/karyogamy:** at the leading edges of the two haploid nuclei before fusion.
- **Meiotic prophase:** at the leading tip of the oscillating horsetail nucleus, where clustered telomeres associate with the SPB.
- **Meiotic divisions:** at meiosis-I and meiosis-II spindle poles.
- **Vegetative growth:** at the SPB, even though *kms1* is not essential for vegetative proliferation.
- **DNA damage:** at additional Sad1–Kms1 foci along microtubules and at persistent DSBs associated with the SPB. (swartz2014arolefor pages 2-3, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 2-3)

In untreated vegetative cells, non-SPB Sad1–Kms1 microtubule-organizing foci were seen in about **4%** of cells. Following DNA damage, Kms1 foci increased, and approximately **40%** of site-specific DSBs at *ade8* comigrated with GFP–Kms1 at the SPB. [Swartz et al., August 2014, *Molecular Biology of the Cell*, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e13-10-0569]. (swartz2014arolefor pages 2-3)

## 3. Mechanism in meiotic chromosome organization

### Telomere–LINC–cytoskeleton axis

The accepted mechanistic pathway is:

**telomeric DNA → Rap1 → Bqt1/Bqt2 → Sad1 (SUN) → Kms1 (KASH) → dynein/microtubules.**

Bqt1 and Bqt2 form a meiosis-specific bridge between telomere-bound Rap1 and Sad1. Sad1 and Kms1 form the membrane-spanning LINC complex, while Kms1 provides the cytoplasmic connection through which microtubule motors exert force. The dynein light chain Dlc1 was reported to interact with Kms1, and the meiotic microtubule organizer Hrs1/Mcp6 also interacts with Kms1. These findings place Kms1 at the mechanical junction rather than at the DNA-recognition step itself. (hiraoka2020phaseseparationdrives pages 1-3, ohtaka2007meiosisspecificcoiledcoil pages 3-5)

The resulting telomere bouquet polarizes chromosomes along the elongated meiotic nucleus. Dynein-driven movement of the SPB produces the approximately **140-minute horsetail period**, during which homolog pairing and recombination occur. Because *S. pombe* lacks a conventional synaptonemal complex, this telomere-led geometry is particularly important for reducing the spatial search required for homolog recognition. (hiraoka2020phaseseparationdrives pages 1-3, hiraoka2020phaseseparationdrives pages 3-5)

### SPB integrity and telomere clustering

In *kms1* mutants, Sad1/SPB material fragments into multiple sites. Telomeres remain attracted to these sites rather than assembling into one compact bouquet: in 20 mutant nuclei, more than **80%** of telomere signals still associated with Sad1 staining, showing that the basic attraction was retained but spatially fragmented. More than **80%** of mutant nuclei had telomeric sequences detached from the normal rDNA-proximal cluster, and approximately **30%** exhibited well-separated telomeric doublets. Thus, Kms1 is required less for telomere recognition than for creating and maintaining a coherent SPB/LINC focus. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 3-5, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 2-3)

The resulting nuclei are frequently deformed and undergo staggered rather than smooth horsetail motion. Chromosome organization is disturbed beyond the telomeres: a centromere-proximal probe appeared in the wrong, rDNA-proximal half of the nucleus in approximately **50%** of mutant cases, and centromere signals became dispersed. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 2-3, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 3-5)

## 4. Consequences for pairing, recombination, and segregation

Direct FISH measurements showed reduced homolog pairing. For one centromere-proximal probe, pairing fell from **80% in wild type to 52% in *kms1***; for another it fell from **56% to 42%**. The latter reduction was modest and the original authors cautioned that limited sample sizes prevented a firm significance claim. Prematurely separated sister chromatids occurred in **17.5% (27/154)** of mutant chromosomes, and separated sisters were associated with unpaired chromosomes. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 5-7)

Genetic experiments establish that bouquet organization governs both the efficiency and fidelity of recombination:

- Allelic recombination in *kms1* was reduced to roughly **30–60% of wild type**.
- Depending on locus geometry, ectopic recombination was unchanged, slightly reduced, or approximately **doubled**.
- Exchange between a linear minichromosome and homologous sequence on chromosome III increased as much as approximately **tenfold**.
- Stable rearranged Ade⁺ products increased from **1/1,800** in a control to **12/2,200** in the relevant experimental cross. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 5-7, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 7-8)

These results support a precise functional interpretation: Kms1-dependent bouquet architecture **promotes productive allelic encounters while restricting ectopic interactions**. The protein does not itself catalyze recombination; it improves template search and partner discrimination by mechanically orienting and moving chromosomes. [Niwa et al., 2000, https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/19.14.3831]. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 8-9, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 1-2)

Earlier *kms1* work also reported elevated chromosome missegregation, consistent with the observed pairing, cohesion, and recombination defects. The downstream reduction in viable meiotic products therefore reflects failures accumulated during chromosome organization rather than a direct sporulation enzyme defect. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 5-7, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 1-2)

## 5. Karyogamy and meiotic quality control

Localization at the facing edges of conjugating nuclei and the original “karyogamy meiotic segregation” phenotype place Kms1 in nuclear congression/fusion. Mechanistically, this is consistent with its role coupling the nuclear envelope to microtubule motors. The strongest detailed retrieved experiments, however, concern post-fusion meiotic architecture, so Kms1’s karyogamy role is less quantitatively resolved here than its bouquet function. (niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 2-3)

In a later study, *kms1Δ* meioses produced many low-quality spores. Only about **10%** of chemically released mutant spores formed colonies after four days, whereas nearly **60%** of spores released naturally by ascus-wall endolysis were viable. The authors proposed that defective pairing leaves unresolved recombination intermediates, activating Rad3/ATR- and Tel1/ATM-dependent signaling that suppresses ascus-wall breakdown and preferentially releases better-quality progeny. The viability difference was experimental; the chain from Kms1-dependent pairing defects to persistent intermediates and checkpoint control was presented as a mechanistic model. [Guo & King, December 2013, *PLOS ONE*, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0082758]. (guo2013aqualitycontrol pages 6-7)

## 6. Vegetative DNA-repair function

Kms1 is expressed in vegetatively growing cells and contributes to the behavior of persistent S/G2 DNA double-strand breaks. Site-specific DSBs comigrated with Sad1 at the oscillating SPB in approximately **25–40%** of cells. DNA damage induced additional Sad1–Kms1 foci along microtubules; ATR/Rad3 and long-range resection were required for robust Sad1-focus formation. Kms1 loss did not abolish DSB–Sad1 association, but approximately **15%** of *kms1Δ* cells displayed a break associated with Sad1 away from the SPB, indicating defective coupling or consolidation. (swartz2014arolefor pages 2-3, swartz2014arolefor pages 3-5)

Functionally, *kms1Δ* reduced gene-conversion efficiency by about **13%** and shifted outcomes toward NHEJ/sister-chromatid-conversion classes. Because Kms1 provides the physical Sad1-to-microtubule linkage, these findings support a model in which LINC-driven movement changes the spatial search or pathway choice of persistent lesions, rather than Kms1 directly repairing DNA. (swartz2014arolefor pages 5-7)

## 7. Recent developments, 2023–2025

No Kms1-focused primary paper from **2023–2024** was identified. The most relevant 2024 study focused on Sad1 and heterochromatin organization, but it independently retained Kms1’s classification as the KASH-family partner in the Sad1 LINC complex. It found that Sad1 binds H2A–H2B and contributes to nuclear-envelope tethering and heterochromatin silencing; those new activities were demonstrated for Sad1, not Kms1, and should not be automatically transferred to Kms1. [Sun et al., May 2024, *Nature Communications*, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48418-7]. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 1-2)

The newest Kms1-specific extension is a **bioRxiv preprint posted April 18, 2025** and therefore not yet equivalent to peer-reviewed evidence. It proposes that Kms1 and Cbp1 enforce faithful homology-directed repair at the nuclear periphery. Reported findings include:

- In *rad51Δ* cells, spontaneous Rad52-focus-positive cells fell from approximately **50% to 25–30%** after *kms1* deletion.
- *kms1Δ* increased 5-FOA-resistant salvage-repair colonies approximately **threefold** in an incompatible-DSB assay.
- Homeologous recombination between related *pfl* loci increased from **2.1 × 10⁻⁸** in wild type to **6.24 × 10⁻⁸** in *kms1Δ*.
- Mutant repair products were more diverse and included microhomology-mediated junctions, suggesting relaxed template/pathway control. [Laffitte et al., bioRxiv 2025, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.12.648544]. (laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 17-20, laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 15-17, laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 20-21)

These results extend the same organizing principle seen in meiosis: Kms1-dependent nuclear-envelope mechanics appears to favor appropriate homologous templates and suppress ectopic or mutagenic alternatives. That unifying model is compelling, but its newest repair-fidelity details await peer review. (laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 23-25, laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 25-27)

## 8. Applications and broader significance

Kms1 currently has **research-model rather than clinical or industrial implementation**. No drug, diagnostic, engineered production strain, or clinical application specific to Kms1 was identified. Its practical uses are as an experimental handle for:

1. dissecting SUN–KASH/LINC force transmission across the nuclear envelope;
2. testing how telomere-led motion promotes homolog pairing without a conventional synaptonemal complex;
3. distinguishing allelic from ectopic recombination and chromosome-rearrangement pathways;
4. studying how nuclear positioning and microtubule-dependent motion influence DSB template choice; and
5. understanding conserved meiotic chromosome movement relevant to animal and plant fertility biology. (kim2022howandwhy pages 2-4, hiraoka2020phaseseparationdrives pages 1-3, hiraoka2020phaseseparationdrives pages 3-5)

## Evidence-weighted final annotation

**Kms1 is a non-catalytic KASH/LINC structural component at the outer nuclear-envelope/SPB interface of fission yeast.** Its best-established role is to organize a coherent Sad1–Kms1 force-transmission site that links the Rap1–Bqt telomere bouquet to dynein and microtubules. This linkage supports nuclear congression/fusion, coherent horsetail movement, chromosome polarization, homolog pairing, allelic recombination, and accurate segregation while limiting ectopic recombination. In vegetative S/G2 cells, the same mechanical bridge helps organize persistent DSBs at the nuclear periphery and biases repair toward higher-fidelity homologous outcomes. The supplied EF-hand-pair annotation remains computational and functionally unverified in the retrieved literature; it should not supersede the experimentally supported KASH/LINC assignment. (hiraoka2020phaseseparationdrives pages 1-3, laffitte2026thelinccomplex pages 25-27, swartz2014arolefor pages 5-7, niwa2000telomere‐ledbouquetformation pages 8-9)
