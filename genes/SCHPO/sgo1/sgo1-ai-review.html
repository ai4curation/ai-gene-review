Gene Description

Shugoshin-1 (Sgo1) is the meiosis-specific shugoshin of fission yeast that protects centromeric sister-chromatid cohesion during the first meiotic division. It localizes to the centromere/pericentromeric heterochromatin and the inner kinetochore, where it recruits and retains Par1-containing protein phosphatase 2A (Paa1-Par1-Ppa2). PP2A bound by Sgo1 keeps the meiotic cohesin subunit Rec8 dephosphorylated, thereby protecting it from cleavage by separase at anaphase I, so that cohesion between sister centromeres persists until meiosis II and homologous chromosomes (rather than sisters) segregate at meiosis I. Centromeric recruitment of Sgo1 depends on Bub1 kinase (via phosphorylation of histone H2A at Ser121) and on direct binding to the heterochromatin protein Swi6 (HP1). Sgo1 abundance declines after meiosis I. It belongs to the conserved shugoshin/MEI-S332 family; the paralog Sgo2 acts predominantly in mitotic chromosome biorientation and tension sensing.

AIGR Deep Research Report — S. pombe sgo1 (Q9P7A0)

Hypothesis (slug: mitotic-evidence-provenance): Determine whether PMID:20739936 establishes an endogenous mitotic function for S. pombe Sgo1, and distinguish this from engineered expression or experiments on other shugoshins (Sgo2).

Annotation under review (PomBase, retained):
sgo1 → GO:1990758 "mitotic sister chromatid biorientation", evidence IMP, reference PMID:20739936, annotation extension happens_during GO:0000089 "mitotic metaphase". (This is the "mitotic-metaphase IMP annotation" named in the task context.)

Executive Judgment

Verdict: Over-annotated for Sgo1 (weakly supported at best). The endogenous mitotic function in PMID:20739936 is established for the paralog Sgo2, named explicitly in Figure 2.

PMID:20739936 (Tsukahara, Tanno & Watanabe, Nature 2010, 467:719–723, "Phosphorylation of the CPC by Cdk1 promotes chromosome bi-orientation") is a genuine S. pombe mitotic study. Its endogenous mitotic mechanism — Cdk1–cyclin-B phosphorylation of Survivin (Bir1) driving direct binding to shugoshin, which acts as the centromeric adaptor recruiting the chromosomal passenger complex (CPC) for bi-orientation — is demonstrated for Sgo2, the mitotic fission-yeast shugoshin.

Figure-level provenance (recovered in Iteration 2). The publisher HTML (doi:10.1038/nature09390) exposes the abstract, all four figure captions, and the reference list. Figure 2 is titled: "Phosphorylation of Bir1 promotes the association with Sgo2 and targeting the CPC to centromeres." Figures 1, 3 and 4 concern Bir1 phosphorylation, human Borealin, and generic "shugoshins." Across all accessible text, the string "Sgo1" occurs 0 times, while "Sgo2" occurs 4 times (the Fig. 2 title plus citations to human Tripin/hSgo2). Thus the named fission-yeast shugoshin in the paper's figures is Sgo2, not Sgo1.

This matches the paper's own PomBase curation (a Canto session curated by author Y. Watanabe), which assigns every piece of direct mitotic evidence — adaptor activity (GO:0140463 IPI, "recruits Bir1"), protein–protein binding, and two-hybrid interaction phenotypes — to Sgo2 (SPAC15A10.15). Sgo1 (SPBP35G2.03c) received only a bare gene-level GO:1990758 IMP with no allele, no genotype, and no accompanying mutant-phenotype (FYPO) annotation — unusual for an IMP, which by definition requires a mutant phenotype.

Independently, Sgo1 is meiosis-specific (Kitajima 2004; Kawashima 2007; Vanoosthuyse 2007) and its PomBase expression records are entirely meiosis-associated, so an endogenous mitotic role is biologically implausible. The most parsimonious reading is that the Sgo1 IMP is a paralog-parallel / conserved-mechanism carry-over annotation rather than a figure-supported endogenous mitotic function of Sgo1.

Most important caveat (reported honestly): The Nature full body text and Supplementary remain paywalled (not in PMC/Europe PMC/OA; Semantic Scholar returned no open PDF). I could read the abstract, figure captions, and reference list but not the Methods/Supplementary, so a buried Sgo1 mention cannot be fully excluded. However, the main figure explicitly names Sgo2 and Sgo1 is absent from all accessible content, so the residual uncertainty is small. Even if a minor in-vitro Sgo1 binding experiment existed, it would support a biochemical/conserved-binding property (an MF/IPI statement), not an endogenous in-vivo mitotic BP role.

sgo1 (Shugoshin-1) — S. pombe — Review Notes

UniProt: Q9P7A0 (SGO1_SCHPO). PomBase: SPBP35G2.03c. 319 aa. Pfam Shugoshin_N + Shugoshin_C; coiled-coil 33-62.

Summary of function

Sgo1 is the fission-yeast meiosis-specific shugoshin that protects centromeric sister-chromatid cohesion during meiosis I. It localizes to the centromere/pericentromere (kinetochore + pericentric heterochromatin), recruits the PP2A-B56 phosphatase, and thereby keeps the cohesin subunit Rec8 dephosphorylated and protected from separase cleavage at anaphase I, so centromeric cohesion persists until meiosis II. Its paralog Sgo2 acts mainly in mitosis/tension sensing.

Key evidence

  • Identification as protector of centromeric cohesin Rec8 in fission yeast meiosis; Sgo2 paralog required for mitotic segregation; Bub1 required for centromeric localization of Sgo1/Sgo2 [PMID:14730319 "Here we identify Sgo1 (shugoshin), a protector of the centromeric cohesin Rec8 in fission yeast"; "Localization of Sgo1 and Sgo2 at centromeres requires the kinase Bub1"].

  • sgo1 and sgo2 are Mei-S332 homologs; sgo1 required for retention of sister centromere cohesion between meiotic divisions; Rec8 retained at centromeres after meiosis I is reduced in Δsgo1 not Δsgo2; Sgo1 regulates Rec8 cleavage by separase; both localize to centromere regions PMID:14972679.

  • Sgo1 recruits PP2A to centromeres; inactivation causes loss of centromeric cohesin at anaphase I; PP2A blocks Rec8 phosphorylation/cleavage PMID:16541024.

  • Meiotic shugoshin of fission yeast associates with PP2A; both collaboratively protect Rec8-containing cohesin at centromeres; mechanism is dephosphorylation of cohesin PMID:16541025. The IPI interactor UniProtKB:Q10428 is par1 (PP2A B56 regulatory subunit). PomBase:SPCC188.02 is also par1.

  • Sgo1 is a meiosis-specific centromere protein loaded onto the centromere at times similar to the NMS (Ndc80-Mis12-Spc7) group; Sgo1 protects Rec8 at the centromere to maintain cohesion between sister centromeres until meiosis II [PMID:17035632 "another meiosis-specific protein, Sgo1, is loaded at times similar to the NMS group"; "Sgo1 protects Rec8 at the centromere to maintain cohesion between sister centromeres until meiosis II"].

  • Heterochromatin protein Swi6 (HP1) binds directly to meiosis-specific Sgo1; Sgo1(V242E) abolishes Swi6 interaction and impairs centromeric localization/function; forced centromeric Sgo1 restores meiotic segregation in swi6 cells PMID:18716626. swi6 = UniProtKB:P40381. This is the basis for the swi6 IPI.

  • Bub1 phosphorylates histone H2A-S121; this mark localizes shugoshin proteins to centromeres; loss phenocopies localization defect; tethering shugoshin restores CIN defects PMID:19965387. (Paper covers both Sgo1 and Sgo2; PomBase assigned the sgo1 cohesion-protection, pericentric heterochromatin, and meiotic spindle localization annotations.)

  • Survivin/Borealin phosphorylation promotes binding with shugoshin, "a conserved centromeric adaptor of the CPC"; relevant to mitotic bi-orientation PMID:20739936. IPI interactor Q10428 (par1) again. The mitotic sister chromatid biorientation IMP was assigned to sgo1 by PomBase from this paper.
    NOTE/CAUTION: In S. pombe the dominant mitotic CPC/tension-sensing shugoshin is Sgo2; this paper discusses "shugoshin" generically. The IMP mitotic biorientation annotation to sgo1 should be flagged as uncertain (KEEP_AS_NON_CORE / note), not removed — the curator read the full text.

  • Meikin (Moa1)-Plo1 recruits Bub1 to kinetochores in meiosis I; meiotic Bub1 ensures robust Sgo1 localization and cohesion protection cooperating with Swi6 which binds/stabilizes Sgo1; Sgo1 localizes to kinetochore PMID:28497540.

  • Sgo1 declines/degraded after meiosis I (APC ubiquitination) [PMID:14972679 "The abundance of Sgo1 protein normally declines after the first meiotic division"; UniProt: "Ubiquitinated by the anaphase promoting complex (APC) at the onset of anaphase"].

Core function synthesis

  1. Molecular adaptor/PP2A recruitment: Sgo1 acts as a chromatin-protein adaptor (GO:0140463) that recruits PP2A-B56 (Par1/Par2) to the meiotic centromere and bridges to heterochromatin (Swi6). Avoid bare "protein binding".
  2. BP: meiotic centromeric cohesion protection in anaphase I (GO:1990813); meiotic sister chromatid segregation/cohesion; meiotic chromosome segregation.
  3. CC: chromosome centromeric region, condensed chromosome centromeric region, inner kinetochore/kinetochore, pericentric heterochromatin, nucleus.
