| Identity and family **(direct Spg1 evidence)** | The target is the *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* septum-promoting GTPase Spg1: a small Ras-superfamily GTPase in the distinct Tem1/Spg1 family. This agrees with P87027’s P-loop NTPase, small-GTPase, Ras, and Tem1/Spg1 domain annotations. | cDNA/genomic sequencing, sequence alignment, and molecular phylogeny | 198 aa; predicted mass 22,543 Da; 57% identity and 68% conservative similarity to budding-yeast Tem1; 17% identity to human N-Ras; lacks a canonical C-terminal CAAX prenylation motif | Schmidt et al., June 1997, *Genes & Development*, [10.1101/gad.11.12.1519](https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.11.12.1519) (pqac-00000019) |
| Biochemical reaction and regulator specificity **(direct Spg1 evidence)** | Spg1 is a guanine-nucleotide switch: active Spg1–GTP hydrolyzes GTP to GDP + inorganic phosphate. Byr4 binds Spg1 and, together with Cdc16, forms an obligate two-component GAP that accelerates hydrolysis; Cdc16 alone had no detectable activity. | Purified-protein radiolabeled-nucleotide binding, dissociation, and hydrolysis assays; co-precipitation; two-hybrid analysis | Byr4 fragment retained 43% of Spg1 versus 0.7% with GST control. Combined dissociation-plus-hydrolysis rate was >2 min⁻¹ with Byr4–Cdc16 versus 0.2 min⁻¹ without regulators or with Cdc16 alone. None of seven tested Ypt GTPases showed measurable Byr4–Cdc16 regulation. | Furge et al., 12 August 1998, *Current Biology*, [10.1016/S0960-9822(98)70394-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0960-9822(98)70394-X) (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018) |
| Essentiality and null phenotype **(direct Spg1 evidence)** | *spg1* is essential for cytokinesis/septum formation, not for DNA replication or repeated mitosis. Null spores germinate and grow but fail to divide, becoming elongated and multinucleate. | Targeted gene replacement, tetrad dissection, microscopy, and microtubule immunostaining | All 30 dissected heterozygous-null tetrads produced only the two wild-type spores as colonies; null spores underwent multiple nuclear divisions without cell division and showed no excess anaphase cells. | Schmidt et al., June 1997, *Genes & Development*, [10.1101/gad.11.12.1519](https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.11.12.1519) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000019) |
| Dosage and ectopic activation **(direct Spg1 evidence)** | Spg1 abundance/activity is strongly dosage-sensitive. Overexpression can trigger septation outside mitosis—including G1, S, and G2—and can produce repeated septa; the response requires Cdc7 but not Cdc2/CDK activity. Protein and transcript abundance are not detectably cell-cycle periodic, implying regulation mainly through nucleotide state and localization. | Conditional overexpression, cell-cycle arrests, cytology, genetic epistasis, co-immunoprecipitation, and two-hybrid analysis | Septation was followed over 0–300 min after induction; increased Spg1 produced mono- and multiseptate cells, whereas increased Cdc7 could bypass deficient Spg1. Exact fold-overexpression was not reported in the cited experiments. | Schmidt et al., June 1997, *Genes & Development*, [10.1101/gad.11.12.1519](https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.11.12.1519) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) |
| Localization and mitotic asymmetry **(direct Spg1/SIN evidence)** | Spg1 resides on spindle-pole bodies (SPBs) throughout the cell cycle. It is GDP-bound and inhibited by SPB-associated Byr4–Cdc16 during interphase, becomes active at both SPBs around metaphase, and remains active mainly at the new SPB during anaphase as GAP activity silences the old pole. | Live/fixed-cell fluorescence localization, nucleotide-state-dependent effector localization, and GAP-mutant analysis | Loss of Cdc16 leaves Spg1–GTP and its effectors Cdc7/Sid1 on both anaphase SPBs; in wild type, Cdc7 changes from two-SPB localization to one-SPB localization during anaphase. | Fournier et al., January 2001, *Archives of Microbiology*, [10.1007/s002030000238](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002030000238); Rachfall et al., August 2014, *Molecular Biology of the Cell*, [10.1091/mbc.E14-04-0936](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.E14-04-0936) (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000020) |
| Downstream SIN cascade **(direct and pathway-level evidence)** | Spg1–GTP binds/recruits its effector kinase Cdc7 at the SPB. Signaling then proceeds through Sid1–Cdc14 and Sid2–Mob1; Sid2–Mob1 relocates to the contractile ring, where SIN output promotes ring formation/maintenance/constriction and septum synthesis. Sid2 also feeds back through Cdc11 to reinforce Cdc7 association and SIN asymmetry. | Protein interaction, genetic epistasis, localization dependency, kinase/phosphosite analysis, and forced SPB tethering | Five major Sid2-regulated Cdc11 residues were mapped (S121, S122, S264, S345, S418). Spg1-driven ectopic septation requires downstream Cdc7 and Sid2 activity. | Guertin et al., April 2000, *EMBO Journal*, [10.1093/emboj/19.8.1803](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/19.8.1803); Feoktistova et al., May 2012, *Molecular Biology of the Cell*, [10.1091/mbc.E11-09-0792](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.E11-09-0792) (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000021) |
| Meiosis and sporulation **(pathway-level evidence including Spg1 localization)** | SIN proteins, including Spg1, localize to meiotic SPBs. SIN activation during meiosis II is required for correct forespore-membrane extension and encapsulation of the four haploid nuclei; meiotic nuclear divisions can remain grossly normal when sporulation fails. | Chromosomal GFP tagging, microscopy, and SIN-mutant phenotyping during mating/meiosis | Opposite mating types expressing GFP-tagged SIN proteins were examined after 20 h at 25°C. SIN mutants initiated forespore membranes but failed to encapsulate nuclei properly and could not form spores. | Krapp et al., July 2006, *Journal of Cell Science*, [10.1242/jcs.03025](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.03025) (pqac-00000016) |
| Carbon source and PKA regulation **(2024 pathway-level development; not a new Spg1 biochemical mechanism)** | Growth on non-fermentable glycerol lowers—but does not eliminate—the amount of SIN signaling needed for cytokinesis. Much of this effect is reproduced by deleting *pka1*, connecting metabolic state to the Spg1-controlled network; the molecular PKA target remains unresolved. | Genetic suppression, carbon-source shifts, fluorescence localization, SPB tethering, and epistasis | In *etd1Δ*, Mob1–GFP appeared at contractile rings in 10% of glycerol-grown cells and 14% of glucose-grown *pka1Δ* cells, versus none in glucose controls at 29°C. After 5 h at 29°C, 85% of elongated *etd1Δ* cells lacked Sid1 signal. Cdc7 or Sid1 tethering to Cdc11 rescued *etd1Δ* under tested conditions, although Cdc7 tethering caused multiseptation at ≥29°C. | Uysal Özdemir et al., January 2024 (received 13 July 2023; accepted 24 November 2023), *Journal of Cell Science*, [10.1242/jcs.261488](https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.261488) (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015) |
