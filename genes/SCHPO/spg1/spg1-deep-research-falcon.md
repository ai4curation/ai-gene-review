# Research report: *spg1* / Spg1 (UniProt P87027) in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified. *spg1* encodes the fission-yeast septum-promoting GTPase Spg1, also represented historically by the allele name *sid3*, in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*. It is a 198-amino-acid Ras-superfamily small GTPase and the founding fission-yeast member of the Tem1/Spg1 family. This identity agrees with the supplied P87027 annotations—P-loop NTPase, small-GTPase/Ras fold, and Tem1/Spg1-family signatures—and does not refer to a same-symbol gene from another organism. The original sequence study found 57% amino-acid identity to budding-yeast Tem1 and 68% similarity when conservative substitutions were included; Spg1 lacks the canonical C-terminal CAAX prenylation motif. (schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 2-3)

Functionally, Spg1 is the nucleotide-controlled upstream switch of the septation initiation network (SIN). At spindle pole bodies (SPBs), GTP-bound Spg1 recruits and activates signaling through Cdc7, followed by Sid1–Cdc14 and Sid2–Mob1. This kinase cascade promotes formation, maintenance, and constriction of the contractile actomyosin ring and coordinates it with division-septum synthesis. Spg1 is therefore a signaling GTPase, not a metabolic enzyme, transporter, or structural ring component. Its direct biochemical reaction is GTP hydrolysis:

**Spg1–GTP + H₂O → Spg1–GDP + inorganic phosphate**, strongly accelerated by the highly specific two-component GAP Byr4–Cdc16. (furge1998byr4andcdc16 pages 1-2, furge1998byr4andcdc16 pages 5-6, rachfall2014cdk1promotescytokinesis pages 1-2)

## Identity verification and nomenclature

The direct *S. pombe* literature identifies *spg1* as “septum-promoting GTPase 1.” Genomic/cDNA sequencing established a five-exon locus encoding a predicted 22.5-kDa, 198-residue protein. Phylogenetic analysis placed Spg1 and *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* Tem1 in a distinct small-GTPase family, closest overall to Rab/Ypt proteins but lacking defining Rab residues and the usual CAAX prenylation signal. TEM1 could not rescue the tested temperature-sensitive *spg1* mutant, indicating evolutionary relatedness without full functional interchangeability. (schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 2-3, schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 3-4)

The historical *sid3* designation is consistent with *spg1*: studies of the SIN list *sid3* alleles alongside *cdc7, cdc11, cdc14, sid1, sid2,* and *sid4*. No claims below are drawn from unrelated genes called SPG1 in other organisms.

## Primary molecular function

### A guanine-nucleotide signaling switch

Spg1 cycles between an active GTP-bound state and an inactive GDP-bound state. Its intrinsic nucleotide hydrolysis is experimentally demonstrable, but physiological shutoff depends on Byr4 and Cdc16. Byr4 binds both Spg1 and Cdc16; Byr4 alone stabilizes Spg1-bound GTP by inhibiting nucleotide dissociation and hydrolysis, whereas the Byr4–Cdc16 combination has potent GAP activity. Cdc16 alone showed no detectable GAP activity in the purified system. Thus Byr4 is not merely a conventional catalytic GAP subunit: it serves as a Spg1-binding specificity/scaffold component that permits Cdc16-dependent hydrolysis. (furge1998byr4andcdc16 pages 1-2, furge1998byr4andcdc16 pages 5-6)

The specificity was unusually strong. A Byr4 fragment retained 43% of Spg1 in a binding assay versus 0.7% with GST control. The combined GTP-loss rate exceeded 2 min⁻¹ with Byr4 plus Cdc16, compared with approximately 0.2 min⁻¹ without regulators or with Cdc16 alone. Byr4–Cdc16 did not detectably regulate any of seven tested *S. pombe* Ypt GTPases, supporting genuine substrate specificity for Spg1 rather than nonspecific activity toward small GTPases. (furge1998byr4andcdc16 pages 5-6)

No well-established Spg1-specific guanine-nucleotide exchange factor has emerged from the retrieved evidence. Etd1 promotes late SIN signaling and SPB association of SIN kinases, but it lacks a recognizable GEF domain; it is therefore safer to describe Etd1 as a positive SIN regulator rather than the demonstrated catalytic GEF for Spg1. The mechanism by which GDP-bound Spg1 acquires GTP remains incompletely resolved.

### Effector interaction

GTP-bound Spg1 binds the protein kinase Cdc7. Genetic suppression, co-immunoprecipitation, two-hybrid interaction, and nucleotide-state-dependent localization place Cdc7 immediately downstream of Spg1. Increased Cdc7 can bypass deficient Spg1, whereas Spg1-induced ectopic septation requires Cdc7. Spg1 inactivation did not simply eliminate Cdc7 catalytic activity in vitro, suggesting that Spg1 primarily controls Cdc7 recruitment, localization, substrate access, or effective activation at the SPB. (schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 1-2, schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 10-11, schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 3-4, furge1998byr4andcdc16 pages 1-2)

## Cellular localization and spatial regulation

Spg1 resides at the cytoplasmic face of SPBs throughout the cell cycle, assembled with the SIN on the Cdc11–Sid4 scaffold. During interphase, SPB-associated Byr4–Cdc16 maintains Spg1 predominantly in the GDP-bound state and suppresses premature cytokinesis. At mitotic entry, Byr4–Cdc16 is removed from SPBs, allowing Spg1 activation and Cdc7 recruitment. Spg1 is initially active at both SPBs, but anaphase signaling becomes asymmetric: GAP activity returns principally to the old SPB, inactivating Spg1 there, while active Spg1–Cdc7 persists at the new SPB. (rachfall2014cdk1promotescytokinesis pages 1-2, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

This asymmetry is functional rather than merely descriptive. Loss of Cdc16 leaves GTP-bound Spg1 at both anaphase SPBs and produces symmetric Cdc7 and Sid1 localization. Nevertheless, certain mutant contexts can support septation despite visibly symmetric Cdc7, indicating that localization asymmetry improves signaling fidelity but may not be an absolute logical requirement in every engineered background. (fournier2001bypassofthe pages 7-8)

Spg1 does not need a CAAX lipid anchor for this localization. Instead, direct interaction with the SPB-associated Cdc11 scaffold and associated SIN proteins provides the most consistent localization mechanism. Cdc11’s N-terminal region interacts with Spg1, Cdc16, and Sid2, while its C-terminal repeats attach to Sid4. (schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 2-3, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

## Pathway position and biological process

A simplified evidence-supported pathway is:

**Plo1/Cdk1-dependent removal or inhibition of Byr4–Cdc16 → Spg1–GTP → Cdc7 → Sid1–Cdc14 → Sid2–Mob1 → contractile-ring maintenance/constriction and septum synthesis.**

Cdk1 has a dual temporal role. Although declining Cdk1 activity later permits full cytokinesis, Cdk1-mediated phosphorylation helps remove Byr4 from metaphase SPBs, thereby priming Spg1 activation in concert with Polo-like kinase Plo1. A nonphosphorylatable Byr4 mutant caused elongated multinucleate cells, poor ring maintenance, and reduced Cdc7 association with SPBs. (rachfall2014cdk1promotescytokinesis pages 1-2)

The terminal Sid2–Mob1 kinase complex occupies both SPBs and subsequently the medial contractile ring. It supplies major SIN output and also feeds back to strengthen the pathway by phosphorylating the Cdc11 scaffold. Five major Sid2-responsive Cdc11 residues—Ser121, Ser122, Ser264, Ser345, and Ser418—were mapped; their regulation promotes Cdc7 association and maximal anaphase SIN signaling. Thus the pathway is not strictly linear but incorporates positive feedback that stabilizes spatial asymmetry and signaling amplitude. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

SIN output coordinates several tightly related processes: contractile-ring formation and maintenance, ring constriction, membrane ingression, and deposition of the medial division septum. Spg1 should not be annotated as a glucan synthase or cytoskeletal structural protein; instead, it is the upstream signal that licenses and times those downstream mechanical and biosynthetic events. (ozdemir2024arolefor pages 1-2, rachfall2014cdk1promotescytokinesis pages 1-2, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

## Genetic evidence for physiological necessity

Targeted disruption provided strong evidence that *spg1* is essential. In 30 dissected tetrads from a heterozygous knockout, only the two wild-type spores per tetrad formed colonies. Null spores germinated and continued cellular growth and repeated nuclear divisions but failed to divide, producing elongated multinucleate cells. Normal-looking spindle/interphase microtubules and no excess of anaphase cells argued against a primary defect in DNA replication, spindle assembly, or mitotic exit. The principal indispensable function is cytokinesis/septation. (schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 2-3)

Spg1 is also strongly dosage sensitive. Overexpression triggers septation in G1, S phase, or G2 rather than only after mitosis and can produce mononucleate, multiseptate cells. Conversely, loss of positive SIN activity allows cell growth and nuclear cycling to continue without cytokinesis. RNA and protein abundance do not vary substantially through the cell cycle, indicating that nucleotide state, localization, and protein interactions—not periodic synthesis or degradation—are the dominant regulatory variables. (schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 1-2, schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 10-11, schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 7-8, schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 2-3)

Ectopic Spg1-induced septa are often not cleaved, showing that turning on the SIN is sufficient to initiate much of the septation program but not necessarily all downstream cell-separation events. This is an important boundary on the interpretation of overexpression phenotypes. (schmidt1997thespg1pgtpase pages 10-11)

## Meiosis and sporulation

Spg1 and other SIN proteins also localize to SPBs during meiosis. In this context the network is activated during meiosis II, when forespore membrane formation begins. SIN-deficient cells can complete apparently normal meiotic nuclear divisions and initiate forespore membranes, yet fail to encapsulate the four haploid nuclei correctly and consequently fail to form mature spores. The meiotic role is therefore another compartmentalization function—coordinating membrane development around segregated nuclei—rather than conventional medial septation. (krapp2006theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-2)

This evidence is strongest for the SIN as a network; it does not establish that every observed sporulation phenotype is caused solely by Spg1 rather than by downstream SIN kinases. Nonetheless, meiotic SPB localization of tagged Spg1 and the network’s dependence on Spg1-controlled signaling support inclusion of forespore-membrane organization/spore formation as a secondary biological role.

## Recent developments, 2023–2024

The gene-specific biochemical model remains founded mainly on detailed 1997–2014 experiments. Recent work has refined the physiological inputs and experimental resources rather than replacing that mechanism.

A January 2024 *Journal of Cell Science* study showed that cellular carbon source changes how much SIN signaling is required. Growth on non-fermentable glycerol partially rescued loss of the positive regulator Etd1, and deletion of *pka1* partly reproduced this effect in glucose. The study therefore connected respiratory/fermentative state and cAMP–PKA signaling to the Spg1-controlled SIN. However, it did not identify Spg1 itself as a direct PKA substrate; the authors favored regulation through SIN inhibitors such as PP2A, and the molecular target remains unresolved. (ozdemir2024arolefor pages 1-2, ozdemir2024arolefor pages 5-6)

Quantitatively, Mob1–GFP was detected at contractile rings in 10% of glycerol-grown *etd1Δ* cells and 14% of glucose-grown *etd1Δ pka1Δ* cells, versus none in glucose-grown *etd1Δ* controls at 29°C. After 5 hours at 29°C, 85% of elongated *etd1Δ* cells lacked detectable Sid1 signal. Forced SPB tethering of Cdc7 or Sid1 to Cdc11 rescued *etd1Δ* under tested conditions, although persistent Cdc7 tethering caused multiseptation at temperatures of at least 29°C. These findings reinforce the view that correct SPB recruitment and limited signal duration are as important as kinase presence. (ozdemir2024arolefor pages 5-6)

A 2024 microPublication characterized temperature-sensitive *spg1* alleles, providing modern conditional reagents for dissecting this essential GTPase. The retrieved search record identifies the publication and DOI—Bowman Fletcher et al., June 2024, https://doi.org/10.17912/micropub.biology.001193—but full experimental text was unavailable in the evidence set. It should therefore be treated as a resource development, not used here to support additional mechanistic claims.

## Current applications and real-world relevance

Spg1 itself has no established clinical or industrial application. Its principal current use is as an experimentally tractable switch for studying how a centrosome/SPB-associated signaling network converts cell-cycle information into spatially restricted cytokinesis. Conditional alleles, overexpression, fluorescent tagging, forced SPB tethering, phosphomutants, and GAP mutants allow researchers to manipulate signaling onset, duration, and symmetry with unusually high precision. (ozdemir2024arolefor pages 1-2, ozdemir2024arolefor pages 5-6, rachfall2014cdk1promotescytokinesis pages 1-2)

The wider relevance is conceptual and evolutionary. The SIN is analogous to the budding-yeast mitotic exit network, and several kinase/scaffold principles have parallels in metazoan Hippo-family signaling. Fission yeast is also valuable because failed cytokinesis can be separated experimentally from continued nuclear cycling, modeling how division failure creates multinucleate or polyploid cells. These analogies do not imply that Spg1 is a human protein or validated drug target. (rachfall2014cdk1promotescytokinesis pages 1-2)

In fungal biology, Tem1/Spg1-like switches and related septation networks affect development and pathogenicity in some species, but such ortholog studies should not be interpreted as direct evidence about P87027. For P87027 specifically, the application remains fundamental cell biology and functional annotation.

## Expert assessment and annotation recommendation

The most defensible primary annotation is:

> **Spindle-pole-body-associated small GTPase that initiates and spatially organizes septation initiation network signaling. GTP-bound Spg1 recruits the Cdc7 kinase and activates the Cdc7–Sid1/Cdc14–Sid2/Mob1 cascade, promoting contractile-ring function and division-septum formation; Spg1 is inactivated by the highly specific Byr4–Cdc16 two-component GAP.**

Recommended biological-process terms include regulation of cytokinesis, septum formation, contractile-ring organization/maintenance, SIN signaling, and—secondarily—forespore-membrane organization and sporulation. Recommended cellular-component terms are spindle pole body/SIN scaffold, with indirect pathway-dependent association of downstream effectors at the medial contractile ring. The protein itself should not be assigned septum, plasma-membrane, or contractile-ring structural activity without qualification.

The principal unresolved issues are the identity and mechanism of the physiological Spg1 GEF, the exact molecular step by which Etd1 sustains late SIN signaling, how metabolic/PKA inputs reach the network, and the complete set of terminal Sid2 substrates that translate SPB signaling into ring constriction and septum synthesis. Current evidence is strongest for the GAP reaction, SPB localization, Cdc7 effector relationship, and essential cytokinetic function; it is weaker or network-level for metabolic regulation and meiotic outputs.
