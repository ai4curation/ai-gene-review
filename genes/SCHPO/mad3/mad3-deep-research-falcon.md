# Functional annotation report: *mad3* in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*

## Executive summary

The target is correctly identified as fission-yeast *mad3* (UniProt O59767; ORF aliases SPCC1795.01c/SPCC895.02), encoding mitotic spindle-checkpoint component Mad3. The direct literature describes a 933-bp chromosome III gene encoding an approximately 36-kDa protein. Its sequence and architecture are consistent with the supplied Bub1/Mad3, Mad3/Bub1_I and TPR-like domain annotations. It is a kinase-less Mad3/BubR1-family protein—not the kinase Bub1 and not an unrelated protein carrying a similar symbol. The original comparison found 31% identity to budding-yeast Mad3 and 28% identity to human BubR1. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 4-6)

Mad3 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. Its primary role is as a non-catalytic spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) effector: it assembles with Mad2 and Slp1/Cdc20 in the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC), enabling inhibition of Slp1-activated anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C). This delays anaphase and preserves sister-chromatid cohesion while kinetochores remain unattached or improperly attached to spindle microtubules. Mad3 acts both at unattached kinetochores, where checkpoint complexes are generated, and in soluble MCC/APC/C assemblies within the mitotic nucleus. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 10-12, sczaniecka2008thespindlecheckpoint pages 4-5)

## Identity verification and nomenclature

The 2002 cloning study identified fission-yeast *mad3* in chromosome III cosmid SPCC895 as a 933-bp open reading frame encoding an approximately 36-kDa checkpoint protein. This agrees with the supplied UniProt accession, organism and SPCC895 locus alias. A full-ORF deletion was viable under ordinary growth, while an endogenous C-terminal Mad3-GFP fusion was phenotypically indistinguishable from wild type, enabling functional localization experiments. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 4-6, millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 3-4)

The domain assignment is also coherent. Mad3 belongs to the Mad3/BubR1 branch of the broader Bub1/Mad3 checkpoint family but lacks the C-terminal protein-kinase domain found in Bub1. Its N-terminal region is a TPR-like helical domain, and functional sequence mapping identifies an N-terminal KEN1 motif plus a C-terminal region containing ABBA, KEN2 and D-box-mimicking elements. Thus, the UniProt/InterPro annotations Bub1/Mad3 (IPR015661), Mad3/Bub1_I (IPR013212), TPR-like helical domain (IPR011990) and PF08311 are consistent with the experimental literature. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 10-12, sewart2017differentfunctionalityof pages 3-3)

No evidence was used from an unrelated “mad3” gene. Results from budding yeast, animals or other fungi are treated only as comparative context, not as direct annotation evidence for O59767.

## Primary molecular function

### MCC assembly and inhibition of APC/C

Mad3’s central biochemical function is to help convert the kinetochore-generated SAC signal into inhibition of APC/C–Slp1/Cdc20. Co-immunoprecipitation from *S. pombe* extracts demonstrated association of Mad3 with Mad2, Slp1/Cdc20 and Bub3. Functionally, Mad2 overexpression caused a short-spindle metaphase arrest in approximately 70% of wild-type, *mad1Δ*, *bub1Δ* and *bub3Δ* cells, but only 8% of *mad3Δ* cells, despite comparable Mad2 induction. Mad3 therefore acts with or downstream of Mad2 at the checkpoint-effector stage rather than merely controlling Mad2 abundance. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 6-8, millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 1-2)

Biochemical work subsequently placed Mad3–Mad2–Slp1 in an APC/C-associated MCC. Mad3, Mad2 and Slp1 co-fractionated with Lid1-tagged APC/C in the approximately 19S range. Quantitative depletion estimated that up to 8% of APC/C could be co-depleted with Mad2 and that at least 5% of core APC/C was Mad2-associated under the assay conditions. Stable checkpoint-protein association with mitotic APC/C required functional Slp1/Cdc20. (sczaniecka2008thespindlecheckpoint pages 4-5)

### Functional motifs and substrate mimicry

The N-terminal KEN1 motif is crucial for core MCC formation. Substitution of the KEN20 sequence with alanines eliminated detectable APC/C association of both Mad3 and Mad2. Mad3 association with APC/C was also Mad2-dependent, demonstrating reciprocal cooperation within the inhibitory complex. (sczaniecka2008thespindlecheckpoint pages 4-5, sczaniecka2008thespindlecheckpoint pages 1-1)

The C-terminal region provides a second layer of Cdc20 control. Endogenous motif mutagenesis showed that KEN2 and neighboring ABBA and D-box-mimicking elements are dispensable for formation of the core Mad3–Mad2–Slp1 MCC but are required for full checkpoint activity. Deleting residues 282–310 largely abolished checkpoint function; ABBA1 mutation produced a strong defect, ABBA2 mutation a weaker defect and mutation of the D-box mimic a partial defect. These observations support a model in which the Mad3 C terminus engages an additional Slp1/Cdc20 molecule and occupies substrate-recognition surfaces, allowing Mad3 to operate as a pseudosubstrate inhibitor of APC/C–Cdc20. (sewart2017differentfunctionalityof pages 3-3, sewart2017differentfunctionalityof pages 1-3)

The detailed two-Cdc20 arrangement is supported by *S. pombe* genetics and biochemistry integrated with structural information from other systems. It should therefore be considered a strong mechanistic model rather than a complete atomic structure determined for full-length O59767 itself. (sewart2017elucidatingthemolecular pages 105-110)

## Recruitment pathway and interaction partners

The best-supported pathway is:

**unattached kinetochore → Mph1/Mps1 activity and Bub1–Bub3 scaffold → Mad1–Mad2 and Mad3 recruitment → MCC production → Slp1/Cdc20 inhibition → APC/C restraint → delayed securin/cyclin destruction and anaphase onset.**

Bub1 has a direct structural role in Mad3 recruitment. Ectopic tethering showed that Mad3 colocalized with full-length Bub1 and a Bub1 fragment containing its N-terminal TPR domain, but not with Bub1 lacking that domain. With purified proteins, Bub1-TPR and Mad3-TPR eluted separately by size-exclusion chromatography at 15.2 and 14.3 mL, respectively, whereas their mixture eluted as a larger complex at 12.1 mL. The Bub1 TPR domain is therefore necessary and sufficient for recruitment and can bind Mad3 directly. (leontiou2019thebub1tprdomain pages 1-3, leontiou2019thebub1tprdomain pages 7-8)

This supports an expert scaffold model in which Bub1 simultaneously organizes checkpoint inputs: its CD1 region recruits Mad1–Mad2, whereas its TPR region recruits Mad3, increasing the local concentration of MCC components. The suggestion that this proximity directly promotes Mad3 phosphorylation or MCC incorporation remains plausible but was not itself demonstrated by the binding experiment. (leontiou2019thebub1tprdomain pages 1-3, leontiou2019thebub1tprdomain pages 7-8)

## Cellular localization

Functional Mad3-GFP appears as punctate nuclear foci at kinetochores early in mitosis, colocalizes with the centromeric histone Cnp1/CENP-A at unattached kinetochores and disappears during anaphase. During prolonged checkpoint activation, kinetochore accumulation becomes more prominent. Stable recruitment requires Bub1, Bub3 and the Mph1 kinase; in their absence, the bright focus is lost and Mad3 fluorescence becomes diffuse. Mad1 and Mad2 are not strictly required for kinetochore targeting. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 10-12, millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 1-2)

After hydroxyurea release, approximately 50% of wild-type cells displayed a bright Mad3-GFP focus at 165 minutes, compared with approximately 20% of *mad1Δ* or *mad2Δ* cells. The reduced frequency in these mutants likely reflects altered mitotic kinetics rather than an absolute recruitment requirement. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 10-12)

Accordingly, the biologically relevant locations are:

1. **Unattached kinetochores in the mitotic nucleus**, where Bub1/Bub3/Mph1-dependent checkpoint signaling recruits Mad3.
2. **Soluble nuclear MCC**, containing Mad3, Mad2 and Slp1/Cdc20.
3. **APC/C-associated inhibitory complexes**, where Mad3 motifs restrain Cdc20-dependent ubiquitin-ligase activity.

There is no evidence that Mad3 is secreted, membrane-integrated or associated with an extracellular compartment.

## Biological consequences and mutant phenotypes

*mad3* is dispensable for vegetative viability under favorable conditions but becomes critical when spindle assembly or kinetochore attachment is defective. A complete deletion produced benomyl hypersensitivity, elevated chromosome loss, failure to sustain mitotic arrest, premature sister-chromatid separation, DNA rereplication in a *cut7-24* spindle-defective background and lethal “cut” phenotypes when cytokinesis occurred without proper chromosome segregation. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 6-8, millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 4-6, millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 3-4)

The quantitative effects are substantial:

- *mad3Δ* cells failed to form colonies at 7.5 μg/mL benomyl. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 3-4)
- In a chromosome-I LacO/LacI-GFP cohesion assay under microtubule perturbation, *mad3Δ* sister chromatids began separating approximately 45 minutes after G2 release, whereas checkpoint-proficient controls maintained arrest and cohesion. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 6-8)
- After 6 hours at 16°C, *mad3Δ nda3-KM311* viability declined to approximately 45%, compared with greater than 95% for either single mutant. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 4-6)
- Only 8% of *mad3Δ* cells underwent the short-spindle arrest caused by Mad2 overexpression, versus approximately 70% in checkpoint-competent comparison cultures. (millband2002fissionyeastmad3p pages 6-8)

These phenotypes define Mad3’s biological role narrowly and precisely: it is not a constitutive spindle structural component but a surveillance and inhibitory adaptor required to preserve chromosome-segregation fidelity when attachment is incomplete.

## Recent developments, 2023–2024

The literature search found no major 2023 study that redefined the primary molecular function of this exact *S. pombe* protein. The most relevant 2024 advance connects the canonical Mad3 checkpoint machinery to environmental-stress regulation of mitotic exit.

Iglesias-Romero and colleagues reported in March 2024 that the Pmk1 MAPK pathway helps sustain SAC signaling and promotes rapid degradation of mitotic Slp1/Cdc20 under stress through a mechanism requiring MAPK activity, the proteasome and Mad3. *pmk1Δ* cells showed as much as a twofold elevation of Slp1/Cdc20 during unperturbed growth, were hypersensitive to microtubule damage and were defective in maintaining metaphase arrest. Additivity between *pmk1Δ* and loss of Mad3 or Mad2 suggests overlapping but not simply linear contributions. The extracted source did not provide a Mad3-specific degradation percentage or complete kinetic series, so Mad3’s exact quantitative contribution remains unresolved. Published 18 March 2024: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-024-01865-6. (iglesiasromero2024mapkdependentcontrolof pages 1-2)

A separate September 2024 study independently showed that Pmk1/Sty1 signaling and phosphorylation-dependent attenuation of Slp1 restrain APC/C during osmotic or cell-wall stress. Experiments used 0.6 M KCl or 2 μg/mL caspofungin and monitored recovery for up to 50 minutes after release from a six-hour *nda3-KM311* arrest. Fewer than 60% of *pmk1Δ* or *sty1-T97A* cells retained Cdc13-GFP-positive spindle-pole bodies, versus approximately 90% of wild type and *spk1Δ*. However, that study did **not** test a *mad3* deletion in the relevant experiments; Mad3-GFP was used in MCC/APC/C association assays. It therefore strengthens the MAPK–Slp1 pathway but does not independently prove Mad3 dependence. Published September 2024: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.97896.2. (sun2024negativeregulationof pages 3-5, sun2024negativeregulationof pages 15-16)

Thus, the current view is that Mad3 retains its canonical MCC role while also contributing to integration of spindle status with environmental stress. The precise molecular connection between Mad3 and stress-induced Slp1 turnover remains less well resolved than its canonical checkpoint mechanism.

## Functional annotation conclusion

**Recommended primary annotation:** Mad3 is a non-enzymatic, kinase-less spindle assembly checkpoint adaptor and MCC component. At unattached kinetochores, Mph1- and Bub1/Bub3-dependent recruitment positions Mad3 near Mad1–Mad2. Mad3 then binds Mad2 and Slp1/Cdc20, using KEN, ABBA and D-box-like motifs to assemble inhibitory MCC species and block APC/C–Slp1 recognition or processing of anaphase-promoting substrates. The resulting delay in anaphase maintains sister-chromatid cohesion and protects chromosome-segregation fidelity.

**Localization annotation:** Mitotic nucleus; transiently enriched at unattached kinetochores during prometaphase/metaphase; subsequently present in soluble MCC and APC/C-associated checkpoint complexes; kinetochore signal disappears during anaphase.

**Pathway annotation:** Spindle assembly checkpoint/mitotic checkpoint → MCC formation → negative regulation of APC/C–Slp1/Cdc20 → control of metaphase-to-anaphase transition and chromosome segregation. A newer, incompletely resolved branch links Mad3-dependent Slp1 regulation to Pmk1-mediated environmental-stress responses.

**Evidence confidence:** High for protein identity, non-catalytic checkpoint function, Mad2/Slp1/APC/C association, Bub1-TPR binding, kinetochore localization and spindle-stress phenotypes. Moderate-to-high for the detailed second-Cdc20 pseudosubstrate model and Mad3’s 2024 stress-regulatory extension, because parts of those interpretations rely on integrated structural models or incompletely quantified Mad3-specific experiments.
