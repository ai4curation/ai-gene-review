| Identity and architecture | *S. pombe mad3* is a 933-bp chromosome III gene encoding an ~36-kDa, kinase-less Mad3/BubR1-family spindle-checkpoint protein. Its N-terminal Bub1/Mad3 region has a TPR-like fold; conserved sequence features include KEN1, KEN2, ABBA motifs and a D-box-mimicking segment. This matches UniProt O59767 and the supplied SPCC895 locus designation. | Gene cloning and sequencing; comparative sequence/domain analysis; endogenous motif mapping. | 933-bp ORF; ~36-kDa product; 31% identity to budding-yeast Mad3 and 28% identity to human BubR1. | Millband & Hardwick (2002), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.22.8.2728-2742.2002); Sewart & Hauf (2017), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2017.03.007) (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006) | **High for identity and kinase-less family assignment.** Exact modern InterPro/Pfam boundaries derive partly from database/comparative annotation rather than an atomic structure of full-length *S. pombe* Mad3. |
| Primary molecular function | Mad3 is not an enzyme. It is a non-catalytic mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC) component/adaptor and pseudosubstrate inhibitor that cooperates with Mad2 to restrain Slp1/Cdc20-activated APC/C, delaying anaphase until correct kinetochore–microtubule attachment. | Genetic epistasis, Mad2-overexpression arrest, co-immunoprecipitation, APC/C fractionation and motif mutagenesis. | Mad2 overexpression yielded short-spindle arrest in ~70% of wild-type and several upstream-mutant cells, but only 8% of *mad3Δ* cells. | Millband & Hardwick (2002), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.22.8.2728-2742.2002); Sczaniecka et al. (2008), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M803594200) (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000007) | **High.** “Pseudosubstrate” is a mechanistic interpretation supported by motif/function studies; Mad3 has no demonstrated catalytic activity. |
| Slp1/Cdc20 and APC/C association | Mad3 binds Mad2 and Slp1/Cdc20 in mitotic extracts and participates in an MCC–APC/C assembly. Stable Mad3/Mad2 association with APC/C requires functional Slp1 and Mad3 KEN1. | Endogenous tagged-protein co-IP; Mad2 depletion; Lid1-TAP APC/C pull-down; 19S sucrose-gradient co-fractionation; conditional *slp1-362*. | Up to 8% of APC/C was co-depleted with Mad2; at least 5% of core APC/C was estimated to be Mad2-associated. | Sczaniecka et al. (2008), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M803594200); Millband & Hardwick (2002), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.22.8.2728-2742.2002) (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000007) | **High for association and functional dependency.** Co-IP establishes complex membership, not necessarily every pairwise contact or binding affinity. |
| Bub1-TPR interaction | Bub1’s N-terminal TPR domain is necessary and sufficient to recruit Mad3 and forms a stable direct complex with the Mad3 TPR region. This supports a scaffold model in which Bub1 concentrates Mad3 near Mad1–Mad2 for efficient MCC production. | Ectopic tethering and fluorescence colocalization; domain deletion; purified-protein size-exclusion chromatography. | Bub1-TPR and Mad3-TPR eluted separately at 15.2 and 14.3 mL, but together as a larger complex at 12.1 mL. | Leontiou et al. (2019), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.06.011) (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) | **High for direct binding and recruitment.** Promotion of Mad3 phosphorylation or MCC incorporation by this proximity remains a proposed mechanism rather than directly demonstrated. |
| Kinetochore localization | Functional Mad3-GFP forms punctate foci at unattached kinetochores during early mitosis, colocalizes with Cnp1/CENP-A, and disappears during anaphase. Robust recruitment requires Bub1, Bub3 and Mph1, but not Mad1 or Mad2. | Endogenous functional Mad3-GFP; live/fixed-cell fluorescence; Cnp1 colocalization; deletion backgrounds; hydroxyurea-release experiments. | At 165 min after HU release, bright Mad3-GFP foci occurred in ~50% of wild-type versus ~20% of *mad1Δ* or *mad2Δ* cells; foci were absent/diffuse in *bub1Δ*, *bub3Δ* and *mph1Δ*. | Millband & Hardwick (2002), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.22.8.2728-2742.2002) (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000014) | **High.** Reduced signal in *mad1Δ/mad2Δ* likely reflects altered mitotic kinetics; these proteins were not required for the core recruitment mechanism. |
| Deletion phenotypes | *mad3* is nonessential during unperturbed growth but is required for checkpoint arrest, sister-chromatid cohesion maintenance and survival when spindle assembly is compromised. Deletion causes microtubule-poison sensitivity, chromosome loss, premature chromatid separation, rereplication and “cut” phenotypes. | Full-ORF replacement; benomyl/CBZ treatment; *nda3-KM311* and *cut7-24* backgrounds; LacO/LacI-GFP chromatid assay; viability and chromosome-loss assays. | *mad3Δ nda3-KM311* viability fell to ~45% after 6 h at 16°C versus >95% for either single mutant; *mad3Δ* chromatids separated ~45 min after G2 release; no colonies formed at 7.5 μg/mL benomyl. | Millband & Hardwick (2002), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.22.8.2728-2742.2002) (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | **High.** These are stress/checkpoint phenotypes rather than evidence that Mad3 is essential for ordinary vegetative proliferation. |
| KEN1 motif | The N-terminal KEN1/KEN20 motif is essential for core MCC formation and stable Mad3/Mad2 association with Slp1–APC/C. | Endogenous KEN-to-AAA mutagenesis; Mad2 IP; Lid1-TAP APC/C pull-down; checkpoint assays. | KEN20AAA eliminated detectable APC/C association of both Mad3 and Mad2. | Sczaniecka et al. (2008), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M803594200) (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000011) | **High.** Strong direct *S. pombe* genetic and biochemical evidence. |
| KEN2, ABBA motifs and D-box mimic | The C-terminal ABBA–KEN2–ABBA region engages an additional Slp1/Cdc20 molecule and enables full checkpoint inhibition beyond core MCC assembly. KEN2 and ABBA1 are especially important; ABBA2 is weaker, while the D-box mimic makes a partial contribution. | Endogenous motif mutations and C-terminal truncation; *nda3-KM311* arrest; Plo1-mCherry mitotic timing; Mad3-GFP/Slp1 IP. | Deletion of residues 282–310 largely abolished checkpoint activity; ABBA1 mutation caused a strong defect, ABBA2 a weaker defect and D-box-mimic mutation a partial defect. | Sewart & Hauf (2017), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2017.03.007) (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000010) | **Moderate–high.** Functional separation is direct, but the detailed two-Cdc20/pseudosubstrate model integrates *S. pombe* experiments with structural knowledge from other systems. |
| 2024 MAPK/stress integration | One 2024 study directly reports that mitotic stress-induced Slp1/Cdc20 degradation requires Mad3, MAPK activity and the proteasome, extending Mad3’s role into environmental-stress control of mitotic exit. Pmk1 loss is additive with *mad3Δ*, suggesting partially distinct contributions to SAC maintenance. | Stress treatments, genetics/epistasis, Slp1 abundance and degradation assays, mitotic-arrest measurements. | *pmk1Δ* cells showed up to a twofold increase in Slp1/Cdc20 during unperturbed growth; the available excerpt gives no Mad3-specific degradation percentage or time course. | Iglesias-Romero et al. (2024; published March 2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-024-01865-6) (pqac-00000015) | **Moderate–high for the reported Mad3 requirement**, but exact *mad3Δ* kinetics were unavailable in the extracted text; additivity cautions against placing Pmk1 and Mad3 in one simple linear pathway. |
| Independent 2024 MAPK study | A separate 2024 study shows that Pmk1/Sty1 signaling and Slp1 phosphorylation restrain APC/C and sustain SAC signaling under KCl or caspofungin stress, but it does **not** test *mad3* deletion; Mad3-GFP is used in APC/C–MCC association assays only. It therefore cannot independently establish Mad3 dependence. | *nda3-KM311* arrest/release; 0.6 M KCl or 2 μg/mL caspofungin for 1 h; MAPK and Slp1-site mutants; Lid1-TAP/Mad2-GFP/Mad3-GFP co-IP. | After a 6-h arrest and release, <60% of *pmk1Δ* or *sty1-T97A* cells retained Cdc13-GFP-positive spindle-pole bodies versus ~90% of wild type and *spk1Δ*. | Sun et al. (2024; published September 2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.97896.2) (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017) | **High for MAPK–Slp1 regulation; no direct Mad3-dependency evidence.** Any Mad3-specific interpretation from this paper alone would be inference from established MCC biology. |
