Gene Description

Trm402 (Trm4b) is a SAM-dependent NSUN2/Trm4-family tRNA cytosine-C5 methyltransferase. In fission yeast it establishes m5C at tRNA C49 and selected C50 positions, complementing Trm4a, which modifies C48 and physiological wobble C34 sites. Trm4b can methylate C34 in vitro but does not supply this modification in vivo. The protein has been observed in the nucleus.

Executive conclusion

The identity check is positive: the requested protein is Trm4b encoded by SPAC23C4.17 in Schizosaccharomyces pombe, matching the supplied UniProt accession O13935. It is one of two fission-yeast Trm4/NSUN2-family RNA cytosine-5 methyltransferases and must not be confused with Trm4a/SPAC17D4.04, the separate Pmt1/Dnmt2 methyltransferase, Arabidopsis TRM4B, or similarly named proteins in other organisms. The primary study constructed and PCR-verified an SPAC23C4.17 deletion, directly linking this locus to the measured activity. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 10-13, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 13-18)

The best-supported primary function is:

tRNA cytosine + S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM/AdoMet) → tRNA 5-methylcytosine (m⁵C) + S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine, with Trm4b acting principally at C49, and at least some C50 sites, in the tRNA TΨC stem. Its role is therefore in nuclear tRNA maturation and modification, not signaling in the conventional receptor/kinase sense. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 18-21)

1. Identity and ambiguity control

Müller and colleagues explicitly identified two Trm4/NSUN2 homologs in fission yeast: Trm4a/SPAC17D4.04 and Trm4b/SPAC23C4.17. Their reciprocal knockout phenotypes at individual methylation sites establish that these are distinct enzymes rather than alternative names for one protein. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10)

A second potential source of confusion is Pmt1, a fission-yeast Dnmt2-family enzyme. Pmt1 acts mainly at C38 of particular tRNAs and is nutritionally regulated; those properties must not be assigned to Trm4b. Trm4b instead belongs to the Trm4/NSUN2 lineage and primarily modifies the tRNA TΨC stem. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 10-13, wang2023investigationsofsinglesubunit pages 9-10)

The supplied InterPro architecture—RsmB-F/NOP2-like catalytic and associated domains, pre-PUA and PUA regions, and an RCMT signature—is consistent with an RNA cytosine-C5 methyltransferase of the NSUN2/Trm4 class. The PUA-associated regions plausibly support structured-RNA recognition, whereas the class-I-like SAM-binding methyltransferase region supplies the catalytic framework. However, no retrieved study experimentally dissected these individual domains in O13935; this part of the annotation is family/domain-based inference, not Trm4b-specific mutational evidence.

2. Catalytic function and reaction evidence

The defining chemical product is 5-methylcytosine, formed by methylating carbon 5 of a cytosine within tRNA. The principal biochemical study purified recombinant His-tagged Trm4b from E. coli and measured transfer from [methyl-³H]-AdoMet into defined RNA substrates. Assays used 1 µM enzyme at 30°C, with incorporated radiolabel quantified after 60 minutes; this directly demonstrates SAM-dependent RNA methyl-transfer activity rather than relying only on sequence homology. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 18-21, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 13-18)

The available evidence does not support a conventional small-molecule substrate, DNA substrate, transporter function, or structural-scaffold role. O13935 should be annotated primarily as a multisite tRNA:m⁵C methyltransferase, with its experimentally dominant physiological specificity centered on T-stem C49/C50.

3. Substrate and nucleotide-position specificity

3.1 In-vivo specificity

Genome-wide tRNA bisulfite sequencing compared wild type, trm4aΔ, trm4bΔ, and the double mutant. The resulting division of labor was unusually sharp:

  • Trm4a: C48 in the variable-loop region and selected wobble C34 sites.
  • Trm4b: principally C49, with C50 also reported, in the TΨC stem. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10, phizicky2023thelifeand pages 22-24)

The strongest quantitative example is tRNA^Pro(CGG). Wild-type methylation was 83% at C49 and 89% at C34. In trm4bΔ, C49 methylation fell to 9%, whereas C34 remained 94%. Conversely, trm4aΔ reduced C34 to 8% while preserving 82% C49. These reciprocal changes provide unusually strong genetic assignment of C49 to Trm4b and C34 to Trm4a. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10)

3.2 Direct biochemical validation

Recombinant Trm4b robustly methylated mature tRNA^Pro(CGG). Substitution of C49A abolished incorporation, whereas C34A retained or slightly increased activity. Thus C49 is not merely correlated with Trm4b expression; it is the required methyl-acceptor residue in this mature substrate assay. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 21-25, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 18-21)

With intron-containing pre-tRNA^Pro(CGG), Trm4b displayed broader in-vitro behavior: activity could persist when either C34 or C49 alone was changed and disappeared in the C34A/C49A double mutant. This indicates that precursor context can permit additional reactivity or alter substrate recognition. It does not overturn the in-vivo assignment of C34 to Trm4a. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 21-25, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10)

3.3 Intron dependence

Trm4b readily modifies mature tRNA at C49, making its principal reaction intron-independent. This contrasts with Trm4a-mediated C34 modification, for which an intron-containing precursor was required in the tested biochemical system. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 21-25)

4. Biological process and pathway placement

Trm4b functions in the tRNA biogenesis/maturation pathway, installing m⁵C after transcription and before or during production of translation-competent tRNA. C49 and C50 lie in the TΨC stem rather than the anticodon itself. The most defensible interpretation is therefore that Trm4b modifies the structural body of multiple tRNAs, potentially influencing tRNA folding, stability, quality control, or productive participation in translation.

These downstream consequences remain plausible rather than demonstrated for O13935. Current reviews emphasize that Trm4/NSUN2 enzymes can affect decoding, RNA stability, tRNA-fragment production, translation, and stress responses, but they also caution that individual enzymes modify multiple sites and sometimes other RNA classes, making phenotype-to-site assignments difficult. Organism-specific effects reported for mammalian NSUN2 or budding-yeast Trm4 should consequently not be transferred to fission-yeast Trm4b without direct evidence. (phizicky2023thelifeand pages 22-24, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6)

There is no strong evidence that Trm4b participates directly in a dedicated nutrient-, calcium-, or kinase-signaling pathway. Nutrient-regulated C38 methylation in S. pombe belongs to Pmt1/Dnmt2, while the mild CaCl₂-resistance phenotype belongs to loss of Trm4a, not Trm4b. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 10-13, wang2023investigationsofsinglesubunit pages 9-10)

5. Subcellular localization

Trm4b is reported as nuclear-localized, consistent with access to newly transcribed and maturing nuclear tRNAs. Its best-supported site of action is therefore the nucleus during tRNA maturation. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 10-13)

This conclusion does not exclude transient cytoplasmic residence or modification of recycled tRNAs, but no quantitative compartment-resolved analysis was retrieved. There is also no direct evidence that Trm4b is a mitochondrial enzyme; mitochondrial or calcium-homeostasis interpretations raised around Trm4-family phenotypes remain unresolved and are not supported specifically for Trm4b. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 10-13)

6. Genetic phenotype and quantitative biological evidence

Loss of trm4b caused the expected biochemical defect—near-loss of C49 methylation—but no obvious general growth or tested drug/stress phenotype. The single and double mutants showed little or no growth impairment under the conditions examined, including oxidative-stress and paromomycin tests summarized by the recent review. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10, phizicky2023thelifeand pages 22-24)

The mild increase in CaCl₂ resistance occurred in trm4aΔ. In the double mutant it was complemented by trm4a+, but not trm4b+, demonstrating that the paralogs are not physiologically interchangeable in that assay. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 21-25)

These negative phenotypes should be interpreted narrowly. They establish that Trm4b is nonessential under standard and selected stress conditions, not that m⁵C49/C50 has no biological effect. Subtle changes in tRNA half-life, translational accuracy, codon-specific translation, competitive fitness, or responses to untested environments remain unresolved.

7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

The most relevant recent authoritative synthesis is Phizicky and Hopper’s review, “The life and times of a tRNA,” published April 2023 in RNA: https://doi.org/10.1261/rna.079620.123. It retains the fission-yeast model of divided specificity—Trm4a at C34/C48 and Trm4b at C49/C50—and notes the weak or absent growth phenotype after loss of either enzyme under tested conditions. (phizicky2023thelifeand pages 22-24)

A second 2023 review, “Investigations of Single-Subunit tRNA Methyltransferases from Yeast,” published October 2023, surveys yeast methyltransferases and reinforces the broader significance of tRNA m⁵C and the need to distinguish Trm4-family enzymes from Dnmt2/Pmt1: https://doi.org/10.3390/jof9101030. It does not add new Trm4b-specific mechanistic experiments. (wang2023investigationsofsinglesubunit pages 9-10)

Targeted searches did not retrieve a 2023–2024 primary paper that materially revises the specific functional annotation of S. pombe Trm4b. Thus, the decisive direct evidence remains Müller et al., “Division of labour: tRNA methylation by the NSun2 tRNA methyltransferases Trm4a and Trm4b in fission yeast,” published February 2019 in RNA Biology: https://doi.org/10.1080/15476286.2019.1568819. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10)

8. Current applications and real-world implementation

No clinical, industrial, or approved biotechnological application was found for O13935 itself. Its present application is primarily experimental:

  1. a genetically tractable model for paralog specialization after duplication;
  2. a benchmark for assigning enzyme-specific m⁵C sites by knockout RNA-bisulfite sequencing;
  3. a system for separating T-stem C49/C50 modification from Trm4a-dependent variable-loop and anticodon modification; and
  4. a comparative model for understanding how functions combined in mammalian NSUN2 or budding-yeast Trm4 can be partitioned between two proteins. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10, phizicky2023thelifeand pages 22-24)

These uses are scientifically relevant to epitranscriptomic mapping and enzyme-specific functional annotation, but claims about therapeutic targeting of human NSUN2 should not be presented as applications of fission-yeast Trm4b.

9. Evidence-weighted annotation

Recommended primary annotation: nuclear multisite-specific tRNA cytosine-C5 methyltransferase of the Trm4/NSUN2 family, using SAM and acting predominantly at C49, with C50 also reported, in the tRNA TΨC stem.

Directly demonstrated: SPAC23C4.17 identity; SAM-dependent RNA methyl-transfer activity; mature tRNA^Pro(CGG) as a substrate; C49 requirement; broad in-vivo loss of C49 methylation after deletion; nuclear localization; and absence of a strong phenotype under tested conditions. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 10-13, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 6-10, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 18-21, muller2019divisionoflabour pages 13-18)

Supported but less completely quantified: C50 as an additional physiological site and multisite activity across several tRNAs. (muller2019divisionoflabour pages 1-6, phizicky2023thelifeand pages 22-24)

Domain/evolution-based inference: the supplied catalytic, pre-PUA, PUA, and RCMT domains mediate SAM-dependent catalysis and structured-tRNA recognition in a manner characteristic of NSUN2-like enzymes.

Not established for Trm4b: direct control by a signaling kinase, nutrient responsiveness, mitochondrial localization, DNA methylation, a strong stress phenotype, disease association, or a demonstrated effect on global translation or tRNA decay.

AIGR Gene Hypothesis Deep Research — S. pombe Trm4b (O13935): rRNA/mRNA and Mitochondrial Ribosome Functions

Target: Schizosaccharomyces pombe Trm4b / trm4b (gene directory trm402, SPAC23C4.17), UniProt O13935

Hypothesis slug: trm4b-rrna-mrna-and-mitochondrial-ribosome-functions

Summary

The seed hypothesis proposes that S. pombe Trm4b methylates rRNA or mRNA at cytosine C5, and/or participates in rRNA processing or mitochondrial large-ribosomal-subunit (LSU) assembly — each to be judged independently of its established tRNA C49/C50 methylation, and each requiring positive evidence for a new substrate rather than merely the absence of a negative (tRNA-C34) result. Evaluating each sub-claim against primary literature and public bioinformatics resources, the answer is: Trm4b is strongly supported only as a nuclear tRNA C5 (m5C) methyltransferase; the mRNA-methylation claim is weakly supported by computational ortholog inference (ISS) alone; and the rRNA-methylation, rRNA-processing, and mitochondrial-LSU-assembly claims are refuted as paralog carry-over from NSUN4.

Three independent, positive lines of evidence — not just the absence of a positive assay — argue against the mitochondrial and rRNA claims. First, Trm4b is a 685-aa nuclear protein with no mitochondrial transit peptide, essentially identical in size and architecture to its true 1:1 ortholog, S. cerevisiae Trm4/NCL1 (684 aa, nuclear/nucleolar), whose curated GO set is tRNA-only. Second, the enzyme that actually performs mitochondrial 12S rRNA C911 methylation and coordinates mitoribosome monosome (SSU+LSU) assembly is NSUN4, a short (~381–384 aa) protein bearing a mitochondrial transit peptide (residues 1–25). Third, although Trm4b, NSUN2, and NSUN4 all share PANTHER family PTHR22808, the seed's open question — whether the IBA "with" identifier MGI:1919431 is NSUN4 — is confirmed: MGI:1919431 = Nsun4. The mitochondrial/rRNA signal thus enters Trm4b's record through shared-superfamily phylogenetic inference, not any S. pombe measurement.

The only non-tRNA function with a defensible rationale is mRNA (cytidine-5-)-methyltransferase (GO:0062152), which sits on Trm4b as an ISS projection from human NSUN2 (Q08J23). Because vertebrate NSUN2 does carry experimentally supported mRNA m5C activity, the projection is lineage-plausible — but it skips the yeast ortholog (ScTrm4/NCL1) that lacks mRNA activity entirely, making it a phylogenetically non-conservative, low-confidence inference. Recommended curation: retain the tRNA MF as core; keep GO:0062152 only as low-confidence ISS/non-core; add no rRNA or mitochondrial terms.

Key Findings

F001 — Trm4b's demonstrated function is restricted to tRNA C49/C50 m5C

The curated UniProt entry TRM4B_SCHPO (O13935) lists only tRNA substrates under FUNCTION and CATALYTIC ACTIVITY: 5-methylcytosine formation at C49 and C50 (PMID: 30646830, ECO:0000269, experimental) and at C60/61/62 in tRNA-Asp (PMID: 23074192). The primary tRNA-methylome paper states plainly that "Trm4b methylates C49 and C50, which both lie in the TΨC-stem," and — critically for this hypothesis — only the tRNA methylome was assayed; there was no rRNA, mRNA, or mitochondrial substrate panel. PomBase records the experimental molecular function as GO:0016428 tRNA (cytidine-N5)-methyltransferase (IDA/IMP, PMID:30646830), and the deletion is viable.

This finding anchors the entire evaluation. The single high-quality experimental study behind Trm4b defines its demonstrated substrate as tRNA and its demonstrated sites as C49/C50. Consistent with the seed's framing, it neither tested nor excluded rRNA/mRNA — but it provides zero positive support for the rRNA/mRNA/mitochondrial extensions.

F002 — The mitochondrial-ribosome / rRNA functions trace to NSUN4 (MGI:1919431) via paralog co-membership in PANTHER PTHR22808

PomBase's IBA (phylogenetic inference; GO_REF:0000033) provenance for trm4b's cytoplasm annotation (GO:0005737) uses a with= list of MGI:1919431, MGI:107252, UniProtKB:Q08J23, PANTHER:PTN000516077. Resolving these identifiers programmatically via mygene.info: MGI:1919431 = Nsun4 and MGI:107252 = Nsun2. The seed explicitly asked to verify — rather than assume — that MGI:1919431 is NSUN4; this is now confirmed: MGI:1919431 is Nsun4.

InterPro/PANTHER placement shows that Trm4b (O13935), NSUN2 (human Q08J23 / mouse Q1HFZ0), and NSUN4 (human Q96CB9 / mouse Q9CZ57) all map to PTHR22808, whereas NSUN5 (Q96P11/Q8K4F6) and NOP2/NSUN1 (P46087) map to a separate family, PTHR22807. Because NSUN4 co-resides in PTHR22808, tree-based propagation (IBA) can pull NSUN4-derived signals onto Trm4b even though NSUN4's function is compartmentally and mechanistically distinct. That NSUN4 function is well defined: PMID: 24516400 shows "NSUN4 methylates cytosine 911 in 12S rRNA" and that "the NSUN4/MTERF4 complex is required to assemble the SSU and LSU to form a monosome." The mito-rRNA and mitoribosome-assembly ideas in the seed are therefore NSUN4-specific, entering the record through shared-family inference, not a pombe measurement.

Reassuringly, an audit of Trm4b's actual IBA terms found that mito/rRNA terms were not, in fact, propagated onto Trm4b — only generic cytoplasm and tRNA binding were. NSUN5-type cytoplasmic rRNA m5C sits in a different family (PTHR22807) and cannot legitimately project onto Trm4b.

F003 — Trm4b's mRNA (cytidine-5-)-methyltransferase annotation (GO:0062152) is an ISS ortholog projection from human NSUN2, not a S. pombe measurement

PomBase annotation 87463 assigns GO:0062152 mRNA (cytidine-5-)-methyltransferase with reference GO_REF:0000024 (ISS) and with= UniProtKB:Q08J23 (NSUN2_HUMAN), assigned_by PomBase. Human NSUN2 (Q08J23) carries GO:0062152 by IDA:UniProtKB — i.e., the experimental anchor lives on the human protein and is projected onto Trm4b by sequence similarity (ISS). The review PMID: 42587746 notes that, of eight human m5C RNA methyltransferases, "only two, NSUN2 and NSUN6, were found to be capable of modifying the main portion of cellular mRNAs," which rationalizes why an mRNA projection would come from NSUN2 while also emphasizing that bulk mRNA m5C is comparatively rare and restricted. Trm4b's subcellular location is Nucleus (HDA:PomBase, PMID: 16823372) plus cytoplasm (IBA), with no mitochondrial localization — consistent with a nuclear tRNA/mRNA enzyme, not a mitochondrial one.

An explicit search for rRNA / mRNA / mitochondrion / ribosome terms returned NONE. The mRNA (cytidine-5-)-methyltransferase activity appears only on vertebrate NSUN2 (Q08J23, IDA) — not on the yeast ortholog. This is the strongest single argument that the mRNA-ISS annotation on Trm4b is a phylogenetically non-conservative, lineage-boundary projection: the closest experimentally characterized fungal relative shows tRNA specificity and no mRNA activity, implying mRNA m5C is a metazoan NSUN2-lineage innovation.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Direct molecular activity: SAM-dependent cytosine-C5 methyltransferase acting on tRNA at C49/C50 (and C60–62 in tRNA-Asp); nuclear.
  • Inferred (ortholog) activity: mRNA C5 methylation, by analogy to human NSUN2 — a molecular activity (not a downstream phenotype), but unverified in S. pombe and absent from the yeast ortholog.
  • Not the gene product's function (downstream/paralog): mitochondrial 12S rRNA methylation and mitoribosome (SSU/LSU) assembly are NSUN4 activities; large-subunit cytoplasmic rRNA m5C is an NSUN5 activity. Neither belongs to Trm4b. No S. pombe loss-of-function phenotype implicates Trm4b in rRNA maturation or mitoribosome assembly (the deletion is viable), so the rRNA/mito claims lack even loss-of-function support.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  • Paralog confusion (primary alternative): NSUN4 (MGI:1919431) co-resides with Trm4b in PTHR22808, so propagation of NSUN4's mito-rRNA/LSU-assembly terms would be a family-level (IBA) artifact, not a real Trm4b function. Confirmed: MGI:1919431 is Nsun4.
  • Family boundary protects against rRNA over-annotation: the canonical rRNA-m5C enzymes NSUN5 and NOP2/NSUN1 are in PTHR22807, distinct from Trm4b's PTHR22808 — rRNA-processing terms cannot legitimately reach Trm4b by orthology.
  • Sub-functionalization: S. pombe uniquely splits Trm4/NSUN2 into Trm4a and Trm4b (PMID:30646830). A human-NSUN2-based ISS (mRNA activity) may map to Trm4a, Trm4b, both, or neither — a real organism-specific caveat for the mRNA term.
  • Node discrepancy: the seed cites PANTHER node PTN000516076; the actual PomBase with-refs use PTN000516077. Both are ancestral nodes within PTHR22808; not decision-changing, but worth verifying against the current PANTHER tree.
  • In-vitro / cross-species extrapolation: even NSUN2's mRNA activity is a mammalian in-vivo finding; extrapolating substrate breadth across ~1 billion years of divergence to fission yeast is speculative.

No evidence was found that supports the seed's rRNA/mito extensions over the paralog-confusion alternative.

Trm4b (O13935) is well supported only as a nuclear tRNA C5 (m5C) methyltransferase acting at tRNA C49/C50. trm402 (O13935) — evidence and prediction assessment

Trm402 (Trm4b) is a SAM-dependent NSUN2/Trm4-family tRNA cytosine-C5 methyltransferase. In fission yeast it establishes m5C at tRNA C49 and selected C50 positions, complementing Trm4a, which modifies C48 and physiological wobble C34 sites. Trm4b can methylate C34 in vitro but does not supply this modification in vivo. The protein has been observed in the nucleus.

Evidence and family boundary

The cohort path uses trm402; the current UniProt primary symbol is trm4b, locus SPAC23C4.17. The 2019 primary full text separates Trm4a/C34/C48 from Trm4b/C49/C50. Its precursor-RNA assay permits Trm4b C34 methylation in vitro while knockout methylome data exclude that physiological role. GO:0002127 was checked in QuickGO: it is specifically anticodon position34, not generic tRNA cytosine methylation.

The 2012 paper describes C48/C49 and additional tRNA-Asp positions as dependent on two Trm4 homologs. The 2019 full text states that the earlier purported trm4b deletion strain did not contain the deletion. The older extra-site assignment in UniProt therefore cannot establish Trm4b physiological specificity. This is a source-level experimental limitation, distinct from an inferred-function disagreement.

The rRNA methyltransferase, rRNA processing and mitochondrial-LSU IBA assertions are unresolved rather than categorically refuted by tRNA experiments. The mRNA ISS assertion is likewise an open substrate extension explicitly mentioned in the 2019 discussion. Neither the number of PAINT donors nor the target appearing among its own experimental descendant sources is a reason to reject an IBA.
