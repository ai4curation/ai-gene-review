# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe apc11* (Q9UT86)

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified. In *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*, **Apc11 is the product of ORF SPAC343.03**, an approximately **11-kDa** component of the 13-subunit anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C). This matches UniProt Q9UT86 and is not the similarly named budding-yeast gene. Its RING-H2/RING-box annotations are consistent with the conserved APC11 family. Direct *S. pombe* proteomics and structural studies establish complex membership, one-copy stoichiometry, and placement in the APC/C catalytic region; purified-protein studies in budding yeast and humans establish the detailed RING-E3 mechanism. (yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 1-2, yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 2-4, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 9-10, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 4-6)

The best-supported primary function is therefore:

> **Apc11 is the zinc-binding RING subunit of the APC/C E3 ubiquitin ligase. Together with the cullin-like Apc2 subunit, it recruits/activates ubiquitin-charged E2 enzymes and promotes transfer of ubiquitin to APC/C-bound cell-cycle substrates. It does not itself provide most physiological substrate specificity.**

Its principal pathway consequences are polyubiquitylation and proteasomal destruction of **Cut2/securin**, permitting separase-dependent sister-chromatid separation, and **Cdc13/B-type cyclin**, lowering Cdk1 activity and enabling mitotic exit. (tang2001apc2cullinprotein pages 1-2, leverson2000theapc11ringh2 pages 1-2, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2, yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity and domain verification

Yoon and colleagues reported on **10 December 2002** that genome searches identified the *S. pombe* APC11 homolog as **SPAC343.03**. In two independent APC/C purifications, the predicted approximately 11-kDa protein was represented by **3 unique tryptic peptides in Lid1-TAP material and 5 in Apc13-TAP material**. These data directly connect the exact fission-yeast ORF to the APC/C and support the supplied Q9UT86 annotation. [DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0960-9822(02)01331-3]. (yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 1-2, yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 2-4)

The supplied InterPro/Pfam assignments—RING-box E3 ubiquitin ligase, RING-H2 APC11, zinc-finger RING, and zf-ANAPC11—are mutually consistent. APC11 proteins contain a compact RING-H2 metal-binding fold with conserved cysteine and histidine ligands. In budding yeast, mutation of zinc-coordinating Apc11 residues disrupted complementation, showing that RING integrity is essential for function; the *S. pombe* sequence was included in the cross-species RING alignment. This is strong conserved-family evidence, although equivalent residue-by-residue mutagenesis has not been reported for Q9UT86 itself. [Published July 2000; DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.11.7.2315]. (leverson2000theapc11ringh2 pages 1-2, leverson2000theapc11ringh2 pages 2-3, leverson2000theapc11ringh2 pages 3-5)

## 2. Molecular function and catalyzed reaction

### Reaction

Apc11 is a **RING-type E3 ligase component**, not a HECT-type ubiquitin carrier. It promotes the direct reaction:

**E2–S~ubiquitin + substrate-Lys → E2-SH + substrate-Lys–NH–CO–ubiquitin**, followed by additional cycles that build a proteasome-targeting ubiquitin chain.

Accordingly, no covalent Apc11–ubiquitin catalytic intermediate is expected. The RING domain binds and positions an E2~ubiquitin conjugate in a transfer-competent state, while APC/C-associated recognition machinery positions the substrate lysine.

Purified budding-yeast Apc11 bound Ubc4 directly and supported E1-, E2-, and ATP-dependent ubiquitination of Clb2 in vitro. RING integrity was required. [Published July 2000; https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.11.7.2315]. (leverson2000theapc11ringh2 pages 1-2, leverson2000theapc11ringh2 pages 3-5)

Independent experiments with recombinant human APC11 showed formation of multiubiquitin chains with E1 and UBC4; about **60%** of the chains remained in solution after APC11 removal, demonstrating that many were unanchored rather than merely APC11 autoubiquitylation products. The reaction required E1, UBC4, ATP, and ubiquitin and was not supported by every tested E2. [Published 1 August 2000; https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.97.16.8973]. (gmachl2000theringh2finger pages 1-2, gmachl2000theringh2finger pages 3-4)

### Minimal catalytic module

Human reconstitution demonstrated that **APC2–APC11 is the minimal APC/C ubiquitin-ligase module**. The heterodimer supported UbcH10- or Ubc4-dependent ubiquitylation of securin and cyclin B1. APC11 interacted strongly with APC2; Ubc4 bound APC11 directly, whereas UbcH10 primarily contacted the C-terminal cullin-homology region of APC2. [Published December 2001; https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.12.12.3839]. (tang2001apc2cullinprotein pages 1-2, tang2001apc2cullinprotein pages 4-6)

This division of labor is the most defensible functional annotation for Q9UT86. The exact physical interface between *S. pombe* Apc11 and each endogenous E2 has not been purified and measured directly, but the relevant fold, catalytic-module organization, and pathway are conserved.

## 3. Substrate specificity

Apc11 should **not** be annotated as independently recognizing Cut2, Cdc13, or a particular degron. The APC2–APC11 minimal module also ubiquitylated destruction-box-deleted substrates in vitro, showing that isolated catalytic activity lacks normal substrate discrimination. Physiological specificity is generated principally by the coactivators **Slp1/Cdc20** and **Ste9/Cdh1**, together with **Apc10**, which recognize D-box, KEN-box, and related degrons and orient substrates toward the catalytic module. (tang2001apc2cullinprotein pages 1-2, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2)

In *S. pombe*, the major well-established APC/C outputs are:

- **Cut2/securin destruction:** releases separase activity and permits sister-chromatid separation at anaphase.
- **Cdc13/B-type cyclin destruction:** reduces Cdc2/Cdk1 activity and promotes mitotic exit.

Purified mitotic *S. pombe* APC/C ubiquitylated a Cdc2–Cdc13 substrate in an E1-, E2-, and ATP-dependent assay, while cellular APC/C activity generated ubiquitylated Cut2. (yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 1-2, yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 2-4)

## 4. APC/C membership, structural role, and quantitative architecture

The fission-yeast APC/C has **13 core subunits**. Quantitative double-tag co-immunoprecipitation indicated that Apc11 is present at **one copy per particle**, whereas Cut9, Cut23, and Nuc2 occur at approximately two copies. Apc11, Apc2, and Apc10 occupy the catalytic side of the assembly, spatially separated from much of the TPR scaffold. (ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 4-6, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 9-10)

The mitotic *S. pombe* APC/C–Slp1 complex was reconstructed at **27 Å** resolution and measured approximately **19 × 17 × 15 nm**. It contains a central cavity of approximately **11.5 × 9.5 × 6.5 nm**. STEM yielded a mass of **890 ± 133 kDa**, while subunit stoichiometry predicted approximately **986 kDa with Slp1** and **933 kDa without Slp1**. These are measurements of the intact assembly, not free Apc11. [Published 14 December 2007; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2007.10.003]. (ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 9-10, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 4-6, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2)

Functionally, Apc11 is therefore both catalytic and structural: it supplies the RING platform for E2-dependent transfer while remaining anchored to the complex through the Apc2-centered catalytic module. It is not the principal scaffold for the whole APC/C and is not the degron receptor.

## 5. Cellular localization

The defensible localization is **intracellular, within the APC/C mitotic cell-cycle machinery**. Structural labeling places Apc11 on the catalytic side of purified mitotic APC/C. APC/C activity acts on soluble/nuclear mitotic regulators associated with chromosome segregation and mitotic exit. (ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 9-10, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2)

However, the retrieved literature did not provide an Apc11-specific live-cell localization series establishing enrichment at nuclei, spindle-pole bodies, kinetochores, spindles, or chromosomes. Therefore, such finer localization should not be assigned to Q9UT86 without additional imaging evidence. Localization reported for Slp1, checkpoint proteins, or other APC/C subunits is not automatically equivalent to Apc11 localization.

## 6. E2 partners and ubiquitin-chain formation

In fission yeast, APC/C activity uses **Ubc4 and Ubc11** in complementary roles. Depletion of either causes mitotic arrest and cyclin accumulation. Ubc11 can generate short ubiquitin chains in the absence of Ubc4, whereas Ubc4 cannot ubiquitylate APC/C substrates without Ubc11, supporting a model in which Ubc11 participates in initial substrate modification and Ubc4 supports chain extension. (mclean2011stateofthe pages 12-14)

Chain topology should be annotated cautiously. Budding-yeast APC/C has been associated predominantly with K48-linked chains, whereas human APC/C prominently uses K11-linked chain assembly machinery. The exact linkage distribution generated by the Q9UT86-containing *S. pombe* complex was not established in the retrieved evidence. (mclean2011stateofthe pages 12-14)

## 7. Biological pathways and regulation

Apc11 functions in the **ubiquitin–proteasome system**, specifically the APC/C arm controlling metaphase-to-anaphase transition and mitotic exit. The sequence is:

1. APC/C associates with Slp1/Cdc20 during mitosis.
2. Slp1/Apc10 recruit degron-containing substrates.
3. The Apc2–Apc11 catalytic module engages ubiquitin-charged E2 enzymes.
4. Polyubiquitylated Cut2 and Cdc13 are degraded by the 26S proteasome.
5. Separase activation drives chromatid separation, while Cdk1 inactivation promotes mitotic exit.
6. The spindle-assembly checkpoint inhibits APC/C until chromosome attachment requirements are satisfied. (ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2, yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 1-2)

### 2024 fission-yeast development

Sun and colleagues reported in the **16 October 2024** version of record that the Pmk1 MAP kinase binds the APC/C coactivator Slp1/Cdc20 and phosphorylates it, including at **Thr480**. This promotes Slp1 ubiquitylation and degradation, restrains APC/C activation, and helps sustain the spindle checkpoint under environmental stress. Their evidence included recombinant binding/pull-down assays, mutagenesis, phosphoproteomics, phospho-specific antibodies, and cellular checkpoint assays with at least **300 cells per time point** and three experimental repeats. [https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.97896]. (sun2024negativeregulationof pages 8-9, sun2024negativeregulationof pages 1-2)

This is important current context for Q9UT86 because it regulates activity of the Apc11-containing holoenzyme. It is **not evidence that Pmk1 directly phosphorylates Apc11**.

## 8. Recent structural research and evolutionary interpretation

High-resolution human APC/C structures published in **November 2024** resolved apo-APC/C at **3.2 Å** and APC/C–CDH1–EMI1 at **2.9 Å**, strengthening atomic models of APC2–APC11 organization and regulated catalytic access. [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-54398-5]. These data support the conserved catalytic interpretation but do not directly determine the conformation of fission-yeast Q9UT86. (vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

A comparative cryo-EM study published in **October 2024** found broad architectural conservation between budding-yeast and human APC/C but significant regulatory differences: budding-yeast apo-APC/C already positioned its APC2–APC11 module for E2 binding, whereas human APC/C undergoes a coactivator-linked catalytic-module rearrangement. The study also found no budding-yeast equivalent of the human APC1 phosphoregulatory autoinhibitory segment. [https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.100821]. (vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

The expert interpretation is therefore conservative: **APC11’s core RING-E3 chemistry is deeply conserved, but detailed conformational regulation cannot simply be transferred from humans or budding yeast to *S. pombe*.** Direct fission-yeast structural work remains at substantially lower resolution.

## 9. Phenotypes and essentiality

Direct loss-of-function phenotyping specifically for *S. pombe apc11/SPAC343.03* was limited in the retrieved primary literature. Therefore, essentiality and exact null phenotype should not be claimed solely by transferring results from other organisms.

Nevertheless, conserved evidence strongly predicts an essential mitotic function. Budding-yeast APC11 RING mutants arrested with predominantly 2N DNA; after 3 hours at restrictive temperature, **more than 70%** of cells had large buds, short spindles, and DNA at the bud neck. Zinc-ligand mutations failed to complement the temperature-sensitive allele. These observations demonstrate what disruption of APC11 catalysis does in a close fungal homolog, not a directly measured Q9UT86 phenotype. (leverson2000theapc11ringh2 pages 2-3, leverson2000theapc11ringh2 pages 3-5)

At the exact-species pathway level, defective APC/C or depletion of its E2 machinery causes mitotic arrest, cyclin accumulation, failed chromosome segregation, and characteristic cut phenotypes. These support the expected consequences of disabling Apc11-containing APC/C but are not substitutes for an *apc11*-specific deletion experiment. (mclean2011stateofthe pages 12-14, yoon2002proteomicsanalysisidentifies pages 2-4)

## 10. Current applications and real-world implementation

Apc11/Q9UT86 currently has greatest practical value as a **mechanistic research component**, not as a clinical or industrial target:

- *S. pombe* APC/C provides a genetically tractable system for dissecting chromosome segregation, checkpoint control, and proteolysis.
- Purified APC/C and recombinant Apc2–Apc11 modules support biochemical assays of E2 selection and ubiquitin transfer.
- Apc11’s RING fold provides an evolutionary comparison with canonical cullin–RING ligases such as CUL1–RBX1.
- Structural differences among yeast and human APC/C warn against assuming that inhibitors or engineered degradation strategies will transfer identically across species.

No direct therapeutic, diagnostic, agricultural, or biotechnological implementation specifically using *S. pombe* Q9UT86 was identified. Human APC/C is relevant to proliferative disease and targeted-proteolysis research, but that does not make the fission-yeast protein itself a validated drug target.

## 11. Recommended functional annotation

**Molecular function:** RING-type E3 ubiquitin-ligase catalytic subunit of APC/C; binds/activates ubiquitin-conjugating E2 machinery as part of the Apc2–Apc11 catalytic module and promotes ubiquitin transfer to APC/C-recruited substrates.

**Biological process:** APC/C-dependent proteasomal turnover during the metaphase–anaphase transition and mitotic exit; regulation of sister-chromatid separation, cyclin-dependent kinase inactivation, and chromosome segregation.

**Complex:** One-copy core component of the 13-subunit *S. pombe* APC/C/20S cyclosome; catalytic region associated with Apc2 and Apc10.

**Principal substrates of the holoenzyme:** Cut2/securin and Cdc13/B-type cyclin. Substrate specificity is conferred mainly by Slp1/Cdc20 or Ste9/Cdh1 with Apc10, not by Apc11 alone.

**Localization:** Intracellular APC/C-associated protein acting in mitotic cell-cycle control; finer Apc11-specific live-cell localization remains insufficiently resolved.

**Confidence:** High for identity, APC/C membership, RING-family assignment, complex stoichiometry, and conserved catalytic role; moderate for exact endogenous E2 contacts and subcellular distribution; presently insufficient for Q9UT86-specific catalytic kinetics, ubiquitin-chain linkage preference, detailed mutant phenotype, or direct post-translational regulation.
