# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe mik1* (UniProt P30290)

## Executive summary

The requested protein is correctly identified as **Mik1**, encoded by ***mik1* / SPBC660.14** in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*. Comparative kinome analysis assigns SPBC660.14 to the **WEE family** of eukaryotic protein kinases, alongside—but distinct from—the separate *S. pombe* Wee1 kinase. This agrees with the supplied UniProt accession P30290 and its protein-kinase-domain annotations. The unrelated *S. pombe* proteins **Pmk1** (a MAP kinase) and **Pik1** (a phosphatidylinositol 4-kinase) were excluded. (mirandasaavedra2007thecomplementof pages 8-10)

Mik1’s best-established primary function is to transfer phosphate from ATP to **Tyr15 of the cyclin-dependent kinase Cdc2/Cdk1**, principally in Cdc2–Cdc13 complexes. Tyr15 phosphorylation inhibits Cdc2 activity and therefore restrains entry into mitosis. Although UniProt classifies the protein within the Ser/Thr protein-kinase superfamily and assigns EC 2.7.11.1, the experimentally important physiological reaction is protein **tyrosine** phosphorylation—a recognized property of Wee-family dual-specificity-like kinases. No comparably well-validated additional physiological substrate or quantitative substrate-consensus profile was found. (kanoh1998theproteinkinase pages 1-2, wu1997nif1anovel pages 1-2)

Mik1 is partially redundant with Wee1 during ordinary vegetative growth but assumes greater importance during **S phase, replication stress, and sustained checkpoint arrest**. Replication or DNA-damage checkpoint signaling increases Mik1 activity and/or abundance, preserving inhibitory Cdc2 Tyr15 phosphorylation until replication or repair is sufficiently complete. (rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2, rhind2001rolesofthe pages 7-7)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

The literature independently maps ***mik1*** to **SPBC660.14** and classifies its product in the WEE kinase family. In the same analysis, *S. pombe wee1* is SPCC18B5.03 and budding-yeast Swe1 is a separate homolog, confirming that these names refer to related but nonidentical proteins. This matches the requested organism, *S. pombe* strain 972 lineage, and the supplied UniProt description “mitosis inhibitor protein kinase Mik1.” (mirandasaavedra2007thecomplementof pages 8-10)

The domain information supplied by the user—protein-kinase domain, kinase-like fold, Ser/Thr kinase active-site signature, and CC_SR_Kinase classification—is therefore coherent with the literature. The apparently conflicting descriptions “Ser/Thr protein kinase” and “tyrosine kinase” should be interpreted carefully: the first reflects broad sequence/domain classification and EC nomenclature, whereas direct cell-cycle evidence identifies **Cdc2 Tyr15** as the biologically relevant phosphoacceptor. (kanoh1998theproteinkinase pages 1-2, wu1997nif1anovel pages 1-2, mirandasaavedra2007thecomplementof pages 8-10)

## 2. Primary biochemical function and substrate specificity

The supported reaction is:

**ATP + Cdc2 Tyr15 → ADP + Cdc2 phospho-Tyr15.**

Phosphorylation at Tyr15 suppresses the activity of the M-phase-promoting Cdc2–Cdc13 kinase and delays the G2/S-to-mitosis transition. The opposing phosphatases Cdc25 and Pyp3 remove the inhibitory phosphate, placing Mik1 in the core bistable control system governing mitotic commitment. (wu1997nif1anovel pages 1-2)

The evidence supports **Cdc2/Cdk1 as the principal physiological substrate**. It does not establish a broad Mik1 substrate repertoire, peptide-recognition motif, kinetic constants, or differential preference among Cdc2–cyclin complexes. Accordingly, annotation of additional substrates solely from kinase-family membership would be speculative. (kanoh1998theproteinkinase pages 1-2, rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2)

Mik1 overexpression produces persistent Cdc2 phosphorylation, delayed mitotic entry, and pronounced cell elongation, providing functional evidence that increased Mik1 dosage inhibits the Cdc2 mitotic switch. This phenotype disappears in an Hsp90-defective background, linking it to functional Mik1 protein rather than merely to transgene expression. (goes2001hsp90chaperonecomplexes pages 3-5)

## 3. Role in the cell-cycle control pathway

### Normal proliferative growth

Wee1 is the dominant inhibitory Cdc2 kinase during unperturbed growth. Loss of *mik1* alone is viable and produces little or no change in division length, whereas loss of *wee1* causes cells to enter mitosis at approximately **half the wild-type cell length**. Nevertheless, simultaneous inactivation of Wee1 and Mik1 produces lethal premature mitosis—“mitotic catastrophe”—before DNA replication is complete. Thus, Mik1 supplies a normally subordinate but essential backup capacity for Cdc2 Tyr15 inhibition. (kanoh1998theproteinkinase pages 1-2, wu1997nif1anovel pages 1-2, frazer2011regulationofthe pages 29-34)

This genetic architecture explains why a single *mik1* deletion can appear phenotypically mild: Wee1 masks much of the defect. It also cautions against interpreting viability of the single deletion as evidence that Mik1 lacks an important biochemical function. The strong double-mutant phenotype is the decisive evidence for overlapping control of mitotic entry. (wu1997nif1anovel pages 1-2)

### DNA-replication checkpoint

When replication is inhibited—for example by hydroxyurea—checkpoint signaling prevents mitosis largely by maintaining Cdc2 Tyr15 phosphorylation. Mik1 becomes substantially more important in this state. Replication arrest increases ***mik1* mRNA**, apparently stabilizes Mik1 protein, and produces a marked increase in its steady-state abundance. Cds1, the principal replication-checkpoint effector kinase, is required for the large checkpoint-associated increase in Mik1 abundance, although the retrieved evidence does not establish that Mik1 is a direct Cds1 phosphosubstrate. (kanoh1998theproteinkinase pages 1-2, rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2)

Mik1 alone can impose much of the hydroxyurea-induced mitotic delay in experimental backgrounds that bypass other checkpoint outputs. Cells lacking both Wee1 and Cdc25 can still arrest before mitosis under replication blockade, consistent with residual Mik1-dependent Cdc2 inhibition, although Pyp3-related effects cannot always be excluded in such engineered backgrounds. (rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2, rhind2001rolesofthe pages 7-7)

### DNA-damage checkpoint

Mik1 also contributes to the G2 DNA-damage response. Its immediate role appears smaller than Wee1’s during checkpoint establishment, but it becomes important for maintaining prolonged arrest. Checkpoint signaling can rapidly increase Mik1-dependent inhibition before a detectable rise in protein abundance; persistent damage subsequently increases Mik1 protein. This later increase is weaker than that observed during replication arrest and reportedly occurs without a corresponding increase in *mik1* mRNA. Chk1 is required for these damage-associated Mik1 responses. (rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2, rhind2001rolesofthe pages 7-7)

The mechanistic interpretation is that checkpoint control operates on multiple convergent branches: it inhibits Cdc25-mediated Tyr15 dephosphorylation while enhancing or preserving Wee1/Mik1-mediated phosphorylation. Mik1 is therefore an **effector of checkpoint enforcement**, not the upstream DNA-lesion sensor. (rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2, rhind2001rolesofthe pages 7-7)

## 4. Regulation of Mik1 protein

Mik1 regulation occurs at several levels:

1. **Transcription/abundance:** replication arrest increases *mik1* transcript and protein; prolonged DNA damage increases protein with little or no transcript induction. (rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2)
2. **Rapid functional activation:** checkpoint-dependent Mik1 activity can rise before protein accumulation, implying post-translational control, although the responsible modification was not definitively identified in the retrieved studies. (rhind2001rolesofthe pages 7-7)
3. **Protein folding and stability:** Mik1 associates through its kinase domain with an Hsp90 chaperone complex containing Hsp90, p23/Wos2, and cyclophilin-40. Hsp90 impairment or geldanamycin treatment lowers kinase abundance and promotes rapid proteasome-dependent degradation. (goes2001hsp90chaperonecomplexes pages 1-2, goes2001hsp90chaperonecomplexes pages 3-5)

These findings identify Mik1 as an **Hsp90 client** and suggest that checkpoint effectiveness depends not only on *mik1* transcription but also on chaperone-assisted maturation and stabilization of the kinase. The studies did not provide Mik1-specific catalytic constants, degradation half-lives, or a complete map of regulatory phosphorylation sites. (goes2001hsp90chaperonecomplexes pages 1-2)

## 5. Subcellular localization

Localization evidence is substantially less complete than the functional genetics. Mik1 has been reported to **accumulate in the nucleus during S phase after hydroxyurea treatment**, consistent with access to nuclear Cdc2 complexes during replication-checkpoint arrest. This accumulation is lost in checkpoint-defective contexts. However, the available evidence does not support a definitive claim that Mik1 is constitutively nuclear, nor does it provide a high-resolution localization atlas across an unperturbed cell cycle. (yimit2011pyp3involvementin pages 21-26)

The conservative annotation is therefore: **Mik1 carries out its best-supported checkpoint function in the nuclear compartment, where it can inhibit Cdc2 during S-phase/replication stress, but its basal distribution and possible cytoplasmic pool remain insufficiently resolved.** This conclusion is less certain than the catalytic and genetic annotation. (yimit2011pyp3involvementin pages 21-26)

## 7. Quantitative and experimentally discriminating observations

* Loss of Wee1 causes division at approximately **50% of wild-type cell length**, while *mik1* loss alone has little detectable effect on cell length; this demonstrates unequal contributions under ordinary growth. (wu1997nif1anovel pages 1-2)
* Combined Wee1/Mik1 inactivation is **lethal** and drives mitosis before replication is complete, whereas *mik1* single deletion is viable. (kanoh1998theproteinkinase pages 1-2, wu1997nif1anovel pages 1-2, frazer2011regulationofthe pages 29-34)
* Mik1 is reported as an approximately **66-kDa** kinase. (goes2001hsp90chaperonecomplexes pages 1-2)
* Replication arrest produces a “dramatic” increase in steady-state Mik1, whereas prolonged DNA damage produces a smaller increase. Exact fold changes were not available in the retrieved evidence and should not be invented. (rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2)
* Mik1 overexpression causes sustained Cdc2 phosphorylation and strong elongation in wild-type cells but not in the conditional Hsp90 mutant *swo1-26*, experimentally connecting Hsp90 function to Mik1 output. (goes2001hsp90chaperonecomplexes pages 3-5)

## 8. Current research status and recent developments

No Mik1-focused primary study from **2023–2024** was identified in the retrieved literature. Recent fission-yeast cell-cycle studies continue to use inhibitory Cdc2 phosphorylation as established pathway context, but they do not materially revise Mik1’s direct functional annotation. Consequently, the most informative sources remain the mechanistic studies published between 1997 and 2001 and later comparative or methodological syntheses.

This absence of recent dedicated work is itself important: Mik1 should be regarded as a **mechanistically well-supported but incompletely characterized checkpoint kinase**, rather than as a protein with a modern structural, phosphoproteomic, or systems-level characterization. Major unresolved questions include its full substrate spectrum, three-dimensional structure, quantitative enzymology, direct upstream phosphorylation sites, basal localization dynamics, and whether checkpoint-dependent stabilization is mediated by a defined ubiquitin ligase.

## 9. Applications and broader significance

Mik1 is principally a **research-system component**, not a current industrial or clinical target. Its practical applications include genetic dissection of replication and DNA-damage checkpoints, analysis of redundancy in mitotic control, testing of Cdc2 Tyr15-dependent checkpoint models, and investigation of Hsp90 client-kinase maturation. Because the Cdk1–Wee-family–Cdc25 regulatory architecture is conserved, *S. pombe* Mik1 experiments provide conceptual insight into eukaryotic checkpoint control; however, Mik1 should not be treated as a one-to-one functional equivalent of every vertebrate WEE1 or MYT1 protein. (rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2, mirandasaavedra2007thecomplementof pages 8-10, goes2001hsp90chaperonecomplexes pages 1-2)

## 10. Overall annotation and confidence

**Recommended functional annotation:** *Mik1 is a WEE-family protein kinase that inhibits mitotic entry by phosphorylating Cdc2/Cdk1 on Tyr15. It acts partly redundantly with Wee1 during normal growth and becomes especially important during S phase and replication- or DNA-damage-checkpoint arrest, when its activity and abundance increase. Its checkpoint function is associated with nuclear accumulation, and its stability/activity depend on the Hsp90 chaperone system.* (kanoh1998theproteinkinase pages 1-2, rhind2001rolesofthe pages 1-2, rhind2001rolesofthe pages 7-7, goes2001hsp90chaperonecomplexes pages 1-2, yimit2011pyp3involvementin pages 21-26)

Confidence is **high** for identity, family, Cdc2 Tyr15 targeting, mitotic inhibition, and redundancy with Wee1; **moderate-to-high** for checkpoint-dependent accumulation and Hsp90 regulation; and **low-to-moderate** for detailed localization. Evidence is currently insufficient to assign additional physiological substrates or a precise consensus motif.
