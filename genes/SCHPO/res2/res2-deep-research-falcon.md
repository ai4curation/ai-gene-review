# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe res2*

## Executive conclusion

The requested protein is **Res2**, historically also called **Pct1**, encoded by *res2/pct1* (systematic locus **SPAC22F3.09c**) in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* strain 972 and represented by **UniProt P41412**. It is an approximately **73-kDa, nonenzymatic DNA-binding transcriptional regulator** and a core component of the MluI-binding factor (**MBF**, also called DSC1). Its primary function is to help recognize MluI cell-cycle box (**MCB**) promoter elements and regulate genes required for mitotic G1/S progression and premeiotic DNA replication. It has no catalytic reaction or small-molecule substrate; its relevant “substrate” is MCB-containing chromatin, with the canonical motif **ACGCGT**. (zhu1994pct1+whichencodes pages 1-2, ayte1997thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

Res2 acts primarily in the **nucleus on chromatin**, in complexes containing Cdc10 and Res1 during vegetative growth and a Res2–Cdc10-centered complex during meiotic differentiation. Its mitotic role overlaps with Res1, whereas its requirement during premeiotic S phase and meiosis is substantially more specialized. (ayte1997thefissionyeast pages 7-7, eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 2-2)

## 1. Mandatory identity verification and disambiguation

### 1.1 Correct gene and organism

Independent 1994–1997 primary studies identify *S. pombe res2* and *pct1* as names for the same cell-cycle regulator, described as p73Res2/Pct1. The cloned product binds p85Cdc10, recognizes MCB DNA, contains a Swi4/Mbp1-related N-terminal DNA-binding region and ankyrin repeats, and is especially important for meiosis. These properties agree with the supplied identity—P41412, SPAC22F3.09c, Res2/Pct1—and with the listed KilA/APSES-like HTH, SWI6-like, and ankyrin-repeat annotations. (zhu1994pct1+whichencodes pages 1-2, ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-1, miyamoto1994res2+anew pages 2-2)

### 1.2 Critical synonym ambiguity

There is an **unrelated fission-yeast protein also called Pct1** in later literature: a 303-residue, 35.4-kDa RNA 5′-triphosphatase involved in mRNA capping. That enzyme hydrolyzes the γ-phosphate from nascent RNA, requires divalent metal, and is essential for viability. It is not Res2, lacks the Res2/MBF domain architecture, and was excluded from this annotation. The strong differences in molecular mass, domain family, biochemical activity, and phenotype resolve the ambiguity. (takagi2002divergentsubunitinteractions pages 2-3)

Accordingly, literature about the 303-aa RNA-triphosphatase Pct1—including interactions with Spt5 or Pce1—must not be transferred to UniProt P41412.

## 2. Protein class and domain architecture

Res2 belongs to the fungal Cdc10/Swi4/Mbp1-related family of cell-cycle transcription factors. It has **28% amino-acid identity to Res1 and 17% to Cdc10**, with strongest conservation in an N-terminal “Res/Swi4” region corresponding to the DNA-binding APSES/KilA-like helix-turn-helix superfamily. (miyamoto1994res2+anew pages 2-2)

Biochemical deletion analysis maps homodimerization to the first **152 amino acids**. Res2 also contains two approximately **33-residue Cdc10/Swi6-like ankyrin repeats** in its central/C-terminal region. The repeats were dispensable in reported assays for DNA binding and Cdc10 association, but the central region was nevertheless required for full biological function, consistent with a regulatory or transcriptional-effector role rather than direct sequence recognition. (ayte1997thefissionyeast pages 1-2, zhu1997functionalcharacterizationof pages 9-11)

No experimentally determined full-length Res2 structure was identified. The InterPro assignments therefore provide credible evolutionary/structural classification, but not an atomic mechanism for the intact complex.

## 3. Primary molecular function

### 3.1 MBF DNA-binding subunit

Res2 is a **sequence-specific, nonenzymatic transcription-factor subunit** of MBF/DSC1. Original DNA-binding experiments showed that Res2–Cdc10 complexes recognize MCB elements in vitro and in vivo, while failing to recognize SCB or E2F sites. The canonical MCB sequence is **5′-ACGCGT-3′**. (zhu1994pct1+whichencodes pages 1-2, ayte1997thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

Full-length Res2 binds DNA only weakly by itself; Cdc10 strongly enhances binding. Res2 can form DNA-binding homodimers and Res1–Res2 heterodimers, while both Res proteins interact with Cdc10. A tetrameric Res2–Cdc10 arrangement has been proposed, but native stoichiometry remains insufficiently resolved. (zhu1997functionalcharacterizationof pages 9-11)

Loss of either Res1 or Res2 disrupts normal MBF DNA-binding activity, supporting a vegetative complex containing **Cdc10, Res1, and Res2**, rather than a simple model in which mutually exclusive Cdc10–Res1 and Cdc10–Res2 dimers alternate through the cell cycle. Res1 and Res2 remain Cdc10-associated through the mitotic cycle. (ayte1997thefissionyeast pages 7-7, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 47-52)

### 3.2 Activator and repressor functions

An early model classified Res1 as an activator and Res2 as a repressor. Later expression profiling showed that this is too simple: each contributes to activation or repression of different MBF targets. Most MBF-dependent transcripts become constitutively derepressed in *res2Δ*, supporting a major role in transcriptional shutoff, but **yox1, cig2, and mik1** can retain near-wild-type expression. Thus, Res2 is best viewed as a gene- and context-dependent DNA-binding regulatory scaffold rather than a universal repressor. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 47-52, dutta2008thednareplication pages 7-8)

## 4. Biological processes and pathway placement

### 4.1 Mitotic G1/S transcription

MBF activity peaks around G1/S and drives expression of genes needed for DNA replication and S-phase progression. Supported targets of the broader Res2-containing MBF program include **cdc18, cdc22, cdt1, cig2, mik1, mrc1, ssb1, rhp51, ctp1,** and **pfh1**. Direct promoter evidence is strongest for representative MCB-containing genes such as *cdc18* and *cdc22*; assignment of every listed gene specifically to Res2, rather than to MBF collectively, is less certain. (ayte1997thefissionyeast pages 7-7, dutta2008thednareplication pages 7-8, ivanova2011g1stranscriptionand pages 1-3)

Genome-wide ChIP-chip using chromosomally tagged Res2 placed it at MBF-associated promoter and intragenic sites. The study used arrays with approximately **33-bp resolution** and found a comparatively stable Cdc10/Res1/Res2 promoter core, whereas recruitment of the coactivator Rep2 was more temporally dynamic. (eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 1-2, eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 2-2)

The *res2* deletion is viable, reflecting overlap with Res1, but it impairs growth and normal periodic transcription. Overexpression of Res2 can compensate for defects caused by loss of Res1, while genetic interactions with the Pas1–Pef1 cyclin/CDK module support a specific route that activates the Res2–Cdc10 pathway. (tanaka2000apcllikecyclin pages 10-11, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 47-52)

### 4.2 Premeiotic S phase and meiotic differentiation

Res2 has a stronger specialized role in meiosis than in vegetative growth. Its expression/activity rises during nitrogen starvation, conjugation, and premeiotic DNA synthesis; *res2/pct1* deletion is viable during vegetative growth but causes severe defects in premeiotic DNA replication and meiotic completion. (zhu1994pct1+whichencodes pages 1-2, ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-1, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 47-52)

The foundational model proposes that nitrogen starvation reduces Res1 abundance and disrupts the mitotic tripartite MBF complex while Res2 increases and remains associated with Cdc10. This meiotic complex promotes *mat1* expression, leading to induction of **mei3**; Mei3 inhibits Pat1 kinase, permitting activation of the Mei2/Ste11 meiotic program. Evidence is strong that Res2 is required for meiotic progression, but the exact composition and direct genomic targets of meiotic Res2 are less comprehensively mapped than those of mitotic MBF. (ayte1997thefissionyeast pages 7-7)

A March 2024 review confirms the current framework that MBF contains Res1, Res2, and Cdc10, binds MCB elements, and controls G1/S genes such as *cig2*. It places this module within the broader nutrient-starvation → G1 arrest → mating-type/Mei3 → Pat1 inhibition pathway initiating sexual differentiation. The review focuses chiefly on events leading to conjugation, so it does not resolve the detailed composition of post-conjugation Res2 complexes. [Kawamukai, 2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bbb/zbae019). (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 1-2)

### 4.3 Negative feedback and replication-stress signaling

At the end of S phase, Nrm1 acts mainly as an adaptor that loads the Yox1 repressor onto MBF. Nrm1/Yox1 therefore terminates the G1/S transcriptional wave. During replication stress, the Cds1 checkpoint kinase phosphorylates Yox1 at **Ser114 and Thr115**, releases it from MBF, and sustains transcription of genes such as *cdc22* and *cdc18* to replenish replication capacity and promote recovery. These established modifications affect associated repressors, not Res2 directly. (ivanova2011g1stranscriptionand pages 3-4, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 145-154)

Cds1 can phosphorylate the C-terminal region of Cdc10 in vitro, while phosphomimetic **cdc10-2E** constitutively activates MBF transcription. Restoring MBF output with cdc10-2E improves hydroxyurea resistance in *cds1Δ* and *rad3Δ* backgrounds; Rep2 overexpression also partially suppresses checkpoint-mutant hydroxyurea sensitivity. This shows that sustained S-phase transcription makes a causal contribution to replication-stress survival. (dutta2008thednareplication pages 5-7)

DNA damage produces a distinct response. Chk1 phosphorylates the Cdc10 core subunit and promotes eviction of MBF from chromatin, repressing MBF-dependent transcription. Again, available evidence supports regulation of the Res2-containing complex through **Cdc10, Nrm1, Yox1, and Rep2**, not direct checkpoint phosphorylation of Res2. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127)

### 4.4 Chromatin remodeling

The INO80 chromatin-remodeling complex physically associates with MBF and is recruited to *cdc18* and *cdc22* promoters. INO80 occupancy peaks at the M/G1 transition approximately **20 minutes before maximal mRNA accumulation**. Depending on the mutated INO80 subunit, Cdc10 promoter occupancy fell to about **60% or below 30%** of control, while a *cdc22*-YFP reporter declined by **10–30%**. (knezevic2018theino80complex pages 4-7)

This regulation involves the H2A.Z variant Pht1 and promoter nucleosome organization. A nonacetylatable Pht1-4KR allele reduced MBF reporter output to **62% ± 0.6** of wild type, close to the **56% ± 0.5** observed in *rep2Δ*. These results indicate that acetylated H2A.Z and INO80 help establish an accessible promoter architecture needed for full, timely MBF transcription. They do not demonstrate an isolated direct INO80–Res2 interaction; the strongest physical evidence is through Cdc10-containing MBF. [Knezevic et al., September 2018, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/febs.14640). (knezevic2018theino80complex pages 4-7, knezevic2018theino80complex pages 7-10)

## 5. Cellular localization

The functional site of Res2 is the **nucleus, at chromatin associated with MCB-containing genes**. This conclusion is supported directly by formaldehyde cross-linking and ChIP-chip detection of tagged Res2 at MBF-bound genomic loci, together with its sequence-specific DNA-binding activity. (eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 1-2, eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 2-2)

However, the retrieved literature did not provide a definitive Res2-specific fluorescence-microscopy study measuring nuclear/cytoplasmic partitioning or cell-cycle-dependent movement. “Nuclear/chromatin-associated” is therefore a strong functional conclusion, while fine-scale localization dynamics remain insufficiently characterized.

## 7. Recent developments and current applications

The direct mechanistic literature on Res2 itself is concentrated in 1994–2000. More recent work has shifted from identifying the subunit to resolving how the **Res2-containing MBF system** is controlled by checkpoint signaling, dynamic cofactor recruitment, and chromatin architecture. Major developments include genome-wide Res2/MBF occupancy mapping, identification of Cds1–Yox1 and Chk1–Cdc10 checkpoint inputs, and demonstration that INO80 and acetylated H2A.Z are required for full temporal activation of MBF promoters. (eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 2-2, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127, knezevic2018theino80complex pages 4-7)

No Res2-focused primary paper from 2023–2024 was identified. The most relevant 2024 authoritative source is the sexual-differentiation review cited above, which retains Res2 within the established MBF/meiotic-entry framework rather than proposing a substantially revised molecular function. (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 1-2)

Current applications are principally experimental rather than clinical:

1. **Cell-cycle transcription model:** Res2/MBF is used to dissect periodic G1/S transcription and conserved principles analogous to fungal MBF/SBF and metazoan E2F–Rb regulatory logic.
2. **Replication-checkpoint model:** MBF reporters and phosphomutants test how transcription contributes to hydroxyurea tolerance and replication restart. (dutta2008thednareplication pages 5-7)
3. **Chromatin-regulation model:** *cdc18*/*cdc22* promoters provide tractable systems for studying INO80, H2A.Z acetylation, nucleosome-depleted regions, and timed polymerase recruitment. (knezevic2018theino80complex pages 4-7, knezevic2018theino80complex pages 7-10)
4. **Meiotic-entry model:** *res2* mutants separate vegetative G1/S functions from the specialized premeiotic-S and Pat1/Mei2 pathway.

No direct therapeutic, diagnostic, or industrial implementation specific to Res2 was found.

## 8. Expert assessment and remaining uncertainties

The strongest annotation is:

> **Res2 is a chromatin-associated DNA-binding regulatory subunit of the fission-yeast MBF transcription complex. It recognizes MCB-containing promoters in cooperation with Cdc10 and Res1, contributes context-dependent activation and repression of mitotic G1/S genes, and is specifically required for premeiotic DNA replication and meiotic progression.**

The evidence is strongest for protein identity, Cdc10 association, MCB specificity, MBF membership, and meiotic requirement. Important unresolved questions are the native stoichiometry and full-length structure of MBF; quantitative Res2-only DNA-binding affinity; the complete direct target set in meiosis; direct live-cell localization dynamics; and whether Res2 itself undergoes regulatory post-translational modification. Current checkpoint evidence should not be overinterpreted as direct Res2 regulation because the experimentally demonstrated targets are chiefly Cdc10, Yox1, Nrm1, and Rep2-associated mechanisms. (zhu1997functionalcharacterizationof pages 9-11, ivanova2011g1stranscriptionand pages 3-4, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127)
