| Identity and synonym ambiguity | **Res2/Pct1** is the approximately 73-kDa *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* Cdc10-binding cell-cycle transcription factor corresponding to P41412. It is distinct from the unrelated *S. pombe* **Pct1 RNA triphosphatase**, a 303-aa, 35.4-kDa mRNA-capping enzyme. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000007) | Cloning, interaction, DNA-binding, deletion, and enzyme studies | **High.** Molecular size, domains, function, and essentiality resolve the genuine historical name ambiguity. |
| Primary molecular function | Res2 is a **nonenzymatic, sequence-specific transcription-factor subunit** of MluI-binding factor, also called MBF or DSC1. It helps recognize MCB promoters and regulate mitotic G1/S and premeiotic-S transcription. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000011) | EMSA, genetics, protein-interaction assays, and transcriptional phenotypes | **High.** No catalytic reaction or enzymatic substrate applies; activity depends on the complete MBF complex. |
| Protein family and domains | Res2 belongs to the Cdc10/Swi4/Mbp1-related class. Its N-terminal APSES/KilA-like helix-turn-helix region supports DNA binding and dimerization, while two approximately 33-aa ankyrin/Cdc10-Swi6 repeats occur centrally or toward the C terminus. Res2 has 28% identity to Res1 and 17% identity to Cdc10. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) | Sequence comparison and truncation/domain-function experiments | **High** for architecture and homology; **moderate** for exact mechanism. Ankyrin repeats were dispensable for DNA binding and Cdc10 association in reported assays but required for full biological function. |
| Complex partners | Res2 associates with **Cdc10** and can form DNA-binding homodimers and Res1–Res2 heterodimers. Mitotic MBF contains Cdc10, Res1, and Res2; Rep2 acts as a dynamically recruited coactivator in this setting. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000023) | Co-immunoprecipitation, EMSA/supershift, deletion analysis, and ChIP-chip | **High** for Cdc10 and Res1 associations; **moderate** for native stoichiometry. A proposed Res2–Cdc10 tetramer remains unconfirmed. |
| DNA recognition | Res2-containing MBF recognizes the six-base MluI cell-cycle box, **MCB: ACGCGT**, and did not recognize SCB or E2F sites in original specificity experiments. Cdc10 strongly enhances the otherwise weak DNA binding of full-length Res2. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000011) | In-vitro and in-vivo DNA-binding assays and EMSA | **High** for MCB specificity. No modern dissociation constant or comprehensive Res2-only motif-selection dataset was identified. |
| Mitotic G1/S targets | Res2-containing MBF controls genes needed for DNA synthesis and S-phase progression. Supported MBF targets include **cdc18, cdc22, cdt1, cig2, mik1, mrc1, ssb1, rhp51, ctp1,** and **pfh1**. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) | Mutant expression analysis, promoter binding, arrays, and ChIP | **High** for MBF-level regulation; **moderate** for assigning every target specifically to Res2. Res2 can activate some targets and repress others. |
| Mitotic phenotype | *res2* deletion is viable but perturbs growth and periodic MBF transcription. Most MBF-dependent genes become derepressed, whereas **yox1, cig2,** and **mik1** can retain near-wild-type expression. Res2 and Res1 have overlapping mitotic functions. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010, pqac-00000023) | Deletion genetics, rescue, and expression profiling | **High** for viability and altered transcription. Gene-specific effects rule out a simple universal-repressor model. |
| Meiotic role | Res2 is particularly important for premeiotic DNA synthesis and meiotic progression. Its abundance or activity rises during nitrogen starvation and premeiotic S phase, while deletion causes severe meiotic-DNA-synthesis and sporulation defects. Res2–Cdc10 activity has also been connected to **mat1** expression and the downstream **mei3–Pat1–Mei2** pathway. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029) | Starvation/meiosis time courses, deletions, overexpression, DNA-binding, and expression assays | **High** for meiotic requirement; **moderate** for the detailed mat1-to-mei3 causal model, which rests mainly on foundational studies. |
| Checkpoint regulation | Replication stress maintains MBF transcription through **Cds1**, which phosphorylates Yox1 at S114/T115 and regulates Nrm1/Cdc10-linked repression. DNA damage activates **Chk1**, which phosphorylates Cdc10 and removes MBF from chromatin. These pathways regulate the Res2-containing complex but have not been shown to modify Res2 directly. (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000022) | Phosphorylation assays, phosphomutants, ChIP, and stress-survival assays | **High** for regulation through Yox1, Nrm1, and Cdc10; direct checkpoint phosphorylation of Res2 is **unsupported**. |
| Chromatin regulation and quantitative effects | INO80 associates with MBF and reaches **cdc18/cdc22** promoters about 20 minutes before maximal mRNA accumulation. INO80-subunit mutants reduce Cdc10 occupancy to approximately 60% or below 30% and lower a cdc22-YFP reporter by 10–30%. Nonacetylatable H2A.Z/Pht1-4KR lowers reporter output to **62% ± 0.6** of wild type, compared with **56% ± 0.5** for *rep2Δ*. (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000026) | Affinity purification, co-IP, synchronized ChIP, reporter assays, and nucleosome mapping | **High** for MBF-level chromatin regulation. INO80 association was demonstrated chiefly through Cdc10, not as an isolated direct INO80–Res2 interaction. |
| Cellular localization | Res2 acts in the **nucleus on chromatin**. Formaldehyde ChIP-chip with HA-tagged Res2 mapped it to MBF-associated promoter and intragenic sites at approximately **33-bp resolution**. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000015) | Genome-wide ChIP-chip and promoter-occupancy analysis | **Moderate to high** for chromatin association and nuclear action. Direct microscopy or live-cell compartmental dynamics were not identified, so nuclear localization is partly a functional inference. |
| Applications and model relevance | Res2/MBF provides a tractable model for conserved G1/S transcription, checkpoint–transcription coupling, negative feedback, and chromatin-remodeler/H2A.Z control. Constitutive MBF activation can improve hydroxyurea resistance in checkpoint mutants, enabling mechanistic tests of replication-stress survival. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000024) | Synchronization, reporter strains, checkpoint genetics, ChIP, and chromatin perturbation | **High** as a basic-research model. No direct clinical or industrial implementation specific to Res2 was identified. |
| Overall annotation | **Chromatin-associated DNA-binding regulatory subunit of the fission-yeast MBF transcription complex that controls mitotic G1/S transcription and has a stronger specialized requirement in premeiotic S phase and meiosis.** (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000015) | Integrated biochemical, genetic, genomic, and evolutionary evidence | **High.** Remaining gaps include native complex structure, quantitative Res2-only DNA affinity, direct live-cell localization, and possible Res2-specific post-translational regulation. |
