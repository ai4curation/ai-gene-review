Gene Description

met16 (SPAC13G7.06) encodes the thioredoxin-dependent 3'-phosphoadenylyl-sulfate (PAPS) reductase of fission yeast (EC 1.8.4.8), the ortholog of Saccharomyces cerevisiae MET16 and of bacterial CysH. It reduces PAPS, made by the ATP sulfurylase sua1 and the APS kinase met14, to free sulfite and adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate, taking the two electrons from reduced thioredoxin, which it releases as the disulfide. Native trx1 deletion and sulfite rescue support cytosolic Trx1 as an important physiological reductant, without proving that it is the only donor under every condition. The sulfite is then reduced to sulfide by the sir1/met10 NADPH-sulfite reductase. met16 is a cytosolic member of the PAPS reductase (CysH) family, and its deletion causes methionine auxotrophy.

Core Functions

Thioredoxin-dependent reduction of 3'-phosphoadenylyl sulfate (PAPS) to sulfite and adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate (EC 1.8.4.8), the step of cytosolic sulfate assimilation that yields sulfite for the sir1/met10 sulfite reductase; native trx1 deletion/sulfite-rescue evidence supports Trx1 electron donation, while purified budding-yeast biochemistry establishes the conserved reductase chemistry.
