# Research report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe cut9* (UniProt P41889)

## Executive conclusion

**Cut9 is the fission-yeast ortholog of Cdc16/Apc6 and is an essential, non-catalytic tetratricopeptide-repeat scaffold subunit of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C).** Its primary molecular role is to assemble and stabilize the APC/C TPR lobe, including contacts with Hcn1/Cdc26 and adjacent TPR subunits. It does **not** independently catalyze a reaction or recognize a unique small-molecule substrate. Instead, the Cut9-containing holo-APC/C functions as an E3 ubiquitin ligase that, with E2 enzymes and coactivators such as Slp1/Cdc20, polyubiquitylates mitotic regulators—principally Cut2/securin and Cdc13/cyclin B—for proteasomal destruction. This triggers sister-chromatid separation, anaphase onset, and mitotic exit. Direct localization within the APC/C particle is strong, but the available literature does not justify assigning Cut9 itself an exclusive nuclear, kinetochore, spindle, or cytoplasmic localization. (zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 7-10, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 9-10, ors2009thetranscriptionfactor pages 1-2, bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 1-2)

## 2. Primary molecular function

### 2.1 Structural adaptor rather than enzyme

Cut9 is best annotated as a **structural APC/C assembly factor or adaptor**. Its TPR superhelix creates protein-binding surfaces that organize the TPR lobe and couple it to the remainder of the APC/C. Mutations in a conserved surface ridge abolished Cdc23–Apc13 pull-down and strongly impaired association with Cdc27 while leaving Apc9 binding relatively intact, providing direct evidence that Cut9 mediates selective inter-subunit contacts. The catalytic ubiquitin-transfer machinery resides principally in the APC2–APC11 module; therefore, Cut9 should not be assigned an intrinsic ubiquitin-ligase reaction. (zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 7-10, zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 11-12)

Cut9 is nevertheless required for the activity of the holoenzyme because its scaffold function preserves APC/C assembly. Cut9 and Nuc2 co-sediment with a high-molecular-weight approximately 20S fraction. In the temperature-sensitive **cut9-665** mutant shifted to **36°C for 4 hours**, Lid1 shifted from the 20S complex into a smaller species. Native co-immunoprecipitation also detected Cut9–Lid1 association. These results connect loss of Cut9 function to physical APC/C disassembly rather than merely to a downstream cell-cycle defect. (berry1999theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 7-8)

### 2.2 Structural mechanism and quantitative data

The Cut9–Hcn1/Cdc26 structure was solved at **2.6 Å resolution**. Cut9 is approximately **110 Å long and 60 Å in diameter**, with TPR1–7 forming an N-terminal dimerization region and TPR8–14 forming the C-terminal region that binds Hcn1. Two Cut9 chains associate through a homotypic interface burying **6,233 Å²**, producing a Cut9–Hcn1 heterotetramer in solution. The architecture resembles that of the related APC/C TPR subunit Cdc27, supporting descent from a common ancestral helical scaffold. (zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 2-4, zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 7-10)

Hcn1/Cdc26 binds the C-terminal TPR region. Its acetylated N-terminal methionine is enclosed within a Cut9-generated chamber, leading to the mechanistic proposal that association with Cut9 protects Hcn1 from Doa10-dependent degradation and helps maintain subunit stoichiometry. This protection model is structurally persuasive but should be distinguished from direct evidence that Cut9 controls Hcn1 turnover in living fission yeast. (zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 1-2, zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 6-7)

## 3. Biochemical output and substrate specificity

The relevant reaction is catalyzed by the **Cut9-containing APC/C**, not Cut9 alone:

**E2~ubiquitin + substrate → E2 + polyubiquitylated substrate**, followed by 26S-proteasome-dependent substrate degradation.

In *S. pombe*, the best-established mitotic substrates are:

- **Cut2/securin:** its degradation releases separase/Cut1, permitting cohesin cleavage and sister-chromatid separation.
- **Cdc13/cyclin B:** its degradation lowers Cdc2/CDK activity and promotes mitotic exit.

A recent fission-yeast study describes APC/C as an approximately **1.2-MDa** complex that works with at least two E2 enzymes and Slp1/Cdc20 to ubiquitylate these substrates. Earlier structural analysis estimated mitotic APC/C at approximately **0.9–1 MDa** and identified 13 core subunits; the differing estimates reflect methods and complex composition rather than different biological entities. (ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 9-10, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2, bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 1-2)

Substrate specificity is supplied chiefly by coactivators and substrate degrons, not by Cut9. Slp1/Cdc20 binds APC/C during mitosis and recognizes substrates through a WD40-repeat domain; Ste9/Cdh1 acts during later cell-cycle states. Thus, the most precise annotation is: **Cut9 enables APC/C architecture and activity, whereas Slp1/Ste9 and the coreceptor machinery confer regulated substrate recruitment.** (ors2009thetranscriptionfactor pages 1-2, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2)

## 4. Biological process and pathway position

### 4.1 Metaphase-to-anaphase transition

Temperature-sensitive cut9 mutations destabilize the 20S APC/C and cause metaphase arrest at restrictive temperature. The foundational genetic study concluded that **cut9⁺ is required to initiate anaphase**, while subsequent biochemical work linked the phenotype to APC/C integrity. Therefore, the principal biological annotation is **APC/C-dependent metaphase–anaphase progression**, not a generic chromosome-segregation association. (yamashita1999fissionyeastapccyclosome pages 15-16, berry1999theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 7-8, zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 11-12)

Mechanistically, the sequence is:

1. Unattached or improperly attached kinetochores activate the spindle-assembly checkpoint.
2. The mitotic checkpoint complex inhibits Slp1/Cdc20-associated APC/C.
3. Once attachments are satisfactory and checkpoint signaling is silenced, APC/C–Slp1 ubiquitylates Cut2 and Cdc13.
4. Cut2 destruction activates separase and chromosome disjunction; Cdc13 destruction reduces mitotic CDK activity and facilitates mitotic exit.

Cut9 is the structural APC/C component in this pathway; it is not itself demonstrated to sense kinetochore attachment. In fission yeast, Dnt1 associates with APC/C during checkpoint activation and promotes recovery by limiting prolonged MCC binding and maintaining Slp1 abundance, illustrating how the Cut9-containing complex is dynamically regulated. (ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2, bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 1-2)

### 4.2 Additional regulation

Fission-yeast Atf1 physically binds APC/C and stimulates APC/C-dependent ubiquitylation of cyclin B and securin in a cell-free system. This supplies evidence that stress-responsive regulation can converge directly on APC/C independently of Atf1 DNA binding, but it does not show that Atf1 binds Cut9 specifically. Cut9 has also been observed in slower-migrating phosphorylated forms in high-molecular-weight APC/C fractions, suggesting cell-cycle-dependent post-translational regulation, although the available evidence does not establish the functional consequence of each Cut9 phosphorylation site. (berry1999theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 7-8, ors2009thetranscriptionfactor pages 1-2)

### 4.3 Meiosis

The same core APC/C participates in fission-yeast meiosis. Mes1 acts as both APC/C substrate and inhibitor, and the meiotic coactivator Mfr1 contributes to regulated meiotic progression. A cut9 mutation has been used in studies of this system, and one historical cut9 mutant was sterile. These observations support a meiotic requirement for an intact Cut9-containing APC/C, but there is insufficient evidence for a unique Cut9-specific meiotic biochemical activity. (yamashita1999fissionyeastapccyclosome pages 15-16, vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

## 5. Cellular and intracomplex localization

The strongest localization evidence is **within the APC/C itself**. At **27 Å cryo-EM resolution**, Cut9, Cut23, and Nuc2 were assigned to a clustered TPR region around the particle’s upper lip, spatially separated from the APC2–APC11–APC10 catalytic region. The overall fission-yeast particle is an asymmetric triangular assembly with a central cavity and horn-like projection. This organization supports a model in which the TPR lobe provides an interaction framework while the catalytic module performs ubiquitin transfer. (ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 9-10, ohi2007structuralorganizationof pages 1-2)

By contrast, the retrieved primary literature does not establish a definitive Cut9-specific intracellular localization throughout the cell cycle. It is reasonable to state that Cut9 acts **intracellularly as part of mitotic and meiotic APC/C**, but claims of exclusive nuclear, spindle-pole-body, kinetochore, chromosome, or cytosolic localization would exceed the direct evidence reviewed here.

## 6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

No 2023–2024 paper retrieved here directly redefines *S. pombe* Cut9/P41889. The recent literature instead strengthens the conserved APC/C framework in which Cut9 functions.

A 2024 comparison of budding-yeast and human cryo-EM structures confirmed a conserved overall APC/C architecture but uncovered species-specific differences in coactivator-mediated E2 recruitment, APC1 autoinhibition, and phosphorylation-dependent activation. Consequently, detailed human or budding-yeast regulatory mechanisms should not automatically be transferred to fission-yeast Cut9 even though the architectural scaffold is conserved. **Vazquez-Fernandez et al., October 2024**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.100821. (vazquezfernandez2024acomparativestudy pages 1-2)

High-resolution human APC/C structures published in **November 2024** reached **2.9 Å** for APC/C–CDH1–EMI1 and **3.2 Å** for apo-APC/C, clarifying disordered regulatory regions and a zinc-stabilized APC2 module. These data refine understanding of the holoenzyme but are not direct structural measurements of P41889. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-54398-5.

Recent spatial work in human cells showed that nucleosome binding can concentrate cyclin B1 with APC/C at mitotic chromosomes; mutation of a cyclin-B1 arginine anchor delayed degradation and produced aneuploidy. This raises a testable question about spatial substrate capture by fission-yeast APC/C but does not establish Cut9 localization at chromosomes. **Cirillo et al., August 2024**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s44318-024-00194-2.

Likewise, 2024 biochemical work showed that the mitotic checkpoint complex stabilizes Cdc20 association with APC/C, refining how checkpoint inhibition and eventual anaphase activation are coordinated. This is authoritative conserved context rather than Cut9-specific evidence. **Sitry-Shevah et al., September 2024**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2413089121.

## 7. Applications and real-world implementation

### Experimental applications

Cut9 is principally a **research tool and mechanistic model**, not a clinical target in fission yeast. Temperature-sensitive alleles such as cut9-665 permit conditional APC/C disruption, metaphase arrest, analysis of complex stability, checkpoint recovery, chromosome segregation, and genetic suppressor screens. Tagged Cut9 has also served as an affinity handle for monitoring APC/C assembly and MCC/Slp1 occupancy. These applications exploit Cut9’s essential scaffold function rather than enzymatic activity. (berry1999theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 7-8, bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 1-2)

The Cut9–Hcn1 structure provides a tractable model for TPR-mediated assembly, homodimerization, and small-subunit stabilization. It also supplies experimentally resolved constraints for comparative modeling of APC6 orthologs and interpretation of mutations in conserved APC/C scaffolds. (zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 2-4, zhang2010theapccsubunit pages 6-7)

### Translational relevance

The direct translational target is the conserved APC/C pathway, not yeast Cut9 itself. APC/C inhibition or activation is being explored in cancer models because altered mitotic exit can promote cell death or reverse drug resistance. However, such applications should not be presented as implemented therapies based on P41889. The fission-yeast Cut9 system instead offers a genetically precise platform for dissecting mechanisms that may inform later work on human APC6 and the APC/C.

## 8. Evidence-weighted annotation

**Recommended primary annotation:**

> Essential tetratricopeptide-repeat scaffold subunit Apc6/Cdc16 of the *S. pombe* anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome. Cut9 stabilizes the APC/C TPR assembly and mediates inter-subunit contacts required for holo-complex integrity. The Cut9-containing APC/C, activated by Slp1/Cdc20 or Ste9/Cdh1, catalyzes ubiquitin-dependent degradation of Cut2/securin and Cdc13/cyclin B to initiate anaphase and promote mitotic exit.

**Evidence confidence:**

- **Very high:** identity as Cut9/Cdc16/Apc6; APC/C membership; 14-TPR scaffold architecture; Cut9–Hcn1 interaction; requirement for APC/C integrity.
- **High:** essential role in metaphase–anaphase progression and complex-level control of Cut2/Cdc13 degradation.
- **Moderate:** detailed placement within the TPR lobe and regulatory effects mediated through neighboring APC/C subunits.
- **Limited:** exclusive intracellular localization, unique Cut9 substrate recognition, direct catalytic activity, or a Cut9-specific meiotic reaction.

Overall, the evidence supports a precise structural-functional interpretation: **Cut9 is the load-bearing TPR scaffold of an essential cell-cycle ubiquitin ligase, not an autonomous enzyme.**
