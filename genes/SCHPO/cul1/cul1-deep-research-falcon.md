# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe cul1/pcu1* (UniProt O13790)

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified. **O13790 is the fission-yeast Cullin-1 protein Pcu1/Cul1**, encoded by *cul1* (synonym *pcu1*; ORF SPAC17G6.12) in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*. The literature identifies Pcu1 as the Cdc53/CUL1-family component of SCF ubiquitin ligases. It is not the distinct fission-yeast cullins Pcu3/Cul3 or Pcu4/Cul4, and findings about mammalian CUL1 are relevant only as conserved mechanistic context—not as organism-specific evidence. In particular, Pcu1 has the CUL1-type N-terminal Skp1-binding architecture that Pcu4 lacks, and it assembles experimentally with Psh1/Skp1, Pip1/Rbx1, and F-box proteins. These observations agree with the supplied cullin-family and cullin-homology domain annotations. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, liu2003cop9signalosomesubunitsand pages 7-8)

**Primary annotation:** Pcu1 is an essential, predominantly nuclear **cullin scaffold for SCF/CRL1 E3 ubiquitin ligases**. It does not catalyze a standalone chemical reaction and therefore has no intrinsic small-molecule substrate specificity. Instead, it organizes substrate-receptor and ubiquitin-transfer modules; interchangeable F-box proteins determine which phosphorylated regulatory proteins are recognized and polyubiquitylated for proteasomal degradation. Its best-supported physiological functions are cell-cycle control, replication licensing/ploidy control, mitotic entry, and cadmium-stress adaptation. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, qiu2018fboxproteinspof3 pages 4-5, harrison2005scfpof1‐ubiquitinandits pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

### Correct protein

Direct *S. pombe* work calls this protein **Pcu1p**, **Cul1**, or the fission-yeast homolog of budding-yeast Cdc53 and metazoan CUL1. Endogenous co-immunoprecipitation recovered Pcu1 with the expected SCF components Psh1—the *S. pombe* Skp1 homolog—and Pip1—the Hrt1/Rbx1/Roc1 homolog. Pop1 and Pop2 F-box proteins were recovered in the same system, and size fractionation placed the heterooligomeric assembly at approximately **500 kDa**. This biochemical identity strongly supports the UniProt assignment rather than relying only on symbol similarity. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4)

### Exclusion of similarly named or related proteins

* **Pcu3/Cul3 is different:** deletion causes severe growth retardation rather than the lethality reported for *pcu1* deletion; its characterized biology includes a separate cullin ligase and stress-response context. (zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 9-11, zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 1-2)
* **Pcu4/Cul4 is different:** Pcu4 lacks the N-terminal Skp1-binding motif characteristic of Pcu1 and instead participates in Ddb1-associated pathways, including Spd1 turnover and ribonucleotide-reductase regulation. Those Pcu4 findings should not be assigned to O13790. (liu2003cop9signalosomesubunitsand pages 7-8)
* **Metazoan CUL1 is an ortholog, not O13790:** recent structures of mammalian CRL1 complexes explain conserved mechanics but do not directly establish the atomic structure of fission-yeast Pcu1. (zhang2024proteinneddylationand pages 8-10, lin2024diversityofstructure pages 1-3)

## 2. Molecular function and biochemical mechanism

### Pcu1 is an SCF scaffold, not a catalytic enzyme

SCF denotes **Skp1–Cullin–F-box**. Pcu1 provides the elongated central scaffold. Its N-terminal portion binds the Skp1–F-box receptor module, while its C-terminal cullin-homology region binds Pip1/Rbx1, whose RING domain recruits a ubiquitin-charged E2 enzyme. The F-box protein recognizes the substrate—often after substrate phosphorylation—and positions it for ubiquitin transfer. Thus, the relevant “substrate specificity” belongs primarily to the interchangeable F-box receptor, not to Pcu1 itself. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, zhang2024proteinneddylationand pages 8-10, wang2024cullinconciliatedregulationof pages 2-4)

The current structural model portrays CRLs as flexible machines rather than rigid molecular rulers. A recent review notes that a CUL1–Skp1–F-box–Skp2 assembly can place substrate approximately **80 Å** from RBX1; RBX1 tethering and neddylation-dependent conformational changes then bring E2~ubiquitin into productive geometries. This interpretation is persuasive for Pcu1 because its family domains and core partners are conserved, but it remains a structural inference: no O13790-specific high-resolution structure was identified in this search. (lin2024diversityofstructure pages 1-3, lin2024diversityofstructure pages 3-5)

### Neddylation activates the Pcu1 system

Pcu1 is covalently modified by the ubiquitin-like protein Ned8/NEDD8. In modern CRL models, NEDD8 attaches near the cullin C-terminal RBX-binding region and rearranges the winged-helix B/RBX module, increasing conformational freedom and promoting productive E2-mediated ubiquitin transfer. The 2024 review by Zhang and colleagues summarizes structures of unmodified and neddylated CUL1 assemblies, including substrate-bound complexes, and identifies cullin neddylation as the canonical activation mechanism for CRLs. (zhang2024proteinneddylationand pages 8-10, lin2024diversityofstructure pages 1-3)

In *S. pombe*, this is supported experimentally rather than solely by homology: modified Pcu1 is detectable, neddylation is required for cullin/SCF function, and the Ned8 pathway is essential for viability. These studies establish the functional importance of Pcu1 modification, although the atomic conformational transition has not been solved directly for O13790. (zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2, zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

### COP9 signalosome and Ubp12 regulation

The COP9 signalosome (CSN) opposes constitutive cullin neddylation. In CSN-mutant fission yeast, Pcu1 accumulates in a hyperneddylated state, and Pcu1-dependent Rum1 ubiquitylation and substrate-independent ligase activity increase. CSN therefore acts as a regulator and recycling factor, not simply as an inhibitor that permanently shuts down SCF. (zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2, zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 7-9)

CSN also recruits the deubiquitylating enzyme Ubp12. This arm suppresses cullin activity and helps preserve selected substrate-receptor components, including Pop1, from autocatalytic destruction. The expert interpretation from this work is that productive SCF function requires cycles of activation, deneddylation, receptor maintenance, and complex remodeling rather than maximal constitutive Pcu1 activity. (zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2, schmidt2008regulationofprotein pages 46-49)

## 3. Established substrate-receptor modules and biological pathways

### SCF–Pop1/Pop2: Rum1, Cdc18, G1/S control, and ploidy

The strongest biochemical substrate evidence concerns the CDK inhibitor **Rum1**. Pcu1, Psh1/Skp1, Pip1/Rbx1, Pop1, and Pop2 form an approximately **500-kDa** complex. Immunopurified SCF–Pop1/Pop2 produces high-molecular-weight, polyubiquitylated Rum1 in vitro. Recognition depends on phosphorylated Rum1, including phosphorylation at **Ser58 and Thr62**. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4)

Pop1 and Pop2 are also required for efficient destruction of **Cdc18**, a DNA-replication initiation/licensing factor. Loss of either receptor causes Rum1 accumulation, cell-cycle defects, and polyploidy; Cdc18 turnover is supported strongly by genetic and degradation evidence, although the retrieved evidence is less biochemically complete than for Rum1. Consequently, this Pcu1 module couples SCF-mediated proteolysis to G1/S progression, replication licensing, and maintenance of normal ploidy. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4)

Pop1 appears to provide the principal receptor function in the Pop1–Pop2 heterocomplex: its F-box is required, whereas Pop2 can be recruited through Pop1. Nevertheless, individual Pop1- and Pop2-containing SCFs also show ligase activity, implying combinatorial receptor assemblies and additional unidentified substrates. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

### SCF–Pof3/Pof1: Wee1 degradation and mitotic entry

Pof3 and Pof1 form partly redundant SCF receptor modules that target the inhibitory kinase **Wee1**, thereby promoting timely G2/M entry. Both proteins associate with SCF core components and physically associate with Wee1; Pof3 has the stronger functional contribution. Impaired receptor or Skp1 function increases sensitivity to Wee1 overexpression and produces elongated cells with increased division size, consistent with delayed mitosis. The 2018 study used approximately **20-fold Wee1 overexpression** and quantified more than **100 septated cells per genotype** in division-size analyses. (qiu2018fboxproteinspof3 pages 4-5)

This finding refines the annotation beyond a broad “cell-cycle role”: Pcu1 supports degradation of a negative regulator of Cdc2/CDK activity through specific F-box receptors, thereby controlling the timing of mitotic commitment. (qiu2018fboxproteinspof3 pages 4-5)

### SCF–Pof1: Zip1 and cadmium adaptation

Pof1 recruits the transcription factor **Zip1** to a Pcu1-containing SCF during cadmium stress. Zip1 is stabilized in *pof1* mutants, binds Pof1 in a phosphorylation-dependent manner, and is ubiquitylated in vivo. Genome-wide expression analysis linked Zip1 to many cadmium-induced genes, while *zip1* mutants lost viability during cadmium exposure. Cadmium also elevates Zip1 abundance and induces growth arrest with reduced cell size. (harrison2005scfpof1‐ubiquitinandits pages 1-2)

This is an important example in which SCF-dependent turnover supports environmental adaptation rather than simply eliminating an inhibitor. Controlled Zip1 abundance shapes a transcriptional and growth-arrest program needed for survival under metal stress. (harrison2005scfpof1‐ubiquitinandits pages 1-2)

### Other SCF-associated processes

Pcu1-dependent SCFs have been connected through their F-box components to genome integrity, telomere function, and cell separation. However, the evidence retrieved here is strongest for receptor-level genetic associations and weaker for a defined Pcu1-dependent substrate in these pathways. For example, Pop1 mutants show chromosome-loss and UV-sensitivity phenotypes, and Pof6/Skp1 is essential for cell separation, but a precise Pcu1 substrate and degradation mechanism were not established in the inspected evidence. These should therefore be treated as plausible SCF functions rather than annotations of equal confidence to Rum1, Wee1, or Zip1. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 5-6)

## 4. Cellular localization

Pcu1 has been reported in the **nucleus**, consistent with its best-established substrates Rum1 and Cdc18, which are primarily nuclear. Pop1 is predominantly or exclusively nuclear, whereas Pop2, Pcu1, and other core components can support assemblies whose components are found in both nucleus and cytoplasm. This organization permits receptor-dependent spatial specialization: Pop1-rich complexes favor nuclear substrates, while Pop2 or other receptors may support additional cytoplasmic activity. (zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

CSN mutations altered Pcu1 neddylation and activity without detectably changing cullin localization. The regulatory effect is therefore best explained by modification state and complex dynamics, not wholesale relocalization. Overall, **nucleus/nucleoplasm is the most strongly supported functional compartment**, but Pcu1 should not be annotated as obligatorily nucleus-exclusive. (zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2, zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 9-11)

## 5. Essentiality and physiological importance

Deletion of *pcu1* is lethal, whereas deletion of *pcu3* causes severe growth retardation. This direct genetic distinction indicates that Pcu1 supplies nonredundant SCF activity required for viability. Essentiality is mechanistically consistent with the simultaneous need to regulate Rum1, Cdc18, Wee1, and likely additional cell-cycle and homeostasis substrates. (zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 9-11)

The neddylation system is likewise essential in fission yeast. However, this does not prove that every essential Ned8 function passes exclusively through Pcu1, because other cullins are also neddylated. The defensible annotation is that Pcu1 activity requires Ned8 regulation and that both Pcu1 deletion and disruption of the broader neddylation system compromise viability. (zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2, zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

## 6. Recent developments and current understanding, 2023–2024

No substantive 2023–2024 primary study focused specifically on *S. pombe* Pcu1/O13790 was identified in the retrieved literature. The organism-specific mechanistic foundation therefore remains concentrated in classic biochemical and genetic studies from 2000–2005, supplemented by the 2018 Wee1 study. Recent work mainly updates how those findings should be interpreted structurally.

Two 2024 reviews are particularly relevant:

1. **Zhang et al., April 2024, *Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy***, DOI [10.1038/s41392-024-01800-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41392-024-01800-9), integrates modern structures of CUL1–RBX1, neddylated CUL1 assemblies, substrate-bound SCFs, CSN complexes, and CAND1-mediated receptor exchange. It supports a dynamic activation/remodeling cycle rather than a static scaffold model. (zhang2024proteinneddylationand pages 8-10)
2. **Lin and Komives, October 2024, *Current Opinion in Structural Biology***, DOI [10.1016/j.sbi.2024.102879](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sbi.2024.102879), emphasizes diversity and flexibility among cullin ligases. Its key caution is that mechanisms established for one cullin cannot be transferred uncritically to every CRL. For Pcu1, the CUL1/SCF comparison is strong because the defining Skp1–F-box and RBX1 architecture is experimentally verified, but the exact conformational ensemble remains inferred. (lin2024diversityofstructure pages 1-3, lin2024diversityofstructure pages 3-5)

Thus, the latest understanding does not overturn the established Pcu1 annotation. It strengthens the mechanistic explanation: Pcu1 is a dynamically regulated platform in which receptor exchange, NEDD8-driven conformational activation, RBX1 flexibility, CSN deneddylation, and adaptor protection collectively govern substrate ubiquitylation.

## 7. Current applications and real-world relevance

### Experimental applications

Pcu1 and its regulators make *S. pombe* a useful model for:

* dissecting SCF substrate recognition using genetically tractable F-box receptors;
* studying phosphorylation-dependent degradation, as illustrated by Rum1 and Zip1;
* linking protein turnover to replication licensing, ploidy, and mitotic timing;
* analyzing reversible cullin neddylation and COP9-signalosome function;
* testing how stress-responsive transcription is coupled to proteolysis. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, harrison2005scfpof1‐ubiquitinandits pages 1-2, zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2)

Pcu1 should not itself be described as a current clinical target in fission yeast. Its translational relevance is comparative: conserved cullin neddylation and CRL control are actively investigated in human disease and drug development. The 2024 neddylation review describes disease-associated pathway activation and small-molecule strategies targeting the neddylation cascade, but extrapolation from human therapy to yeast Pcu1 must remain explicit. (zhang2024proteinneddylationand pages 8-10)

### Annotation and assay implications

For functional screens, loss-of-function approaches require conditional alleles, regulated depletion, or partial pathway perturbation because *pcu1* deletion is lethal. Substrate-turnover studies should monitor both receptor identity and Pcu1 neddylation state; measuring total Pcu1 abundance alone is unlikely to capture SCF activity. Compartment-resolved assays are also appropriate because receptor composition can separate nuclear from cytoplasmic substrate pools. These are mechanistic recommendations derived from the direct essentiality, localization, and complex-assembly evidence. (zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 9-11, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4)

## 8. Evidence-based final annotation

**Recommended concise annotation:**

> *cul1/pcu1* encodes the essential Cullin-1 scaffold of *S. pombe* SCF ubiquitin ligases. Pcu1 binds Psh1/Skp1–F-box substrate-receptor modules at its N-terminal region and Pip1/Rbx1 at its C-terminal cullin-homology region, enabling E2-dependent polyubiquitylation and proteasomal turnover of regulatory proteins. Its activity is stimulated by Ned8/NEDD8 conjugation and cycled by the COP9 signalosome/Ubp12 system. Established receptor-defined targets include Rum1 and Cdc18 through Pop1/Pop2, Wee1 through Pof3/Pof1, and Zip1 through Pof1. These pathways regulate G1/S progression, replication licensing and ploidy, G2/M entry, and cadmium-stress adaptation. Pcu1 functions prominently in the nucleus but participates in receptor-dependent complexes with broader nucleocytoplasmic distributions. (zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, qiu2018fboxproteinspof3 pages 4-5, harrison2005scfpof1‐ubiquitinandits pages 1-2)

## 9. Key limitations and research gaps

* No Pcu1/O13790-specific atomic structure was identified; structural descriptions rely on strongly conserved CUL1-family complexes. (zhang2024proteinneddylationand pages 8-10, lin2024diversityofstructure pages 1-3)
* The substrate repertoire is incomplete. Individual Pop1, Pop2, and other F-box assemblies possess activity toward substrates that remain unknown. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)
* Localization is receptor- and complex-dependent; “nuclear” is well supported but should not be interpreted as exclusively nuclear. (zhou2003fissionyeastcop9signalosome pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)
* Recent 2023–2024 literature advances general CRL structural biology and neddylation more than the organism-specific annotation of Pcu1. New endogenous proteomics, degron mapping, conditional depletion, and Pcu1-focused structural work would materially improve annotation confidence.
