# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* **pop1/ste16** (UniProt P87060)

## Executive summary

The requested target is correctly identified. In *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*, **ste16+ is sequence-identical to pop1+**, and the encoded product is an approximately 82-kDa F-box/WD40 protein. Primary literature describes a central F-box and seven C-terminal WD repeats, matching the supplied UniProt P87060 and InterPro F-box/WD40 annotations. This report therefore concerns fission-yeast Pop1/Ste16—not unrelated proteins called POP1 in other organisms and not *S. pombe* Wat1/Pop3. [Maekawa et al., February 1998, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002940050305); [Wolf et al., April 1999, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0960-9822(99)80165-1). (maekawa1998theste16wdrepeat pages 7-8, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2, maekawa1998theste16wdrepeat pages 1-2)

**Primary function:** Pop1 is not an enzyme with an independently catalyzed reaction. It is a predominantly nuclear **substrate-recognition/adaptor component of Skp1–Cullin–F-box (SCF) E3 ubiquitin-ligase assemblies**. Its best-supported physiological substrates are the CDK inhibitor Rum1 and DNA-replication initiator Cdc18. Pop1-dependent polyubiquitylation commits these proteins to 26S-proteasomal degradation, thereby coordinating CDK activity, replication licensing, nutrient-responsive cell-cycle control, and genome-ploidy maintenance. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7, kominami1997fissionyeastwdrepeat pages 9-10, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5)

## 1. Identity, nomenclature, and architecture

The identity evidence is unusually strong for a historically named yeast gene: Maekawa and colleagues reported that the **ste16+ nucleotide sequence is identical to pop1+**. Their Ste16 protein was approximately 82 kDa and WD-repeat-containing; Wolf and colleagues subsequently explicitly identified Pop1 as Ste16p and described a **central F-box plus seven C-terminal WD repeats**. These observations align with the supplied annotations IPR001810/IPR036047 (F-box) and IPR015943/IPR020472/IPR019775 (WD40-related). (maekawa1998theste16wdrepeat pages 7-8, maekawa1998theste16wdrepeat pages 1-2, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

Functionally, the F-box is the SCF-assembly module, whereas the WD-repeat β-propeller-type region is consistent with protein-recognition functions. Deleting Pop1’s F-box or replacing it with the Pop2 F-box largely abolished complementation of Rum1-turnover and ploidy defects, demonstrating that the Pop1 F-box is not merely a sequence annotation but is functionally required. A high-resolution Pop1 structure or atomic structure of a Pop1–substrate complex was not identified, so the exact substrate-contact residues remain unresolved. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 7-9)

## 2. Molecular mechanism and complex composition

Pop1 participates in SCF complexes containing the cullin **Pcu1**, Skp1 ortholog **Psh1**, RING component **Pip1**, and, in one major assembly, the related F-box/WD protein **Pop2/Sud1**. Endogenous co-immunoprecipitation recovered all five proteins, and gel filtration placed the Pop1–Pop2-containing assembly at approximately **500 kDa**. Its precise subunit stoichiometry was not determined. [Seibert et al., 7 August 2002, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2091-3-22). (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

Pop1 and Pop2 form heterooligomeric complexes, but each can also occur in a separately active SCF assembly. In a Δpop2 background, immunopurified Pop1 complexes retained ubiquitin-ligase activity, indicating that Pop1 is not simply an inert bridge for Pop2. Conversely, heteromeric Pop1–Pop2 appears especially important for Rum1 recognition. Their interaction maps to N-terminal regions rather than requiring either WD-repeat region; Pop2 residues 1–241 and a Pop1 segment between residues 228–402 were implicated. The exact architecture was inferred from truncation/co-immunoprecipitation experiments rather than solved structurally. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 7-9)

Mechanistically, Pop1 supplies substrate selection and SCF organization rather than catalytic chemistry. In reconstituted assays, immunopurified complexes generated polyubiquitylated products in the presence of E1, UBC3 E2, ATP, and lysine-containing ubiquitin; catalytic-cysteine-mutant UBC3 or lysine-free ubiquitin served as negative controls. Thus, ubiquitin activation and transfer are performed by the E1/E2/RING-SCF machinery, while Pop1 acts as a substrate receptor/adaptor. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5)

## 3. Substrate specificity

### Rum1

Rum1 is a Cdc2/CDK inhibitor that helps establish low-CDK G1 physiology. The Pop1–Pop2 SCF binds phosphorylated Rum1 and supports its polyubiquitylation in vitro. Rum1 was phosphorylated by Cig1–Cdc2, with **Ser58 and Thr62** implicated in degradation-linked phosphorecognition. This is the clearest available definition of Pop1 substrate specificity: Pop1-containing SCF preferentially recognizes an appropriately phosphorylated cell-cycle regulator rather than an unmodified small-molecule substrate. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4)

The quantitative effect is substantial. Rum1’s half-life increased from approximately **20 minutes in wild type to more than 100 minutes in pop1 or pop2 mutants**. Wild-type Pop1 restored turnover, whereas F-box-deleted Pop1 did not and could further stabilize Rum1. These results link the structural F-box requirement directly to substrate degradation. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 7-9)

### Cdc18

Cdc18 is an S-phase initiation/licensing regulator. In pop1 mutants, Cdc18 accumulated, its ubiquitinated forms were lost, and Pop1 was shown to associate with Cdc18 in vivo. Pop1 and Pop2 also formed complexes that bound and directed Cdc18 proteolysis; Cdc18 binding to Pop2 required Pop1 in the overexpression/co-purification system. Together, these genetic, biochemical, and physical-interaction data establish Cdc18 as a second physiological substrate. [Kominami & Toda, June 1997, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/gad.11.12.1548); [Wolf et al., April 1999, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0960-9822(99)80165-1). (wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2, kominami1997fissionyeastwdrepeat pages 9-10)

The strongest mechanistic evidence therefore supports **Rum1 and Cdc18**. Other nuclear substrates are plausible because independent SCF-Pop1 assemblies retain activity, and pop1 mutants have additional chromosome-loss or stress phenotypes, but these should not be promoted to established direct substrates without binding and ubiquitylation evidence. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

## 4. Cellular localization

Pop1 is **predominantly nuclear**. This conclusion was supported through low-expression GFP fusion imaging, immunofluorescence of an endogenous C-terminal 13×Myc fusion, and nuclear/cytoplasmic fractionation using PCNA and tubulin controls. Pop2, Pcu1, Psh1, and Pip1 were detected in both nucleus and cytoplasm, whereas Pop1 was chiefly nuclear. Rum1 and Cdc18 are likewise primarily nuclear, making the nucleus the best-supported site of Pop1’s physiological substrate-recognition function. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5)

Pop1’s distribution did not require Pop2, and Pop2’s distribution did not require Pop1. This supports the existence of independent assemblies rather than obligatory co-transport of the two receptors. The localization evidence applies to the tested vegetative conditions; dynamic redistribution under every nutrient or mating condition has not been comprehensively established. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 7-9)

## 5. Biological pathways

### Cell-cycle and genome-ploidy control

Pop1 controls two complementary barriers to rereplication:

1. **Rum1 degradation relieves CDK inhibition**, helping establish the appropriate rise in CDK activity.
2. **Cdc18 degradation limits replication-initiation competence**, preventing relicensing or repeated S phases.

Loss of Pop1 therefore permits accumulation of both a CDK inhibitor and a replication initiator, uncoupling DNA replication from normal mitotic progression. pop1 mutants become polyploid, and eliminating rum1 suppresses the high-ploidy phenotype, whereas Cdc18 accumulation remains Rum1-independent. This places the two substrates in related but distinguishable branches of the phenotype. (kominami1997fissionyeastwdrepeat pages 9-10)

The related pop2 deletion illustrates the magnitude of the system: approximately **50% of Δpop2 cells exceeded normal 2C DNA content after 16 hours in rich medium**, while Δpop1 produced still stronger rereplication and Rum1-accumulation phenotypes. The 50% value must not be misattributed directly to Δpop1. (wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

### Nutrient sensing, G1/G0 entry, and sexual differentiation

Under nitrogen starvation, wild-type haploid fission yeast normally arrests in G1 and becomes competent for mating and sporulation. ste16/pop1 disruption instead caused arrest in G2, marked Rum1 protein accumulation without a corresponding apparent increase in rum1 mRNA, and efficient diploidization after nutritional upshift. Deleting rum1 completely suppressed the reported diploidization phenotype, showing that excessive Rum1 is a central causal mediator rather than merely a correlated marker. [Maekawa et al., February 1998, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002940050305). (maekawa1998theste16wdrepeat pages 7-8, maekawa1998theste16wdrepeat pages 1-2)

Accordingly, Pop1/Ste16 connects nutrient status to cell-cycle transitions required for entry into G0 and sexual development. The safest mechanistic interpretation is that Pop1-dependent proteolysis tunes Rum1—and probably Cdc18—to coordinate starvation-induced arrest with replication control. Not every mating or sporulation defect has been assigned to one substrate, so broader developmental effects should not all be attributed exclusively to Rum1. (maekawa1998theste16wdrepeat pages 7-8, kominami1997fissionyeastwdrepeat pages 9-10)

### SCF regulation

The Pop1 pathway also depends on the normal SCF regulatory system. NEDD8 modification of Pcu1 promotes SCF activity, and work on fission-yeast NEDD8 showed its importance for SCF function and Rum1 control. In Pop1/Pop2 complex assays, increased Nedd8-modified Pcu1 in csn5 mutants correlated with enhanced Rum1 ubiquitylation. Thus, Pop1 should be understood as the receptor arm of a regulated cullin-RING ligase, not as a stand-alone degradation factor. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5)

## 6. Evidence quality and expert interpretation

The annotation is supported by convergent methods: mutant genetics and epistasis, flow cytometry, protein half-life measurements, immunoblotting, in-vivo association, affinity purification, endogenous co-immunoprecipitation, gel filtration, microscopy/fractionation, and in-vitro ubiquitylation. Particularly persuasive are the loss of Rum1/Cdc18 ubiquitinated species in pop1 mutants, physical association with Cdc18, phosphorylation-dependent Rum1 binding and ubiquitylation, and rescue failure of F-box-defective Pop1. (kominami1997fissionyeastwdrepeat pages 9-10, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 7-9)

Important limitations remain. Some early interaction studies relied on overexpression because endogenous Pop1/Pop2 abundance was low; the endogenous Pop1–Pop2 complex was later supported by immunoprecipitation and size fractionation. The approximately 500-kDa estimate does not specify stoichiometry. There is no retrieved atomic structure explaining phosphodegron recognition, and the physiological targets of putative Pop1-only SCFs remain unknown. Endogenous Pop1 was reported to be approximately **twofold more abundant than Pop2**, but abundance alone does not establish functional stoichiometry. (wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4)

## 7. Recent developments, applications, and present status

A 2024 authoritative review of *S. pombe* sexual differentiation retains SCF Pop1/Pop2-mediated Rum1 degradation within the accepted regulatory framework. [Kawamukai, March 2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bbb/zbae019). However, the targeted search did **not** retrieve a 2023–2024 primary paper reporting a new Pop1-specific substrate, structure, localization mechanism, or physiological application. The mechanistic annotation therefore still rests primarily on the strong 1997–2002 experimental literature rather than on a recent revision. (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 15-16, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

Pop1 currently has no direct clinical or industrial implementation evident from this literature. Its real-world scientific use is as a genetically tractable model for understanding SCF substrate recognition, phosphodegron-directed proteolysis, replication licensing, nutrient-responsive cell-cycle decisions, and mechanisms that prevent genome reduplication. Because these principles are conserved across eukaryotic cullin-RING ligases, Pop1 experiments are conceptually relevant to broader ubiquitin-system and genome-stability research, but direct equivalence to a specific human therapeutic target should not be inferred.

## Final annotation

**Pop1/Ste16 (P87060) is a predominantly nuclear F-box/WD40 substrate receptor and organizational component of fission-yeast SCF ubiquitin ligases. Its principal established function is to promote phosphorylation-dependent polyubiquitylation and proteasomal turnover of Rum1 and Cdc18. By restraining these regulators, Pop1 coordinates CDK activation with replication licensing, supports nitrogen-starvation-associated cell-cycle remodeling and sexual differentiation, and prevents rereplication and polyploidization.** It is a noncatalytic adaptor rather than an enzyme or transporter; its functional “specificity” is for regulated protein substrates, especially phosphorylated Rum1 and Cdc18.
