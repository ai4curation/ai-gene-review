| Verified identity and aliases | The target is Schizosaccharomyces pombe Pop1, also named Ste16. The reported ste16+ nucleotide sequence is identical to pop1+, consistent with UniProt P87060 and distinct from unrelated Pop1 proteins or Wat1/Pop3. | Sequence comparison in the Ste16 study and independent identification of fission-yeast Pop1 as Ste16p. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000003) | **High.** Organism, aliases, and phenotypes agree across independent primary studies. |
| Domain architecture | Pop1 is an approximately 82-kDa F-box/WD40 protein with a central F-box and seven C-terminal WD repeats. The F-box links Pop1 to the SCF core, while the WD-repeat region is consistent with protein and substrate recognition. | Primary-sequence analysis and deletion/complementation experiments showing that the Pop1 F-box is required for Rum1 degradation and ploidy control. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000009) | **High** for the architecture and F-box requirement. A structure of the Pop1-substrate interface has not been reported. |
| SCF complex | Pop1 participates in SCF assemblies with Pop2, the cullin Pcu1, Skp1 ortholog Psh1, and RING protein Pip1. A Pop1-Pop2-containing assembly migrated at approximately 500 kDa. | Endogenous co-immunoprecipitation of all five subunits, gel filtration, and biochemical assays supporting both heteromeric and separate Pop1- and Pop2-containing SCFs. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000002) | **High** for association and approximate native size. Exact subunit stoichiometry remains unresolved. |
| Primary biochemical function | Pop1 is a substrate-recognition and adaptor component of an SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase, not a catalytic enzyme. It recruits cell-cycle regulators for polyubiquitylation and subsequent degradation by the 26S proteasome. | Pop1 loss abolished detectable substrate polyubiquitylation; Pop1 associated with Cdc18 and active SCF complexes, while immunopurified complexes generated ubiquitin-dependent high-molecular-weight products. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002) | **High.** Catalytic chemistry is supplied by the E1, E2, and RING-SCF machinery; Pop1 provides substrate selection and complex organization. |
| Direct substrates | The best-supported physiological substrates are Rum1, a Cdc2/CDK inhibitor, and Cdc18, an S-phase replication-initiation factor. Their degradation restrains inappropriate cell-cycle transitions and genome rereplication. | Rum1 and Cdc18 accumulated and lacked ubiquitinated forms in pop1 mutants; Pop1 bound Cdc18 in vivo. Purified Pop1-Pop2 SCF bound phosphorylated Rum1 and promoted its polyubiquitylation in vitro. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000005) | **High** for Rum1 and Cdc18. Additional proposed nuclear substrates remain unidentified. |
| Rum1 phosphorylation dependence | CDK phosphorylation of Rum1 at Ser58 and Thr62 promotes recognition by the Pop1-Pop2 SCF and is required for efficient degradation. | Cig1-Cdc2-phosphorylated Rum1 was examined in binding and ubiquitylation assays; Ser58 and Thr62 were identified as degradation-linked sites. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000007) | **High** for phospho-dependent targeting in the tested system. Atomic details of phosphodegron recognition are unavailable. |
| Cellular localization | Pop1 is predominantly nuclear, consistent with turnover of the chiefly nuclear substrates Rum1 and Cdc18. | Low-expression GFP fusions, endogenous 13×Myc immunofluorescence, and nuclear/cytoplasmic fractionation using PCNA and tubulin controls. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005) | **High.** Pop2 and other SCF subunits also occur in the cytoplasm, but Pop1 was chiefly nuclear under the tested conditions. |
| Starvation, G1 arrest, and sexual differentiation | Pop1/Ste16 connects nutrient conditions to cell-cycle state. Following nitrogen starvation, disruption prevents normal G1 arrest, produces G2 arrest, and impairs entry into the sexual-development or G0 program; elevated Rum1 is central to this phenotype. | Nitrogen-starvation experiments, DNA-content analysis, Rum1 protein and mRNA comparison, and genetic suppression in which rum1 deletion suppressed nutritional-upshift-associated diploidization. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) | **High** for the starvation cell-cycle phenotype and Rum1 dependence. Not every mating or sporulation defect has been assigned directly to a particular Pop1 substrate. |
| Ploidy maintenance | Pop1 prevents rereplication and polyploidization by limiting Rum1 and Cdc18 abundance. High ploidy strongly depends on Rum1, whereas Cdc18 accumulation occurs independently of Rum1. | Flow cytometry, mutant epistasis, immunoblotting, ubiquitination analysis, and rescue experiments. Approximately 50% of Pop2-deficient cells exceeded normal 2C DNA content after 16 hours, while Pop1 loss produced a stronger rereplication phenotype. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004) | **High** for pathway assignment. The 50% statistic applies to Pop2-deficient cells and must not be attributed quantitatively to Pop1 loss. |
| Quantitative Rum1 stability | Rum1 half-life increases from approximately 20 minutes in wild type to more than 100 minutes in pop1 or pop2 mutants. Wild-type Pop1 restored turnover, whereas F-box-deleted Pop1 did not. | Protein half-life measurements combined with F-box deletion, complementation, flow cytometry, and Rum1 immunoblotting. (pqac-00000006) | **High** under the reported strain and assay conditions. Half-life may vary with growth state and phosphorylation. |
| Recent evidence gap, 2023-2024 | A 2024 review retains SCF Pop1/Pop2-mediated Rum1 degradation in the accepted sexual-differentiation model, but the retrieved 2023-2024 literature contained no new Pop1-specific primary mechanistic finding. The core annotation still rests mainly on studies from 1997-2002. | Targeted recent-literature search and the 2024 review's synthesis and reference trail. (pqac-00000010) | **Moderate.** Failure to retrieve a recent study does not prove none exists; unrelated proteins sharing the Pop1 symbol were excluded. |
