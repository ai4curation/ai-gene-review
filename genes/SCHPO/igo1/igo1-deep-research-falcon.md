# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* **mug134/igo1** (UniProt P79058)

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is the fission-yeast protein **Igo1**, formerly designated **Mug134** (ORF **SPAC10F6.16**), from *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* strain 972. Organism-specific literature consistently identifies Igo1 as the single fission-yeast **Endosulfine** component of the Greatwall–Endosulfine–PP2A-B55 pathway. No conflicting use of *mug134* for another protein was found. The mapping to UniProt P79058 and the InterPro/Pfam Endosulfine-domain annotations derive from the supplied UniProt record; the literature independently corroborates the organism, Igo1 identity, Endosulfine family, and pathway function (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9).

Igo1 is **not an enzyme or transporter**. Its primary function is that of a phosphorylation-regulated phosphatase inhibitor. When nutrients become limiting, Greatwall-family kinase activity phosphorylates Igo1—most importantly at **Ser64**—and activated Igo1 inhibits **PP2A-B55**, whose B55 regulatory subunit is Pab1. This switch couples reduced TORC1 signaling to cell-cycle remodeling, TORC2–Gad8 activation, sexual differentiation, and entry into G0 quiescence (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 123-125, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 99-102, perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9).

## 1. Identity and molecular classification

### Verification

- **Protein:** mRNA stability protein Mug134 / initiation-of-G0 protein 1
- **Current gene name:** *igo1*
- **Synonym:** *mug134*
- **ORF:** SPAC10F6.16
- **UniProt:** P79058
- **Organism:** *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* strain 972 / ATCC 24843
- **Family/domain:** Endosulfine family; Endosulphine/Endosulfine domain according to the supplied UniProt–InterPro/Pfam annotation

The functional literature usually uses **Igo1**, not Mug134. It specifically treats Igo1 as the *S. pombe* Endosulfine in the conserved Greatwall–Endosulfine–PP2A-B55 module. Thus, studies on budding-yeast Igo1/Igo2 or animal ENSA/ARPP19 are relevant only as evolutionary context and were not substituted for evidence about this target (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9).

The historical description “mRNA stability protein” is incomplete relative to current evidence. The best-supported primary molecular annotation is **phosphorylation-dependent inhibitor of PP2A-B55/Pab1**. Effects on RNA abundance, translation, and stability are downstream consequences of this phosphatase switch rather than evidence that Igo1 is an RNA-binding enzyme.

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### Greatwall-dependent inhibition of PP2A-B55

Under nitrogen-rich conditions, TORC1 activity is high, the Greatwall–Endosulfine switch is relatively inactive, and PP2A-B55/Pab1 activity remains high. Nitrogen limitation lowers TORC1 signaling, activates Greatwall-family input, and leads to phosphorylation of Igo1. Phosphorylated Igo1 then strongly suppresses PP2A-B55 activity (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9).

**Ppk18 phosphorylation of Igo1 at Ser64** is the central activating event supported by the mechanistic literature. Loss of *igo1* causes abnormally high PP2A-B55 activity under nitrogen-poor or nitrogen-free conditions, while genetically reducing PP2A-B55 can rescue multiple *igo1Δ* phenotypes. This genetic epistasis strongly supports PP2A-B55 as Igo1’s principal functional effector (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 123-125, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 99-102, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 107-110, moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 10-13).

There is not yet a retrieved purified-complex structure, dissociation constant, or definitive direct Igo1–Pab1 binding measurement. Accordingly, the strongest formulation is that phosphorylated Igo1 **functionally inhibits the PP2A-B55 holoenzyme**; claims about a structurally defined binding interface would exceed the evidence.

### Additional phosphorylation

Cdk1–Cyclin B phosphorylates Igo1 in vitro. Mass spectrometry detected phosphorylation at Cdk1-consensus residues **Ser31, Ser89, and Ser102**; Ser76 and Ser115 corresponded to PKA-consensus sites. An Igo1-4A protein carrying S31A, S89A, S102A, and S118A substitutions was not phosphorylated by Cdk1–Cyclin B in vitro and produced nutrient-dependent cell-size phenotypes. These findings suggest additional cell-cycle modulation, although their physiological importance is less established than Ppk18-dependent Ser64 phosphorylation (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 123-125).

## 3. Pathway position and biological processes

### TORC1–Greatwall–Igo1–PP2A-B55 signaling

The pathway can be summarized as:

**Nitrogen starvation → TORC1 downregulation → Ppk18/Cek1 Greatwall activation → Igo1-Ser64 phosphorylation → PP2A-B55/Pab1 inhibition.**

A 2024 review also identifies Ppk31 as part of the broader Greatwall input controlling this switch. In nutrient-rich cells the switch is off and PP2A-B55 is active; under poor nitrogen, phosphorylated Endosulfine suppresses the phosphatase (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9).

PP2A-B55 counteracts Cdk-dependent phosphorylation. Its inhibition therefore permits mitotic entry at lower Cdk activity after TORC1 downregulation. *igo1Δ* cells do not execute the normal acceleration of G2/M or starvation-associated reduction in division size, placing Igo1 between nutrient sensing and the core cell-cycle machinery (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 99-102, perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5).

### TORC2–Gad8 activation

Igo1 also mediates crosstalk from TORC1 to TORC2. PP2A/Pab1 associates with Gad8 and opposes phosphorylation of Gad8 at **Ser546**. Inhibiting PP2A-B55 through Igo1 permits accumulation of Gad8-S546 phosphorylation and greater TORC2 output. Conversely, *igo1Δ* lowers both Gad8 abundance and S546 phosphorylation (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 9-11).

This is an indirect signaling role: Igo1 does not phosphorylate Gad8. Rather, it prevents PP2A-B55 from reversing the phosphorylation state needed for TORC2–Gad8 signaling.

### G1 arrest, quiescence, and differentiation

During nitrogen starvation, the Igo1 switch promotes extension or arrest in G1, sexual differentiation, sporulation, and entry into quiescence. Cells lacking Igo1 fail to arrest normally in G1, show impaired starvation-induced cell-size reduction, and have reduced sporulation. The pathway consequently acts as a nutrient-responsive decision module rather than merely a constitutive mitotic regulator (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 99-102, perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5).

For scale, reported wild-type cell lengths were approximately **16.74 ± 0.48 μm** in nitrogen-rich medium and **14.29 ± 0.52 μm** under nitrogen-poor conditions. Igo1-pathway mutants failed to reproduce the normal nutrient-dependent shortening, although exact values for every mutant were not available in the retrieved text (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 99-102).

## 4. Major 2023–2024 development: control of codon-biased translation

The principal recent advance is the demonstration that Igo1-dependent PP2A-B55 inhibition sustains a specialized translational program during entry into quiescence. The definitive peer-reviewed paper was published in **Nature Communications in December 2024**, following a December 2023 preprint: del Dedo et al., “The Greatwall-Endosulfine-PP2A/B55 pathway regulates entry into quiescence by enhancing translation of Elongator-tunable transcripts,” DOI [10.1038/s41467-024-55004-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55004-4) (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 9-11).

The proposed chain is:

1. Nitrogen starvation suppresses TORC1.
2. Ppk18/Cek1–Igo1 inhibits PP2A-B55/Pab1.
3. Reduced phosphatase activity permits TORC2-dependent Gad8-S546 phosphorylation.
4. Gad8 signaling promotes Elongator activity, including through inhibition of Gsk3.
5. Elongator and associated tRNA-modification systems support **mcm5s2U34** and **t6A37** modifications in the anticodon loop, especially affecting decoding by tRNA-Lys-UUU.
6. These modifications preferentially sustain translation of transcripts enriched in **AAA**, rather than synonymous **AAG**, lysine codons (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 9-11, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2).

This mechanism was supported by unusually strong codon-recoding evidence. In *igo1Δ* cells, polysome-to-monosome ratios were **78% of wild type in nitrogen-rich medium** and only **27% after four hours of starvation**. Translation fell to approximately **40% for *rap1*** and **30% for *ctu1***, whereas low-AAA-bias controls such as *fil1* and *act1* were minimally affected. Replacing lysine codons with synonymous AAG codons in *rap1* and *ctu1* rescued protein production, directly implicating codon composition rather than a nonspecific collapse in transcription (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 9-11).

The mutant showed reduced mcm5s2U34 and a **20–25% reduction in t6A37** after quiescence induction. It was sensitive to paromomycin, consistent with impaired decoding fidelity. Partial rescue by *gsk3Δ* or an Elp4-S114A allele further connected TORC2/Gad8 signaling to Elongator control (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 9-11).

## 5. Telomere, subtelomere, chromatin, and autophagy effects

AAA-enriched Igo1-dependent transcripts include genes encoding the telomere-associated proteins Rap1 and Lem2, the subtelomeric regulator Sgo2, SHREC components Clr2 and Clr3, TOR regulators, and tRNA-modification proteins such as Trm112 and Ctu1. Their defective translation explains several apparently pleiotropic *igo1Δ* phenotypes (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-7).

After nitrogen withdrawal, *igo1Δ* cells showed reduced Rap1, Lem2, Sgo2, Clr2, and Clr3, detachment of telomeres from the nuclear envelope, and derepression of a group of subtelomeric genes by **more than tenfold**. Repressing Pab1 restored Rap1 abundance and telomere attachment, demonstrating that these effects arise from excessive PP2A-B55 activity rather than Igo1 being a physical telomere component (moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 7-10, moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 10-13).

Igo1-associated and Pab1-associated proteomic datasets also implicated chromatin-remodeling, histone-modification, translation, ribosome, aminoacylation, and tRNA-modification proteins. Some proposed chromatin mechanisms remain provisional: for example, Pab1 proteomics detected Mst2 with **52.6% sequence coverage**, but reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation failed, so a direct PP2A-B55–Mst2 interaction was not established (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 107-110).

Igo1 loss also reduces autophagic flux, and deletion of the PP2A catalytic subunit gene *ppa2* rescues this defect. This places autophagy among the PP2A-dependent starvation responses controlled by the switch, but it remains downstream of Igo1’s primary phosphatase-inhibitory function (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 125-126).

## 6. Cellular localization

The available literature supports an **intracellular** signaling role but does not establish a specific steady-state localization for Igo1 itself. The retrieved studies report nuclear and telomeric localization changes for downstream proteins, not direct Igo1 microscopy or fractionation. It would therefore be inappropriate to annotate P79058 as specifically nuclear, cytosolic, membrane-associated, or telomeric on present evidence (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2).

Functionally, Igo1 must act where it can regulate PP2A-B55/Pab1, but the relevant complex may be spatially dynamic. A fluorescently tagged endogenous Igo1 analyzed across nitrogen-rich growth, early starvation, G1 arrest, and quiescence would be needed for a confident compartment-level annotation.

## 7. Applications and broader significance

There is no established clinical, agricultural, or deployed industrial application specific to *S. pombe igo1*. Its current application is as a **mechanistic model** for understanding how nutrient sensing is coupled to phosphatase control, cell-cycle transitions, quiescence, tRNA epitranscriptomic modification, codon-biased translation, chromatin maintenance, and aging.

The pathway is evolutionarily conserved: fungal Igo proteins correspond functionally to animal ENSA/ARPP19, while PP2A-B55, TOR, and Greatwall-family signaling are broadly conserved. This makes *S. pombe* Igo1 useful for dissecting general principles of phosphatase regulation. Nevertheless, observations in fission yeast should not be presented as direct evidence for human disease mechanisms without validation in mammalian systems.

## 8. Expert assessment and annotation confidence

**High-confidence primary annotation:** Igo1/Mug134 is a nutrient-responsive Endosulfine phosphoprotein that, after Greatwall-family phosphorylation at Ser64, inhibits PP2A-B55/Pab1.

**High-confidence pathway functions:** coupling of TORC1 downregulation to cell-cycle remodeling, TORC2–Gad8 activation, G1/quiescence entry, and sexual differentiation; maintenance of Elongator-dependent tRNA modification and AAA-biased translation during nitrogen starvation (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2, perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 9-11).

**Downstream rather than primary functions:** telomere attachment, subtelomeric silencing, autophagy, translational fidelity, and production of Rap1/Sgo2/Clr proteins. Igo1 is not established as a telomere protein, chromatin enzyme, or tRNA-modifying enzyme (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 7-10, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 125-126).

**Important unresolved issues:** Igo1’s direct subcellular localization, the structural basis and affinity of phospho-Igo1 inhibition of the *S. pombe* PP2A-B55 holoenzyme, the physiological integration of Cdk1/PKA phosphosites with Ser64 regulation, and whether all proposed chromatin interactors are direct.
