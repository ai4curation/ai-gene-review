Gene Description

Bub3 is the WD40-repeat spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) protein of fission yeast, a 320-residue non-enzymatic beta-propeller encoded by SPAC23H3.08c. It forms constitutive, mutually exclusive complexes with the checkpoint kinase Bub1 and with Mad3 (the BubR1 orthologue), each of which engages the propeller through a GLEBS motif. In the Bub1-Bub3 complex, Bub3 is the reader of Mph1 (Mps1)-phosphorylated MELT motifs on the outer-kinetochore scaffold Spc7 (Knl1): non-phosphorylatable Spc7 MELT mutants abolish kinetochore targeting of Bub1 and Bub3, phospho-mimetic Spc7 recruits them throughout the cell cycle without Mph1, and Bub1 loading onto phospho-mimetic Spc7 still requires Bub3. Bub1 and Bub3 are mutually dependent for kinetochore localization, and Bub3 is in turn needed for efficient kinetochore enrichment of Mad3 and of the Mad1-Mad2 complex, so it assembles the kinetochore signaling layer from which the mitotic checkpoint complex (Mad3-Mad2-Slp1, with which Bub3 co-immunoprecipitates) is generated to inhibit APC/C-Slp1 and delay anaphase. Fission yeast is unusual in that bub3 deletion does not abolish the checkpoint: most bub3 cells still mount a Mad2-dependent arrest when microtubules are depolymerized, because a soluble Spc7-Mph1-Bub1-Mad1 signaling pool can operate without kinetochore enrichment. Instead, Bub3 tunes the checkpoint in both directions: when the Bub1-Bub3 complex is not docked on phosphorylated Spc7 it restrains premature Bub1-Mad1 complex formation and ectopic nucleoplasmic signaling, and docking of Bub1-Bub3 on Spc7 promotes checkpoint silencing and timely cyclin B destruction once attachments are corrected. Independently of shugoshin, Bub3 with Bub1 also promotes the conversion of mono-oriented chromosomes to bipolar attachment after chromosomes have been retrieved to a spindle pole, so bub3 cells mis-segregate chromosomes chiefly when kinetochores must be recaptured following microtubule depolymerization. Because fission yeast undergoes a closed mitosis, Bub3 acts within the nucleus: it is enriched at the central core of centromeres and at kinetochores from early mitosis until metaphase, and is otherwise a soluble nuclear protein. Bub3 is phosphorylated by Bub1, and bub3 cells are viable but sensitive to microtubule poisons.

Core Functions

Phospho-MELT reader and kinetochore adaptor: in the constitutive Bub1-Bub3 complex, the Bub3 WD40 propeller binds Mph1-phosphorylated MELT motifs of Spc7/Knl1 while holding the Bub1 GLEBS motif, docking Bub1 at unattached kinetochores; kinetochore Bub1-Bub3 is then required for efficient recruitment of Mad3 and the Mad1-Mad2 complex, so Bub3 converts Mph1 kinetochore phosphorylation into assembly of the checkpoint signaling platform and, once attachments are made, its Spc7-docked form promotes checkpoint silencing. In fission yeast this activity strengthens and spatially organizes the checkpoint but is not essential for arrest.

1. Molecular class and structural interpretation

Bub3 is not an enzyme, transporter, receptor, or structural component of the spindle itself. It is a soluble protein-interaction scaffold/adaptor in the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC). Consequently, there is no catalytic reaction or small-molecule substrate specificity to annotate.

The supplied InterPro/Pfam assignments—WD40 repeats and WD40/YVTN-repeat-like superfamily—fit the conserved Bub3 architecture. Crystal structures of Bub3 homologues show a compact seven-bladed WD40 β-propeller, approximately 42 Å across and 27 Å thick, with four antiparallel β-strands per blade and a central channel. Conserved interaction surfaces cluster on the top face and around the central pore. These structures are from budding yeast or metazoan proteins rather than O42860 itself, so they are strong evolutionary/structural inference, not a solved S. pombe Bub3 structure. (larsen2007structuralanalysisof pages 2-3, larsen2004crystalstructureof pages 3-4, larsen2004crystalstructureof pages 1-1)

Conserved Bub3 proteins bind GLEBS-region peptides from Bub1 or Mad3/BubR1 across the propeller’s top face. The partner segment becomes ordered upon binding and forms an extensive interface; structural measurements indicate a 1:1 complex and, in the budding-yeast system, peptide affinity of approximately 5 μM. Bub1 and Mad3 occupy substantially overlapping Bub3 surfaces and therefore bind mutually exclusively. This explains how a WD40 propeller can organize alternative checkpoint assemblies, although the detailed binding constants and Mad3-GLEBS geometry should not be transferred quantitatively to S. pombe, whose Mad3 interaction architecture is not identical. (larsen2007structuralanalysisof pages 3-5, larsen2007structuralanalysisof pages 1-2, larsen2007structuralanalysisof pages 2-3)

2. Cellular localization

Bub3 acts primarily in the nucleus at mitotic kinetochores. Bub3-GFP appears as punctate foci in early mitosis, colocalizes with the kinetochore markers Cnp1 and Ndc80, and is associated with short mitotic spindles. Chromatin immunoprecipitation places it at the centromeric central core—cnt and imr regions—rather than the outer heterochromatic repeats. The signal becomes weaker around metaphase and largely disappears before or at anaphase onset. Approximately 90% of cells with short spindles had detectable Bub3/Mad3 foci in the principal localization study. (vanoosthuyse2004kinetochoretargetingof pages 7-8, vanoosthuyse2004kinetochoretargetingof pages 6-7, vanoosthuyse2004kinetochoretargetingof pages 5-6)

Bub3 and Bub1 are mutually dependent for efficient kinetochore enrichment. Bub3 foci and centromeric ChIP enrichment disappear in bub1Δ, while Bub1 kinetochore association is strongly lost in bub3Δ. Bub3 loss also removes or reduces Mad1, Mad2, and Mad3 enrichment by at least 90%, while localization of the outer-kinetochore protein Nuf2 remains intact. Thus, Bub3 loss does not destroy the kinetochore globally; it selectively compromises assembly of its checkpoint-signaling layer. (vanoosthuyse2004kinetochoretargetingof pages 8-12, vanoosthuyse2004kinetochoretargetingof pages 7-8, vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 2-3)

3. Core pathway mechanism

3.1 Recruitment to Spc7/KNL1

At unattached or improperly attached kinetochores, the Mps1-family kinase Mph1 phosphorylates MELT motifs in Spc7, the fission-yeast KNL1 orthologue. These phosphorylated motifs create docking sites for the Bub1–Bub3 module. In the original analysis, Spc7 contained nine tested MELT motifs; replacing all nine threonines with alanine reduced Mph1-dependent in-vitro phosphorylation by 63.5% and strongly impaired Bub1/Bub3 recruitment. Phosphomimetic Spc7-9TE recruited the complex constitutively and could bypass kinase-dead Mph1 for recruitment, but it could not bypass the requirement for Bub3. Bub3 loading likewise required Bub1. (shepperd2012phosphodependentrecruitmentof pages 2-4, shepperd2012phosphodependentrecruitmentof pages 1-2)

The recruitment mechanism is therefore best represented as:

unattached/incorrectly attached kinetochore → Mph1 activation → Spc7-MELT phosphorylation → Bub3–Bub1 docking.

3.2 Bub3 as a signaling switch, not merely an address label

Bub3 does more than carry Bub1 to kinetochores. Experiments with progressively nonphosphorylatable Spc7 alleles showed that loss of phospho-MELT docking reduces Bub1 kinetochore concentration and checkpoint arrest. In a spc7-12TA background, deleting Bub3 paradoxically restored Bub1–Mad1/Mad2 interaction and partly suppressed the checkpoint defect; additional Bub1 also improved signaling, whereas additional Bub3 did not. These observations support a switch model: unengaged Bub3 can restrain productive Bub1–Mad1/Mad2 association, whereas binding of Bub3–Bub1 to phosphorylated Spc7 licenses the Mph1-dependent Bub1–Mad1/Mad2 signaling platform. (morasantos2016bub3bub1bindingto pages 6-9, morasantos2016bub3bub1bindingto pages 1-4)

A conserved central Bub1 motif containing Ser381, Thr383, and Thr386 is required for Mad1/Mad2 recruitment and checkpoint signaling but not for Bub1–Mad3 association. Thus, Bub3–Bub1 kinetochore docking and Mph1-dependent modification cooperate to assemble the catalytic SAC platform; docking alone is insufficient, because constitutive Bub1/Bub3 recruitment during interphase does not recruit Mad1/Mad2. (morasantos2016bub3bub1bindingto pages 6-9, shepperd2012phosphodependentrecruitmentof pages 4-7)

3.3 Downstream output

The downstream SAC inhibits the Cdc20 homologue Slp1 and therefore the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), delaying securin and cyclin-B destruction until chromosome attachments become acceptable. Bub3’s principal contribution is to organize upstream kinetochore signaling and attachment correction. In S. pombe, Bub3 is dispensable for efficient Mad2/Mad3 association with APC/C under some arrest conditions, providing a mechanistic explanation for residual checkpoint arrest when checkpoint proteins are no longer visibly enriched at kinetochores. (vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 2-3, vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 3-5)

4. Checkpoint arrest: resolution of apparently conflicting literature

An early deletion study concluded that bub3Δ likely eliminated spindle-checkpoint function because cells failed to maintain arrest in the cold-sensitive nda3-KM311 background, lost viability, and developed the “cut” phenotype. Bub3 was nevertheless nonessential for vegetative growth and gross spindle structure. (vanoosthuyse2004kinetochoretargetingof pages 5-6)

Later, more detailed work revised that interpretation. Approximately 70% of bub3Δ nda3 cells retained mitotic arrest, and the delay was Mad2-dependent; only approximately 10–15% underwent cytokinesis despite unattached kinetochores. By comparison, bub1Δ or mad2Δ produced roughly 90% checkpoint failure under the reported conditions. The current expert interpretation is therefore that Bub3 strengthens and spatially organizes the SAC but is not absolutely required for arrest in S. pombe. Checkpoint signaling can evidently be generated or amplified away from visibly enriched kinetochore assemblies. (vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 1-2, vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 3-5)

This conclusion is specific to fission yeast and should not be generalized uncritically to budding yeast or metazoans.

5. Checkpoint silencing and chromosome-attachment correction

Bub3 has two important functions that are separable from simply initiating arrest.

First, it promotes checkpoint silencing and mitotic recovery. In bub3Δ cells, cyclin-B destruction and APC/C reactivation are delayed after spindle recovery or Ark1/Aurora inhibition. Loss of Bub3 produced an approximately 20-minute metaphase delay after release from an nda3 arrest and a roughly 40–60-minute delay in a silencing assay. After prolonged spindle arrest, only about 30% of mutant cells formed colonies, with most deaths occurring during the first division. (vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 2-3, vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 1-2, vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 7-8)

Second, Bub3 promotes conversion of mono-oriented attachments to bipolar attachments, cooperating with Bub1 independently of shugoshin localization. After microtubule depolymerization and recovery, only 7% of wild-type chromosomes remained clearly mono-oriented for more than 10 minutes, versus 20% in bub3Δ cells, where correction was delayed or absent. Bub3-dependent organization of Sgo2 and Ark1/Aurora at centromeres may contribute to this error-correction function, although Bub3 is dispensable for shugoshin localization in another experimental context; the safest annotation is that it supports, rather than uniquely determines, the centromeric error-correction environment. (windecker2009bub1andbub3 pages 2-3, windecker2009bub1andbub3 pages 1-2, vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 7-8)

6. Phenotypes and quantitative evidence

The bub3Δ strain is viable but sensitive to microtubule poisons such as benomyl. Its unstressed chromosome-segregation phenotype is milder than that of bub1Δ: reported chromosome-2 missegregation, artificial chromosome Ch16 loss, and lagging-chromosome frequencies were approximately 0.2%, 0.2%, and 2.4%, respectively, compared with 5.3%, 3.5%, and 17.5% for bub1Δ. This difference demonstrates that Bub1 has substantial Bub3-independent segregation functions. (vanoosthuyse2004kinetochoretargetingof pages 5-6, vanoosthuyse2004kinetochoretargetingof pages 2-5)

Under prolonged checkpoint stress, however, Bub3 loss has major consequences: poor recovery, delayed silencing, attachment-correction defects, and loss of viability. Accordingly, the mild baseline chromosome-loss rate should not be interpreted as evidence that Bub3 is unimportant; its importance is most apparent after attachment perturbation and during recovery. (vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 2-3, vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 7-8)

7. Additional and noncanonical checkpoint contexts

Disruption of actin and astral-microtubule contact with the medial cortex by latrunculin A prevents normal spindle rotation, disturbs chromosome congression, and delays sister separation, securin destruction, and cyclin-B destruction. This delay requires Bub1, Bub3, and Mad3, but not Mad1 or Mad2. The result suggests that the Bub1–Bub3 branch can monitor or respond to spindle-position/orientation defects through a pathway partly separable from the canonical unattached-kinetochore Mad1/Mad2 response. (tournier2004disruptionofastral pages 1-2)

Consistent with this branch specificity, loss of the EB1-family microtubule protein Mal3 shows synthetic growth defects with bub3, bub1, mad3, or mph1, but not equivalently with mad1 or mad2. In mal3 cells, Bub1 kinetochore localization rises from about 2.5% to 10% of exponentially growing cells, while Bub1–Mad2 colocalization falls from 80% to 20%. These data connect Bub3-associated signaling to prevention or correction of persistent monopolar attachment. (asakawa2005mal3thefission pages 2-4, asakawa2005mal3thefission pages 1-2)

8. Recent developments, 2023–2024

No retrieved 2023–2024 paper directly re-characterized O42860/Bub3 itself. The recent advances instead refine the pathway in which Bub3 operates.

  • Mph1–Bub1 signaling (published January 2024): Mph1 phosphorylates S. pombe Bub1 at Ser532 in vivo and in vitro. The nonphosphorylatable Bub1-S532A allele impairs Bub1 and Mad1/Mad2 kinetochore signaling and produced anaphase lagging chromosomes in 54% (21/39) of cells versus 8% (3/38) with wild-type Bub1 in one live-cell assay. This adds a second regulatory layer downstream of Bub3–Bub1 docking but does not directly test Bub3. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2023.105559; published January 2024. (jian2024phosphorylationofbub1 pages 1-2, jian2024phosphorylationofbub1 pages 7-10)

  • Stress integration through MAPK and Slp1/Cdc20 (published March and September 2024): Pmk1 contributes to maintenance of metaphase arrest under environmental stress, binds the N terminus of Slp1/Cdc20, and promotes its phosphorylation-dependent ubiquitylation and degradation. pmk1Δ cells contain up to twofold more Slp1 during unperturbed growth. These findings place the canonical Bub3-associated SAC within a broader stress-responsive network controlling APC/C activation, but they do not establish a direct Bub3–Pmk1 interaction. DOIs: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12915-024-01865-6, March 2024; https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.97896.2, September 2024. (iglesiasromero2024mapkdependentcontrolof pages 1-2, sun2024negativeregulationof pages 1-2, sun2024negativeregulationof pages 2-3)

  • Current structural understanding (2023): modern SAC models treat unattached kinetochores as catalytic platforms for MCC generation. Bub3 is a seven-repeat WD40 propeller and conserved organizer of Bub-family proteins in these models. However, canonical metazoan MCC composition and kinetics should not be mapped wholesale onto fission yeast, where Bub3 is not required for APC-associated Mad2/Mad3 assembly or robust arrest under every condition. (vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 2-3, larsen2007structuralanalysisof pages 1-2)

9. Research applications and real-world relevance

There is no established industrial, diagnostic, or therapeutic use for S. pombe Bub3 itself. Its principal application is as an experimental model component for dissecting:

  1. phosphodependent kinetochore signaling through Mph1/Mps1 and Spc7/KNL1;
  2. spatial versus nonspatial SAC-signal generation;
  3. chromosome-attachment correction and bi-orientation;
  4. checkpoint silencing and APC/C reactivation; and
  5. integration of spindle surveillance with environmental-stress signaling.

Common implementations include bub3Δ, GFP-tagged Bub3, nda3-KM311 microtubule depolymerization, benomyl/TBZ/CBZ challenge, Spc7 MELT phosphomutants, live chromosome-marker imaging, centromeric ChIP, and checkpoint-protein coimmunoprecipitation. The unusual persistence of arrest without Bub3 makes fission yeast especially useful for separating kinetochore enrichment from downstream APC/C inhibition. (vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 2-3, shepperd2012phosphodependentrecruitmentof pages 2-4, shepperd2012phosphodependentrecruitmentof pages 1-2)

The broader translational relevance lies in the evolutionary conservation of Mps1–KNL1–Bub signaling and APC/C control, which are important to chromosome instability and antimitotic-drug responses. Nevertheless, therapeutic claims based on human BUB3 must not be assigned directly to O42860.

10. Recommended functional-annotation wording

Molecular function: WD40-repeat protein-binding scaffold; forms a complex with Bub1 and mediates phospho-Spc7/KNL1-dependent organization of spindle-checkpoint signaling.

Biological process: spindle assembly checkpoint regulation; kinetochore assembly of checkpoint factors; chromosome bi-orientation and correction of monopolar attachments; checkpoint silencing and timely APC/C reactivation.

Cellular component: nucleus; mitotic kinetochore/centromeric central core, especially in early mitosis and at unattached or improperly attached kinetochores.

Functional qualifier: contributes strongly to checkpoint efficiency but is not absolutely required for Mad2-dependent mitotic arrest in S. pombe. This qualification reconciles later mechanistic work with the stronger loss-of-checkpoint interpretation in early studies. (vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 2-3, windecker2009bub1andbub3 pages 2-3, vanoosthuyse2009bub3pfacilitatesspindle pages 3-5)

Notable curation decisions

  1. Both GO:0007094 (SAC signaling) and GO:0140499 (negative regulation of SAC signaling)
    are accepted, and GO:0090266 (regulation of SAC) alongside them.     This is not a
    contradiction: Bub3 promotes kinetochore-based signaling by delivering Bub1 to phospho-Spc7
    and restrains ectopic signaling when the complex is undocked. The PomBase curators chose the
    generic regulation term for PMID:22660415 (which reports both arms) and the specific
    negative-regulation term for PMID:28017606 (which isolates the inhibitory arm). Both
    choices were respected rather than "harmonised".
  2. The GO:0007094 IBA was accepted despite bub3 deletion not abolishing the checkpoint.
    The fission yeast peculiarity is that Bub3 is non-essential for arrest, not that it is
    uninvolved; the authors who showed the non-essentiality still describe Bub3 as intimately
    linked to the SAC, and the Spc7 docking-site mutants show a maintenance defect. Arguing
    against the node placement would require target-specific evidence of functional loss, which
    does not exist.
  3. GO:0043130 ubiquitin binding (IBA) kept as non-core rather than removed. The
    descendant evidence (SGD IDA from PMID:21070969) is a survey in which the cached running
    text never names Bub3; the inference is phylogenetically reasonable for the propeller
    family, but no physiological ubiquitin binding by Bub3 is known and the binding surface is
    GLEBS-occupied in vivo. Consistent with the yeast and human BUB3 reviews. Not REMOVE,
    because the SGD curator read the full figure set and the rule is to defer.
  4. GO:1990758 and GO:1990942 were both accepted as direct participation. The temptation
    was to read the bi-orientation defect as a checkpoint side-effect (less time to correct);
    Windecker et al. rule this out because the SAC is active in bub3 cells and Bub3 stays on the
    mono-oriented kinetochore, and they argue for modulation of kinetochore-microtubule
    interactions. The two rows are two views of one phenotype rather than two functions, which
    the reasons say explicitly.
  5. No NEW terms. GO:0033597 (mitotic checkpoint complex) was considered because Mad3p
    co-immunoprecipitates Bub3p with Mad2p and Slp1, and both the yeast and human BUB3 reviews
    carry it. PomBase has not annotated bub3 to it, Bub3 is dispensable for the
    Mad2-overexpression arrest and (per the deep research) for Mad2/Mad3 association with
    APC/C, and the S. pombe MCC structure work does not include it, so this was raised as a
    suggested question instead of asserted. GO:0034501 (protein localization to kinetochore)
    was likewise not proposed; the Bub1-docking role is expressed through GO:0140483
    kinetochore adaptor activity in core_functions.
  6. Molecular function. GOA carries no MF for S. pombe bub3 apart from ubiquitin binding
    and the GO-CAM types it as protein binding. core_functions uses GO:0140483 kinetochore
    adaptor activity (phospho-MELT reader that docks Bub1; same MF as the yeast and human
    reviews) and GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity for the nucleoplasmic
    restraint of Bub1. These are validator warnings ("not reflected in existing_annotations")
    and are left as such deliberately.

Open questions

  • Is S. pombe Bub3 a bona fide MCC subunit (GO:0033597)? It co-IPs with Mad3-Mad2-Slp1 but
    is dispensable for Mad2-dependent arrest and for Mad2/Mad3-APC/C association. A direct
    stoichiometry/reconstitution experiment would settle whether the budding yeast/human
    convention applies.
  • Mechanism of the restraint: does Bub3 sterically limit Bub1 autophosphorylation of the
    Mad1-binding motif, or block Mph1 access, and is the restraint relieved by a conformational
    change on phospho-Spc7 binding (Yuan et al. model)?
  • How does kinetochore-bound Bub1-Bub3 move a pole-proximal mono-oriented chromosome toward
    the opposing pole: Bub1 kinase output, a recruited motor/microtubule regulator, or
    Ark1/Sgo2-independent modulation of Ndc80-Dam1 attachment dynamics?
  • The UniProt PTM line "Phosphorylated by bub1" (PMID:15509783) has no functional follow-up;
    whether Bub3 phosphorylation affects MELT binding or the Bub1/Mad3 partner choice is
    unknown.

  Bub1 loading onto phospho-mimetic Spc7 still requires Bub3. Bub1 and Bub3 are mutually
  dependent for kinetochore localization, and Bub3 is in turn needed for efficient kinetochore
  enrichment of Mad3 and of the Mad1-Mad2 complex, so it assembles the kinetochore signaling
  layer from which the mitotic checkpoint complex (Mad3-Mad2-Slp1, with which Bub3
  co-immunoprecipitates) is generated to inhibit APC/C-Slp1 and delay anaphase. Fission yeast is
  unusual in that bub3 deletion does not abolish the checkpoint: most bub3 cells still mount a
  Mad2-dependent arrest when microtubules are depolymerized, because a soluble Spc7-Mph1-Bub1-Mad1
  signaling pool can operate without kinetochore enrichment. Instead, Bub3 tunes the checkpoint
  in both directions: when the Bub1-Bub3 complex is not docked on phosphorylated Spc7 it
  restrains premature Bub1-Mad1 complex formation and ectopic nucleoplasmic signaling, and
  docking of Bub1-Bub3 on Spc7 promotes checkpoint silencing and timely cyclin B destruction
  once attachments are corrected. Independently of shugoshin, Bub3 with Bub1 also promotes the
  conversion of mono-oriented chromosomes to bipolar attachment after chromosomes have been
  retrieved to a spindle pole, so bub3 cells mis-segregate chromosomes chiefly when kinetochores
  must be recaptured following microtubule depolymerization. Because fission yeast undergoes a
  closed mitosis, Bub3 acts within the nucleus: it is enriched at the central core of centromeres
  and at kinetochores from early mitosis until metaphase, and is otherwise a soluble nuclear
  protein. Bub3 is phosphorylated by Bub1, and bub3 cells are viable but sensitive to
  microtubule poisons.
