sel0 (O13890) Catalytic Competence — Focused Curation Report

Gene: sel0 / SPAC20G4.05c (S. pombe, taxon 284812) · UniProt: O13890 Hypothesis (slug: catalytic-competence): S. pombe sel0 is a catalytically competent protein AMPylase rather than a catalytically inactive SelO-family pseudoenzyme. Iterations: 1–3 of 3 (final)

Executive Judgment

Verdict: SUPPORTED (by strong evolutionary + structural conservation; not directly assayed in S. pombe).

The "pseudoenzyme" framing rests on a well-known naming artifact: SelO adopts a protein-kinase-like fold but lacks canonical kinase catalytic residues, so it is called a pseudokinase. Sreelatha et al. 2018 (Cell, PMID 30270044) showed this is misleading — SelO binds ATP in an inverted orientation and is a bona fide active protein AMPylase/nucleotidyltransferase. It is a pseudokinase but not a catalytically inactive enzyme.

For the S. pombe ortholog specifically, all evidence is homology/structure-based (there is no direct in vitro or in vivo assay of sel0/O13890). Within that limit, the evidence is unusually strong:

  • O13890 is in the SELO family (Pfam PF02696; InterPro IPR003846; PANTHER PTHR32057).
  • 10/10 UniProt-annotated catalytic/nucleotide-binding residues are identical to the experimentally characterized, crystallized Pseudomonas syringae SelO (Q87VB1) and E. coli YdiU (P77649), and to human SELENOO (Q9BVL4).
  • The AlphaFold model (mean pLDDT 95.8) shows these residues forming a single, high-confidence, compact active-site pocket.

The most important caveat: catalytic competence for S. pombe is inferred. The UniProt functional annotations are ECO:0000250 (by similarity to S. cerevisiae Q08968), and PomBase lists sel0's characterisation status as "biological role inferred." No experiment has measured S. pombe sel0 AMPylation activity, substrate, or the effect of active-site mutation in fission yeast.

Update (Iteration 2) — the inference is better-anchored than distant-bacterial homology: - The by-similarity source, S. cerevisiae SelO (Q08968), carries experimental evidence (ECO:0000269) for FUNCTION and CATALYTIC ACTIVITY (references include Sreelatha 2018, 30270044) and ECO:0000314 mitochondrial localization. The closest experimentally characterized ortholog is therefore fungal, not bacterial. - PomBase already curates sel0 with MF GO:0070733 (protein adenylyltransferase activity), BP GO:0098869 (cellular oxidant detoxification), CC GO:0005739 (mitochondrion) — i.e., the AMPylase MF term is already the curated state. - Nucleotide-specificity nuance: the bacterial homolog YdiU is a Mn²⁺-dependent protein UMPylase (32966796), whereas the eukaryotic mitochondrial branch (human SELENOO, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) is the AMPylase branch. The branch-appropriate MF term for sel0 is protein adenylyltransferase activity (GO:0070733), not a UMP-transferase term.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Immediate molecular function (what is being tested): transfer of AMP from ATP onto a hydroxyl side chain (Tyr, and in the family Ser/Thr) of substrate proteins — protein AMPylation/adenylylation — Mg²⁺-dependent, via a kinase-like fold with inverted ATP.
  • Downstream / non-core (do not conflate with the MF claim): redox homeostasis, regulation of protein S-glutathionylation, and cellular oxidative-stress response are downstream consequences of AMPylating redox enzymes (e.g. glutaredoxins). These are pathway/phenotype-level, not the direct catalytic activity, and are orthology-inferred for S. pombe.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  • "Pseudoenzyme" reading: The only basis is the pseudokinase label (missing kinase catalytic residues). This is resolved by the 2018 discovery that SelO is an active AMPylase — the kinase catalytic machinery is irrelevant to its true activity. No conflicting primary evidence indicates S. pombe sel0 is inactive.
  • Paralog confusion: Low risk. sel0 is single-copy in the SELO family; the human accession Q9Y6D0 is not SELENOO (it returns Selenoprotein K) — the correct human ortholog is Q9BVL4. Curators reusing accessions should note this.
  • Selenocysteine caveat: Some SelO orthologs (e.g., certain vertebrates) carry an active-site-proximal selenocysteine; S. pombe sel0 ends in a Cys-rich C-terminus (…QCSCSS) and is not predicted to be a selenoprotein. This does not affect the transferase active site (the AMP-transfer residues above are all standard and conserved).
  • Organism-specificity: All functional data derive from bacterial/human/S. cerevisiae systems; a fission-yeast-specific loss of activity cannot be excluded from sequence alone, though nothing suggests it.

Discriminating Tests

  1. In vitro AMPylation assay of recombinant sel0 with ATP (radiolabeled or biotin-ATP analog) on candidate substrates — directly distinguishes active enzyme from pseudoenzyme.
  2. Active-site mutagenesis (D287A catalytic Asp; K144A invariant Lys; D297A Mg-Asp) — loss of activity confirms these residues are catalytic, matching the family.
  3. Genetic complementation: does S. pombe sel0 rescue an oxidative-stress phenotype of a SelO-null (yeast/bacterial) strain, and is rescue abolished by the catalytic mutation?
  4. Substrate capture with biotin-ATP (35101215) to identify AMPylated targets and test the glutaredoxin/redox-homeostasis link in fission yeast.
