# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* **pop2/sud1** (UniProt O14170)

## Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified as **Pop2/Sud1 of *Schizosaccharomyces pombe***, encoded by **pop2/sud1 (SPAC4D7.03)**. It is a **Cdc4-like F-box/WD-repeat protein and substrate-recruiting component of SCF ubiquitin ligases**, not an enzyme with an independently demonstrated catalytic reaction. Its best-supported primary function is to help recognize cell-cycle proteins—especially phosphorylated **Cdc18** and **Rum1**—and present them to an SCF ubiquitin-ligase machinery for polyubiquitylation and subsequent 26S-proteasome degradation. This restrains replication licensing and CDK inhibition, thereby preventing DNA re-replication and inappropriate increases in ploidy. Later work implicates the same machinery in regulation of the translesion polymerase **Rev1**, while **Cig2** is a reported SCF^Pop1/Pop2 target for which the Pop2-specific evidence recovered here is less complete. Pop2 is detected in both the nucleus and cytoplasm, although its established Cdc18/Rum1 cell-cycle function is principally nuclear. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, uchiyama2015theproteinlevel pages 8-10, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

## 1. Mandatory identity verification and ambiguity control

### Correct target

The primary literature uses **Pop2p**, **Sud1p**, and **Sud1p/Pop2p** for the same fission-yeast F-box/WD-repeat protein. Pop2 was identified in the *S. pombe* genomic region represented by cosmid c4D7 through similarity to the Cdc4 class of F-box/WD-repeat proteins; target-specific knockout, immunoblotting, co-immunoprecipitation, and complementation experiments were subsequently performed in *S. pombe*. These properties agree with the supplied UniProt identity—O14170, SPAC4D7.03, organism strain 972/ATCC 24843—and with the listed F-box and WD40-domain annotations. (wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 3-4, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 4-5)

### Critical same-symbol exclusion

The name **POP2 is ambiguous across fungi**. Much recent literature uses Pop2/Caf1 for the **CCR4–NOT mRNA deadenylase**, including work in budding yeast and metazoan CNOT7/CNOT8 systems. That nuclease is biologically and structurally different from O14170. Searches for 2023–2024 publications returned many such deadenylase papers, but these were excluded rather than transferred to the fission-yeast Pop2/Sud1 annotation. The defining identity checks for O14170 are: *S. pombe* organism, Sud1 alias, SPAC4D7.03 locus, F-box/WD-repeat architecture, and SCF-dependent protein proteolysis—not CCR4–NOT-mediated RNA deadenylation. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, uchiyama2015theproteinlevel pages 8-10, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

## 2. Protein architecture and molecular classification

Pop2/Sud1 is an **F-box/WD-repeat adaptor protein**. In the canonical SCF arrangement, the cullin–RING core supplies the ubiquitin-ligase scaffold and catalytic geometry, while an F-box protein connects to the Skp1 module and contributes substrate recognition, commonly through additional WD repeats. Therefore, Pop2 should not be annotated as a protease or ubiquitin-transfer enzyme; its primary molecular role is **protein binding and substrate recruitment within an E3 ubiquitin-ligase complex**. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, kipreos2000thefboxprotein pages 4-5)

Domain-swap experiments provide target-specific structure–function evidence. An amino-terminal Pop2 region mediates functional association with Pop1, while the carboxy-terminal region contains WD repeats implicated in substrate-facing interactions. A chimera comprising the first 241 residues of Pop2 fused to full-length Pop1 rescued both defective proteolysis and polyploidy in `pop1Δ pop2Δ` cells. This indicates that juxtaposition of the Pop1 and Pop2 amino termini is functionally important, although it does not establish an atomic structure or exact substrate-contact surface. (wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 3-4)

The InterPro assignments supplied by the user—F-box domain/superfamily and WD40-repeat/PAC1-related annotations—are consequently well aligned with experimental literature. The precise biochemical division of labor is nevertheless unusual: in the Pop1–Pop2 heteromer, the **Pop1 F-box is essential**, whereas deletion of the Pop2 F-box can retain Rum1 degradation and ploidy control. Pop2 may therefore enter that heteromer primarily through Pop1, while its own F-box supports separate Pop2-containing assemblies. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

## 3. SCF complex organization and biochemical mechanism

Endogenous co-immunoprecipitation identified a complex containing **Pop1, Pop2, Pcu1/Cullin-1, Psh1/Skp1, and Pip1/Rbx1-like RING subunit**. Gel filtration placed the assembly at approximately **500 kDa**. The proteins form both a heterooligomeric SCF^Pop1–Pop2 complex and separately recoverable Pop1- or Pop2-containing SCF assemblies with ubiquitin-ligase activity. Pop1–Pop2 association remains relatively constant through the cell cycle, suggesting that periodic substrate destruction is controlled chiefly by substrate state—such as phosphorylation—rather than assembly and disassembly of the receptor complex. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

In reconstituted assays, phosphorylated Rum1 bound the Pop1–Pop2 complex and was polyubiquitylated in the presence of E1, UBC3 E2, ATP, and ubiquitin. Pip1 complexes isolated from `pop2Δ` cells failed to support Rum1 polyubiquitylation. These results support the following mechanism:

1. A cell-cycle substrate is phosphorylated and thereby acquires an SCF-recognition state.
2. Pop1/Pop2 binds the substrate through its adaptor/receptor surfaces.
3. The Pcu1–Pip1 core positions an E2–ubiquitin conjugate for substrate polyubiquitylation.
4. The polyubiquitylated substrate is degraded by the 26S proteasome. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4)

Thus there is no meaningful small-molecule “substrate specificity” or catalytic reaction to report for Pop2 itself. Its specificity is for **protein degrons and associated protein complexes**, particularly cell-cycle-regulated, phosphorylated substrates.

## 4. Established and proposed substrates

### Cdc18: replication-licensing control

**Cdc18**, the fission-yeast Cdc6-family replication-initiation factor, is the strongest direct Pop2 target. Disruption of `pop2` caused marked accumulation and slowed turnover of GFP-tagged and endogenous Cdc18 in cycloheximide-chase experiments. Cdc18 co-immunoprecipitated with Pop2 in wild-type cells, but this association was lost in `pop1Δ` cells, showing that Pop1 facilitates or is required for substrate engagement by Pop2 in the heteromeric receptor. (wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 3-4, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 5-5, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

Functionally, removing excess Cdc18 after origin licensing is essential because persistent licensing activity can permit repeated genome duplication without intervening mitosis. Pop2-dependent Cdc18 degradation therefore constitutes an anti-rereplication mechanism and explains much of the ploidy phenotype of `pop2Δ` cells. (wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 4-5, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

### Rum1: CDK-inhibitor turnover

**Rum1** is a cyclin-dependent-kinase inhibitor whose degradation promotes cell-cycle progression. Phosphorylated Rum1 bound purified Pop1–Pop2 complexes and was polyubiquitylated in vitro. The retrieved evidence identifies **Ser58 and Thr62** as relevant CDK-phosphorylated residues. Loss of Pop2 compromises this pathway, although the phenotype is generally milder than loss of Pop1. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

Cdc18 and Rum1 connect the same molecular activity to two coordinated outcomes: elimination of a replication-licensing factor and removal of a CDK inhibitor. Their combined stabilization favors inappropriate S-phase entry or repetition and provides a mechanistic explanation for unwanted diploidization/polyploidization. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 15-15, kipreos2000thefboxprotein pages 4-5)

### Rev1: control of mutagenic DNA-damage tolerance

A 2015 study extended the pathway to **Rev1**, a translesion-synthesis polymerase. Rev1 accumulated in proteasome-defective strains and in `pop2Δ`; Rev1 also co-immunoprecipitated with Pop2. A stabilized Rev1 variant lacking residues **761–818** failed to associate efficiently with Pop1, linking this region to SCF recognition. Pop1 loss produced the stronger effect, so Pop2 appears contributory rather than sufficient by itself. The pathway limits Rev1 around G1/S, plausibly reducing unnecessary access of a potentially mutagenic polymerase to undamaged DNA. (uchiyama2015theproteinlevel pages 8-10)

DNA damage also increases Rev1 abundance through a Rad3-dependent response described in that study, indicating that basal SCF/proteasome turnover and damage-induced regulation are integrated rather than constitutive. The exact Pop2-binding degron, requisite phosphorylation sites, and direct in-vitro ubiquitylation of Rev1 were not established in the retrieved evidence. (uchiyama2015theproteinlevel pages 8-10)

### Cig2: reported but less completely documented here

The S-phase cyclin **Cig2** is reported to require SCF^Pop1/Pop2 for degradation, placing Pop2 in the network that sharpens the Cig2 pulse at the G1/S boundary. However, the full experimental text of the principal 2004 Cig2 paper was not retrieved, and one accessible earlier passage discussed a Pop1 effect on `cig2` RNA abundance without resolving transcription versus RNA stability. Cig2 should therefore be retained as a **supported literature target with moderate confidence for a Pop2-specific mechanistic annotation**, below Cdc18, Rum1, and Rev1 in the evidence hierarchy. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

## 5. Biological pathways and cellular consequences

The most precise pathway annotation is **SCF-dependent ubiquitin–proteasome regulation of the G1/S transition and replication licensing**. Pop2 acts at the intersection of:

- **DNA-replication licensing:** degradation of Cdc18 prevents origin re-licensing and re-replication.
- **CDK regulation:** degradation of Rum1 removes inhibition of cyclin–CDK activity.
- **S-phase cyclin control:** reported contribution to Cig2 turnover helps confine its abundance to the G1/S boundary.
- **DNA-damage tolerance:** regulation of Rev1 limits translesion-polymerase abundance in the normal cell cycle. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, uchiyama2015theproteinlevel pages 8-10, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

The broad phenotype—genome instability and altered ploidy—is consequently informative rather than merely pleiotropic: it follows directly from failure to eliminate proteins that license DNA replication or restrain CDKs.

## 6. Cellular localization

Biochemical fractionation of endogenously Myc-tagged proteins, supported by GFP localization experiments, detected **Pop2 in both nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions**. Pop1 was predominantly or exclusively nuclear in the same study. The complete five-subunit Pop1–Pop2 SCF assembly can coexist in the nucleus, consistent with nuclear Cdc18 and Rum1 regulation. Pop2-only SCF assemblies may operate in the cytoplasm, but their physiological cytoplasmic substrates remain unidentified. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

Accordingly, the most defensible localization statement is: **nuclear and cytoplasmic protein, with experimentally established cell-cycle substrate degradation centered on the nuclear compartment**. It would be premature to assign Pop2 to a specific organelle or stable subnuclear body.

## 7. Genetic evidence and quantitative phenotype

`pop2Δ` cells are viable but morphologically heterogeneous and prone to excess genome duplication. In one study, approximately **50% of cells accumulated DNA content above the normal 2C level after 16 hours in rich liquid medium**. Red colonies on phloxin-B plates and flow-cytometric DNA-content measurements independently documented polyploid isolates. The phenotype was milder than `pop1Δ`, matching the biochemical finding that Pop1 supplies indispensable functions within the heteromer while Pop2 enhances substrate recognition and complex diversity. (wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 4-5, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

Cdc18 accumulated strongly in `pop2Δ`, while Rum1 accumulation and re-replication were less severe than in `pop1Δ`. This substrate-selective difference argues against Pop1 and Pop2 being simple redundant paralogues. Instead, they cooperate non-equivalently, and Pop2 may also form independent complexes with currently unknown substrates. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

## 8. Recent developments and current status of the field

A focused search for **2023–2024** studies using `pop2`, `sud1`, `SPAC4D7.03`, O14170, and *S. pombe* did **not identify a recent paper directly investigating this protein**. The latest target-specific experimental study recovered was the Rev1 paper published **6 July 2015**. Most mechanistic foundations date from 1998–2004. Current understanding remains robust for the core SCF/cell-cycle role but incomplete for Pop2-only substrates, degron recognition, structural organization, and regulation of nuclear–cytoplasmic partitioning.

## 9. Applications and real-world implementation

No clinical, agricultural, or industrial implementation specific to O14170 was found. Its applications are presently **basic and translationally informative model-system uses**:

1. **Replication-licensing and ploidy models:** `pop2Δ` provides a genetically tractable system for studying how failed proteolysis produces re-replication and polyploidization.
2. **SCF-substrate recognition:** the Pop1–Pop2 heteromer is a useful example of combinatorial F-box receptor assembly in which the two related proteins are not functionally equivalent.
3. **Proteostasis and cell-cycle timing:** Cdc18, Rum1, and Cig2 connect ubiquitin-dependent degradation to the G1/S transition.
4. **Mutagenesis control:** Rev1 regulation links SCF/proteasome activity with translesion synthesis and genome stability. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, uchiyama2015theproteinlevel pages 8-10, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

These applications illuminate general eukaryotic principles, but direct extrapolation to human F-box proteins or therapeutic targets requires orthology and mechanism-specific validation.

## 10. Expert assessment, confidence, and outstanding questions

**High-confidence annotation:** Pop2/Sud1 is a nonenzymatic F-box/WD-repeat substrate adaptor in *S. pombe* SCF ubiquitin ligases. It cooperates with Pop1 and the Pcu1–Psh1–Pip1 core to polyubiquitylate cell-cycle substrates, particularly Cdc18 and phosphorylated Rum1, causing proteasomal degradation and preventing re-replication. It is present in nucleus and cytoplasm. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, wolf1999fboxwdrepeatproteinspop1p pages 1-2)

**Moderate-confidence extensions:** Pop2 contributes to Rev1 turnover around G1/S, supported by deletion and interaction data, but direct reconstituted Rev1 ubiquitylation remains to be shown. Cig2 is a published Pop1/Pop2 SCF target, but Pop2-specific molecular evidence was incompletely accessible in this search. (uchiyama2015theproteinlevel pages 8-10, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

**Key unresolved questions:**

- Which degron surfaces in the Pop2 WD-repeat region contact each substrate?
- What are the physiological substrates of Pop2-only cytoplasmic SCF complexes?
- What is the stoichiometry and high-resolution structure of the approximately 500-kDa Pop1–Pop2 assembly?
- How are Pop2 abundance, localization, and post-translational modifications regulated?
- Does Pop2 directly recognize Rev1, or is recognition mediated chiefly through Pop1?
