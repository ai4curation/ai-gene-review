# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe skp1* (Q9Y709)

## Executive conclusion

The specified protein is the fission-yeast Skp1 ortholog encoded by **SPBC409.05**, historically named **Psh1p/Sph1p**, in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* strain 972. Its primary function is **not enzymatic catalysis**. It is a conserved, noncatalytic adaptor and structural core subunit of **SCF/CRL1 ubiquitin E3-ligase complexes**, connecting substrate-selecting F-box proteins to the Pcu1/Cullin-1–Pip1/Rbx1 catalytic scaffold. Different F-box partners consequently give Skp1-containing complexes different substrates, locations, and biological functions. Direct biochemical evidence shows association with Pcu1, Pip1, Pop1 and Pop2, fully consistent with the supplied SKP1-family and SKP1-like domain annotations. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

A critical nomenclature warning is necessary: ***S. pombe* Psh1p is Skp1/Q9Y709**, whereas ***Saccharomyces cerevisiae* Psh1** is a different CENP-A/Cse4-directed E3-ligase protein. Literature about budding-yeast Psh1-mediated Cse4 degradation must not be transferred to Q9Y709.

## 1. Identity verification

- **Accession:** UniProt Q9Y709.
- **Gene:** *skp1*; synonyms *psh1* and *sph1*; systematic locus SPBC409.05.
- **Organism:** *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* strain 972/ATCC 24843.
- **Protein identity:** suppressor of kinetochore protein 1/P19-Skp1 homolog.
- **Family/domain agreement:** membership in the SKP1 family and the presence of SKP1-like, Skp1 dimerization, and Skp1/BTB/POZ-related structural annotations agree with its experimentally demonstrated assembly into canonical SCF complexes. Specifically, Psh1p was identified as the fission-yeast Skp1 homolog and was recovered with Pcu1/Cullin-1, Pip1/Rbx1, Pop1 and Pop2. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

Thus, the requested symbol, organism, protein description, and family/domain architecture are mutually consistent. Research on plant, animal, filamentous-fungal, or budding-yeast Skp1 proteins is useful only as comparative context and was not treated as direct evidence for Q9Y709.

## 2. Primary molecular function

Skp1 serves as a **molecular bridge within SCF E3 ubiquitin ligases**. Its F-box-binding surface recruits an F-box substrate receptor, while the SCF assembly places receptor-bound substrates near the Cullin–RING/E2 ubiquitin-transfer machinery. Skp1 itself does not possess a catalytic ubiquitin-transfer residue and does not determine one fixed substrate specificity. Rather, specificity is supplied principally by the associated F-box protein, often after phosphorylation generates a substrate phosphodegron.

In fission yeast, biochemical co-purification and co-immunoprecipitation place Skp1/Psh1 in complexes containing **Pcu1**, **Pip1**, and the F-box proteins **Pop1** and **Pop2**. These components co-eluted as an approximately **500-kDa complex**. The Pop1/Pop2 hetero-assembly supported polyubiquitylation of the CDK inhibitor Rum1, while independently assembled Pop1- and Pop2-containing complexes also exhibited ubiquitin-ligase activity. Pop1’s F-box was required for Rum1-directed function of the heterocomplex; Pop2 could be recruited through Pop1. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

Accordingly, the most precise functional annotation is:

> **Core SCF/CRL1 adaptor that binds F-box substrate receptors and enables receptor-specific ubiquitination, commonly followed by proteasomal degradation.**

It would be misleading to annotate Skp1 as having one catalytic reaction or one intrinsic substrate. Ubiquitin transfer is executed by the larger E1–E2–SCF system, and substrate choice changes with the F-box partner.

## 3. Cellular localization

Skp1/Psh1 was detected in **both nucleus and cytoplasm**. This distribution is biologically coherent with its participation in multiple SCF assemblies: Pop1 is mainly nuclear, whereas Pop2 and other SCF core components occupy both compartments. Nuclear Pop1/Pop2 complexes act on nuclear cell-cycle regulators such as Rum1 and Cdc18, while cytoplasmic Pop2-containing assemblies probably act on other substrates. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

Skp1 should therefore not be assigned exclusively to the kinetochore despite its historical description as “suppressor of kinetochore protein.” Its best-supported steady-state annotation is a **nuclear-and-cytoplasmic SCF component**. Its location at a particular time depends on the receptor-containing complex and substrate pathway.

## 4. Major Skp1-dependent pathways

### 4.1 Cell-cycle proteolysis and ploidy control

The Pop1/Pop2 branch provides the clearest direct biochemical model. A Skp1–Pcu1–Pip1 core combines with Pop1 and/or Pop2 to form alternative SCF assemblies. The heterooligomeric complex polyubiquitylates **Rum1**, a CDK inhibitor; Pop1/Pop2 pathways also regulate destruction of **Cdc18**, a replication-initiation factor. These reactions prevent inappropriate persistence of G1/S regulators and thereby constrain rereplication and ploidy. Direct evidence is strongest for complex composition and Rum1 polyubiquitylation; some Cdc18 assignments derive from associated genetic and biochemical studies summarized in the retrieved literature. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 6-6)

### 4.2 DNA-damage checkpoint and mitosis

Temperature-sensitive **skp1-a7** cells undergo a G2 delay associated with activation of the Rad3-dependent DNA-damage checkpoint. Removing **rad3** suppresses the high-temperature G2 arrest and also suppresses the bent-spindle phenotype. Skp1 loss of function additionally causes abnormal spindle morphology and defective nuclear segregation during anaphase. These findings indicate that compromised SCF function creates genome or chromosome-structure defects that activate the checkpoint; they do not establish Skp1 as a checkpoint kinase or DNA-repair enzyme. (prakash2016skp1acomponent pages 2-3, okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 1-2)

### 4.3 Meiotic recombination-intermediate resolution

At the semi-restrictive temperature of **33°C**, approximately **60%** of *skp1-a7* zygotes displayed abnormal meiosis-II spindles, generally arising from spindle bending in meiosis I. Mutants formed fewer than four chromosome masses and frequently produced two- or three-spored rather than four-spored asci. Eliminating Rec12/Spo11-dependent meiotic double-strand breaks suppressed spindle bending, and persistent Rhp51/Rad51–Rad22/Rad52 foci demonstrated accumulation of recombination intermediates. Among examined F-box mutants, **fbh1Δ** reproduced the bent-spindle phenotype, supporting cooperation between SCF/Skp1 and the F-box helicase Fbh1 in resolving meiotic recombination structures before chromosome segregation. (okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 1-2, okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 2-3)

The mechanistic interpretation is that unresolved DNA linkages generate physical tension and chromosome entanglement, which bend the spindle. Skp1’s role remains that of an SCF component; Fbh1 supplies pathway specificity and helicase-related activity.

### 4.4 Cell separation and cytokinesis

Skp1 interacts functionally with the F-box protein **Pof6**, and both are required for normal fission-yeast cell separation. Loss-of-function phenotypes connect this assembly to cytokinesis, septation/endocytic organization, and successful daughter-cell separation. However, the exact ubiquitylation substrate responsible for this phenotype is less securely established than Rum1 in the Pop pathway or Hst4 in the Pof3 pathway. The conservative annotation is therefore “Skp1–Pof6-dependent cell-separation machinery,” not a specific catalytic reaction toward a confirmed substrate. (prakash2016skp1acomponent pages 2-3, hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 5-6)

### 4.5 Replication stress and chromatin regulation: SCF^Pof3–Hst4

The most recent direct mechanistic study retrieved was published in **October 2021**. Under replication stress, DDK/Hsk1 phosphorylates the Hst4 histone deacetylase at four C-terminal residues—**S354, S355, S362 and S363**—forming a degradation signal recognized by **SCF^Pof3**. Hst4 was stabilized both in *pof3Δ* cells and in **skp1-94**, a Skp1 mutant defective in Pof3 interaction; deletion of Pop1 did not have this effect, establishing receptor specificity. (aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 19-20, aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 11-13)

Degradation of Hst4 raises H3K56 acetylation and promotes recruitment or function of replication-fork-protection machinery. A nondegradable four-serine-to-alanine Hst4 mutant showed sensitivity to methyl methanesulfonate and hydroxyurea, defective recovery from replication stress, impaired fork restart and reduced viability. Thus, Skp1 is required indirectly for a chromatin-state transition that protects stalled forks; it is neither the Hst4 kinase nor the deacetylase. [Aricthota & Haldar, eLife, 20 October 2021, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.70787] (aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 19-20, aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 11-13)

### 4.6 Histone homeostasis and centromere integrity

SCF^Pof3 also participates in turnover of **Ams2**, the GATA-type activator of core histone transcription. Restricting Ams2 abundance helps confine histone production to the appropriate cell-cycle window; excess canonical histone can compromise centromeric chromatin. Consequently, Skp1 contributes to centromere integrity indirectly through receptor-specific protein turnover rather than through a demonstrated intrinsic kinetochore-structural function. This interpretation is consistent with the Pof3 pathway’s broader roles in genome and telomere stability. (aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 19-20, aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 31-32)

### 4.7 Stress-response pathways

Distinct Skp1 complexes permit environmental responses. **SCF^Pof1** regulates the Zip1 transcription factor in the cadmium response, whereas **SCF^Pof3** responds to replication stress. These examples illustrate why broad *skp1* phenotypes are pleiotropic: impairing a shared core adaptor simultaneously compromises several receptor-defined ligases. Evidence for Pof1–Zip1 is pathway-level and less direct for Skp1/Q9Y709 than the Pop1/Pop2 and Pof3 biochemical evidence. (hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 6-6, hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 5-6)

## 5. Evidence hierarchy and expert interpretation

The strongest evidence for the annotation consists of:

1. **Direct complex biochemistry:** endogenous co-purification/co-immunoprecipitation of Skp1/Psh1 with Pcu1, Pip1, Pop1 and Pop2; approximately 500-kDa complex formation; and ubiquitination activity. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)
2. **Direct substrate-pathway evidence:** Rum1 polyubiquitylation by Pop complexes and Hst4 stabilization in *pof3Δ* and *skp1-94*. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 11-13)
3. **Localization evidence:** presence in both nucleus and cytoplasm. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)
4. **Genetic and cell-biological evidence:** checkpoint-dependent G2 arrest, spindle defects, persistent recombination foci, and suppression by removing meiotic DSB formation. (okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 1-2, okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 2-3)

The unifying expert interpretation is that Skp1 is a **shared platform**, not a pathway-specific enzyme. Phenotypes involving replication, recombination, cytokinesis, stress, ploidy and centromeres reflect failure of different F-box-receptor modules. Claims that Skp1 directly recognizes every listed substrate should be avoided: direct recognition belongs primarily to Pop1/Pop2, Pof3, Pof6, Pof1 or Fbh1, depending on the pathway.

## 7. Current applications and real-world relevance

Q9Y709 has no direct clinical or industrial application established in the retrieved literature. Its practical value is as a genetically tractable model for:

- dissecting modular SCF/CRL1 ubiquitin-ligase assembly;
- determining how phosphorylation-dependent proteolysis controls the cell cycle;
- studying replication-fork protection and chromatin responses to genotoxic stress;
- understanding recombination-intermediate resolution and chromosome segregation;
- analyzing cytokinesis and cell separation; and
- testing conserved principles of Cullin–RING ligase biology relevant to higher eukaryotes.

These are research applications rather than organism-specific therapeutic uses. Because human CRL1 systems use the same general Skp1–Cullin–F-box logic, fission yeast provides mechanistic models, but individual substrates and receptor relationships cannot automatically be extrapolated to humans.

## Final annotation

**Q9Y709/Skp1 is an essential, nuclear-and-cytoplasmic, nonenzymatic SCF/CRL1 core adaptor. It binds F-box proteins and links them to the Pcu1–Pip1 Cullin–RING scaffold, thereby enabling receptor-specific substrate polyubiquitylation and often proteasomal degradation. Its best-supported functional branches regulate Rum1/Cdc18-dependent cell-cycle control, Pof3-dependent Hst4 and Ams2 turnover, Fbh1-associated recombination processing, Pof6-linked cell separation, and stress-responsive proteostasis.** The annotation is experimentally well supported at the level of SCF assembly and several receptor-specific pathways, but Skp1 itself has no single intrinsic substrate or catalytic reaction.
