Gene Description

Skp1 (Psh1/Sph1; SPBC409.05) is the small, essential adaptor subunit of Schizosaccharomyces pombe SCF (Skp1-Pcu1/cullin-1-F-box-Pip1/Rbx1) ubiquitin ligases, the ortholog of human SKP1 and budding-yeast Skp1. Its N-terminal BTB/POZ-like domain binds the cullin Pcu1 and its C-terminal helices bind the ~40-residue F-box motif of an interchangeable substrate receptor, so that Skp1 holds the receptor-bound, phosphorylated substrate in register with the Pip1-bound E2. Skp1 has no catalytic activity and does not itself select substrates; the receptor supplies specificity and the Pcu1-Pip1 module supplies the ubiquitin-transfer chemistry. Fission yeast has about seventeen F-box proteins and Skp1 co-purifies with most of them (Pop1, Pop2, Pof1-Pof14, Fbh1). Through SCF(Pop1/Pop2) it drives destruction of the CDK inhibitor Rum1 and the replication-licensing factor Cdc18 at G1/S, preventing re-replication and polyploidy; through SCF(Pof3) it turns over the histone-gene activator Ams2, the histone deacetylase Hst4 and DNA polymerase epsilon under replication stress, protecting genome and centromere integrity; through SCF(Pof1) it controls the Zip1 transcription factor; it is required for Cig2 cyclin instability in G2/M and for SCF-dependent autocatalytic turnover of F-box proteins such as Pof14. Skp1 also forms cullin-free complexes: the Fbh1-Skp1 heterodimer is a ssDNA-dependent ATPase/3'-5' helicase that disrupts Rad51 filaments and, with Pcu1-Rbx1, ubiquitinates Rad51 to modulate homologous recombination in mitosis and meiosis; the essential Skp1-Pof6-Sip1 complex is needed for septum processing and cell separation; the RAVE-like Rav1-Rav2-Skp1 complex regulates V-ATPase assembly; and Skp1 associates with the CAK subunits Mcs2 and Pmh1 without promoting their degradation. Temperature-sensitive skp1 alleles that weaken F-box binding show a Rad3-dependent G2 delay, mitotic arrest with high Cdc2 activity, arched and collapsing anaphase spindles with unsegregated nuclear membranes, persistent recombination foci in meiosis and thick, unresolved septa. The protein is distributed between the nucleus and the cytoplasm.

Core Functions

Adaptor/scaffold of SCF (Skp1-Pcu1-F-box-Pip1) ubiquitin ligases: the BTB/POZ-like N-terminus binds the cullin Pcu1 while the C-terminal helices bind the F-box motif of an interchangeable receptor (Pop1, Pop2, Pof1, Pof3, Pof14, Fbh1 and others), holding the receptor-bound phospho-substrate in register with the Pip1/Rbx1-bound E2 so that Rum1, Cdc18, Cig2, Ams2, Hst4, Pol2, Rad51 and other targets are polyubiquitinated for proteasomal destruction; Rum1 and Cdc18 removal drives the G1/S transition and limits re-replication, and Ams2/Hst4/Pol2 turnover safeguards genome integrity. The adaptor populates both nuclear SCF (the site of the characterised substrates) and cytoplasmic SCF(Pop2) assemblies.

Functional annotation report: Schizosaccharomyces pombe skp1 (Q9Y709)

Executive conclusion

The specified protein is the fission-yeast Skp1 ortholog encoded by SPBC409.05, historically named Psh1p/Sph1p, in Schizosaccharomyces pombe strain 972. Its primary function is not enzymatic catalysis. It is a conserved, noncatalytic adaptor and structural core subunit of SCF/CRL1 ubiquitin E3-ligase complexes, connecting substrate-selecting F-box proteins to the Pcu1/Cullin-1–Pip1/Rbx1 catalytic scaffold. Different F-box partners consequently give Skp1-containing complexes different substrates, locations, and biological functions. Direct biochemical evidence shows association with Pcu1, Pip1, Pop1 and Pop2, fully consistent with the supplied SKP1-family and SKP1-like domain annotations. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

A critical nomenclature warning is necessary: S. pombe Psh1p is Skp1/Q9Y709, whereas Saccharomyces cerevisiae Psh1 is a different CENP-A/Cse4-directed E3-ligase protein. Literature about budding-yeast Psh1-mediated Cse4 degradation must not be transferred to Q9Y709.

1. Identity verification

  • Accession: UniProt Q9Y709.
  • Gene: skp1; synonyms psh1 and sph1; systematic locus SPBC409.05.
  • Organism: Schizosaccharomyces pombe strain 972/ATCC 24843.
  • Protein identity: suppressor of kinetochore protein 1/P19-Skp1 homolog.
  • Family/domain agreement: membership in the SKP1 family and the presence of SKP1-like, Skp1 dimerization, and Skp1/BTB/POZ-related structural annotations agree with its experimentally demonstrated assembly into canonical SCF complexes. Specifically, Psh1p was identified as the fission-yeast Skp1 homolog and was recovered with Pcu1/Cullin-1, Pip1/Rbx1, Pop1 and Pop2. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

Thus, the requested symbol, organism, protein description, and family/domain architecture are mutually consistent. Research on plant, animal, filamentous-fungal, or budding-yeast Skp1 proteins is useful only as comparative context and was not treated as direct evidence for Q9Y709.

2. Primary molecular function

Skp1 serves as a molecular bridge within SCF E3 ubiquitin ligases. Its F-box-binding surface recruits an F-box substrate receptor, while the SCF assembly places receptor-bound substrates near the Cullin–RING/E2 ubiquitin-transfer machinery. Skp1 itself does not possess a catalytic ubiquitin-transfer residue and does not determine one fixed substrate specificity. Rather, specificity is supplied principally by the associated F-box protein, often after phosphorylation generates a substrate phosphodegron.

In fission yeast, biochemical co-purification and co-immunoprecipitation place Skp1/Psh1 in complexes containing Pcu1, Pip1, and the F-box proteins Pop1 and Pop2. These components co-eluted as an approximately 500-kDa complex. The Pop1/Pop2 hetero-assembly supported polyubiquitylation of the CDK inhibitor Rum1, while independently assembled Pop1- and Pop2-containing complexes also exhibited ubiquitin-ligase activity. Pop1’s F-box was required for Rum1-directed function of the heterocomplex; Pop2 could be recruited through Pop1. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

Accordingly, the most precise functional annotation is:

Core SCF/CRL1 adaptor that binds F-box substrate receptors and enables receptor-specific ubiquitination, commonly followed by proteasomal degradation.

It would be misleading to annotate Skp1 as having one catalytic reaction or one intrinsic substrate. Ubiquitin transfer is executed by the larger E1–E2–SCF system, and substrate choice changes with the F-box partner.

3. Cellular localization

Skp1/Psh1 was detected in both nucleus and cytoplasm. This distribution is biologically coherent with its participation in multiple SCF assemblies: Pop1 is mainly nuclear, whereas Pop2 and other SCF core components occupy both compartments. Nuclear Pop1/Pop2 complexes act on nuclear cell-cycle regulators such as Rum1 and Cdc18, while cytoplasmic Pop2-containing assemblies probably act on other substrates. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

Skp1 should therefore not be assigned exclusively to the kinetochore despite its historical description as “suppressor of kinetochore protein.” Its best-supported steady-state annotation is a nuclear-and-cytoplasmic SCF component. Its location at a particular time depends on the receptor-containing complex and substrate pathway.

4. Major Skp1-dependent pathways

4.1 Cell-cycle proteolysis and ploidy control

The Pop1/Pop2 branch provides the clearest direct biochemical model. A Skp1–Pcu1–Pip1 core combines with Pop1 and/or Pop2 to form alternative SCF assemblies. The heterooligomeric complex polyubiquitylates Rum1, a CDK inhibitor; Pop1/Pop2 pathways also regulate destruction of Cdc18, a replication-initiation factor. These reactions prevent inappropriate persistence of G1/S regulators and thereby constrain rereplication and ploidy. Direct evidence is strongest for complex composition and Rum1 polyubiquitylation; some Cdc18 assignments derive from associated genetic and biochemical studies summarized in the retrieved literature. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13, hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 6-6)

4.2 DNA-damage checkpoint and mitosis

Temperature-sensitive skp1-a7 cells undergo a G2 delay associated with activation of the Rad3-dependent DNA-damage checkpoint. Removing rad3 suppresses the high-temperature G2 arrest and also suppresses the bent-spindle phenotype. Skp1 loss of function additionally causes abnormal spindle morphology and defective nuclear segregation during anaphase. These findings indicate that compromised SCF function creates genome or chromosome-structure defects that activate the checkpoint; they do not establish Skp1 as a checkpoint kinase or DNA-repair enzyme. (prakash2016skp1acomponent pages 2-3, okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 1-2)

4.3 Meiotic recombination-intermediate resolution

At the semi-restrictive temperature of 33°C, approximately 60% of skp1-a7 zygotes displayed abnormal meiosis-II spindles, generally arising from spindle bending in meiosis I. Mutants formed fewer than four chromosome masses and frequently produced two- or three-spored rather than four-spored asci. Eliminating Rec12/Spo11-dependent meiotic double-strand breaks suppressed spindle bending, and persistent Rhp51/Rad51–Rad22/Rad52 foci demonstrated accumulation of recombination intermediates. Among examined F-box mutants, fbh1Δ reproduced the bent-spindle phenotype, supporting cooperation between SCF/Skp1 and the F-box helicase Fbh1 in resolving meiotic recombination structures before chromosome segregation. (okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 1-2, okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 2-3)

The mechanistic interpretation is that unresolved DNA linkages generate physical tension and chromosome entanglement, which bend the spindle. Skp1’s role remains that of an SCF component; Fbh1 supplies pathway specificity and helicase-related activity.

4.4 Cell separation and cytokinesis

Skp1 interacts functionally with the F-box protein Pof6, and both are required for normal fission-yeast cell separation. Loss-of-function phenotypes connect this assembly to cytokinesis, septation/endocytic organization, and successful daughter-cell separation. However, the exact ubiquitylation substrate responsible for this phenotype is less securely established than Rum1 in the Pop pathway or Hst4 in the Pof3 pathway. The conservative annotation is therefore “Skp1–Pof6-dependent cell-separation machinery,” not a specific catalytic reaction toward a confirmed substrate. (prakash2016skp1acomponent pages 2-3, hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 5-6)

4.5 Replication stress and chromatin regulation: SCF^Pof3–Hst4

The most recent direct mechanistic study retrieved was published in October 2021. Under replication stress, DDK/Hsk1 phosphorylates the Hst4 histone deacetylase at four C-terminal residues—S354, S355, S362 and S363—forming a degradation signal recognized by SCF^Pof3. Hst4 was stabilized both in pof3Δ cells and in skp1-94, a Skp1 mutant defective in Pof3 interaction; deletion of Pop1 did not have this effect, establishing receptor specificity. (aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 19-20, aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 11-13)

Degradation of Hst4 raises H3K56 acetylation and promotes recruitment or function of replication-fork-protection machinery. A nondegradable four-serine-to-alanine Hst4 mutant showed sensitivity to methyl methanesulfonate and hydroxyurea, defective recovery from replication stress, impaired fork restart and reduced viability. Thus, Skp1 is required indirectly for a chromatin-state transition that protects stalled forks; it is neither the Hst4 kinase nor the deacetylase. [Aricthota & Haldar, eLife, 20 October 2021, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.70787] (aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 19-20, aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 11-13)

4.6 Histone homeostasis and centromere integrity

SCF^Pof3 also participates in turnover of Ams2, the GATA-type activator of core histone transcription. Restricting Ams2 abundance helps confine histone production to the appropriate cell-cycle window; excess canonical histone can compromise centromeric chromatin. Consequently, Skp1 contributes to centromere integrity indirectly through receptor-specific protein turnover rather than through a demonstrated intrinsic kinetochore-structural function. This interpretation is consistent with the Pof3 pathway’s broader roles in genome and telomere stability. (aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 19-20, aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 31-32)

4.7 Stress-response pathways

Distinct Skp1 complexes permit environmental responses. SCF^Pof1 regulates the Zip1 transcription factor in the cadmium response, whereas SCF^Pof3 responds to replication stress. These examples illustrate why broad skp1 phenotypes are pleiotropic: impairing a shared core adaptor simultaneously compromises several receptor-defined ligases. Evidence for Pof1–Zip1 is pathway-level and less direct for Skp1/Q9Y709 than the Pop1/Pop2 and Pof3 biochemical evidence. (hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 6-6, hermand2006fboxproteinsmore pages 5-6)

5. Evidence hierarchy and expert interpretation

The strongest evidence for the annotation consists of:

  1. Direct complex biochemistry: endogenous co-purification/co-immunoprecipitation of Skp1/Psh1 with Pcu1, Pip1, Pop1 and Pop2; approximately 500-kDa complex formation; and ubiquitination activity. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)
  2. Direct substrate-pathway evidence: Rum1 polyubiquitylation by Pop complexes and Hst4 stabilization in pof3Δ and skp1-94. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2, aricthota2021ddkhsk1phosphorylatesand pages 11-13)
  3. Localization evidence: presence in both nucleus and cytoplasm. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)
  4. Genetic and cell-biological evidence: checkpoint-dependent G2 arrest, spindle defects, persistent recombination foci, and suppression by removing meiotic DSB formation. (okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 1-2, okamoto2012scfensuresmeiotic pages 2-3)

The unifying expert interpretation is that Skp1 is a shared platform, not a pathway-specific enzyme. Phenotypes involving replication, recombination, cytokinesis, stress, ploidy and centromeres reflect failure of different F-box-receptor modules. Claims that Skp1 directly recognizes every listed substrate should be avoided: direct recognition belongs primarily to Pop1/Pop2, Pof3, Pof6, Pof1 or Fbh1, depending on the pathway.

7. Current applications and real-world relevance

Q9Y709 has no direct clinical or industrial application established in the retrieved literature. Its practical value is as a genetically tractable model for:

  • dissecting modular SCF/CRL1 ubiquitin-ligase assembly;
  • determining how phosphorylation-dependent proteolysis controls the cell cycle;
  • studying replication-fork protection and chromatin responses to genotoxic stress;
  • understanding recombination-intermediate resolution and chromosome segregation;

Final annotation

Q9Y709/Skp1 is an essential, nuclear-and-cytoplasmic, nonenzymatic SCF/CRL1 core adaptor. It binds F-box proteins and links them to the Pcu1–Pip1 Cullin–RING scaffold, thereby enabling receptor-specific substrate polyubiquitylation and often proteasomal degradation. Its best-supported functional branches regulate Rum1/Cdc18-dependent cell-cycle control, Pof3-dependent Hst4 and Ams2 turnover, Fbh1-associated recombination processing, Pof6-linked cell separation, and stress-responsive proteostasis. The annotation is experimentally well supported at the level of SCF assembly and several receptor-specific pathways, but Skp1 itself has no single intrinsic substrate or catalytic reaction.

skp1 (SPBC409.05, Q9Y709) - curation notes

Fission yeast Skp1 (historical names psh1 "pombe skp1 homologue" and sph1). Not to be
confused with S. cerevisiae Psh1, a Cse4-directed E3 ligase. Exemplars used for style:
genes/yeast/SKP1/ and genes/human/SKP1/. Sibling SCHPO reviews: pop1 (COMPLETE),
cul1 and rbx1 (INITIALIZED at time of writing).

    quotable framing: "Skp1 itself does not possess a catalytic ubiquitin-transfer residue
    and does not determine one fixed substrate specificity." It also reports the 2021
    SCF(Pof3)-Hst4 study (Aricthota & Haldar, eLife) which is not in GOA for skp1 and not

Biology, with provenance

  • Identity and core complex: Psh1/Skp1 and Pip1/Rbx1 were identified by similarity and
    co-purify with Pop1, Pop2 and Pcu1 PMID:12167173; endogenous
    proteins co-elute in a ~500 kDa complex PMID:12167173;
    the immunopurified complex polyubiquitylates phosphorylated Rum1 in vitro; the
    Pip1/Pcu1/Psh1 core is constant across the cell cycle; Pop1 and Pop2 each bind the core
    independently (cytoplasmic SCF(Pop2)).
  • Localisation: GFP-Psh1 in both nucleus and cytoplasm PMID:12167173; ORFeome YFP survey agrees
    PMID:16823372.
  • F-box repertoire: two-hybrid screen with Skp1 as bait isolated six interactors including
    Pof10, whose F-box is required for Skp1 binding; Pof10 overexpression is lethal by
    sequestering Skp1 from Pop1 PMID:11820777. Systematic co-IP of 12 tagged F-box proteins
    and Pcu1: ts Skp1 alleles (substitutions on the F-box-binding face) retain Pcu1 binding
    but lose Pof1, Pof3 and Pof10 [PMID:15147268 "This systematic analysis showed that ts
    Skp1 retains binding to Pcu1"; "binding to three specific F-box proteins, essential
    Pof1, Pof3 involved in maintaining genome integrity, and nonessential Pof10, was
    reduced"]. UniProt records the F-box-binding region as residues 102-161 and I110T as
    losing Pof1/Pof3/Pof10 binding.
  • Checkpoint: skp1 ts alleles show a G2 delay due to DNA-damage-checkpoint activation that
    is rescued by checkpoint abrogation PMID:15147268.
  • Mitosis: skp1-A7 enters mitosis but fails anaphase; arched intranuclear spindles
    collapse; attributed to failed nuclear membrane segregation [PMID:15147872 "Temperature-
    sensitive skp1-A7 mutants enter mitosis, but fail to execute anaphase."; "These abnormal
    phenotypes appear to stem from defects in nuclear membrane segregation."]. Later work
    shows the mitotic bent spindle is suppressed by rad3 deletion and that the responsible
    F-box protein is unknown [PMID:22292001 "In mitosis, the bent-spindle phenotype was no
    longer observed in either the skp1-a7 rad3Δ or the fbh1Δ rad3Δ mutants"; "The F-box
    protein responsible for this bent-spindle phenotype has not been identified."].
  • Meiosis: skp1-a7 bent MI spindles are caused by chromosome entanglement from
    unresolved recombination intermediates; suppressed by rec12 deletion; Rhp51/Rad22 foci
    persist; fbh1 deletion and F-box mutants phenocopy [PMID:22292001 "Rhp51/Rad51-
    Rad22/Rad52 foci persisted until meiosis I in skp1 cells, proving accumulation of
    recombination intermediates."; "Skp1 and Fbh1 act together to resolve the meiotic
    recombination intermediates"].
  • Fbh1-Skp1: Fbh1 can only be purified as a heterodimer with Skp1; the heterodimer has
    ssDNA-dependent ATPase and 3'-5' helicase activity (Skp1 alone has none), disrupts Rad51
    filaments, stimulates strand exchange after initiation, and with Pcu1-Rbx1 forms
    SCF(Fbh1) that ubiquitinates Rad51 with Ubc4 [PMID:25165823 "Because we could not purify
    Fbh1 alone, we purified Fbh1 and Skp1 as a protein complex"; "The Fbh1-Skp1 heterodimer
    exhibited a robust, ssDNA-dependent ATPase activity."; "Rad51 was ubiquitinated in an
    Ubc4- and SCFFbh1-dependent manner."].
  • SCF(Pof3) substrates: Ams2 is stabilised in pof3 and skp1 cells and Skp1 co-IPs with
    Pof3-Ams2 PMID:20230746;
    Pol2 is stabilised in skp1-94 at 35 C and in pof3 deletion PMID:23349636. Hst4 is stabilised
    in pof3 deletion and skp1-94 (deep research; Aricthota & Haldar 2021, not in GOA).
  • Cig2: G2/M instability requires Skp1 but not Pop1/Pop2 or APC/C PMID:11163211.
  • Pof14: forms a canonical F-box-dependent SCF; autocatalytic turnover needs its F-box,
    Skp1 and the proteasome; skp1-3 behaves as wild type under peroxide, so the Pof14 stress
    function is SCF-independent PMID:17016471.
  • Cullin-free complexes: Skp1-Pof6 lacks Pcu1 and is essential for cell separation
    [PMID:12511573 "Purification of Pof6 demonstrates association of Skp1, whereas the Pcu1
    cullin was absent from the complex."; "Other skp1-3f cells as well as the skp1-deleted
    cells accumulate abnormal thick septa leading to defects in cell separation."]; Sip1 is
    the third subunit, Skp1 is among the most abundant proteins in Sip1-TAP, no cullin
    peptides PMID:19243310. RAVE-like complex: Skp1 co-IPs with Rav1 and with Rav2 in a Rav1-dependent way;
    rav1 loss gives V-ATPase defects PMID:18441123. CAK subunits Mcs2 and Pmh1 associate with Skp1 without
    Pcu1 and are not degraded PMID:15555586.

Decisions

  • Protein binding (30 IPI rows): F-box partners (Pop1, Pop2, Pof1-14, Fbh1) -> MODIFY to
    F-box domain binding (GO:1990444), adding ubiquitin ligase complex scaffold activity
    (GO:0160072) where a Pcu1-containing SCF is documented (Pop1, Pop2, Pof1, Pof2, Pof3,
    Pof14, Fbh1); Pof6 and the uncharacterised F-box proteins get GO:1990444 only. Pcu1
    rows -> MODIFY to cullin family protein binding (GO:0097602) + GO:0160072. Pip1/Rbx1,
    Mcs2, Pmh1 and Sip1 rows -> REMOVE as uninformative (complex membership or unknown
    function; interaction not disputed), following the protein-binding policy and the yeast
    SKP1 exemplar.
  • GO:0045841 negative regulation of mitotic metaphase/anaphase transition (IMP,
    PMID:15147872, abstract-only): UNDECIDED. The abstract describes a positive requirement
    for anaphase execution; the direction of the annotation cannot be checked without full
    text. Raised as a suggested question.
  • GO:0006998 and GO:0101026 (same paper): KEEP_AS_NON_CORE - phenotype real but
    rad3-dependent and F-box unknown.
  • GO:0000712 (meiosis, IMP): KEEP_AS_NON_CORE - follows the authors' wording; Fbh1-Skp1 is
    an anti-recombinase/helicase rather than a resolvase; flagged as a question.
  • GO:0000018 and GO:0017117 (Fbh1 paper, IDA): ACCEPT - Skp1 is a stoichiometric,
    obligate subunit of the purified active heterodimer.
  • GO:0030163 protein catabolic process (IMP, Cig2): MODIFY to GO:0031146.
  • RAVE rows (GO:0043291 x2, GO:0070072, GO:0012505): KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, as in yeast.
  • IBA rows (GO:0000278, GO:0005634, GO:0005737, GO:0031146, GO:0097602): ACCEPT.
  • No NEW terms. Comparator check: PomBase annotated pof6 to GO:0000920 septum digestion
    after cytokinesis from PMID:12511573 but did not annotate skp1 from the same paper,
    which is read as a deliberate curator decision; raised as a question instead.
  • Core functions: (1) SCF scaffold (GO:0160072, contributes to GO:0061630, in GO:0019005);
    (2) F-box-binding subunit of the Fbh1-Skp1 helicase heterodimer (GO:1990444, in
    GO:0017117, regulation of DNA recombination). Pof6/RAVE/CAK associations described in
    prose only.

description: 'Skp1 (Psh1/Sph1; SPBC409.05) is the small, essential adaptor subunit of Schizosaccharomyces pombe
  SCF (Skp1-Pcu1/cullin-1-F-box-Pip1/Rbx1) ubiquitin ligases, the ortholog of human SKP1 and budding-yeast Skp1.
  Its N-terminal BTB/POZ-like domain binds the cullin Pcu1 and its C-terminal helices bind the ~40-residue F-box
  motif of an interchangeable substrate receptor, so that Skp1 holds the receptor-bound, phosphorylated substrate
  in register with the Pip1-bound E2. Skp1 has no catalytic activity and does not itself select substrates; the
  receptor supplies specificity and the Pcu1-Pip1 module supplies the ubiquitin-transfer chemistry. Fission yeast
  has about seventeen F-box proteins and Skp1 co-purifies with most of them (Pop1, Pop2, Pof1-Pof14, Fbh1). Through
  SCF(Pop1/Pop2) it drives destruction of the CDK inhibitor Rum1 and the replication-licensing factor Cdc18 at G1/S,
  preventing re-replication and polyploidy; through SCF(Pof3) it turns over the histone-gene activator Ams2, the
  histone deacetylase Hst4 and DNA polymerase epsilon under replication stress, protecting genome and centromere
  integrity; through SCF(Pof1) it controls the Zip1 transcription factor; it is required for Cig2 cyclin instability
  in G2/M and for SCF-dependent autocatalytic turnover of F-box proteins such as Pof14. Skp1 also forms cullin-free
  complexes: the Fbh1-Skp1 heterodimer is a ssDNA-dependent ATPase/3''-5'' helicase that disrupts Rad51 filaments
  and, with Pcu1-Rbx1, ubiquitinates Rad51 to modulate homologous recombination in mitosis and meiosis; the essential
  Skp1-Pof6-Sip1 complex is needed for septum processing and cell separation; the RAVE-like Rav1-Rav2-Skp1 complex
  regulates V-ATPase assembly; and Skp1 associates with the CAK subunits Mcs2 and Pmh1 without promoting their degradation.
  Temperature-sensitive skp1 alleles that weaken F-box binding show a Rad3-dependent G2 delay, mitotic arrest with
  high Cdc2 activity, arched and collapsing anaphase spindles with unsegregated nuclear membranes, persistent recombination
  foci in meiosis and thick, unresolved septa. The protein is distributed between the nucleus and the cytoplasm.'

suggested_questions:
- question: PomBase annotated pof6 (but not skp1) to septum digestion after cytokinesis (GO:0000920) from Hermand
    et al. 2003, although skp1 deletion and skp1-3f cells show the same thick-septum, cell-separation phenotype
    and Skp1 is a stoichiometric subunit of the essential, cullin-free Skp1-Pof6-Sip1 complex. Was the omission
    deliberate (Skp1 regarded as a passive partner), and what does Skp1 contribute inside a complex that contains
    no cullin and no evident ubiquitin-ligase activity?
- question: Lehmann & Toda 2004 is annotated to negative regulation of mitotic metaphase/anaphase transition (GO:0045841,
    IMP), yet the abstract reports that skp1-A7 cells enter mitosis and fail to execute anaphase, and the mitotic
    phenotype is later shown to be rad3-dependent. Does the full text contain evidence that Skp1 restrains the transition
    (e.g. persistent Cdc13 or securin, premature APC/C activation), or should the annotation be re-expressed as
    a positive requirement for anaphase B spindle elongation / nuclear division?
- question: The meiotic annotation resolution of meiotic recombination intermediates (GO:0000712) follows the authors'
    wording, but Fbh1-Skp1 is a helicase/anti-recombinase that removes Rad51 and ubiquitinates it rather than a
    Holliday-junction resolvase. Would a term describing Rad51 filament disassembly or regulation of meiotic recombination
    better reflect the mechanism, and is the Skp1 role in meiosis exercised through SCF(Fbh1) ligase activity, the
    cullin-free heterodimer, or both?
- question: What is the function of the cullin-free Skp1 associations with the CAK subunits Mcs2 and Pmh1 (no degradation,
    no Pcu1)? Do they modulate Mcs6 kinase activity or specificity as proposed, and should they carry any molecular-function
    term beyond the removed protein-binding rows?
