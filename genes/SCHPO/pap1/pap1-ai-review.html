Gene Description

Pap1 (also caf3, Caffeine resistance protein 3) is the fission yeast AP-1-like basic-leucine-zipper (bZIP) transcription factor of the Yap1/c-Jun (YAP) subfamily. It is the principal redox-regulated transcriptional activator of the oxidative-stress response and a determinant of multidrug/caffeine resistance. Pap1 binds AP-1/TRE-like sites (consensus TTACGTAA) as a homodimer through its bZIP domain and bends the target DNA. Its activity is controlled by regulated nucleocytoplasmic shuttling: in unstressed cells reduced Pap1 is continuously exported to the cytoplasm by the exportin Crm1, whereas under low hydrogen peroxide the peroxiredoxin Tpx1 transmits a disulfide relay that oxidizes the cysteine-rich domains of Pap1 (forming an intramolecular Cys278-Cys501 disulfide) and masks its C-terminal nuclear export signal, causing nuclear accumulation. Nuclear Pap1 then activates antioxidant genes (catalase ctt1, thioredoxin reductase trr1, sulfiredoxin srx1, thioredoxin) - many in concert with the response regulator Prr1 - and drug-efflux/MDR genes (the ABC transporters bfr1/hba2 and pmd1, the efflux pump caf5, obr1). At high peroxide doses the Sty1/Atf1 MAP kinase pathway dominates the response. Pap1 also participates in the response to electrophiles such as diethylmaleate, methylglyoxal, and to diamide/cadmium-induced disulfide stress through a cooperative pathway with the HMG protein Oxs1.

Functional annotation report: Schizosaccharomyces pombe pap1

Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified as Pap1 (UniProt Q01663; gene pap1, synonym caf3, ORF SPAC1783.07c) from Schizosaccharomyces pombe strain 972. The literature’s description of Pap1 as a redox-regulated, AP-1-like basic-leucine-zipper (bZIP) transcription factor of the fungal Yap family agrees with the supplied UniProt family and domain annotations. Publications about PAP1-like symbols in plants, mammals, bacteria, or other fungi were not used as evidence for this protein.

Pap1 is not an enzyme or transporter. Its primary molecular function is sequence-specific transcriptional regulation: after relatively low oxidative stress, Pap1 accumulates in the nucleus and activates antioxidant, thiol-homeostasis, and multidrug-efflux genes. Its localization switch is controlled by oxidation of cysteine-rich regulatory regions, transmitted principally through the peroxiredoxin Tpx1, which suppresses Crm1-dependent nuclear export. At stronger peroxide stress, Pap1 activity declines and the Sty1–Atf1 program assumes a larger role. Thus, Pap1 is best annotated as a redox-sensitive transcriptional switch that couples peroxide sensing to nuclear gene expression.

1. Identity verification and domain consistency

The foundational S. pombe literature identifies Pap1 as a bZIP protein homologous to budding-yeast Yap1 and structurally/DNA-binding related to mammalian AP-1 proteins such as c-Jun. Pap1 binds AP-1-responsive promoter elements and activates transcription, directly supporting the supplied designation “AP-1-like transcription factor.” (toone1998regulationofthe pages 2-3, toone1998regulationofthe pages 1-2)

Its experimentally inferred architecture includes:

  • a bZIP region, providing sequence-specific DNA binding and dimerization;
  • N- and C-terminal cysteine-rich redox-regulatory regions;
  • a C-terminal nuclear-export signal, embedded in or adjacent to the redox-sensitive region and recognized by Crm1/exportin;
  • a Yap1-like redox regulatory mechanism involving reversible intramolecular disulfide formation. (latimer2017mechanismsofh₂o₂induced pages 51-55, toone1998regulationofthe pages 8-9)

These features align with the supplied InterPro annotations—AP-1-like, bZIP, TF_PAP1, and Yap1 redox-domain superfamily. The evidence therefore passes all mandatory identity checks: gene name, species, protein family, and functional domains are concordant.

2. Primary molecular function

Pap1 is a redox-responsive transcriptional activator. It does not itself destroy peroxide, catalyze thiol reactions, or transport xenobiotics. Rather, nuclear Pap1 binds AP-1-like promoter elements and stimulates genes whose products perform those activities. Pap1 also cooperates with the two-component response regulator Prr1; Prr1 is required for maximal expression of at least part of the Pap1 regulon. (latimer2017mechanismsofh₂o₂induced pages 51-55, dantas2011theroleof pages 83-86)

Established Pap1-responsive genes include:

  • trr1, encoding thioredoxin reductase;
  • trx2, encoding a thioredoxin;
  • ctt1, encoding catalase;
  • apt1;
  • hba2/bfr1 and pmd1, encoding energy-dependent multidrug-resistance transporters. (toone1998regulationofthe pages 2-3, toone1998regulationofthe pages 6-8, toone1998regulationofthe pages 3-5)

Expression experiments showed that oxidative induction of trr1 and ctt1 requires both Pap1 and Sty1, whereas trx2 displays partial dependence. The promoter and mutant evidence establishes a functional regulon, although not every transcriptionally Pap1-dependent gene has been proven to be a direct DNA-binding target. (toone1998regulationofthe pages 6-8, toone1998regulationofthe pages 2-3)

3. Redox-sensing mechanism

3.1 Basal state

In unstressed cells, Pap1 is predominantly cytoplasmic because it undergoes active Crm1-dependent nuclear export. The thioredoxins Trx1 and Txl1 help maintain Pap1 in a reduced state, preserving recognition of its nuclear-export signal. (latimer2017mechanismsofh₂o₂induced pages 51-55, toone1998regulationofthe pages 3-5)

3.2 Activation at low peroxide

Low or moderate H₂O₂ is relayed to Pap1 through Tpx1, the major 2-Cys peroxiredoxin. Tpx1’s thioredoxin-peroxidase activity is required for Pap1 oxidation; glutathione peroxidase Gpx3 is not required in this system. Pap1 therefore behaves less like a freely reacting peroxide sensor than the recipient of a peroxiredoxin-mediated redox relay. (latimer2017mechanismsofh₂o₂induced pages 51-55, dantas2011theroleof pages 83-86)

Mechanistic work supports reversible oxidation of cysteines in Pap1’s two cysteine-rich regions. An intramolecular linkage involving Cys278 and Cys501 has been reported, while Cys278, Cys285, and Cys532 are important for activation. Oxidation remodels the C-terminal export region, weakens productive recognition by Crm1, and causes Pap1 to accumulate in the nucleus. Nuclear Pap1 then binds target promoters and cooperates with Prr1 to activate transcription. (latimer2017mechanismsofh₂o₂induced pages 51-55)

This mechanism can be summarized as:

H₂O₂ → Tpx1 oxidation/redox relay → Pap1 intramolecular disulfides → impaired Crm1 export → nuclear Pap1 → antioxidant and efflux-gene transcription.

3.3 Sty1’s role and pathway separation

The 1998 study placed Pap1 downstream of or under the control of the Sty1/Spc1 stress-activated MAP kinase: sty1 loss blocked normal oxidative-stress-dependent Pap1 nuclear accumulation, while increased Wis1 pathway activity accelerated the response. However, Sty1 activation alone was insufficient; an oxidant-specific event was also needed. Subsequent redox-relay work explains that additional event as Tpx1-dependent Pap1 oxidation. Sty1’s contribution is therefore important but at least partly indirect, and Pap1 should not simply be described as a conventional direct MAPK substrate without qualification. (toone1998regulationofthe pages 8-9, toone1998regulationofthe pages 3-5, dantas2011theroleof pages 83-86)

4. Subcellular localization

Pap1 acts principally in two compartments:

  1. Cytoplasm under basal conditions: Pap1 continually exits the nucleus through the Crm1/exportin–Ran pathway.
  2. Nucleus during activating oxidative stress: oxidation inhibits export, producing nuclear retention and promoter occupancy.

The nuclear-export interpretation is supported by Pap1 localization experiments, constitutive nuclear accumulation in crm1 mutants, and dependence on Dcd1/Pim1, the Ran nucleotide-exchange machinery. It is regulated export—not simply increased nuclear import—that is the central localization switch. In one reported assay, 0.2 mM H₂O₂ for 30 minutes produced rapid nuclear relocalization. (toone1998regulationofthe pages 1-2, toone1998regulationofthe pages 8-9, toone1998regulationofthe pages 5-6)

Pap1 is therefore appropriately annotated as cytoplasmic/nuclear with stress-regulated nuclear accumulation, rather than as constitutively nuclear.

5. Dose-dependent pathway logic

A major feature of the S. pombe peroxide response is that Pap1 does not respond monotonically to increasing H₂O₂. Pap1 dominates transcription at low peroxide, whereas Atf1, controlled by the Sty1 MAPK pathway, dominates under high peroxide. Quinn and colleagues established this graded division experimentally in 2002. [Quinn et al., March 2002, Molecular Biology of the Cell, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.01-06-0288]. (quinn2002distinctregulatoryproteins pages 1-2)

The mechanistic explanation is that high peroxide hyperoxidizes and inhibits Tpx1’s peroxidase/redox-relay activity, thereby limiting Pap1 oxidation. Recovery involves Sty1-dependent induction of sulfiredoxin Srx1, which repairs hyperoxidized Tpx1. This architecture creates a stress-intensity discriminator: Pap1 handles adaptive low-ROS signaling, whereas Sty1–Atf1 supplies the stronger general stress response. (latimer2017mechanismsofh₂o₂induced pages 51-55, chen2008multiplepathwaysdifferentially pages 1-2)

A global analysis integrated 158 microarray experiments across oxidants, doses, and mutant backgrounds. It identified a small Pap1/Prr1-controlled core response shared among oxidative conditions and concluded that Pap1 dominates mild ROS responses while Sty1–Atf1 predominates under severe stress. [Chen et al., January 2008, Molecular Biology of the Cell, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e07-08-0735]. (chen2008multiplepathwaysdifferentially pages 1-2)

6. Biological processes and phenotypic evidence

Oxidative and disulfide-stress resistance

Deletion of pap1 causes hypersensitivity to H₂O₂, tert-butyl hydroperoxide, and diamide. The biochemical interpretation is coherent with the regulon: failure to induce catalase and thioredoxin-system genes impairs peroxide removal and restoration of reduced protein thiols. (toone1998regulationofthe pages 2-3)

Heavy-metal and xenobiotic resistance

pap1-deficient cells are sensitive to cadmium and arsenite and to drugs including staurosporine, cycloheximide, and anisomycin. Pap1-dependent induction of hba2/bfr1 and pmd1 connects the transcription factor mechanistically to energy-dependent drug efflux. The historical synonym caf3 (“caffeine-resistance protein 3”) is consistent with a broader chemical-stress phenotype, but the retrieved evidence is substantially stronger for oxidants, heavy metals, and the named cytotoxic drugs than for a precise caffeine-specific mechanism. (toone1998regulationofthe pages 2-3, toone1998regulationofthe pages 1-2, toone1998regulationofthe pages 5-6)

Relationship to Atf1

Pap1 and Atf1 are not interchangeable. Pap1 is specialized for redox-responsive antioxidant and efflux transcription, especially at low peroxide, while Sty1–Atf1 controls a broader environmental-stress program and assumes prominence at high peroxide. Some responses may show overlap or redundancy, including aspects of cisplatin/DNA-damage resistance, but this does not erase their distinct sensing ranges and target programs. (quinn2002distinctregulatoryproteins pages 1-2, chen2008multiplepathwaysdifferentially pages 1-2, toone1998regulationofthe pages 2-3)

7. Current applications and expert interpretation

Pap1 is primarily a research model, not a clinical or industrial product. Its principal applications are:

  • dissecting peroxiredoxin-mediated H₂O₂ signaling;
  • studying redox control of nuclear transport;
  • modeling how eukaryotic cells encode oxidant intensity through parallel transcriptional pathways;
  • identifying principles of fungal multidrug and metal resistance;
  • serving as a comparator for Yap/AP-1-family oxidative-stress regulators in pathogenic and industrial fungi. (latimer2017mechanismsofh₂o₂induced pages 51-55, chen2008multiplepathwaysdifferentially pages 1-2)

The strongest expert interpretation is that Pap1 is a thiol-based redox switch embedded in a systems-level dose discriminator. Its activation couples a chemical modification—reversible disulfide formation—to a physical localization decision—Crm1 export inhibition—and then to a transcriptional output. This is more precise than describing Pap1 merely as an “oxidative-stress gene.”

Pap1 and homologous fungal AP-1 circuits may inform antifungal strategies because they support oxidant tolerance and drug efflux. Nevertheless, direct therapeutic targeting of S. pombe Pap1 has not been established, and conclusions about pathogenic fungi require organism-specific validation rather than simple transfer from Q01663.

8. Recent research status, 2023–2024

A focused search prioritized 2023–2024 publications but found little directly focused primary work that materially revises the molecular annotation of Q01663. A 2023 dissertation addressed caffeine and antifungal resistance in S. pombe, but it did not displace the foundational Pap1 mechanism established between 1998 and 2008. No robust 2024 Pap1/Q01663-specific mechanistic paper was retrieved.

Accordingly, the current annotation remains anchored in authoritative primary studies of localization, dose dependence, and global transcription, supplemented by later redox-mechanism syntheses. This is a literature-availability limitation, not justification for substituting recent studies of similarly named proteins or AP-1 homologs from other organisms.

9. Evidence-weighted annotation

Recommended primary-function annotation:

Pap1 is a redox-sensitive AP-1-like bZIP transcriptional activator in Schizosaccharomyces pombe. At low-to-moderate H₂O₂, Tpx1-dependent oxidation of Pap1 cysteine-rich regions inhibits Crm1-mediated nuclear export, causing nuclear accumulation and activation of antioxidant, thiol-homeostasis, and multidrug-resistance genes. Pap1 primarily governs mild oxidative-stress adaptation, whereas Sty1–Atf1 predominates during severe peroxide stress.

Localization: cytoplasm under basal conditions; nucleus during activating oxidative or chemical stress.

Key pathway: H₂O₂/Tpx1–Pap1–Prr1 transcriptional response, functionally coordinated with but mechanistically distinguishable from the Sty1–Atf1 stress-activated MAPK pathway.

Evidence strength: high for identity, transcription-factor function, Crm1-regulated localization, oxidative-stress phenotypes, and named target genes; moderate-to-high for cysteine-resolved Tpx1 relay details; limited for a Pap1-specific 2023–2024 update and for direct real-world therapeutic implementation.

Pap1 (SCHPO, UniProt Q01663) — Direct Nucleosome-Binding Hypothesis Review

Hypothesis under evaluation: Schizosaccharomyces pombe Pap1 directly binds nucleosomes
as a biologically meaningful molecular function, rather than nucleosome association being an
incidental signal in the 2025 transcription-factor interaction atlas.

Focus type: free_text · Slug: direct-nucleosome-binding · Iteration: 1 of 3

Executive Judgment

Verdict: Partially supported / weakly supported (do not upgrade to a confident direct-MF claim).

The hypothesis contains two separable claims that resolve differently:

  1. "The nucleosome signal is not merely incidental atlas background." → Supported.
    In the source study (Skribbe et al., Molecular Cell 2025, PMID 40015273), core histones
    were pervasive contaminants co-purifying in all low-stringency IPs, including the untagged
    control. Critically, under high-stringency conditions the histone association was retained
    for only three transcription factors — Pap1, Atf1, and Pcr1 — out of the 89-TF library.
    Because the authors explicitly controlled for the generic histone background and Pap1 still
    scored, the signal is specific, not indiscriminate atlas noise.

  2. "Pap1 directly binds nucleosomes as a biologically meaningful molecular function
    (pioneering activity)."     → Weakly supported / unresolved. This is an inference, not a
    demonstrated activity. The evidence is co-immunoprecipitation/IP-MS, which cannot distinguish
    direct core-histone contact from a stable indirect/bridged association (e.g., Pap1 bound to its
    target DNA within a nucleosomal/chromatin context, carrying histones along). No reconstituted-
    nucleosome binding assay (EMSA, fluorescence anisotropy, NCAP-SELEX, Pioneer-seq) or functional
    nucleosome-displacement/pioneering assay has been performed for Pap1. The atlas authors
    themselves use suggestive language: the co-purification "indicates interactions with the
    nucleosome and suggests pioneering activity."

Most important caveat: Pap1's dominant, experimentally established molecular function is
sequence-specific promoter DNA binding as a bZIP (AP-1/Jun-Fos-like) oxidative-stress activator.
The nucleosome association must be interpreted against this backdrop; "direct nucleosome binding"
is currently a plausible-but-unproven secondary property carried largely by orthology analogy to
human Jun/Fos.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Immediate molecular activity tested: whether Pap1 makes a direct physical contact with the
    nucleosome core particle     (histone octamer ± nucleosomal DNA) as an intrinsic binding activity.
  • What the data actually show: Pap1 co-purifies with core histones under stringent IP
    conditions — a physical-association readout at the level of the assembled complex, not a
    reconstituted binary binding measurement.
  • Separated downstream/secondary layers (NOT the direct activity):
  • Transcriptional activation of oxidative-stress/drug-resistance regulons (BP consequence).
  • Nuclear accumulation via redox-controlled Crm1/NES switching (regulatory input, not nucleosome MF).
  • Increased efflux and drug resistance phenotypes (organismal/phenotypic downstream effects).
  • Chromatin-context effects on Pap1 occupancy (e.g., H2A.Z replacement enabling Pap1 access,
    PMID 34740252) — this frames Pap1 as a beneficiary of remodeling rather than a demonstrated
    pioneer that opens chromatin itself.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  1. Direct vs. bridged interaction (primary alternative). IP-MS cannot distinguish Pap1↔histone
    direct contact from Pap1 bound to nucleosomal target DNA that co-precipitates histones. Pap1's
    very strong, well-documented sequence-specific DNA binding makes the "carried along on chromatin"
    explanation a serious competitor. High stringency reduces but does not eliminate this ambiguity.
  2. Orthology overreach. The strongest positive framing (Jun/Fos bind nucleosomes in vitro) is
    an analogy across a >500 Myr divergence; pombe Pap1 has diverged CRDs and its own regulatory
    logic. Orthology motivates the hypothesis but is not evidence of the pombe activity.
  3. Chromatin facilitates Pap1, not vice versa. PMID 34740252 shows histone-variant replacement
    increases Pap1 promoter occupancy — consistent with Pap1 needing accessible chromatin rather
    than acting as a strong intrinsic nucleosome invader/pioneer.
  4. Shared-artifact possibility across bZIPs. Atf1/Pcr1 (an obligate heterodimer) and Pap1 all
    scoring could reflect a common bona fide bZIP property or a shared behavior (e.g., particularly
    stable chromatin residence) that co-retains histones under stringency without direct core contact.
  5. Database carry-over risk. Once GO:0031491 (EXP) is on the record, it can propagate to
    orthologs/paralogs by inference; curators should ensure downstream IBA/ISS annotations do not
    over-generalize an unvalidated pioneering activity.

pap1 (Q01663, SPAC1783.07c, caf3) — S. pombe AP-1-like bZIP transcription factor

Summary

Pap1 (Caffeine resistance protein 3; AP-1-like / Yap1/c-Jun family bZIP) is the principal redox-regulated
transcriptional activator of the oxidative-stress and multidrug-resistance responses in fission yeast.
Under low H2O2 it senses peroxide via the peroxiredoxin Tpx1, which forms a transient disulfide relay that
oxidizes the C-terminal cysteine-rich domain (c-CRD), forming an intramolecular interdomain disulfide
(Cys278–Cys501) that masks the Crm1-dependent nuclear export signal. Nuclear Pap1 binds AP-1/TRE-like
sites (consensus TTACGTAA) and induces antioxidant genes (ctt1 catalase, trr1, trx2, srx1) and
drug-efflux/MDR genes (bfr1/hba2, pmd1, caf5, obr1). At high H2O2 the Sty1/Atf1 pathway dominates.

Identity / family

  • AP-1-like bZIP transcription factor; bZIP family, YAP subfamily [UniProt Q01663].
  • "The other gene pap1+ encodes an AP-1-like transcription factor that contains a region rich in basic amino acids followed by a 'leucine zipper' motif." PMID:1899230
  • bZIP DNA-binding domain crystal structure (PDB 1GD2); binds novel consensus TTACGTAA. PMID:11017199
  • Homodimer (PubMed:11017199, UniProt SUBUNIT).

DNA binding / TF activity

  • Binds AP-1 site and a 14-bp palindrome by DNase I footprinting; activates target gene p25. PMID:1448080
  • Required for transcription activation: "p25 is overproduced when the pap1+ gene is overexpressed, whereas p25 is not produced at all in the pap1 deletion mutant." PMID:1448080
  • DNA bending: Pap1 bends DNA ~26° toward protein. PMID:9070843
  • Crystallographic characterization of Pap1-DNA complex (DNA-binding domain). PMID:9757124
  • Genome-wide TF atlas: ChIP-seq DNA binding sites and nucleosome binding (EXP). PMID:40015273

Oxidative stress / redox regulation

  • Sty1 regulates Pap1; both required for oxidative-stress and MDR gene expression. PMID:9585505
  • Genes induced incl. hba2/bfr1 and pmd1 (MDR transporters). PMID:9585505
  • H2O2-induced disulfide bond precedes nuclear accumulation. PMID:15165244
  • Tpx1 (2-Cys Prx) required for peroxide-induced Pap1 activation. PMID:15824112
  • H2O2 reversibly oxidizes Cys278 and Cys501; DEM non-reversibly modifies Cys523/Cys532. PMID:12100563
  • Pap1 + Prr1 heterodimer needed for antioxidant (but not drug-tolerance) genes. PMID:22344694
  • Methylglyoxal activates Pap1 via c-CRD; nuclear accumulation, target gene activation. PMID:16141205
  • Tpx1 hyperoxidation/Txl1 role in switching off early Pap1 response. PMID:22245228

Subcellular location & export control

  • Negatively regulated by Crm1/exportin 1. PMID:1448080
  • Oxidant-sensitive NES in c-CRD; Cys532 + two Leu + Ile important. PMID:10329722
  • Cytoplasm in unstressed cells; relocalizes to nucleus on oxidative stress (Sty1-dependent at high dose). PMID:9585505
  • Hba1 (Ran-binding) loss → constitutive nuclear Pap1, MDR. PMID:12896976
  • Genome-wide localization (cytosol/cytoplasm). PMID:16823372

Drug / caffeine / multidrug resistance

  • pap1 first isolated as staurosporine-resistance gene; required for spk1-conferred resistance. PMID:1899230
  • Int6 overexpression drug resistance is Pap1-dependent. PMID:11071922
  • Pap1 a key TF for fission yeast MDR. PMID:22840777
  • Genes required for caffeine tolerance; Pap1 and efflux pumps. PMID:19672306
  • pof14 transcription induced by H2O2 requires Pap1. PMID:17016471
  • ctt1 catalase induction by oxidative stress via Pap1 (element A). PMID:10731689

Disulfide stress / Oxs1

  • Pap1–Oxs1 pathway for diamide/Cd disulfide stress; Oxs1 + Pap1 form complex, co-required for promoter binding. PMID:27664222

Atf1 cross-talk

  • A subset of stress genes co-controlled by Atf1 and Pap1. PMID:28652406
