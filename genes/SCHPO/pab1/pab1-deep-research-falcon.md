# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe pab1* (UniProt Q12702)

## Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified as **Pab1, the sole canonical B-family/B55/PR55 regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A) in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe***, encoded by **pab1 / SPAC227.07c**. This is not a poly(A)-binding protein or another same-symbol PAB1 protein. The literature’s designation **PP2A-B55^Pab1**, the supplied UniProt description, and the PP2A_PR55/WD40 domain annotations are mutually consistent. In *S. pombe*, the scaffold subunit is Paa1 and the catalytic subunits are Ppa proteins; Pab1 is therefore **regulatory rather than catalytic**. (goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 1-2, grallert2015app1–pp2aphosphatase pages 20-21)

Pab1’s primary function is to assemble into a heterotrimeric PP2A holoenzyme and determine its substrate selection, regulation, and intracellular deployment. The catalytic subunit—not Pab1—hydrolyzes phosphate from phosphoserine/phosphothreonine residues. Pab1 directs this activity toward cell-cycle and nutrient-signaling networks, notably Wee1/Cdc25 control, mitotic phosphatase reactivation, cytokinesis restraint, and the TORC1–Greatwall–Endosulfine–PP2A-B55–TORC2 axis. (shah2022determiningthefunction pages 33-36, lucena2017wee1andcdc25 pages 4-8, martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 3-4, grallert2015app1–pp2aphosphatase pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

Direct fission-yeast literature identifies **pab1** as the organism’s single PP2A B-family regulatory-subunit gene and distinguishes it from **paa1**, encoding the A/scaffold subunit, and **ppa1/ppa2/ppa3**, encoding catalytic subunits. Pab1 is repeatedly called **B55**, **PR55**, or the **55-kDa B subunit**. These observations verify the supplied assignment to *S. pombe* strain 972 and support the UniProt Q12702 annotation. (goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 1-2, shah2022determiningthefunction pages 33-36)

The supplied domain architecture—PP2A_PR55 plus WD40-repeat and WD40/YVTN-like superfamily annotations—is also appropriate for a B55 subunit. B55 proteins form WD40-repeat scaffolds that bind the PP2A A/C core and recruit selected substrates. The recent structural literature confirms this general architecture, although the detailed interaction surfaces were characterized principally in other B55 orthologs rather than directly in purified Pab1. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 1-2, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 2-3)

**Ambiguity control:** “Pab1/PAB1” is used for unrelated proteins in other organisms, particularly poly(A)-binding proteins. Those records are not evidence about Q12702 and were excluded. No contradictory identification was found in the *S. pombe* PP2A literature.

## 2. Molecular and biochemical function

### 2.1 Regulatory—not catalytic—activity

PP2A holoenzymes contain an A scaffold, a C catalytic phosphoserine/phosphothreonine phosphatase, and a variable B regulatory subunit. Pab1 supplies the B55 component. Accordingly, it does **not independently catalyze a chemical reaction**. Its primary molecular role is to select and position phosphoprotein substrates for dephosphorylation by the associated catalytic subunit and to influence holoenzyme localization. (shah2022determiningthefunction pages 33-36, goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 1-2)

The operative reaction of a Pab1-containing complex is:

**phosphoprotein-O-phosphate + H₂O → dephosphorylated protein-OH + inorganic phosphate.**

The best-supported substrate class is phosphoserine/phosphothreonine-containing regulatory proteins, especially proteins phosphorylated during cell-cycle progression. Pab1 does not appear to provide a narrow small-molecule specificity comparable to a metabolic enzyme; specificity is generated through protein docking, holoenzyme context, cell-cycle regulation, and localization.

### 2.2 Structural basis of substrate specificity

A 2024 *Science Advances* study found that B55 WD40 proteins can recognize structurally diverse **α-helical docking elements** through a conserved surface containing hydrophobic and electrostatic patches. Common determinants include hydrophobic residues separated by one helical turn, at positions *i* and *i*+4. AlphaFold modeling, systematic mutagenesis, binding assays, and deep-learning protein design supported this mechanism, and a competitive peptide inhibitor was engineered to block B55–substrate docking. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 7-8, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 1-2, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 2-3, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 3-4)

This provides a strong mechanistic model for why Pab1’s WD40 architecture can confer substrate specificity. However, the exact residues, docking helices, and designed inhibitor were **not validated directly for Q12702**, so they should be treated as conserved-family inference rather than a completed Pab1 structural characterization. Alternative docking modes may also exist. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 7-8, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 4-5)

## 3. Cellular localization

Pab1 has been reported in the **cytoplasm, nucleus, and mitotic spindle**. This distribution is consistent with its dual roles in soluble nutrient-signaling pathways and spatially organized cell division. The retrieved localization evidence is a later summary of microscopy attributed to the cytokinesis literature; quantitative partitioning among compartments was not available. (shah2022determiningthefunction pages 33-36)

Thus, Pab1 is not best annotated as an organelle-restricted protein. Its functional location is dynamic and includes:

- **Nucleus:** regulation of nuclear cell-cycle phosphoproteins and mitotic transitions.
- **Cytoplasm:** TOR/nutrient signaling, stress responses, and soluble PP2A substrates.
- **Mitotic spindle/division apparatus:** coordination of mitotic progression and cytokinesis.

## 4. Cell-cycle function and experimentally supported substrates

### 4.1 Wee1 and Cdc25

The most precise Pab1-associated substrate evidence concerns the opposing Cdc2/CDK regulators **Wee1** and **Cdc25**. In synchronized cultures, deletion of *pab1* disrupted the normal cell-cycle-dependent mobility shifts of both proteins: Wee1 remained partially phosphorylated, while Cdc25 persisted in a hyperphosphorylated state. Protein-abundance oscillations remained largely intact, showing that the main defect was phosphoregulation rather than expression or degradation. (lucena2017wee1andcdc25 pages 4-8, lucena2017wee1andcdc25 pages 15-21)

Functionally, PP2A-B55^Pab1 supports the low-CDK state by maintaining the Wee1/Cdc25 control system. The exact consequences are site- and phase-dependent, so it is safer to state that Pab1 is required for their normal phosphorylation cycles than to assign every observed band shift to direct catalytic removal at a single residue. The study did test phosphatase sensitivity and Cdk1-site dependence, but direct reconstitution with purified Pab1 holoenzyme was not reported in the retrieved evidence. (lucena2017wee1andcdc25 pages 15-21)

A useful quantitative phenotype is that synchronized **pab1Δ cells required approximately 180 minutes per cycle, versus 120 minutes for wild type**, a 50% increase under the reported conditions. By comparison, *clp1Δ* lengthened the cycle by about 20 minutes. (lucena2017wee1andcdc25 pages 4-8)

### 4.2 Ordered PP1–PP2A phosphatase relay during mitosis

Pab1 also participates in a conserved mitotic phosphatase relay. Early in mitosis, Cdk1–cyclin B inhibits PP1^Dis2, including phosphorylation of Dis2 at T316. As cyclin B declines, Dis2 auto-reactivates and binds Pab1 through an RVxF/RxVxF-type docking site, reactivating PP2A-B55^Pab1. PP2A-B55 then dephosphorylates a PP1-docking region on B56/Par1, enabling PP1 recruitment and subsequent PP2A-B56 reactivation. (grallert2015app1–pp2aphosphatase pages 1-2)

This mechanism was supported by co-immunoprecipitation of Dis2 with Pab1 and loss of association after docking-site mutation. Holoenzyme analyses indicated that the mutations did not simply destroy PP2A complex assembly. Pab1 is therefore an active regulatory node in an ordered **PP1 → PP2A-B55^Pab1 → PP2A-B56^Par1** sequence coordinating mitotic progression and exit. (grallert2015app1–pp2aphosphatase pages 20-21, grallert2015app1–pp2aphosphatase pages 1-2)

## 5. Cytokinesis and the septation-initiation system

Pab1-containing PP2A acts primarily as a **brake on premature cytokinesis/septation**. During prolonged metaphase arrest induced by repression of the APC/C activator Slp1, fission-yeast cells can eventually initiate septation despite high cyclin B, condensed chromosomes, and incomplete chromosome segregation. PP2A-B55^Pab1 normally restrains this inappropriate response; Pab1 loss or inhibition permits frequent untimely septation. (chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 17-18)

In the 2022 study, Slp1 was repressed with **15 µM thiamine for 6 hours**. Approximately **60%** of relevant phosphatase-mutant populations were metaphase-arrested at one hour, yet septation still occurred, demonstrating that persistent mitotic phosphorylation alone does not prevent cytokinesis. The results implicate PP2A-dependent controls beyond bulk CDK-substrate dephosphorylation. (chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 6-7, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 17-18)

Mechanistically, high mitotic CDK activity activates the Ppk18–Igo1/Greatwall–Endosulfine module, which inhibits PP2A-B55. This feedback can remove the Pab1-dependent cytokinesis brake during sustained arrest. Both PP2A-B55^Pab1 and PP2A-B56^Par1 contribute to mitotic dephosphorylation, but they are not interchangeable: the B55 complex is especially associated with suppression of untimely septation, whereas B56 has additional roles in transcriptional and cytokinetic competence. (chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 6-7, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 17-18)

Earlier genetic work also places Pab1 antagonistically to Etd1 in cytokinesis/SIN control. The available evidence strongly supports pathway regulation, but it does not identify one definitive Pab1-dephosphorylated SIN substrate. Consequently, annotation should state **negative regulation of septation/cytokinesis and SIN output**, not claim a uniquely established direct SIN target.

## 6. Nutrient signaling, TOR crosstalk, and differentiation

Pab1 is a key biochemical link between nutrient availability and cell fate. Under nitrogen-rich conditions, **TORC1 is active**, the Greatwall–Endosulfine switch is relatively inactive, and PP2A-B55^Pab1 activity remains high. Nitrogen limitation reduces TORC1 signaling, activates Greatwall-family kinases, and promotes phosphorylation of Endosulfine/Igo1. Phosphorylated Igo1 inhibits PP2A-B55, permitting increased TORC2–Gad8 signaling, G1 arrest, mating, and sexual differentiation. (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9)

Direct genetic and signaling evidence places Pab1 downstream of TORC1. Homothallic *pab1Δ* cells mated earlier and more extensively than wild type. Loss of Pab1 suppressed differentiation defects caused by Tor2/TORC1 overexpression, restored *mei2* induction, and enhanced pheromone signaling. Following nitrogen removal, phosphorylated Spk1 peaked by **45 minutes in pab1Δ**, whereas wild type showed only a faint signal after **3 hours**. (martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 3-4)

Transcriptome analysis found **240 genes upregulated at least threefold in pab1Δ** and **88 genes enriched in wild type**. The induced set was enriched for reproduction, meiosis, conjugation, nitrogen-depletion, and rapamycin/caffeine-response functions. These data show that Pab1 is not merely a mitotic phosphatase subunit; it is a nutrient-responsive repressor of the differentiation program. (martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 3-4)

A 2024 review synthesizes the current model: TORC1 and TORC2 exert opposing influences over G1 arrest and sexual differentiation, with PP2A-B55^Pab1 negatively regulating Gad8 through the Greatwall–Endosulfine pathway. This is currently the most authoritative recent overview specific to fission-yeast sexual differentiation. (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9)

## 7. Recent 2024 development: quiescence and translational control

A December 2024 *Nature Communications* study extended the pathway into translational control during nitrogen-starvation-induced quiescence. TORC1 downregulation activates Greatwall–Endosulfine, lowers PP2A-B55 activity, and enables TORC2 activation through increased Gad8 phosphorylation. TORC2 then supports Elongator-dependent modifications in the anticodon loops of tRNA^Lys_UUU, tRNA^Glu_UUC, and tRNA^Gln_UUG, including U34 modifications. (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2)

These modifications improve translation efficiency and fidelity for transcripts enriched in AAA relative to AAG lysine codons. The favored transcripts include factors that inhibit TORC1, activate TORC2, modify tRNA, and support telomeric/subtelomeric functions, thereby creating a reinforcing adaptation to nitrogen stress. In contrast, *igo1Δ*, which leaves PP2A-B55 relatively active, reduced translation of AAA-enriched transcripts during entry into quiescence. Measurements included an **8-hour nitrogen-starvation** condition. (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2)

This study directly establishes a PP2A-B55-dependent pathway in *S. pombe*, but the retrieved excerpt did not isolate Pab1 biochemically at every downstream step. Given that Pab1 is the canonical B55 subunit, the pathway is highly relevant to its annotation, while individual tRNA-modification enzymes should not be labeled as direct Pab1 substrates without further evidence.

## 8. Additional processes and candidate substrates

Proteomic and affinity-purification studies have proposed **Gdp1, Tdh1, Sks2, Fba1, and Sam1** as PP2A-associated or candidate substrates. Among these, Gdp1 has stronger Pab1-linked evidence: it physically interacts with PP2A, and PP2A-Pab1-dependent dephosphorylation is implicated in osmotic-stress function. Loss of Pab1 causes defective Gdp1 phosphoregulation, aberrant glycerol elevation, and osmosensitivity. Nevertheless, the other candidate proteins should remain annotated as provisional because the available evidence does not prove that each is directly recognized by Pab1 rather than another PP2A complex. (shah2022determiningthefunction pages 33-36)

Deletion of *pab1* has also been associated with altered morphology, cold sensitivity, cytoskeletal defects, flocculation, cytokinesis defects, and genome-instability-related phenotypes. These are biologically meaningful but are broader downstream consequences. They should not displace the more precise primary annotation: **Pab1 directs PP2A substrate specificity in cell-cycle and nutrient-responsive signaling**. (shah2022determiningthefunction pages 33-36, goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 1-2)

## 9. Current applications and real-world relevance

There is no direct clinical or industrial application of *S. pombe pab1* itself in the retrieved literature. Its principal application is as a **model-system regulator** for understanding conserved PP2A-B55 biology:

1. **Cell-cycle research:** Pab1 provides a genetically tractable system for studying how phosphatases oppose CDK activity, order mitotic exit, and prevent cytokinesis before chromosome segregation. (lucena2017wee1andcdc25 pages 4-8, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 17-18, grallert2015app1–pp2aphosphatase pages 1-2)
2. **Nutrient and aging biology:** The Pab1 pathway connects TORC1, TORC2, differentiation, quiescence, and translation, making it useful for dissecting how nutrient state changes cell fate. (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2)
3. **Selective PP2A modulation:** The 2024 identification of conserved helical B55-docking surfaces and a substrate-competitive peptide establishes a route toward holoenzyme-selective perturbation rather than nonspecific inhibition of all PP2A activity. Translation to Pab1 requires direct binding and structural validation. (kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 7-8, kruse2024substraterecognitionprinciples pages 1-2)
4. **Stress and genome-stability models:** Pab1-dependent regulation of osmotic stress and genetic interaction with SUMO/DDR systems provides experimental access to phosphatase control of stress adaptation, although direct substrates remain incompletely resolved. (shah2022determiningthefunction pages 33-36)

## 10. Evidence-weighted annotation

**Recommended primary molecular-function annotation:**

> Pab1 is the noncatalytic B55/PR55 regulatory subunit of *S. pombe* PP2A. Through its WD40-repeat scaffold, it assembles with PP2A A and C subunits and directs phosphoserine/phosphothreonine dephosphorylation toward selected protein substrates.

**Recommended biological-process annotation:**

> Pab1 regulates mitotic entry and exit, Wee1/Cdc25 phosphorylation, ordered reactivation of PP1/PP2A phosphatases, and suppression of premature septation. It also couples nutrient-responsive TORC1–Greatwall–Endosulfine signaling to TORC2/Gad8, sexual differentiation, and entry into quiescence.

**Recommended localization annotation:**
