# Research report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe sid2* (UniProt Q09898)

## Executive summary

The requested protein is correctly identified as **Sid2**, encoded by *sid2* (systematic ORF **SPAC24B11.11c**) in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* strain 972. The supplied UniProt accession **Q09898**, serine/threonine-protein-kinase annotation (EC 2.7.11.1), and protein-kinase/AGC-family domains agree with the literature: Sid2 is an essential **NDR/LATS-family AGC serine/threonine kinase**, functions with the coactivator Mob1, and is the terminal kinase of the fission-yeast **septation initiation network (SIN)**. The retrieved primary literature independently confirms the organism, symbol, kinase class, Mob1 relationship, and SIN function, although accession Q09898 and ORF SPAC24B11.11c were not printed in the retrieved article excerpts and therefore remain accession-level identifiers supplied by UniProt rather than independently re-established here. No similarly named protein from another organism was substituted. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2, ozdemir2022metabolicregulationofa pages 29-31, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3)

Sid2 converts a mitotic signal assembled at spindle-pole bodies (SPBs) into spatially organized phosphorylation at the SPBs and medial contractile ring. Its principal biological output is timely actomyosin-ring maintenance and constriction, septum formation, and resetting of division-site machinery after cytokinesis. It also feeds back on the SIN scaffold Cdc11 to stabilize maximal, asymmetric anaphase signaling. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3)

## 1. Molecular identity, catalytic reaction, and substrate specificity

Sid2 is a protein kinase rather than a transporter or structural protein. Its generic reaction is:

**ATP + protein serine/threonine → ADP + phosphoprotein serine/threonine.**

Its kinase-like, protein-kinase ATP-binding, catalytic protein-kinase, protein-kinase C-terminal, and AGC-kinase C-terminal domains are fully consistent with an NDR/LATS-family AGC kinase. NDR/LATS kinases use a conserved N-terminal regulatory region to bind a Mob-family cofactor; structural work on this kinase class supports a model in which Mob organizes the regulatory region and hydrophobic-motif activation segment to stimulate the kinase domain. In *S. pombe*, Sid2 partners specifically with Mob1, and Mob1 is required for normal Sid2 localization. (ozdemir2022metabolicregulationofa pages 29-31)

The experimentally used minimal Sid2 substrate consensus is **RXXS**, with serine as the best-established phosphoacceptor; the broader NDR/LATS family can also recognize related RXXS/T sequences. This motif is informative but not sufficient by itself to establish a physiological substrate—localization, cell-cycle timing, docking, biochemical phosphorylation, and phosphosite-mutant phenotypes provide the stronger evidence. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 3-4, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3)

## 2. Position and regulation within the SIN

The SIN is a GTPase-regulated kinase cascade assembled on the Cdc11–Sid4 scaffold at SPBs. The conventional sequence is **Spg1-GTP → Cdc7 → Sid1–Cdc14 → Sid2–Mob1**, followed by Sid2 action at SPBs and the division site. Genetic epistasis, localization, and kinase-activity experiments strongly support this ordering, although authoritative syntheses caution that every activation step has not been reconstructed biochemically; the pathway should therefore not be interpreted as a completely proven chain of direct kinase-to-kinase reactions. (ozdemir2022metabolicregulationofa pages 29-31, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

Sid2 is not merely a terminal passive output. It phosphorylates Cdc11 and thereby creates positive feedback that promotes Cdc7 association with SPBs, maximal SIN activity during anaphase, and appropriate asymmetric signaling between the two SPBs. Counteracting phosphatase activity is thought to help remove Cdc11 phosphorylation at the old SPB. This feedback architecture explains how a scaffolded kinase cascade can become both strongly activated and spatially asymmetric. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 6-7)

## 3. Cellular localization

Sid2 is an **intracellular, dynamically localized kinase**. It first associates with SPBs, where it participates in scaffolded SIN signaling. Its SPB localization is reported to be independent of Spg1, distinguishing physical recruitment from complete catalytic activation. Sid2–Mob1 subsequently appears at the medial contractile actomyosin ring/division site; this medial localization depends on upstream SIN components and coincides with the transition to ring constriction and septation. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2)

At the division site, Sid2 is positioned to phosphorylate ring-associated and cortical substrates. Live imaging measured Sid2-GFP residence there for **28.75 ± 1.1 min** in wild type. Residence fell to **22.75 ± 0.6 min** in the nonphosphorylatable *cdc11-S5A* background and was **30.50 ± 0.9 min** in phosphomimetic *cdc11-S5D*, linking SPB feedback to the duration of division-site signaling. Sid2 arrives as the anillin-like protein Mid1 is removed from the division-site cortex, consistent with Sid2 directly driving that transition. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 3-4, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3)

## 4. Direct substrates and mechanistic outputs

### 4.1 Cdc11: feedback control of the SIN scaffold

Cdc11 is among the best-supported direct substrates. Immunopurified Sid2–Mob1 phosphorylated Cdc11 fragments in vitro, and mass spectrometry identified five major functional target residues: **S121, S122, S264, S345, and S418**. Alanine substitution markedly reduced phosphorylation. The *cdc11-S5A* allele accelerated Cdc7 asymmetry, shortened Cdc7 retention at the old SPB, impaired cytokinesis, and interacted adversely with conditional *cdc7* alleles; phosphomimetic *cdc11-S5D* did not show the same growth defect. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 3-4)

From SPB separation/ring formation to Cdc15-GFP disappearance, cytokinesis required **48 min in wild type, 57 min in cdc11-S5A, and 50 min in cdc11-S5D**. These measurements support a causal model in which Sid2 phosphorylation of Cdc11 prolongs productive SIN signaling rather than simply marking an active pathway. Reported comparisons used approximately 10–27 cells and included significance values of *p*=0.0007, 0.0002, and 0.0137 for measured localization/timing differences. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 3-4, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 6-7)

### 4.2 Mid1/anillin: removal and resetting of division-site machinery

Mid1 is another high-confidence direct substrate. Mid1 contains nine experimentally studied Sid2-consensus serines: **S110, S127, S218, S227, S329, S432, S452, S464, and S531**. Seven were detected by mass spectrometry in purified Mid1, an eighth had appeared in another phosphoproteomic screen, and immunopurified Sid2 phosphorylated a wild-type Mid1 fragment in vitro but not the corresponding nine-alanine mutant. (willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 6-8)

Sid2 phosphorylation removes Mid1 from the cortex as constriction begins and weakens its association with the cortical-node kinase Cdr2. Nonphosphorylatable Mid1-9A remained cortical, accumulated in interphase nodes, recruited ring components prematurely, advanced G2/M, and delayed ring maturation and constriction. Cdr2 co-immunoprecipitated about **twofold more** Mid1-9A than wild-type Mid1; similarly, SIN-inactive *sid2-250* cells showed about a twofold increase in Mid1–Cdr2 association. Cdr2 was recruited to approximately **75%** of Mid1-9A contractile rings, versus only approximately **18%** weak Cdr2/Rlc1 colocalization in wild type. In sensitized node-defective backgrounds, Mid1-9A produced tilted or off-center rings, demonstrating that Sid2 helps erase the preceding division-site state and preserve medial placement in the next cycle. (willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 5-6)

### 4.3 Cdc12 formin: control of actin organization

Cdc12 is a literature-supported direct ring substrate. Sid2-dependent phosphorylation inhibits inappropriate Cdc12 multimerization and the associated F-actin-bundling state. When Sid2-target sites cannot be phosphorylated, Cdc12 persists in clusters and cytokinetic rings become prone to collapse when division is delayed. Thus, Sid2 does not simply activate ring contraction; it restrains a potentially harmful oligomeric state of the formin to maintain ring architecture. The retrieved evidence supports this relationship but did not provide the primary study’s complete site-level quantitative dataset, so confidence is high for the mechanism but lower for unquoted residue-specific details. (sinha2023ageneticstudy pages 24-29)

### 4.4 Clp1/Cdc14 phosphatase

Clp1 is reported as a Sid2 substrate whose phosphorylation promotes cytoplasmic retention, linking SIN activation to persistence of a late-mitotic phosphatase outside the nucleus. This helps coordinate mitotic exit and cytokinesis. A useful distinction is that Clp1 directly dephosphorylates Cdk1-dependent sites on Cdc11; the retrieved later study did **not** indicate that Clp1 removes the Cdc11 Sid2 sites. In that study, cytokinesis failure occurred in **3% of clp1-null**, **18% of csc1-null**, and **35% of double-null** cells at 36 °C, illustrating cooperation between phosphatase systems rather than a simple Sid2–Clp1 reversal pair. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2, chen2013comprehensiveproteomicsanalysis pages 7-8, chen2013comprehensiveproteomicsanalysis pages 13-13)

### 4.5 Additional reported substrates and crosstalk

Cdr2 has functional phosphosite evidence: combining *cdr2-2A* and *mid1-9A* caused off-center septa although either single mutant did not, supporting cooperative Sid2-dependent resetting of node machinery. Klp2 is also named as a Sid2 substrate linked to spindle regulation, but the retrieved excerpts lacked equivalent biochemical details. (willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 6-8, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 5-6)

Nak1 and Sog1 have been reported as Sid2 targets connecting SIN to the competing morphogenesis-related NDR network, while Fin1/NIMA kinase is reported to be activated by Sid2, linking cytokinesis signaling to Pom1–Wee1 control of later mitotic commitment. These are biologically credible and tied to named primary studies, but because the relevant primary experimental passages were unavailable here, they should be treated as **literature-supported relationships rather than revalidated site-level conclusions**. Proposed effects on Cdc7, Byr4, and Plo1 in the SIN feedback loop are still less secure as direct-substrate assignments. (ozdemir2022metabolicregulationof pages 122-124, ozdemir2022metabolicregulationofa pages 122-124, sinha2023ageneticstudy pages 24-29, feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 7-7)

## 5. Biological function and phenotypic interpretation

The primary function of Sid2 is to execute and spatially coordinate late cytokinesis. Its phosphorylation program:

1. reinforces asymmetric SIN signaling at SPBs through Cdc11;
2. preserves contractile-ring architecture through Cdc12 regulation;
3. promotes ring constriction and septum formation;
4. removes Mid1/Cdr2-associated cortical machinery as division progresses;
5. coordinates cytokinesis with phosphatase localization, spindle regulation, cell separation, and restoration of interphase growth programs. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2, sinha2023ageneticstudy pages 24-29, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3)

Loss of Sid2 or Mob1 can block or delay cytokinesis and may cause lysis during division. The phenotypic diversity of conditional alleles cautions against defining Sid2 solely by the classic multinucleate phenotype of upstream SIN mutants. Likewise, overexpression of *sid2* is not by itself strongly toxic, unlike overactivation of Spg1 or Cdc7, implying that Sid2 activity is constrained by Mob1, localization, upstream activation, and cell-cycle context. (ozdemir2022metabolicregulationofa pages 29-31)

## 6. Recent research and current understanding (2023–2024)

The 2023–2024 literature located for this report largely consolidates Sid2 within increasingly detailed models of fission-yeast cytokinesis rather than overturning its established molecular function. Two 2024 reviews emphasize that contractile-ring assembly, maturation, constriction, septum deposition, septin organization, and membrane remodeling are coordinated processes, with SIN activity—and Sid2 at the ring—serving as a central regulatory axis. Relevant publications are Zheng, Zheng & Fu, **“The Roles of Septins in Regulating Fission Yeast Cytokinesis,” January 2024**, DOI [10.3390/jof10020115](https://doi.org/10.3390/jof10020115), and Rezig, Yaduma & McInerny, **“Processes Controlling the Contractile Ring during Cytokinesis in Fission Yeast, Including the Role of ESCRT Proteins,” February 2024**, DOI [10.3390/jof10020154](https://doi.org/10.3390/jof10020154).

A 2023 synthesis also places Sid2–Mob1 downstream of Cdc7 and Sid1 and highlights Cdc12, Klp2, Nak1, and Sog1 as outputs connecting cytokinesis, spindle behavior, and MOR-network suppression. However, its Sid2 statements mostly synthesize earlier mechanistic studies rather than report a new purified-kinase or structural analysis. (sinha2023ageneticstudy pages 24-29)

Accordingly, the most precise current interpretation is that **recent work has broadened the cellular context, whereas substrate-defining evidence remains concentrated in foundational primary studies from 2008–2019**. Absence of a Sid2-focused 2023–2024 paper should not be misconstrued as uncertainty about the core annotation; Cdc11 and Mid1 phosphorylation, SPB-to-ring localization, and terminal SIN function are strongly established.

## 7. Applications and real-world relevance

Sid2 itself has no established clinical or industrial implementation. Its current application is principally as a **research tool and conserved-pathway model**:

- *S. pombe* Sid2 enables live-cell analysis of how kinase signals move from centrosome-equivalent SPBs to the cleavage apparatus.
- Conditional *sid2* alleles and RXXS-site mutants permit temporal and substrate-specific dissection of cytokinesis.
- Sid2–Mob1 provides a tractable fungal counterpart of conserved NDR/LATS–Mob signaling, helping explain general principles relevant to metazoan Hippo signaling, proliferation control, and cytokinesis.
- Quantitative fluorescence, phosphoproteomics, and phosphomutants make Sid2 useful for distinguishing pathway activation from kinase localization and for modeling feedback-generated signaling asymmetry. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 3-4, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3)

The translational relevance is therefore conceptual rather than direct: cytokinesis failure can generate multinucleate or genomically unstable cells, and NDR/LATS signaling is broadly conserved, but findings about Sid2 should not be equated automatically with a therapeutic target in humans.

## 8. Evidence assessment and remaining questions

**High-confidence annotation:** Sid2 is an essential Mob1-associated NDR/LATS-family serine/threonine kinase, terminal SIN component, and intracellular regulator at SPBs and the medial contractile ring. Its best-established consensus is RXXS, and its primary function is cytokinesis/septation. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 1-2, ozdemir2022metabolicregulationofa pages 29-31, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3)

**Strongest substrate evidence:** Cdc11 and Mid1 have convergent in-vitro phosphorylation, mass-spectrometric site mapping, phosphosite mutants, quantitative imaging, and functional phenotypes. Cdc12 and Clp1 are accepted direct targets in the literature, although fewer details from their primary experiments were recoverable in this search. (feoktistova2012thefissionyeast pages 3-4, willet2019ndrkinasesid2 pages 1-3, chen2013comprehensiveproteomicsanalysis pages 13-13)

**Important unresolved issues:** a complete quantitative Sid2 substrate atlas is lacking; RXXS occurs by chance in many proteins; the biochemical mechanism by which each upstream SIN module activates Sid2–Mob1 remains incompletely reconstructed; and several reported targets need modern site-resolved validation. It also remains important to separate Sid2’s direct catalytic outputs from secondary changes caused by failed cytokinesis or altered cell-cycle progression.
