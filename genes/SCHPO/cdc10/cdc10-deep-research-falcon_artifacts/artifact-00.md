| Identity | **Cdc10 is the essential ~85-kDa MBF subunit from *Schizosaccharomyces pombe***, consistent with UniProt **P01129**, gene **cdc10**, ORF **SPBC336.12c**. | Primary molecular function | Cdc10 is a **nonenzymatic transcription-regulatory/core scaffold subunit** of MluI cell-cycle-box binding factor (**MBF**), required to activate the G1/S transcriptional program and permit DNA replication. | Cdc10 depletion reduced *cdc18* mRNA by **50% within 2 h** and shifted cells from 2N to 1N DNA content within **6 h**. | Direct depletion, transcript, and flow-cytometry evidence; **high confidence**. | Ayté et al., 1995, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/mcb.15.5.2589) (pqac-00000002) |
| DNA recognition | MBF recognizes promoter MCB motifs, consensus **ACGCGT**, but the best-supported model is that **Res1 and Res2 provide sequence-specific DNA recognition**, whereas Cdc10 stabilizes and regulates the complex rather than binding DNA directly. | Res1’s N-terminal 157 residues were sufficient for specific *cdc22*-promoter binding; its C-terminal region mediated Cdc10 association. | Domains and architecture | The supplied InterPro calls—**ankyrin repeats**, SWI6-like architecture, and KilA/APSES-related HTH superfamily—fit a fungal G1/S transcription-factor protein. | Cellular localization | Cdc10 acts **in the nucleus at chromatin-bound MBF target promoters**. Nuclear localization is strongly implied by promoter ChIP and transcription-factor function, but the retrieved evidence does not provide quantitative live-cell localization imaging. | ChIP detected Cdc10 at promoters such as *cdc18* and *cdc22*; Cdc10–Res1–Res2 occupancy was observed throughout the cell cycle. | Direct chromatin-localization evidence, but cellular imaging evidence not retrieved; **high confidence for nuclear chromatin function, moderate confidence for detailed spatial dynamics**. | Eshaghi et al., 2011, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/ec.00218-10); Ivanova et al., 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e13-05-0257) (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000009) |
| Key targets | The Cdc10-containing MBF program includes DNA-replication and S-phase genes such as ***cdc18, cdt1, cdc22*** and ***cig2***. Roughly half of the 80 reported sites were not periodically transcribed. | Direct promoter, expression, and genome-wide occupancy evidence; **high confidence**, with target count assay-dependent. | Eshaghi et al., 2011, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/ec.00218-10); Ivanova et al., 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e13-05-0257) (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000004) |
| Essential phenotype | Cdc10 is required for **Start completion and G1-to-S entry**. Loss causes a terminal G1/Start arrest and loss of viability. | After Cdc10 repression in a null background, cells developed a cdc phenotype within **6 h**, shifted to 1N DNA content, and appeared nonviable by **18 h**; expression rescued growth at **35.5°C**. | Direct conditional-null, complementation, and flow-cytometry evidence; **high confidence**. | Ayté et al., 1995, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/mcb.15.5.2589) (pqac-00000002) |
| Cell-cycle regulation | Cdc10, Res1, and Res2 form a relatively stable promoter-associated MBF core; periodic output is controlled mainly through regulatory partners and post-translational mechanisms rather than oscillation in Cdc10 abundance or wholesale cyclical promoter binding. Rep2 functions as an activator, whereas Yox1/Nrm1 provide repression. | Cdc10 overexpression did not abolish periodic transcription; Cdc10–Res1–Res2 occupied targets through the cycle, while Rep2 recruitment was temporally dynamic. The constitutively active **cdc10-C4** allele lacks 61 C-terminal residues. | Direct ChIP/expression and mutant evidence; **high confidence**. | Eshaghi et al., 2011, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1128/ec.00218-10); Ivanova et al., 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e13-05-0257) (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000004, pqac-00000005) |
| DNA-replication checkpoint | During replication stress, **Cds1 activates MBF chiefly by phosphorylating and removing repressors**, especially Yox1 and Nrm1, thereby maintaining S-phase transcription. Direct Cds1 phosphorylation of Cdc10 is not required for this response. | Cds1 phosphorylated Yox1 at **Ser114/Thr115**; a nonphosphorylatable **cdc10-8A** allele still induced transcription after hydroxyurea. A phosphomimetic **cdc10-2E** allele caused constitutive upregulation, HU resistance, and partial rescue of *cds1Δ* lethality. | Direct mutant, phosphorylation, and expression evidence; **high confidence**, with mechanistic emphasis on Yox1/Nrm1. | Ivanova et al., 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e13-05-0257) (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010) |
| DNA-damage checkpoint | Under DNA damage, activated **Chk1 phosphorylates Cdc10’s C-terminal region**, promotes release of MBF from chromatin, and represses G1/S transcription—opposite to the replication-checkpoint de-repression response. | Substitution of candidate Chk1 sites **S720A/S732A** in Cdc10.2A prevented normal Cdc10 release from the *cdc18* promoter after MMS and produced a similar defect after ionizing radiation; the mutant was slightly CPT-sensitive but not detectably MMS- or IR-sensitive in the reported assays. | Direct site-directed mutant, ChIP, genetic, and survival evidence; **high confidence for pathway mechanism**, modest phenotype magnitude. | Ivanova et al., 2013, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e13-05-0257) (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) |
