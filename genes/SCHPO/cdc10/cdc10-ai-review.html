Gene Description

cdc10 encodes the 767-residue, ~85 kDa Start control protein Cdc10, the essential core subunit of the fission-yeast MBF (MluI cell-cycle-box binding factor, also called DSC1) transcription factor and the counterpart of budding-yeast Swi6. Cdc10 has no demonstrated sequence-specific DNA-binding activity of its own: recognition of the MCB element (ACGCGT) in target promoters is performed by the APSES domains of its two alternative partners, Res1 and Res2, whose C-terminal regions bind Cdc10, and the isolated Res1 DNA-binding domain can even bypass the need for cdc10. Cdc10 carries a divergent APSES-like N-terminal region, a central block of ankyrin repeats that mediates protein-protein interactions, and a C-terminal regulatory domain. Bound with Res1 and Res2 at MCB promoters throughout the cell cycle, Cdc10 is the platform on which the periodic output of MBF is assembled: the zinc-finger activator Rep2 (Rep1 in meiosis) supplies transcriptional activation, while the Cdc10 C-terminal 61 residues are required to load the MBF-encoded corepressors Nrm1 and Yox1 that silence the regulon outside G1/S, so that the cdc10-C4 truncation expresses MCB genes constitutively. At Start, MBF activates Pol II transcription of the replication-licensing genes cdc18 and cdt1, the ribonucleotide reductase gene cdc22, the S-phase cyclin gene cig2, cdt2, ctp1 and some twenty further DNA-synthesis and repair genes, and the same Cdc10-Res2-Rep1/Rep2 machinery drives MCB-dependent transcription of rec8, rec11, cdc18 and cdc22 during pre-meiotic S phase; loss of cdc10 arrests cells in G1 with unreplicated DNA. Cig2-Cdc2 phosphorylation of Res1 closes a negative-feedback loop, and pheromone arrests cells downstream of a fully active Cdc10-Res1 factor by inhibiting the G1 CDK. The Cdc10 C terminus is also the point at which the genome-integrity checkpoints act on the G1/S program: under replication stress Cds1 phosphorylation of the Yox1/Nrm1 repressors (and, sufficiently but not necessarily, of Cdc10 Ser720/Thr723) keeps MBF transcription on, whereas after DNA damage Chk1 phosphorylates Cdc10 on Ser720 and Ser732, releasing intact MBF from chromatin and shutting the program off, a modification needed for survival of damage. Cdc10 also binds the rDNA-promoter-binding protein Pol5 and is enriched by ChIP at 5S rRNA genes and near replication origins, but roles outside the MBF regulon remain speculative.

Core Functions

Cdc10 is the shared core subunit of the MBF transcription factor. Through its C-terminal region and ankyrin domain it binds the C termini of the MCB-recognising subunits Res1 and Res2, forming the Res1-Cdc10 and Res2-Cdc10 heteromers that occupy MCB elements in the promoters of cdc18, cdt1, cdc22, cig2, cdt2 and the rest of the G1/S regulon; MBF activity in vivo depends on this interaction, and the complex stays promoter-bound through the cycle. Cdc10 supplies the platform on which the Rep2 activator (Rep1/Rep2 in meiosis) assembles, so that at Start MBF activates Pol II transcription of the replication and S-phase genes; cdc10 loss arrests cells in G1 with unreplicated DNA, and the same Res2-Cdc10 machinery drives MCB-dependent transcription of rec8, rec11, cdc18 and cdc22 in pre-meiotic S phase. Cdc10 does not itself contact the MCB sequence; it contributes to the DNA-binding activity of the complex it nucleates.

Functional annotation report: Schizosaccharomyces pombe cdc10 (UniProt P01129)

Executive conclusion

The requested protein is the fission-yeast Start control protein Cdc10, encoded by cdc10 (ORF SPBC336.12c) in Schizosaccharomyces pombe strain 972. It is the essential, approximately 85-kDa p85Cdc10 subunit of the MluI cell-cycle-box binding factor (MBF), not the unrelated Cdc10 septin found in budding yeast. Cdc10 is a nonenzymatic nuclear transcriptional regulator/scaffold: together with the sequence-recognition subunits Res1 and Res2, it controls transcription of DNA-replication and S-phase genes and is required for passage through Start into S phase. Thus, there is no catalytic reaction, substrate, or transporter cargo to annotate. Its functional “substrates” are the Cdc10-containing MBF complex and MCB-bearing promoters. (ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-1, ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 5-6, kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 1-2)

1. Identity verification and ambiguity control

The supplied identifiers—UniProt P01129, gene cdc10, ORF SPBC336.12c, organism S. pombe—are consistent with the literature’s p85Cdc10. Endogenous p85Cdc10 was detected in MBF, anti-Cdc10 antibodies supershifted an MCB-bound complex, and expression of cdc10 complemented both temperature-sensitive and null alleles. These genetic and biochemical observations independently support the identity and essential MBF assignment. (ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-1, ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 5-6)

The symbol is nevertheless biologically ambiguous across species. In Saccharomyces cerevisiae, “Cdc10” ordinarily denotes a septin, whereas the protein considered here is a Swi6-like fungal G1/S transcription regulator. This report therefore excludes results for budding-yeast Cdc10 and other same-symbol proteins.

The supplied domain annotations—ankyrin repeats, SWI6-like architecture, and KilA/APSES-related HTH superfamily—fit this identity. The ankyrin repeats are consistent with protein–protein interactions between Cdc10 and Res1/Res2. Importantly, a broad HTH-superfamily annotation should not be interpreted as proof that Cdc10 supplies MBF’s principal sequence specificity: experimental literature identifies Res1 and Res2 as the DNA-binding partners and describes Cdc10 itself as not directly binding DNA. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 47-52)

2. Primary molecular function

Cdc10 is a core regulatory and structural subunit of MBF, the fission-yeast G1/S transcription factor. Cdc10 forms complexes with Res1 and Res2; these heteromeric assemblies recognize MluI cell-cycle-box (MCB) motifs, commonly represented by the consensus ACGCGT, in promoters of genes needed for DNA synthesis and S-phase progression. Cdc10 contributes complex stability, partner recruitment, and regulated transcriptional output rather than enzymatic catalysis. (ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-1, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 40-47, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 47-52)

Domain-mapping experiments support a division of labor within MBF. The N-terminal 157 residues of Res1 were sufficient for specific binding to a cdc22 promoter probe, whereas Res1’s C-terminal region was necessary and sufficient for interaction with Cdc10. Accordingly, the most defensible model is that Res1/Res2 supply sequence-specific promoter recognition while the ankyrin-containing Cdc10 subunit serves as an essential interaction and regulatory platform. (ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-1)

Cdc10 abundance alone is not the cell-cycle oscillator. Increasing Cdc10 expression did not abolish periodic transcription, and Cdc10, Res1, and Res2 can remain associated with target promoters across the cell cycle. Periodic output instead depends substantially on associated activators and repressors—including Rep2, Yox1, and Nrm1—and on checkpoint-dependent phosphorylation. Deletion of the final 61 C-terminal residues in the cdc10-C4 allele produces strong constitutive MBF transcription, establishing the C terminus as an important regulatory region. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 47-52, eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 2-2, eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 1-2)

3. Biological process and target program

The central process is Start/G1-to-S control. Canonical MBF targets include:

  • cdc18 and cdt1, which support replication-origin licensing;
  • cdc22, encoding a ribonucleotide-reductase component needed for deoxyribonucleotide production;
  • cig2, encoding an S-phase cyclin;
  • additional genes involved in DNA synthesis, replication, repair, and cell-cycle control. (ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-1, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 40-47)

Different assays yield different target-set sizes. Mechanistic literature describes approximately 20 canonical MBF-controlled genes, whereas genome-wide Cdc10/Yox1 occupancy analysis reported about 80 targets, approximately half of which were not periodically transcribed. The latter work used a genome-tiling ChIP-chip array with roughly 33-bp resolution. These numbers are not contradictory: they reflect differences between a stringent canonical transcriptional regulon and a broader occupancy-defined set. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 40-47, eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 2-2)

MBF activity is essential for Start. Conditional loss of Cdc10 produced a cell-division-cycle phenotype within 6 hours, shifted DNA content from 2N toward 1N within the same interval, and caused apparent nonviability by 18 hours. Following Cdc10 repression, cdc18 mRNA fell by 50% within 2 hours, while cdc22 mRNA eventually declined to approximately 40% of its initial level by 18 hours. Conversely, Cdc10 expression rescued a temperature-sensitive mutant at 35.5°C and supported a cdc10 null background. These data directly link Cdc10-dependent transcription to G1/S passage and viability. (ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 5-6)

4. Cellular localization

Cdc10 carries out its primary function in the nucleus, at chromatin-associated MBF target promoters. ChIP experiments detected Cdc10 at promoters including cdc18 and cdc22, and genome-wide studies mapped Cdc10-containing MBF occupancy across the genome. This is direct evidence for promoter/chromatin localization and is fully consistent with its transcription-factor role. (eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 2-2, eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 1-2, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127)

A qualification is warranted: the retrieved literature did not provide quantitative live-cell imaging of Cdc10’s nucleocytoplasmic distribution. Therefore, “nuclear promoter-associated” is strongly supported, but detailed spatial dynamics, nuclear-import mechanism, and possible nonnuclear pools cannot be specified from this evidence set.

5. Cell-cycle and checkpoint signaling

Normal cell-cycle regulation

Cdc10–Res1–Res2 constitutes a relatively stable MBF core. Rep2 is an activating subunit whose recruitment is more dynamic, while Yox1 and Nrm1 restrict MBF transcription outside the appropriate G1/S window. High-resolution ChIP-chip indicated that Cdc10–Res1–Res2 can occupy targets throughout the cycle, whereas temporal Rep2 recruitment tracks transcriptional activation and RNA-polymerase-II behavior. (eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 2-2, eshaghi2011deconvolutionofchromatin pages 1-2)

DNA-replication checkpoint

During replication stress, the effector kinase Cds1 sustains MBF transcription primarily by inactivating its repressors. Cds1 phosphorylates Yox1 at Ser114 and Thr115, causing Yox1 release and de-repression of S-phase genes; Nrm1 is regulated similarly at the level of promoter association. A nonphosphorylatable cdc10-8A allele could still induce transcription after hydroxyurea, arguing that direct phosphorylation of those Cdc10 sites is not required for the normal replication-checkpoint response. By contrast, a phosphomimetic cdc10-2E allele caused constitutive transcriptional activation, hydroxyurea resistance, and partial rescue of cds1Δ lethality. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 80-86)

DNA-damage checkpoint

Severe DNA damage produces the opposite MBF response. Activated Chk1 phosphorylates Cdc10’s C-terminal region, promotes release of Cdc10/MBF from chromatin, and represses G1/S transcription. Candidate Chk1 sites include Ser720 and Ser732. A Cdc10.2A mutant carrying S720A/S732A substitutions failed to undergo normal release from the cdc18 promoter after methyl methanesulfonate and showed a similar occupancy defect following ionizing radiation. The mutant was slightly sensitive to camptothecin but not detectably sensitive to MMS or ionizing radiation in the reported survival assays, indicating a clear molecular effect but a modest condition-dependent fitness phenotype. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 127-136, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127)

The expert mechanistic interpretation is therefore that Cdc10-containing MBF is an integration node that discriminates between two stresses: replication stress activates needed S-phase transcription through Cds1-mediated removal of Yox1/Nrm1, whereas damaging conditions that should prevent replication activate Chk1-dependent Cdc10 eviction and transcriptional shutdown. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 127-136, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127)

7. Recent developments, 2023–2024

No retrieved 2023–2024 study substantially revised the direct molecular annotation of Cdc10. The most important recent literature instead confirms and extends the MBF pathway context.

A March 2024 review of S. pombe sexual differentiation retained Cdc10, Res1, and Res2 as the MluI-box-binding MBF machinery that activates genes needed for G1/S transition. It places this machinery at the interface between proliferation and differentiation: nutrient depletion promotes G1 arrest and sexual development, but the review does not present a new Cdc10-specific perturbation or localization result. Kawamukai, 2024, published March 2024, DOI. (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 1-2)

An August 2024 RNA-seq study analyzed ploidy-dependent responses to 0.0075% MMS for 4 hours. Haploids had 174 differentially expressed genes—91 induced and 83 repressed—whereas diploids had 86—60 induced and 26 repressed. The two responses correlated at 0.57, and MBF-target transcripts were among those induced, consistent with a replication-stress response. Across 4,641 transcripts, diploids showed an average 1.9-fold increase in transcript abundance. These results demonstrate ongoing real-world use of the MBF regulon as a readout of genotoxic and ploidy-associated stress, but they did not directly measure or manipulate Cdc10. 8. Current applications and real-world implementations

Cdc10 is principally a research tool and model-system regulator, not a clinical or industrial target. Its established applications include:

  1. Conditional G1/Start arrest. Temperature-sensitive cdc10 alleles permit experimentally controlled disruption of MBF-dependent transcription and G1/S progression. The direct phenotype of cdc10 loss makes these alleles useful for cell-cycle synchronization and phase-comparison experiments. (ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 5-6)
  2. MBF reporter and promoter studies. MCB-containing promoters such as cdc18 and cdc22 provide tractable readouts of transcription-factor assembly, periodic transcription, and checkpoint regulation. (ayte1995theschizosaccharomycespombe pages 1-1, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 86-127)
  3. Checkpoint-dissection models. Alleles including cdc10-C4, cdc10-8A, cdc10-2E, and S720A/S732A separate constitutive activation, replication-stress signaling, and Chk1-dependent chromatin release. (ivanova2013thednadamage pages 47-52, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 127-136, ivanova2013thednadamage pages 80-86)
  4. Genotoxic-stress transcriptomics. MBF target expression serves as a pathway-level signature of replication stress in RNA-seq experiments, including the 2024 haploid–diploid MMS comparison. (park2024analysisoftranscriptional pages 2-4, park2024analysisoftranscriptional pages 4-7)

Overall functional annotation

Cdc10/P01129 is the essential Swi6-like regulatory scaffold of the S. pombe MBF transcription complex. In the nucleus it associates with Res1/Res2 at MCB-containing promoters, enables transcription of replication and S-phase genes, and thereby permits Start and G1-to-S progression. Its C-terminal regulatory region integrates cell-cycle and genome-integrity signals: Cds1-dependent removal of MBF repressors sustains transcription during replication stress, whereas Chk1 phosphorylation of Cdc10 promotes MBF eviction and repression during DNA damage.

  cdc10 encodes the 767-residue, ~85 kDa Start control protein Cdc10, the essential core subunit
  of the fission-yeast MBF (MluI cell-cycle-box binding factor, also called DSC1) transcription factor
  and the counterpart of budding-yeast Swi6. Cdc10 has no demonstrated sequence-specific DNA-binding
  activity of its own: recognition of the MCB element (ACGCGT) in target promoters is performed by
  the APSES domains of its two alternative partners, Res1 and Res2, whose C-terminal regions bind
  Cdc10, and the isolated Res1 DNA-binding domain can even bypass the need for cdc10. Cdc10 carries
  a divergent APSES-like N-terminal region, a central block of ankyrin repeats that mediates protein-protein
  interactions, and a C-terminal regulatory domain. Bound with Res1 and Res2 at MCB promoters throughout
  the cell cycle, Cdc10 is the platform on which the periodic output of MBF is assembled: the zinc-finger
  activator Rep2 (Rep1 in meiosis) supplies transcriptional activation, while the Cdc10 C-terminal
  61 residues are required to load the MBF-encoded corepressors Nrm1 and Yox1 that silence the regulon
  outside G1/S, so that the cdc10-C4 truncation expresses MCB genes constitutively. At Start, MBF
  activates Pol II transcription of the replication-licensing genes cdc18 and cdt1, the ribonucleotide
  reductase gene cdc22, the S-phase cyclin gene cig2, cdt2, ctp1 and some twenty further DNA-synthesis
  and repair genes, and the same Cdc10-Res2-Rep1/Rep2 machinery drives MCB-dependent transcription
  of rec8, rec11, cdc18 and cdc22 during pre-meiotic S phase; loss of cdc10 arrests cells in G1 with
  unreplicated DNA. Cig2-Cdc2 phosphorylation of Res1 closes a negative-feedback loop, and pheromone
  arrests cells downstream of a fully active Cdc10-Res1 factor by inhibiting the G1 CDK. The Cdc10
  C terminus is also the point at which the genome-integrity checkpoints act on the G1/S program:
  under replication stress Cds1 phosphorylation of the Yox1/Nrm1 repressors (and, sufficiently but
  not necessarily, of Cdc10 Ser720/Thr723) keeps MBF transcription on, whereas after DNA damage Chk1
  phosphorylates Cdc10 on Ser720 and Ser732, releasing intact MBF from chromatin and shutting the
  program off, a modification needed for survival of damage. Cdc10 also binds the rDNA-promoter-binding
  protein Pol5 and is enriched by ChIP at 5S rRNA genes and near replication origins, but roles outside
  the MBF regulon remain speculative.
