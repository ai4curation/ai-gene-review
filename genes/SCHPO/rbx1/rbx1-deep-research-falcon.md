# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe rbx1/pip1* (UniProt O13959)

## Executive conclusion

The requested identity is verified. UniProt O13959 corresponds to the product of *S. pombe* ORF **SPAC23H4.18c**, historically named **Pip1** (“Pop-interacting protein 1”) and now annotated **Rbx1**. Organism-specific literature identifies Pip1 as the fission-yeast homolog of HRT1/RBX1/ROC1 and as the RING-box component of several cullin–RING ubiquitin ligases (CRLs), consistent with the supplied zf-RBX1/RING-H2 domain annotations. This report does not treat human RBX1 or similarly named proteins from other organisms as direct evidence for O13959. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

The best-supported primary function is therefore: **a RING-type E3 ubiquitin-ligase core subunit that binds cullin scaffolds and promotes transfer of ubiquitin from a charged E2 enzyme to substrates positioned by the assembled CRL**. Rbx1/Pip1 does not appear to determine substrate identity by itself. Substrate specificity is supplied principally by F-box or other receptor/adaptor modules associated with Pcu1, Pcu3, or Pcu4/Cul4. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 7-9, nakagawa2025cul4basedubiquitinligases pages 6-8)

## 1. Identity and domain verification

The 2002 primary study identified the SPAC23H4.18c product Pip1 as the *S. pombe* homolog of human HRT1/RBX1/ROC1. Endogenous Pip1 occurred with Pcu1, Psh1, Pop1, and Pop2 in an approximately 500-kDa SCF complex. This correspondence verifies the gene symbol, synonym, organism, and expected Rbx1-family role specified in the query. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

The supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations—RING-box E3 ubiquitin ligase, zinc-finger RING/RING-H2, and zf-rbx1—fit the experimental literature: Pip1 associates with multiple cullins and ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes and occurs in catalytically active CRLs. The zinc-binding RING fold should be understood as a catalytic interaction platform rather than an independent substrate-binding enzyme. The organism-specific studies demonstrate activity of Pip1-containing complexes; they generally do not assay purified Pip1 as a stand-alone ligase. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 7-9)

## 2. Molecular function and reaction

Rbx1/Pip1 is annotated with EC 2.3.2.27, corresponding to RING-type E3 ubiquitin transfer. The overall CRL reaction is:

**E2–S–ubiquitin + substrate lysine → E2–SH + substrate–NH–CO–ubiquitin**, with repeated cycles producing polyubiquitin chains where appropriate.

Mechanistically, Pip1 binds the C-terminal region of a cullin scaffold and engages a ubiquitin-charged E2 through its RING domain. It promotes transfer rather than forming a covalent E3–ubiquitin intermediate. The substrate is brought into productive proximity by the cullin’s receptor/adaptor arm. Thus, Pip1’s biochemical specificity is primarily for compatible **cullin/E2 machinery**, while substrate specificity resides in the complete complex.

This interpretation is supported directly at complex level by the SCF assay: Pip1-containing complexes polyubiquitylated phosphorylated Rum1 when supplied with ubiquitin, ATP, E1, and active UBC3/CDC34. Activity required ATP, E1, the catalytic E2 cysteine, and lysine-containing ubiquitin, demonstrating canonical E1–E2–E3-dependent chain formation. The experiment establishes activity of the assembled SCF, not intrinsic catalysis or Rum1 recognition by isolated Pip1. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 5-7)

### 3.1 Pcu1/SCF complexes: cell-cycle proteolysis

Pip1 participates in SCF complexes containing the Cul1-related scaffold Pcu1, Skp1 homolog Psh1, and F-box proteins Pop1 and/or Pop2. Co-immunoprecipitation and gel filtration identified a roughly **500-kDa** SCF^Pop1–Pop2 assembly. Pip1-containing complexes support polyubiquitylation of the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor Rum1 after CDK-dependent phosphorylation. Loss of Pop2 abolished the relevant activity, showing that substrate engagement depends on the Pop receptor module rather than Pip1 alone. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 2-4, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

The biological interpretation is that SCF-dependent Rum1 turnover contributes to orderly cell-cycle progression and guards against inappropriate rereplication or polyploidization. Cdc18 has also been linked to Pop-dependent SCF regulation, but the retrieved Pip1-focused assay most directly demonstrates Rum1 as substrate; Cdc18 should not be claimed as a Pip1-direct substrate from these data. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 11-13)

### 3.2 Pcu3 complex: neddylation-regulated ligase activity

Pip1 directly co-immunoprecipitates with both neddylated and unneddylated Pcu3. In overexpression interaction tests, it associated with **7 of 14** tested *S. pombe* ubiquitin-conjugating factors: Ubc1, Ubc7, Ubc11, Ubc12, Ubc13, Ubc14, and Mms2. Because these were binding assays under overexpression, they identify candidates rather than proving that all seven are physiological Pip1 partners. (zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 7-9)

The COP9 signalosome regulates this module through the ubiquitin-like modifier Ned8. Deleting *csn1*, *csn3*, *csn4*, or *csn5* caused accumulation of neddylated Pcu3, and loss of Csn5 increased Pcu3-associated, Ubc7-supported production of lysine-dependent high-molecular-weight ubiquitin products. Pcu3 and CSN components co-fractionated around **550 kDa**. A definitive physiological protein substrate was not identified in that assay. (zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 7-9, zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 5-7)

These observations support the current CRL model in which cullin neddylation increases the productive mobility or E2 accessibility of the RING catalytic module, whereas COP9-mediated deneddylation resets or regulates the complex. They do not show that Pip1 itself is neddylated.

### 3.3 Pcu4/Cul4–CLRC: heterochromatin assembly

Pip1/Rbx1 is also the RING component of the **Clr4–Rik1–Cul4 complex (CLRC)**, together with Pcu4/Cul4, Rik1, Raf1/Dos1/Clr8, Raf2/Dos2/Clr7, and associated H3K9 methyltransferase Clr4. This places Rbx1 in a pathway coupling ubiquitination to H3K9 methylation and heterochromatin formation at centromeres, the mating-type region, and telomeric/subtelomeric domains. (nakagawa2025cul4basedubiquitinligases pages 6-8, kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 1-2)

Affinity-purified CLRC ubiquitylates histone H3, with **H3K14** identified as the preferred site; H3K18 and H3K23 can also be modified. H3K14 ubiquitylation binds the Clr4 catalytic region and stimulates its H3K9 methyltransferase activity by approximately **250-fold**. H3K14 substitution produces a stronger silencing defect than H3K18/H3K23 substitutions, although it reduces rather than completely abolishes H3K9 methylation. These are strong data for CLRC-mediated histone crosstalk but remain complex-level evidence, because isolated Pip1 was not shown to recognize H3. (arsenijevic2023investigatingtherole pages 218-221)

Earlier genetics showed that perturbing Pcu4 compromises heterochromatic silencing and causes pericentric defects and thiabendazole sensitivity. Some Pcu4 effects differed from those of Raf1/Raf2 loss, suggesting that CLRC combines E3-ligase-dependent regulation with structural recruitment of Clr4. This is an important caveat: not every CLRC phenotype can be assigned solely to Pip1-mediated ubiquitin transfer. (horn2005arik1associatedcullindependent pages 6-7)

## 4. Subcellular localization

Direct GFP and endogenous-tag observations place Pip1 in **both nucleus and cytoplasm**. This distribution is consistent with participation in multiple SCF/CRL assemblies: nuclear complexes regulate Rum1 and chromatin, whereas cytoplasmic SCF assemblies may have distinct substrates. Pop1 was reported as predominantly nuclear, while Pip1 and most other SCF components occupied both compartments. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 1-2)

Functional localization is complex-specific. Pcu4–CLRC activity is concentrated on heterochromatic chromatin, including centromeres, the mating-type locus, and telomeres. By contrast, the Pcu3 protein is nuclear-enriched, and COP9 perturbation did not substantially change that localization. Therefore, a useful annotation is **nuclear and cytoplasmic RING-box protein; recruited to chromatin in the Pcu4/CLRC context**, rather than constitutively heterochromatin-restricted. (zhou2001thefissionyeast pages 5-7, kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 1-2)

## 5. Recent developments, 2023–2024

The most important recent target-relevant development is the 2024 *Nature Communications* study of Ubc4–CLRC. It showed that Ubc4-associated CLRC mono-ubiquitylates Clr4 in an intrinsically disordered region. Nine candidate sites were initially identified, with functional analysis emphasizing **K109, K110, K113, and K114**. Mutation of these sites increased H3K9me2 and reduced H3K9me3, supporting a role for ubiquitylation in the transition from RNAi-associated co-transcriptional silencing to stable H3K9me3/Swi6-associated transcriptional silencing. Ubiquitylated Clr4 showed reduced chromatin/ncRNA binding. (kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 4-4, kim2021clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 5-8)

The same study identified Bdf2 as another Ubc4–CLRC target. Its ubiquitylation limits recruitment of Bdf2/Epe1, centromeric transcription, and small-RNA production. *ubc4* and *cul4* mutants displayed a **greater than twofold increase in centromeric small RNA**. Ubp3 counteracted the Clr4 and Bdf2 ubiquitination pathways. These results refine CLRC from a generic heterochromatin E3 into a regulator of the transition between transcription-coupled RNAi and transcriptionally silent heterochromatin. (kim2021clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 5-8, kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 1-2, kim2021clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 1-5)

The Rbx1-specific evidentiary limitation remains important. The 2024 work includes Rbx1 as the CLRC RING-box subunit, but the decisive dependency tests focused on Ubc4, Cul4, Rik1, Raf1, and Raf2 rather than independently manipulating Pip1. Moreover, human UBE2D3 could mono-ubiquitylate recombinant Clr4 without an E3 in vitro. The most defensible annotation is therefore that Pip1 is the expected catalytic RING component of the active CLRC, while the demonstrated Clr4/Bdf2 specificity belongs to Ubc4–CLRC as an assembled system. (kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 4-4, kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 1-2)

## 6. Applications and real-world relevance

No direct clinical, industrial, or agricultural implementation of the *S. pombe* O13959 protein was identified. Its current application is principally as a **model-system component** for dissecting conserved CRL mechanisms:

1. **Cell-cycle proteolysis:** SCF^Pop complexes provide a tractable system for studying phosphorylation-dependent substrate recognition and polyubiquitylation.
2. **Cullin neddylation/COP9 regulation:** Pcu3–Pip1 experiments model how cullin modification controls CRL activity.
3. **Epigenetic regulation:** CLRC connects histone ubiquitylation, H3K9 methylation, RNA interference, and HP1/Swi6-mediated heterochromatin.
4. **Translational hypothesis generation:** Human CRLs, neddylation enzymes, and RBX proteins are pharmacologically important, and CRLs are exploited in targeted-protein-degradation strategies. However, such therapeutic applications involve mammalian proteins and should be described as evolutionary or mechanistic extrapolations—not implementations of *S. pombe* Rbx1.

## 7. Evidence-weighted annotation recommendation

**Recommended primary annotation:**

> Rbx1/Pip1 is the conserved RING-box catalytic subunit of multiple *S. pombe* cullin–RING E3 ubiquitin ligases. It binds cullin scaffolds and ubiquitin-charged E2 enzymes to promote ubiquitin transfer to substrates selected by complex-specific receptor/adaptor modules. It functions in Pcu1/SCF-dependent cell-cycle protein turnover, a neddylation-regulated Pcu3 ligase, and Pcu4/CLRC-dependent heterochromatin regulation.

**Supported substrates at assembled-complex level:** phosphorylated Rum1 for SCF^Pop1–Pop2; histone H3—preferentially H3K14—Clr4, and Bdf2 for Pcu4/CLRC. No evidence supports interpreting these as proteins directly recognized by Pip1 itself. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, arsenijevic2023investigatingtherole pages 218-221, kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 4-4, kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 1-2)

**Localization:** nucleus and cytoplasm overall; chromatin-associated function in CLRC at heterochromatic loci. (seibert2002combinatorialdiversityof pages 4-5, kim2024clr4suv39h1ubiquitinationand pages 1-2)

**Confidence:** high for identity, RING-box/CRL-core function, Pcu1/Pcu3/Pcu4 participation, and dual nuclear/cytoplasmic localization; moderate for assigning every reported whole-CRL substrate or phenotype specifically to Pip1; insufficient for a complete substrate repertoire or Pip1-specific kinetic constants.
