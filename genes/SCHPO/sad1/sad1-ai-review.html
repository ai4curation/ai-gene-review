Gene Description

Sad1 is the single SUN (Sad1/UNC-84) domain protein of fission yeast and the founding member of the SUN family. It is an essential type II integral protein of the inner nuclear membrane: its nucleoplasmic N-terminal region contacts chromatin partners, a single transmembrane segment anchors it, and its C-terminal SUN domain projects into the perinuclear space where it binds the KASH proteins Kms1 and Kms2 of the outer nuclear membrane, forming LINC (linker of nucleoskeleton and cytoskeleton) complexes. Sad1 concentrates beneath the spindle pole body (SPB) throughout the mitotic and meiotic cycles. In vegetative cells it anchors the SPB to the nuclear envelope and, through Csi1 and Lem2, tethers the clustered centromeres beneath the SPB (Rabl configuration); at mitotic entry it redistributes into a ring around the SPB that initiates localized nuclear envelope remodelling and SPB insertion. In meiotic prophase the Bqt1-Bqt2 complex bridges the telomere protein Rap1 to Sad1, so that the Sad1-Kms1 LINC complex connects the telomere bouquet to the SPB and to dynein-driven horsetail nuclear movement, promoting homolog pairing. Sad1-Kms1 foci also form at persistent DNA double-strand breaks and couple them to microtubules, influencing repair pathway choice. An N-terminal histone-binding motif binds H2A-H2B and contributes to peripheral tethering of telomeres and the mating-type locus and to heterochromatin silencing.

Core Functions

SUN-KASH LINC anchor: the luminal SUN domain of Sad1 binds the KASH proteins Kms1 (meiosis, DNA damage) and Kms2 (mitotic SPB), coupling the inner nuclear membrane to the SPB and cytoplasmic microtubules; in meiotic prophase the Sad1-Kms1 complex, bridged to telomeres by Bqt1-Bqt2-Rap1, clusters telomeres at the SPB for horsetail movement.

Functional annotation report: sad1 / Sad1 (UniProt Q09825) in Schizosaccharomyces pombe

Executive conclusion

The requested target is correctly identified. It is Sad1 from Schizosaccharomyces pombe, UniProt Q09825, encoded by sad1 (ORF aliases supplied by UniProt: SPBC12D12.01/SPBC16H5.01c). The 2024 target-specific study explicitly used Q09825, and its description of Sad1 as a single-pass, inner-nuclear-membrane SUN-family protein agrees with the supplied InterPro SUN1–5/SUN-domain and Pfam Sad1_UNC annotations. Papers concerning proteins called Sad1 in Cryptococcus neoformans or other organisms were excluded from the functional conclusions below. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 1-2, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 2-3)

Sad1 is not an enzyme or transporter. Its primary function is that of an essential membrane-embedded structural scaffold and force-transmission adaptor. At the inner nuclear membrane, it organizes nuclear-envelope-spanning LINC assemblies with outer-membrane KASH proteins, especially Kms1 and Kms2. This architecture connects nuclear chromatin to the spindle pole body (SPB) and cytoplasmic microtubules. Depending on cell-cycle state, Sad1 supports SPB organization and insertion, centromere/SPB coupling, meiotic telomere movement, positioning of persistent DNA breaks, and peripheral heterochromatin organization. (bestul2021redistributionofcentrosomal pages 1-2, king2008anetworkof pages 1-2, swartz2014arolefor pages 2-3)

1. Identity, family, topology, and localization

Sad1 is the founding fungal member of the Sad1/UNC-84 (SUN) protein family. It is an integral inner-nuclear-membrane protein with one transmembrane helix. Current topology places its N-terminal region—including residues 1–169—on the nucleoplasmic side, where it can contact chromatin, while the C-terminal SUN region projects into the nuclear-envelope lumen/perinuclear space and associates with KASH proteins in the outer membrane. This trans-envelope SUN–KASH assembly constitutes a LINC complex. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 1-2, king2008anetworkof pages 1-2, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 2-3)

Its dominant localization is the nuclear envelope and SPB attachment region. Sad1 defines the membrane region occupied by the SPB and is also detectable at interphase microtubule-organizing centers. Endogenous Sad1–GFP colocalizes with the SPB component Sid4–mCherry. More sensitive imaging found weak additional nuclear-envelope puncta in more than 82% of cells, averaging 2.9 ± 1.2 puncta per cell. These non-SPB puncta depend on an N-terminal histone-binding motif, whereas SPB localization does not. (king2008anetworkof pages 1-2, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 3-4)

The classical genetic literature identifies Sad1 as essential and required for normal SPB function, although the retrieved foundational evidence summarized rather than reproduced the original deletion/conditional-mutant measurements. Accordingly, “essential SPB/inner-nuclear-membrane scaffold” is a strong annotation, but precise lethality penetrance should be taken from the original Hagan and Yanagida study. (king2008anetworkof pages 11-12)

2. Primary structural function: an adaptable nuclear-envelope bridge

Sad1’s unifying role is to couple otherwise separate structures across the nuclear envelope:

  1. Its nucleoplasmic N terminus associates directly or indirectly with chromatin, centromeres, telomeres, DNA-damage sites, and histones.
  2. Its membrane anchor fixes the protein in the inner nuclear membrane.
  3. Its luminal SUN region engages Kms1/Kms2 KASH proteins in the outer nuclear membrane.
  4. The KASH side couples the assembly to cytoplasmic SPB and microtubule machinery.

Thus, Sad1 converts nuclear-envelope position into mechanical and organizational control. It should be annotated principally as a SUN-domain LINC-complex component/SPB-associated nuclear-envelope organizer, rather than as a generic signaling protein. Different adaptors retarget the same core scaffold to centromeres in vegetative growth, telomeres during meiosis, and persistent DNA lesions during repair. (king2008anetworkof pages 1-2, swartz2014arolefor pages 2-3, yamamoto2014gatheringupmeiotic pages 2-4)

3. Mitotic SPB remodeling and localized nuclear-envelope breakdown

During interphase, the bulky cytoplasmic portion of the fission-yeast SPB lies on the cytoplasmic face of the nuclear envelope. At mitotic entry, the SPB must enter a localized nuclear-envelope fenestra so that it can nucleate spindle microtubules inside the nucleus. Super-resolution microscopy showed that Sad1 redistributes around duplicated SPBs into complete or partial rings. Kms2, Cut12, and Cut11 subsequently form related SPB rings; their proper redistribution depends on Sad1 and on centromere–SPB linkage. (bestul2021redistributionofcentrosomal pages 1-2)

The leading mechanistic model is that centromere attachment first drives Sad1 redistribution. Plo1/Polo kinase activity is not required for Sad1’s initial redistribution but is required for effective reorganization of other ring proteins and completion of localized nuclear-envelope breakdown. The resulting membrane remodeling permits SPB insertion and formation of a functional intranuclear spindle. This interpretation is supported by microscopy and perturbation experiments, although the exact molecular action by which a Sad1 ring changes membrane curvature or permeability remains unresolved. (bestul2021redistributionofcentrosomal pages 1-2)

Sad1 therefore participates in SPB duplication/insertion and anchoring, but it should not be described as the catalytic executor of membrane fenestration. Cut11, Brr6, Plo1, centromere attachment, and additional SPB proteins act with it. The most precise statement is that Sad1 forms an early organizing scaffold for the mitotic membrane-remodeling ring. (bestul2021redistributionofcentrosomal pages 1-2, bestul2021redistributionofcentrosomal pages 14-14)

4. Centromere anchorage, Rabl organization, and nuclear mechanics

In vegetatively growing fission yeast, centromeres cluster near the SPB, producing the Rabl chromosome arrangement. Sad1 is part of the inner-membrane/SPB attachment platform through which centromeric heterochromatin is coupled to cytoplasmic microtubules. Kms1/Kms2 provide the outer-membrane side; proteins such as Ima1 and Csi1 contribute nuclear chromatin/kinetochore connections. Disrupting the Ima1-mediated connection causes abnormal nuclear shape and loss or displacement of SPB and SUN–KASH components, indicating that the chromatin tether also buffers forces transmitted by cytoplasmic microtubules. (king2008anetworkof pages 1-2, yadav2018sad1spatiotemporallyregulates pages 2-3)

This role is partly direct and partly network-dependent. Sad1 is the indispensable membrane scaffold, but it is not established as the only direct centromere receptor. Indeed, the 2024 histone-binding mutant retained a single clustered Cnp1/CENP-A focus at the SPB, showing that the newly defined H2A–H2B-binding motif is dispensable for centromere clustering. Earlier work assigns a distinct contribution to Sad1’s N-terminal 60 residues. Thus, Sad1 contains separable functional modules for centromere/SPB coupling and broader heterochromatin tethering. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 9-10, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 4-5)

5. Major 2024 development: direct H2A–H2B recognition and heterochromatin control

The most important recent advance is the demonstration that Sad1 directly recognizes histone H2A–H2B. Sun and colleagues published this work in Nature Communications in May 2024 (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-48418-7). GST pull-down, size-exclusion chromatography, isothermal titration calorimetry, and endogenous co-immunoprecipitation showed that Sad1 residues 1–169 bind H2A–H2B but not H3–H4. Reported dissociation constants were 7.5 ± 1.0 μM for H2A–H2B and 7.5 ± 0.9 μM for H2A.Z–H2B, indicating little discrimination between canonical H2A and H2A.Z in these assays. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 2-3, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 1-2)

The minimal histone-binding motif lies at Sad1 residues 110–140. A Sad1 peptide–H2A/H2B complex was solved at 2.15 Å resolution. Sad1 F118 occupies a hydrophobic H2B pocket formed by Y42, I54, and M59, while D114, E117, E121, and E122 provide electrostatic contacts. F118A weakened binding to 76.9 ± 23.1 μM, F118R reduced affinity to poorer than 200 μM, and histone-interface mutations H2A-R79E and H2B-Y42R yielded 90.1 ± 25.5 μM and 44.8 ± 13.7 μM, respectively. Combining D114R/E117R/F118R/E121R/E122R in the sad1-5R allele abolished detectable H2A–H2B binding. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 3-4, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 4-5)

In cells, sad1-5R and deletion of the histone-binding motif removed Sad1’s dispersed nuclear-envelope localization while preserving its SPB focus. These mutants displaced telomere clusters and the mating-type locus from the nuclear periphery but did not disrupt centromere clustering. Sad1 also colocalized with the heterochromatin protein Swi6 and was enriched at telomeres by ChIP-qPCR. Localization assays scored approximately 85–120 cells per Sad1 genotype, while telomere/mating-type positioning experiments included about 200 cells in individual experiments. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 3-4, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 4-5, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 5-6)

The same interaction contributes to silencing at subtelomeres, the mating-type locus, and selected pericentromeric repeats. H2A–H2B enhanced Sad1 association with the histone deacetylases Clr3 and Sir2: an apparent Clr3-binding value improved from 42.9 μM for Sad1 alone to 10.3 μM in the Sad1/H2A–H2B condition, whereas Sad1-5R/H2A–H2B remained weak at 47.2 μM. Telomeric Sir2 enrichment decreased and mating-type-locus H3K9 acetylation increased in sad1-5R. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 6-7)

Sad1 also showed histone-enhanced liquid–liquid phase-separation behavior in vitro. Clr3 and Sir2 did not independently form comparable liquid droplets but partitioned into Sad1-containing condensates; most Clr3–GFP signal near the nuclear envelope recovered within approximately 40 seconds after photobleaching. The authors’ model is that H2A–H2B binding both anchors Sad1 to peripheral heterochromatin and promotes dynamic condensates that concentrate histone deacetylases. This is a compelling model supported by structure-guided mutations and cellular phenotypes, but the physiological necessity of phase separation—as distinct from multivalent binding—will require further separation-of-function experiments. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 9-10, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 6-7)

A structural caveat is important: the bound Sad1 peptide would clash with DNA in an intact canonical nucleosome. H2A–H2B may therefore bind Sad1 when locally exposed, exchanged, partially unwrapped, or present in a non-nucleosomal pool. The exact physiological histone substrate remains an active mechanistic question. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 4-5)

6. Meiotic telomere bouquet and horsetail nuclear movement

At meiotic entry, chromosome organization is reprogrammed: centromeres detach from the SPB and telomeres gather there, forming the bouquet. Sad1 relocates to telomere-associated sites, and this recruitment depends on the meiosis-specific Bqt1/Bqt2 adaptor system. Bqt1–Bqt2 associates with Rap1, which is connected to telomeric Taz1, while Bqt3/Bqt4 contribute telomere–nuclear-envelope attachment. The Sad1–Kms LINC apparatus then couples these chromosome ends to the cytoplasmic microtubule system. (katsumata2017positionmattersmultiple pages 1-3, yamamoto2014gatheringupmeiotic pages 2-4)

Cytoplasmic dynein pulls on SPB-derived microtubules to produce the characteristic back-and-forth “horsetail” movement of the nucleus. Bouquet formation and movement improve homolog alignment, pairing, and recombination while suppressing inappropriate contacts. Dynein-chain depletion severely compromises nuclear movement and homolog pairing, and defects in telomere clustering also impair pairing. These outcomes establish the pathway’s biological importance, although the retrieved evidence does not provide a quantitative phenotype uniquely attributable to a sad1 separation-of-function allele. (yamamoto2014gatheringupmeiotic pages 2-4, yamamoto2014gatheringupmeiotic pages 12-13)

The functional interpretation is therefore that Sad1 is the inner-membrane mechanical coupler in the meiotic telomere–LINC–SPB–microtubule axis; Bqt/Rap1/Taz1 proteins confer telomere specificity, and Kms/dynein/microtubules provide the cytoplasmic force pathway.

7. Persistent DNA-break positioning and repair choice

Sad1-containing LINC assemblies are also induced at persistent DNA double-strand breaks in S/G2. Rad52-marked breaks could remain associated with oscillating Sad1–GFP/SPB structures for more than 30 minutes. Approximately 25% of induced breaks at ade3 and 40% at ade8 comigrated with Sad1 for at least 10 minutes; about 40% of ade8 breaks also comigrated with Kms1. Roughly three-quarters of MMS-induced Sad1 foci lay along microtubules. (swartz2014arolefor pages 2-3)

Recruitment required Rad3/ATR and Exo1-dependent long-range resection, was not observed for G1-arrested breaks, and preferentially involved Kms1 rather than Kms2. The data support a mechanism in which resected, persistent lesions are connected to cytoplasmic microtubules through Sad1–Kms1, increasing lesion mobility and affecting homology-directed repair-pathway choice. The study directly assayed repair outcomes in at least three experiments with at least 300 cells per condition, but the retrieved text did not expose the exact homologous-recombination versus end-joining percentages; those values are therefore not asserted here. (swartz2014arolefor pages 2-3, swartz2014arolefor pages 10-11)

8. Recent evidence landscape and applications

The 2024 H2A–H2B study is the principal recent Sad1-specific mechanistic advance. It transforms the view of Sad1 from a largely mechanical SUN scaffold into a direct chromatin-binding organizer with a structurally defined histone interface. By comparison, the 2021 Sad1-ring work refined the mechanism of SPB insertion and localized nuclear-envelope breakdown. Together, these studies show that Sad1 integrates membrane mechanics with genome organization. (bestul2021redistributionofcentrosomal pages 1-2, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 2-3)

There is no clinical or industrial implementation of Sad1 itself. Its real-world value is as a research platform:

  • Endogenous Sad1–GFP/Sad1–mCherry is widely used as an SPB and nuclear-envelope landmark in live-cell microscopy.
  • Sad1 provides a genetically tractable model for conserved human SUN/KASH biology, including nuclear mechanics, centrosome–nuclear-envelope coupling, meiotic chromosome motion, and genome stability.
  • The sad1-5R allele is a useful separation-of-function reagent: it disrupts histone-dependent peripheral distribution and heterochromatin organization while preserving SPB localization and centromere clustering.
  • Sad1 rings provide a temporal and spatial marker for early SPB insertion/localized nuclear-envelope fenestration.
  • Damage-induced Sad1–Kms1 assemblies offer a model for studying how nuclear positioning and cytoskeletal force alter repair-pathway choice. (sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 3-4, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 4-5, swartz2014arolefor pages 2-3)

9. Recommended functional annotation

Molecular function: Essential inner-nuclear-membrane SUN-domain structural adaptor; component of Kms1/Kms2-containing LINC complexes; direct H2A–H2B-binding protein through an N-terminal histone-binding motif; organizer of SPB-associated nuclear-envelope remodeling.

Biological processes: SPB duplication/anchoring and mitotic insertion; localized nuclear-envelope breakdown; centromere–SPB coupling and Rabl chromosome organization; nuclear mechanics; meiotic telomere bouquet formation and horsetail movement; persistent double-strand-break positioning and repair-pathway regulation; telomere and mating-type-locus tethering; peripheral heterochromatin silencing.

Cellular component: Inner nuclear membrane, nuclear envelope, SPB attachment region/SPB-associated ring, meiotic telomere attachment sites, and damage-induced nuclear-envelope foci.

Catalytic activity/substrate: None known. H2A–H2B is a binding partner rather than an enzymatic substrate. Sad1 has no established catalytic reaction or transporter substrate.

10. Evidence-weighted interpretation and remaining questions

The strongest evidence supports Sad1 as a multifunctional structural organizer whose core activity is physical coupling across the nuclear envelope. SPB remodeling, chromosome anchorage, and force transmission are established by genetics and microscopy; H2A–H2B recognition is additionally supported by direct biochemistry and crystallography. Claims that Sad1 itself catalyzes membrane breakdown, recruits every chromosome class directly, or enzymatically modifies chromatin would overstate the evidence. (bestul2021redistributionofcentrosomal pages 1-2, king2008anetworkof pages 1-2, sun2024thesunfamilyprotein pages 2-3)

Key unresolved issues include how Sad1’s SUN domain is structurally coupled to Kms1 versus Kms2, how its binding partners are exchanged between mitosis and meiosis, what physiological form of H2A–H2B engages Sad1 given the predicted clash with nucleosomal DNA, and whether phase separation is required in vivo or merely accompanies multivalent heterochromatin assembly. Quantitative mapping of Sad1 phosphorylation and other cell-cycle modifications also remains an important route to explaining how one membrane scaffold executes these distinct programs.

sad1 (S. pombe, Q09825) review notes

Context: reviewed as a fungal comparative member of modules/linc_complex.yaml (SUN component) and the
nucleokinesis module. Human comparators: SUN1, SUN2.

Biology (with provenance)

  • Essential SUN protein; SPB-associated throughout mitosis and meiosis PMID:7744953.
  • INM SUN protein pairing with KASH Kms1 to form a LINC PMID:30462301.
  • Mitotic partner Kms2; Sad1-Kms2-Ima1 couple centromeric heterochromatin to microtubules PMID:18692466.
  • Centromere clustering: Sad1 + Csi1 link centromeres to the INM near the SPB PMID:23166349; Sad1 residues 2-60 confer centromere-LINC association PMID:27889481.
  • SPB insertion: Sad1 ring at mitotic entry, first in the ring-assembly order Sad1 > Kms2/Cut12 > Cut11 PMID:34133218; "mutation of sad1+ results in SPB insertion errors" PMID:28619713.
  • Meiosis: Bqt1-Bqt2 bridge Rap1 to Sad1 PMID:16615890; bouquet linked to SPB via Sad1-Kms1 LINC PMID:27889481.
  • DNA damage: Sad1-Kms1 foci at persistent DSBs, coupling to microtubules PMID:24943839; basis of GO:1990612.
  • Histone H2A-H2B binding by an N-terminal HBM, crystal structure; peripheral heterochromatin tethering/silencing PMID:38773107.

Decisions

  • Protein binding rows: Kms1 -> MODIFY GO:0140444 (consistent with human SUN reviews); Bqt1 and Csi1 -> MODIFY GO:0043495 (Sad1 as membrane anchor for telomere/centromere complexes); Sif1, Ufe1 (Y2H screen) and Bqt4 (in vitro peptide, in vivo untested) -> REMOVE as uninformative.
  • GO:0034993 IPI with Kms2 -> MODIFY to GO:0106094 (Kms2 is the mitotic SPB KASH partner); IBA GO:0034993 accepted (Sad1-Kms1 is meiotic).
  • GO:1990612 verified in QuickGO: "Sad1-Kms1 LINC complex", defined around DSB repair, is_a GO:0034993. Accepted; ontology question raised.
  • NEW GO:0042393 histone binding (IDA, PMID:38773107).
  • All localization rows accepted; iMTOC and site of DSB kept as non-core.

Deep research

sad1-deep-research-falcon.md read and incorporated (histone binding study, SPB ring model); led to adding PMID:38773107 and PMID:18692466.

Module implications

  • Sad1 confirms the SUN component is deeply conserved: SUN-KASH anchor (GO:0140444), nucleoplasmic partner anchoring (telomeres, centromeres), and meiotic telomere clustering are shared with human SUN1.
  • Fungal-specific: SPB anchoring/insertion into a closed-mitosis nuclear envelope (GO:0106166, GO:0140480) and centromere (Rabl) tethering; these are the fungal counterparts of centrosome-nucleus coupling.
  • Single SUN protein partners with two KASH proteins in different contexts (Kms2 mitotic/SPB; Kms1 meiotic/DSB), mirroring the variant structure of the module's KASH component.
