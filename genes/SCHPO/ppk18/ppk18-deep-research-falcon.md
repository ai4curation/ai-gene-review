# Functional annotation report: *Schizosaccharomyces pombe ppk18* (Q8TFG6)

## Executive summary

Identity is verified: the requested protein is **Ppk18**, encoded by *ppk18* (ORF **SPAPB18E9.02c**) in *Schizosaccharomyces pombe*. The organism-specific literature identifies it as a fungal **Greatwall/Rim15-family serine/threonine kinase**, not an unrelated similarly named protein. This assignment agrees with the supplied UniProt annotation of Q8TFG6 as a PAS-containing protein kinase with protein-kinase and AGC-kinase-associated domains. Ppk18 acts partly redundantly with the related Greatwall kinases Cek1 and Ppk31; consequently, results from *ppk18Δ cek1Δ* or broader pathway mutants must not be attributed solely to Ppk18. (matsuda2020thes.pombe pages 14-17, kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9)

The best-supported primary function is phosphorylation of the Endosulfine protein **Igo1 at Ser64**. Phosphorylated Igo1 inhibits **PP2A-B55/Pab1**, so Ppk18 indirectly raises the phosphorylation state of PP2A-sensitive substrates. This conserved Greatwall–Endosulfine–PP2A/B55 switch couples nutrient-sensitive TORC1 activity to mitotic timing, cell size, TORC2–Gad8 signaling, sexual differentiation, and entry into quiescence. Ppk18 is therefore best annotated as a nutrient-regulated signaling kinase rather than a general metabolic enzyme. (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, martin2018expressyourselfhow pages 4-5, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 123-125)

## 1. Identity, family, and domain interpretation

Ppk18 is consistently described in *S. pombe*-specific literature as a Greatwall kinase homolog. Reviews and pathway studies place Ppk18 with Cek1 and Ppk31 in the fungal Greatwall group and identify Igo1 as their Endosulfine effector. No evidence retrieved indicated confusion with a protein from another organism. (matsuda2020thes.pombe pages 14-17, kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9)

The supplied domain architecture is biologically coherent with this assignment:

- the **protein-kinase domain** explains its ATP-dependent serine/threonine phosphotransferase activity;
- the **AGC-kinase C-terminal region** is compatible with its classification in the extended Greatwall/AGC kinase lineage;
- the **PAS domain** is consistent with a regulatory kinase integrating environmental or metabolic signals, although no Ppk18-specific PAS ligand or sensing mechanism has been demonstrated in the literature retrieved here;
- the CheY-like and kinase-like-superfamily matches are structural-fold classifications and should not be interpreted as evidence that Ppk18 is a bacterial response regulator.

Ppk18 has numerous Ser/Thr-Pro motifs—one study counted 21—suggesting possible CDK control. A mutant altering seven putative Cdk1 sites showed reduced Igo1-Ser64 phosphorylation, but this was reported as preliminary evidence and does not establish every site or prove direct Cdk1 phosphorylation of Ppk18. (matsuda2020thes.pombe pages 14-17, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 123-125)

## 2. Enzymatic function and substrate specificity

As a protein serine/threonine kinase, Ppk18 catalyzes the generic reaction:

**ATP + protein Ser/Thr-OH → ADP + phosphoprotein Ser/Thr-OPO₃²⁻.**

The clearest physiological substrate is **Igo1**, with **Ser64** identified as the functionally relevant Ppk18-dependent phosphorylation site. This phosphorylation converts Igo1 into an inhibitor of PP2A-B55 containing the B55 regulatory subunit Pab1. Thus, Ppk18 does **not** principally inhibit Pab1 by directly phosphorylating it; inhibition is mediated through phospho-Igo1. (martin2018expressyourselfhow pages 4-5, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 123-125)

Beyond Igo1, no comparably well-validated direct Ppk18 substrate emerged from the retrieved literature. Proposed direct regulation of Ppk18 by Pef1/CDK5-like kinase or TORC1 remains inferential: Pef1 has been reported to interact with Ppk18, and Ppk18 contains many CDK motifs, but direct phosphorylation and its functional sites have not been conclusively established. (matsuda2020thes.pombe pages 14-17)

## 3. Position in nutrient-responsive signaling

### Nutrient-rich conditions

When nitrogen and other nutrients are abundant, TORC1 is active and the Greatwall–Endosulfine switch is suppressed. Ppk18–Igo1 signaling is consequently low, PP2A-B55/Pab1 remains active, and PP2A-sensitive phosphorylation is removed. This favors vegetative growth and restrains premature entry into mitosis or differentiation. Sck2, an S6-kinase-family TORC1 effector, is implicated in the negative regulation of the Ppk18–Igo1 branch, although the retrieved evidence does not establish that TORC1 or Sck2 directly phosphorylates Ppk18. (kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9, matsuda2020thes.pombe pages 14-17, alao2023interplaysofampk pages 6-8)

### Nitrogen limitation or starvation

Nitrogen limitation reduces TORC1 signaling and activates the Greatwall switch. Ppk18-dependent Igo1 phosphorylation inhibits PP2A-B55/Pab1, enabling accumulation of phosphorylation on downstream proteins. The pathway thereby translates nutrient status into changes in cell-cycle progression, TORC2 signaling, differentiation, and quiescence. (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, martin2018expressyourselfhow pages 4-5, kawamukai2024regulationofsexual pages 8-9)

A useful simplified pathway is:

**Nitrogen limitation → ↓TORC1/Sck2 restraint → Ppk18 (with Cek1/Ppk31) → Igo1-Ser64-P → ↓PP2A-B55/Pab1 → altered CDK substrates and ↑TORC2–Gad8 signaling.**

## 4. Biological processes controlled by Ppk18

### 4.1 G2/M timing and nutrient-dependent cell size

In nitrogen-poor medium, wild-type cells advance mitosis and divide at a smaller size. *ppk18Δ* and *igo1Δ* cells fail to execute this response normally, whereas moderate *ppk18+* overexpression promotes mitotic entry even in rich medium. These genetic results place Ppk18 upstream of the phosphatase switch controlling mitotic timing. Reduced PP2A-B55 activity allows phosphorylation affecting the Cdc25–Wee1–Cdk1 control system to accumulate, favoring Cdk1 activation and G2/M entry. (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5)

The interpretation is mechanistically strong but should remain precise: Ppk18 influences Cdc25 and Wee1 **through the Igo1–PP2A-B55 phosphatase axis**; neither protein was established here as a direct Ppk18 substrate.

### 4.2 TORC1-to-TORC2 crosstalk and sexual differentiation

Upon conditional TORC1 inactivation, phosphorylation of the TORC2 target Gad8 at **Ser546** rises alongside Igo1 phosphorylation. Deleting *igo1* largely prevents this increase, supporting a model in which Ppk18–Igo1 inhibition of PP2A-B55 permits accumulation of TORC2-dependent phospho-Gad8. (martin2018expressyourselfhow pages 4-5)

Consistent with this connection, deletion of *ppk18* or *igo1* compromises nitrogen-starvation-induced G1 arrest and reduces sporulation, while reducing Pab1 activity produces the opposite, hyperfertile tendency and can suppress defects associated with excessive TORC1 signaling. Ppk18 therefore helps convert nitrogen scarcity into the cell-cycle arrest and signaling state needed for sexual differentiation. (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5)

### 4.3 Quiescence and chronological survival

Ppk18 is important for survival after nitrogen-starvation entry into quiescence. Chronological-lifespan assays found reduced viability of *ppk18Δ* after approximately **10 days** in nitrogen-free medium. In comparison, *igo1Δ* cells began dying after roughly **5–8 days** and were almost entirely dead by day 15; reducing Pab1 expression rescued much of the Igo1-deficient lethality. Single *cek1Δ* or *ppk31Δ* strains behaved more like wild type, whereas combined Greatwall deletions generated stronger, Igo1-like defects, demonstrating partial redundancy among the kinases. (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 83-86)

These data support a genuine Ppk18 contribution but also establish that Ppk18 is not the sole Greatwall activity in fission yeast. A double-mutant phenotype, especially *ppk18Δ cek1Δ*, should be annotated to the **Greatwall module**, not to Ppk18 alone.

### 4.4 Autophagy

The pathway is associated with starvation adaptation and autophagic flux, but the retrieved evidence does not establish a direct Ppk18 autophagy substrate. Recent reviews place Ppk18 downstream of AMPK/TORC1 nutrient signaling, while studies of the Pef1–TORC1 system connect nutrient signaling to autophagy more broadly. Accordingly, “regulation of autophagy” is best treated as an indirect or pathway-level association rather than a demonstrated primary molecular function of Ppk18. (matsuda2020thes.pombe pages 14-17, alao2023interplaysofampk pages 6-8)

## 5. Recent development: translation control during quiescence

The most important recent advance is the primary study by del Dedo and colleagues, published in **Nature Communications in December 2024**, DOI/URL: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55004-4. It extended the Greatwall pathway from conventional cell-cycle regulation to control of the quiescence-entry translation program. (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2)

The study supports the following mechanism:

1. Nitrogen stress suppresses TORC1 and activates Greatwall–Igo1 signaling.
2. Igo1 inhibits PP2A-B55/Pab1, facilitating TORC2-dependent Gad8-Ser546 phosphorylation.
3. TORC2–Gad8 signaling supports Elongator activity and tRNA anticodon-loop modifications.
4. The pathway promotes **mcm⁵s²U34** and **t⁶A37** modifications and improves translation efficiency and fidelity for mRNAs enriched in AAA rather than AAG lysine codons.
5. Relevant encoded proteins include TOR regulators, tRNA-modification factors, and proteins needed for telomeric and subtelomeric organization. (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 16-20, moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-7)

Quantitatively, *igo1Δ* reduced t⁶A37 by approximately **20–25%**. Translation was examined through polysome fractionation/qPCR and codon-engineered Rap1 reporters, while tRNA modifications and protein abundance were measured directly. (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12)

Important attribution caveat: much of this study used *igo1Δ* or the combined **ppk18Δ cek1Δ** Greatwall mutant, not *ppk18Δ* alone. RNA sequencing showed that wild type and *igo1Δ* were similar before starvation, but after four hours *igo1Δ* displayed greater than **10-fold** upregulation of subtelomeric genes on chromosomes I and II; *ppk18Δ cek1Δ* showed a similar transcriptomic pattern. Super-resolution microscopy further showed starvation-dependent telomere detachment in *igo1Δ*, associated with reduced Rap1 protein and reversible by lowering PP2A-B55 activity. These findings strongly establish the pathway but do not prove that loss of Ppk18 alone is sufficient for every phenotype. (moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 10-13, moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 7-10)

## 6. Subcellular localization

No direct, experimentally resolved localization of Ppk18 itself was identified in the retrieved literature. The studies include localization experiments for telomeres, Rap1, Sgo2, and other downstream components, but these should not be misreported as Ppk18 localization. (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2, moreno2023thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 7-10)

The defensible annotation is therefore that Ppk18 is an **intracellular signaling kinase**, with its precise steady-state or nutrient-dependent distribution presently unresolved in the evidence reviewed. Assigning it specifically to the nucleus, cytosol, membrane, or an organelle would be speculative. Given that it phosphorylates soluble Igo1 and controls nuclear/cell-cycle outputs, transient nucleocytoplasmic action is plausible but remains an inference requiring fluorescent tagging, fractionation, or proximity-labeling experiments.

## 7. Applications and real-world relevance

There is no evidence that Ppk18 itself currently has an industrial, diagnostic, or therapeutic implementation. Its present application is as a **model-system node** for studying:

- how TORC1 couples nutrient availability to cell division and cell size;
- how Greatwall–Endosulfine regulates PP2A-B55;
- TORC1–TORC2 communication during differentiation and quiescence;
- chronological aging and starvation survival;
- codon-biased translation, tRNA epitranscriptomic modifications, and telomere maintenance. (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 83-86)

The broader Greatwall–ENSA–PP2A-B55 module is conserved and relevant to mitosis in animal cells, making fission yeast useful for discovering general principles. Translational extrapolation must nevertheless be cautious: Ppk18 is a fungal kinase with partially redundant paralogs, and a Ppk18 phenotype is not automatically equivalent to mammalian MASTL inhibition.

## 8. Evidence-weighted annotation

**Recommended primary molecular-function annotation:** nutrient-regulated Greatwall-family serine/threonine protein kinase that phosphorylates Igo1 at Ser64, enabling phospho-Igo1-mediated inhibition of PP2A-B55/Pab1. (martin2018expressyourselfhow pages 4-5, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 123-125)

**Recommended biological-process annotations:** nutrient-responsive G2/M control and cell-size adaptation; TORC1–TORC2 signaling crosstalk; nitrogen-starvation G1 arrest and sexual differentiation; entry into and survival during quiescence; and, at pathway level, control of Elongator-dependent tRNA modification and codon-sensitive translation. (perezhidalgo2017couplingtorto pages 3-5, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 83-86)

**Localization annotation:** intracellular, precise compartment unknown.

**Principal uncertainty:** Igo1 is the only clearly established direct physiological substrate in the retrieved evidence. Upstream direct phosphorylation of Ppk18, its PAS-domain input, its cellular localization, and the extent to which the 2024 translation phenotypes are caused by Ppk18 alone remain unresolved.
