Gene Description

Ppa2 (SPBC16H5.07c) is the major catalytic (C) subunit of protein phosphatase 2A in Schizosaccharomyces pombe, a 322-residue PPP-family metallophosphatase (EC 3.1.3.16) with a dinuclear Mn2+ active site that hydrolyses phosphoserine and phosphothreonine residues in proteins; its minor, ~80%-identical paralog Ppa1 provides overlapping activity, so neither single deletion is lethal but the double deletion is. Ppa2 does not act as a free enzyme: it assembles with the Paa1 scaffold and either the B55 subunit Pab1 or the B56 subunits Par1 and Par2 to form the canonical heterotrimeric PP2A holoenzymes, whose regulatory subunits select substrates and locations. The bulk of the protein is cytoplasmic. Ppa2-containing PP2A-B55 is the principal brake on mitotic entry: it opposes Cdk1 (Cdc2)-dependent phosphorylation of the Wee1/Cdc25 feedback circuit, so ppa2 deletion advances mitosis and reduces cell size, is lethal with wee1-50 and suppresses cdc25-22, ppa2 is the locus that sets okadaic-acid sensitivity, and overexpression arrests cells in interphase; TORC1 keeps this holoenzyme active in rich medium through inhibition of the Greatwall kinase Ppk18, whereas starvation-induced phosphorylation of the endosulfine Igo1 inhibits it, coupling nutrients to cell size at division and to sexual differentiation (via dephosphorylation of the SAGA subunit Taf12). Later in mitosis, PP1-triggered reactivation of PP2A-B55 and then PP2A-B56 reverses mitotic phosphorylation to coordinate mitotic progression and exit. PP2A-Ppa2 activity restrains the septation initiation network (loss of Ppa2 suppresses SIN mutants and is lethal with the SIN inhibitor cdc16), contributes to Cdc7 asymmetry on anaphase spindle pole bodies, and is required for division-plane positioning, polarized growth, actin-patch localization and normal microtubule arrays. In meiosis I, shugoshin Sgo1 recruits the Par1 (B56)-containing holoenzyme to centromeres, where Ppa2 removes casein kinase 1-dependent phosphates from the cohesin subunit Rec8 and thereby protects centromeric cohesion from separase until anaphase II, a step potentiated by Meikin (Moa1)-Plo1 phosphorylation of Rec8.

Functional annotation report: Schizosaccharomyces pombe ppa2 (UniProt P23636)

Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified: ppa2 (systematic locus SPBC16H5.07c, UniProt P23636) in Schizosaccharomyces pombe strain 972 encodes the major catalytic subunit of protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A). It is not the similarly named gene from another organism. Direct immunoblotting assigned the abundant ≈39-kDa PP2A-like polypeptide to Ppa2 and the less-abundant ≈36-kDa paralog to Ppa1; ppa2 deletion removes the major band, while ppa1 deletion removes the minor band. The two proteins are approximately 80% identical and provide overlapping essential activity, because either single deletion is viable but the double deletion is lethal. These results agree with the supplied PPP/PP2A-family and metallophosphatase-domain annotations. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 1-2, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 6-7, goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 3-4, goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 1-2)

The most defensible primary annotation is therefore: a predominantly cytoplasmic, metal-dependent serine/threonine-protein phosphatase catalytic subunit whose physiological specificity and localization are diversified by the Paa1 scaffold and B-type regulatory subunits. Ppa2-containing PP2A complexes oppose mitotic entry, remove mitotic CDK phosphorylation, regulate cytokinesis and cell architecture, and participate in nutrient-responsive TOR–quiescence signaling. Importantly, many precise substrates are properties of a PP2A holoenzyme—especially PP2A-B55/Pab1 or PP2A-B56/Par1—not demonstrably of free Ppa2 alone.

1. Identity and domain/family verification

The identity matches all mandatory criteria. The literature uses ppa2+ for an S. pombe PP2A-related catalytic-subunit gene and identifies Ppa2 as the major PP2A catalytic species. The supplied InterPro/Pfam assignments—Calcineurin-like phosphoesterase, metallophosphatase, PPA2-like, serine/threonine-specific phosphatase and Metallophos—are mutually consistent with membership in the PPP phosphatase family, PP2A subfamily. Direct functional support includes serine/threonine-phosphatase activity, sensitivity to the PP1/PP2A inhibitor okadaic acid, and the catalytic effect of mutating a conserved metal-coordinating residue. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 1-2, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 2-3, goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 3-4)

Ppa2 must be distinguished from two other catalytic entities in fission yeast. Ppa1 is its closely related, less-abundant PP2A paralog. Ppa3 is instead the alternative catalytic component of the noncanonical SIN-inhibitory phosphatase complex; findings about that complex should not automatically be assigned to Ppa2. (goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 1-2, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 6-7)

2. Primary biochemical function

Catalyzed reaction

Ppa2 catalyzes hydrolytic removal of phosphate from phosphoserine or phosphothreonine residues in proteins:

phosphoprotein–O–PO₃²⁻ + H₂O → dephosphorylated protein–OH + inorganic phosphate.

This is EC 3.1.3.16, consistent with the supplied UniProt description. As with other PPP phosphatases, catalysis depends on a dinuclear-metal active site. The ppa2-6 allele changes Asp70 to Asn; Asp70 participates in catalytic-metal coordination. An analogous phosphatase mutation reduced kcat by more than 10,000-fold, while the S. pombe allele caused cold sensitivity and synthetic lethality with ppa1Δ. The >10,000-fold value is therefore strong family-level mechanistic evidence, not a direct kinetic measurement on purified Ppa2-D70N. (goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 3-4)

Substrate specificity

The catalytic subunit is not appropriately described as having one narrow physiological substrate. PP2A specificity arises from assembly with a scaffold and a regulatory B subunit, which controls substrate recognition and spatial deployment. Ppa2 has direct in vitro activity against phosphorylase and CDK-phosphorylated histone H1, but these are assay substrates rather than established uniquely preferred physiological targets. In extracts, ppa2Δ reduced histone-H1 phosphatase activity by only about 5%; PP2A-like phosphatases contributed an estimated 15–25% of H1/S1 activity under those conditions, while PP1-like Dis2/Sds21 supplied most of it. Thus, histone H1 should not be annotated as Ppa2’s primary cellular substrate. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 6-7, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 7-8)

Physiologically, the best-supported substrate classes are mitotic CDK-phosphorylated proteins, including pathway-level evidence for Wee1 and Cdc25 regulation by PP2A-B55/Pab1, and Gad8 Ser546 regulation in nutrient signaling. These assignments belong to particular holoenzymes and should be distinguished from direct biochemical proof that isolated Ppa2 recognizes those proteins. (martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 3-4, martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 1-3, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 6-7)

3. Holoenzyme organization

Ppa2 operates mainly in PP2A complexes rather than as an autonomous free enzyme. Endogenous Paa1 scaffold immunoprecipitation followed by tandem mass spectrometry detected both Ppa2 and Ppa1 in four replicate experiments, directly supporting Ppa2–Paa1 core-complex formation. Par1/B56 was also detected, although the experiment did not establish stoichiometry or prove that every recovered Ppa2 molecule occupied a Paa1–Par1 heterotrimer. Pab1/B55 and Par2/B56 were not recovered in that particular assay, reportedly likely because of lower abundance; their absence is not evidence against their participation in PP2A complexes. (bernal2012regulationoffission pages 2-3)

The relevant regulatory repertoire is:

  • Pab1/B55, associated especially with mitotic-entry control, reversal of CDK phosphorylation, nutrient signaling and differentiation;
  • Par1 and Par2/B56, associated with mitotic progression, cytokinesis and spatially regulated PP2A functions;
  • Paa1, the common structural scaffold.

Regulatory-subunit localization includes cytoplasm, nucleus, spindle, cell tips and division site depending on subunit and cell-cycle stage. Those patterns cannot automatically be reported as the localization of catalytic Ppa2 itself. (shah2022determiningthefunction pages 33-36, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 6-7)

4. Subcellular localization

Direct antibody-based microscopy, with ppa2Δ as a specificity control, found endogenous Ppa2 predominantly in the cytoplasm, with granular staining and little nuclear signal. Signal was nearly absent in ppa2Δ cells. Upon strong overexpression, Ppa2 remained cytoplasmic but accumulated in restricted bodies and near or on the nuclear periphery; nuclei remained comparatively unstained. This supports “cytoplasm” as the primary localization, while localized holoenzyme action at structures such as the spindle, cell cortex or division site is more likely imposed by regulatory subunits. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 1-2, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 6-7, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 7-8)

Biochemical fractionation was compatible with complex formation: most Ppa1/Ppa2 immunoreactivity eluted near ≈200 kDa rather than as isolated ≈39-kDa catalytic protein. This was preliminary gel filtration and should not be treated as a precise holoenzyme stoichiometry measurement. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 6-7)

5. Cell-cycle signaling

Negative regulation of G2/M entry

The foundational direct study concluded that Ppa2 negatively regulates entry into mitosis. ppa2Δ cells divide at reduced length, ppa2Δ is synthetically lethal with wee1-50 at 33–36°C, and ppa2 deletion partially suppresses cdc25-22: the double mutant grew better at 33°C and completed several divisions after shift to 36°C, whereas cdc25-22 alone did not divide. These genetic relationships place Ppa2-mediated PP2A activity on the inhibitory side of the Cdc2/CDK1 mitotic switch, functionally supporting Wee1 and opposing Cdc25-driven entry. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 1-2, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 4-6)

PP2A-B55/Pab1 is the specific holoenzyme most strongly linked to dephosphorylation of Wee1 and Cdc25, thereby disrupting the positive-feedback loops that activate Cdc2–cyclin B. Consequently, the precise formulation should be: Ppa2 supplies major PP2A catalytic activity, while B55/Pab1 supplies much of the substrate specificity for the Wee1–Cdc25 mitotic-entry circuit. (martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 3-4, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 7-8)

Overexpression gives the reciprocal phenotype. Induced Ppa2 accumulation began after approximately 10–12 h, arrested cell-number increase, increased average length by about 15% between 10 and 14 h, and left abundant interphase microtubules, consistent with delayed mitotic entry. The arrest was substantially reversible rather than immediately lethal. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 4-6, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 6-7)

Mitotic progression and exit

During prolonged metaphase arrest produced by Slp1/Cdc20 depletion, direct ppa2 deletion caused CDK-substrate phosphorylation to appear earlier and remain elevated throughout the experiment. Comparison with clp1Δ led the authors to conclude that PP2A was the major contributor to the observed mitotic dephosphorylation. Regulatory-subunit experiments showed that both PP2A-B55/Pab1 and PP2A-B56/Par1 contribute to this dephosphorylation, even though they have opposite effects on septation under the arrest condition. (chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 3-6, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 7-8, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 6-7)

This fits the established phosphatase-relay model: reactivated PP1 promotes sequential reactivation of PP2A-B55 and PP2A-B56 to order mitotic progression and exit. That relay is holoenzyme-level evidence; it does not imply that free Ppa2 alone has the same timing or localization.

6. Cytokinesis and the septation initiation network

Multiple direct manipulations connect Ppa2 to cytokinesis. The catalytic-site ppa2-6 allele and ppa2 deletion genetically interacted with the SIN: reduced Ppa2 function suppressed several temperature-sensitive SIN mutants, including cdc7, cdc11, sid2 and mob1, yet did not bypass the requirement for SIN signaling. Strong negative interaction with cdc16-116 further demonstrated functional coupling. No single direct SIN protein substrate of Ppa2 has been established by these experiments. (goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 3-4)

The 2022 metaphase-arrest study supplied particularly direct evidence that Ppa2 restrains inappropriate cytokinesis. In ppa2Δ cells, septa appeared after only 1 h of Slp1 repression, when approximately 60% of cells were metaphase arrested, and multiple septa accumulated later. Persistent CDK-substrate phosphorylation did not prevent septation. Specific regulatory-subunit depletion showed that B55/Pab1 normally prevents premature septation, whereas B56/Par1 facilitates septation in this artificial prolonged-arrest setting; the ppa2Δ phenotype most closely resembled loss of B55/Pab1. (chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 6-7, chica2022uncouplingofmitosis pages 7-8)

Thus Ppa2 is not simply an “SIN inhibitor.” It supplies catalytic activity to multiple PP2A assemblies with context-dependent and sometimes opposing effects. The robust annotation is that Ppa2 coordinates mitotic phosphoprotein reversal with the timing and positioning of cytokinesis.

7. Cell morphology and cytoskeleton

Direct ppa2 mutant analysis showed broad but mechanistically coherent architectural defects. Approximately one-quarter of dividing ppa2 mutant cells had misplaced septa. Cell-separation defects occurred in 3.7% of ppa2-6 cells (n=267), compared with 0.5% of controls (n=372). Mutants developed pear- or lemon-like morphology, delocalized cortical F-actin patches, shorter and more numerous interphase microtubule bundles, occasional bent spindles and loss of astral microtubules during anaphase. These observations support roles in division-plane positioning, polarized morphogenesis and maintenance of microtubule organization. (goyal2012characterizationofypa1 pages 8-10)

These effects are likely mediated by several holoenzyme substrates rather than by one structural function of Ppa2. Ppa2 is an enzyme, not a cytoskeletal structural component.

8. Stress, inhibitor response and quantitative phenotypes

Ppa2 is the principal determinant of cellular okadaic-acid sensitivity in the original genetic analysis. At 33°C, 10 µM okadaic acid reduced ppa2Δ division rate by about 50%; 20 µM reduced it by about 90%, compared with only about 20% inhibition in wild type at 20 µM. Supplying wild-type ppa2 restored wild-type sensitivity. Okadaic acid and ppa2 deletion caused overlapping hyperphosphorylation patterns, including increased phosphorylation of ≈45-, 100- and 150-kDa proteins. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 2-3, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 3-4, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 4-6)

Wild-type cells treated with 20 µM okadaic acid were about 15% shorter overall, with septated cells shortening from 13.5 to 11.2 µm. ppa2Δ cells were already about 20% shorter than wild type; drug treatment reduced their mean length to 7.1 µm and produced approximately 50% binucleate cells after 6 h. The high micromolar cellular dose reflects limited yeast uptake and should not be confused with nanomolar biochemical PP2A inhibition constants. (kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 2-3, kinoshita1993negativeregulationof pages 3-4)

9. Nutrient signaling, TOR and differentiation

PP2A-B55/Pab1 links nutrient availability to proliferation versus differentiation. Under nutrient-rich conditions, TORC1 favors PP2A-B55 activity. PP2A-B55 dephosphorylates Gad8 Ser546 and restrains TORC2-dependent differentiation. Nitrogen starvation inactivates TORC1, activates Greatwall–Endosulfine-mediated inhibition of PP2A-B55, increases TORC2–Gad8 signaling and promotes mating/differentiation. In pab1Δ cells, mating-pathway activation was accelerated: Spk1 phosphorylation was maximal after 45 min of nitrogen starvation, whereas wild type showed only a faint signal after 3 h. Transcript profiling found 240 genes elevated at least threefold in pab1Δ; 63 overlapped genes induced by TORC1 inactivation. These experiments manipulated Pab1 rather than Ppa2 and therefore establish a likely Ppa2-containing holoenzyme function, not catalytic-subunit exclusivity. (martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 3-4, martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 4-6, martin2017app2ab55mediatedcrosstalk pages 1-3)

10. Recent development: quiescence and codon-biased translation (2024)

The most important 2023–2024 advance was the December 2024 Nature Communications study, “The Greatwall-Endosulfine-PP2A/B55 pathway regulates entry into quiescence by enhancing translation of Elongator-tunable transcripts” (DOI/URL). It extended the TOR–PP2A model from mitotic control to translational adaptation during nitrogen-starvation-induced quiescence. Greatwall–Endosulfine inhibition of PP2A-B55 permits TORC2–Gad8 activation and supports Elongator and other tRNA-modification systems, including modifications at wobble U34 and A37. This preferentially sustains translation of transcripts enriched in AAA rather than AAG lysine codons, including tsc2, gad8, trm112, ctu1, cgi121 and factors involved in telomeric/subtelomeric organization. (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 1-2)

In igo1Δ cells, which retain excessive PP2A-B55 activity, the study found reduced mcm5s2U34 and an approximately 20–25% reduction in t6A37. Polysome/qPCR and immunoblot analyses tested translation of selected transcripts, with biological triplicates and two-way ANOVA reported for relevant comparisons. This is strong evidence for a PP2A-B55 pathway function, but the 2024 experiments did not directly perturb ppa2/P23636; Ppa1 could potentially contribute. Earlier rescue evidence showed that reducing ppa2 or Pab1 function can suppress aspects of an igo1Δ phenotype, making Ppa2 participation plausible but not uniquely proven. (dedo2024thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 11-12, blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 117-119)

Related quiescence work showed that igo1Δ cells began losing viability after 5–8 days of nitrogen starvation and were nearly all dead by day 15; repression of pab1 partially rescued this defect. Again, this supports the Greatwall–Endosulfine–PP2A-B55 axis rather than a Ppa2-only mechanism. (blanco2021thegreatwallendosulfinepp2ab55pathway pages 83-86)

11. Current applications and model-system relevance

Ppa2 is used experimentally as a tractable catalytic node for studying:

  1. Quantitative control of mitotic commitment, because loss and overexpression shift the G2/M threshold in opposite directions.
  2. Coordination of mitosis and cytokinesis, including mitotic escape under persistent metaphase arrest—a process conceptually related to mitotic slippage and chromosome instability in cancer cells.
  3. PP2A inhibitor biology, through genetically defined okadaic-acid sensitivity.
  4. Nutrient-dependent cell-fate decisions, through PP2A-B55 coupling of TORC1, TORC2, differentiation and quiescence.
  5. Evolutionarily conserved PP2A holoenzyme regulation, because the catalytic core, scaffold/B-subunit architecture, Greatwall–Endosulfine inhibition and CDK-opposing functions are conserved.

These are research-model applications; there is no clinical or industrial implementation specific to S. pombe Ppa2 itself.

12. Evidence gaps and recommended annotation boundaries

Several limits are important for a precise annotation:

  • No comprehensive purified-Ppa2 kinetic study was found that measures metal identity, Km, kcat or peptide-sequence preference directly.
  • Histone H1 and phosphorylase are useful biochemical substrates but not demonstrated primary physiological targets.
  • Endogenous Ppa2 is clearly cytoplasmic, but high-resolution, cell-cycle-resolved localization of the catalytic subunit itself remains less developed than localization studies of its B subunits.
  • Direct binding of Ppa2 to Paa1 is supported, but evidence for each specific Ppa2–Paa1–B-subunit trimer is uneven.
  • Many pathway claims—especially direct Wee1/Cdc25 or Gad8 specificity and the 2024 quiescence mechanism—are best assigned to PP2A-B55/Pab1, with Ppa2 participation described as likely or major rather than exclusive.
  • No major 2023–2024 publication was found that changes the core biochemical annotation of Ppa2 itself; recent advances primarily refine functions of Ppa2-containing PP2A holoenzymes.

Final recommended functional annotation

ppa2/SPBC16H5.07c encodes the major, predominantly cytoplasmic PP2A catalytic subunit of Schizosaccharomyces pombe. Ppa2 is a conserved metal-dependent PPP-family enzyme that dephosphorylates phosphoserine/phosphothreonine residues in proteins. In association with the Paa1 scaffold and B55/Pab1 or B56/Par regulatory subunits, it provides broad catalytic activity whose physiological specificity is imposed by holoenzyme composition. Its best-established functions are negative regulation of Cdc2-dependent mitotic entry through the Wee1–Cdc25 control system, reversal of mitotic CDK phosphorylation, coordination of SIN-dependent cytokinesis, and maintenance of polarized morphology and cytoskeletal organization. Ppa2-containing PP2A-B55 complexes also participate in TOR-regulated differentiation and quiescence, including the recently defined Greatwall–Endosulfine–tRNA-modification pathway, although these nutrient-response functions are supported mainly at the holoenzyme rather than Ppa2-exclusive level.

Identity

  • Ppa2 is the major PP2A catalytic subunit; Ppa1 is the minor ~80%-identical

  • ppa2 deletion removes most extractable PP2A activity and reduces cell size;

  phosphoserine and phosphothreonine residues in proteins; its minor, ~80%-identical paralog Ppa1 provides overlapping
  activity, so neither single deletion is lethal but the double deletion is. Ppa2 does not act as a free enzyme:
  it assembles with the Paa1 scaffold and either the B55 subunit Pab1 or the B56 subunits Par1 and Par2 to form
  the canonical heterotrimeric PP2A holoenzymes, whose regulatory subunits select substrates and locations. The
  bulk of the protein is cytoplasmic. Ppa2-containing PP2A-B55 is the principal brake on mitotic entry: it opposes
  Cdk1 (Cdc2)-dependent phosphorylation of the Wee1/Cdc25 feedback circuit, so ppa2 deletion advances mitosis and
  reduces cell size, is lethal with wee1-50 and suppresses cdc25-22, ppa2 is the locus that sets okadaic-acid sensitivity,
  and overexpression arrests cells in interphase; TORC1 keeps this holoenzyme active in rich medium through inhibition
  of the Greatwall kinase Ppk18, whereas starvation-induced phosphorylation of the endosulfine Igo1 inhibits it,
  coupling nutrients to cell size at division and to sexual differentiation (via dephosphorylation of the SAGA subunit
  Taf12). Later in mitosis, PP1-triggered reactivation of PP2A-B55 and then PP2A-B56 reverses mitotic phosphorylation
  to coordinate mitotic progression and exit. PP2A-Ppa2 activity restrains the septation initiation network (loss
  of Ppa2 suppresses SIN mutants and is lethal with the SIN inhibitor cdc16), contributes to Cdc7 asymmetry on anaphase
  spindle pole bodies, and is required for division-plane positioning, polarized growth, actin-patch localization
  and normal microtubule arrays. In meiosis I, shugoshin Sgo1 recruits the Par1 (B56)-containing holoenzyme to centromeres,
