Gene Description

Nuc2 (Apc3) is an essential tetratricopeptide-repeat (TPR) subunit of the Schizosaccharomyces pombe anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), the 20S, 13-subunit cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase that triggers the metaphase-to-anaphase transition and exit from mitosis. It is the fission yeast orthologue of budding yeast Cdc27 and human ANAPC3/CDC27. The 665-residue protein is built from ten to fourteen tandem 34-residue TPR motifs that pack into an alpha-helical superhelix; with Cut9 (Cdc16/Apc6) and Cut23 (Cdc23/Apc8) it forms the TPR lobe of the complex, Nuc2 being the outermost element whose incorporation is mutually dependent on Cut9 and stabilised by the small subunits Hcn1/Cdc26 and Apc13. Nuc2 has no catalytic activity: ubiquitin transfer is performed by the Apc2 cullin-Apc11 RING module, and the D-box and KEN-box degrons of substrates are read by the coactivators Slp1 (Cdc20) and Ste9/Srw1 (Cdh1) together with Apc10/Doc1. Nuc2 instead scaffolds the ligase, providing the TPR groove that docks the C-terminal Ile-Arg tails of the coactivators and of Apc10 and so couples the substrate-recognition module to the catalytic core. Temperature-sensitive nuc2-663 cells arrest in mitosis with three condensed chromosomes on a short (approximately 3 micron) spindle, unable to elongate the spindle or to destroy Cut2/securin and Cdc13/cyclin B, and at lower restrictive temperatures they septate through the undivided nucleus ('cut' phenotype). Nuc2 is also required, through the APC/C-Ste9 route that removes Cdc13 in G1, for the G1 arrest upon nitrogen starvation that precedes conjugation, so nuc2 mutants are sterile. Independently of the rest of the APC/C, Nuc2 switches off the septation initiation network (SIN) after cytokinesis: loss of Nuc2 leaves the SIN kinases Cdc7 and Sid1 on the spindle pole bodies and Sid2 at the division site and produces multiple septa, whereas Nuc2 overproduction prevents SIN-kinase recruitment, collapses the actomyosin ring and blocks septation. Nuc2 is a nuclear protein that co-fractionates with a salt-resistant nuclear scaffold fraction, consistent with the closed mitosis of fission yeast in which the APC/C acts on nuclear substrates; a bacterially expressed fragment outside the TPR region binds AT-rich DNA in vitro.

Core Functions

Nuc2 forms the outermost element of the APC/C TPR lobe (Cut23-Cut9-Nuc2, stabilised by Hcn1/Cdc26 and Apc13), providing the TPR groove that docks the C-terminal Ile-Arg tails of the coactivators Slp1/Cdc20 and Ste9/Cdh1 and of Apc10, so that the substrate receptor is held against the Apc2-Apc11 catalytic module and Cut2/securin, Cdc13/cyclin B and other D-box/KEN-box substrates are ubiquitinated and degraded at the metaphase/anaphase transition and through mitotic exit.

1. Identity and domain verification

The target is specifically Nuc2 from S. pombe strain 972, corresponding to UniProt P10505 and aliases apc3, SPAC17C9.01c, and SPAC1851.01. Published fission-yeast work describes Nuc2 as a TPR-containing APC/C subunit, while comparative APC/C literature identifies the equivalent subunit as Cdc27/APC3. This agrees with the supplied ANAPC3, TPR_1, TPR_19, TPR-repeat, and TPR-like helical-superfamily annotations. (chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 1-2, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3)

Canonical APC3-family proteins belong to the APC/C’s TPR lobe. Structural syntheses describe Apc3, Apc6, Apc7, and Apc8 as related proteins made largely of approximately 14 TPR motifs. These α-helical repeats create extended protein-interaction surfaces rather than an enzyme active site. Yeast APC/C has 13 core subunits, organized into scaffold, substrate-recognition, and catalytic modules. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3)

2. Primary molecular function

2.1 Structural role in APC/C

Nuc2/APC3 is best annotated as a TPR-repeat scaffold and regulatory docking subunit of the APC/C E3 ubiquitin ligase. In the conserved complex, APC3 contributes to the bowl-shaped TPR lobe and helps position regulatory and substrate-recognition machinery. Repeated TPR-lobe binding sites engage short linear motifs, including C-box and isoleucine–arginine tails on APC/C coactivators or associated subunits. This is a conserved structural interpretation; residue-level interfaces have not all been demonstrated directly in S. pombe Nuc2. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 21-21, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 4-4)

Catalysis must be attributed to the holoenzyme rather than Nuc2. The cullin-like Apc2 and RING-finger Apc11 constitute the catalytic module that recruits ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, whereas a Cdc20- or Cdh1-family coactivator together with Apc10 provides major degron-recognition functions. APC/C substrates commonly contain D-box, KEN-box, or ABBA motifs. Thus, Nuc2 helps create the architecture that makes substrate recognition and ubiquitin transfer possible but does not independently recognize all substrates or catalyze ubiquitin transfer. (alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 4-4, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 2-3)

2.2 Biological output

The Nuc2-containing APC/C promotes the metaphase-to-anaphase transition and mitotic exit through ubiquitin-dependent destruction of proteins such as securin and B-type cyclin. In fission yeast, these correspond principally to Cut2/securin and Cdc13/cyclin B. Destruction of securin permits sister-chromatid separation, while cyclin-B degradation lowers Cdk1 activity and enables mitotic exit. These are holo-APC/C outputs; they should not be represented as direct enzymatic activities of isolated Nuc2. Modern fission-yeast work confirms that APC/C regulation controls the timing of Cut2 and Cdc13 degradation following spindle-checkpoint inactivation. (bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 1-2, bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 18-20)

3. Direct experimental evidence in fission yeast

Essential mitotic function

The founding temperature-sensitive study showed that defective nuc2 blocks spindle elongation during mitotic anaphase. This establishes an essential role near the metaphase–anaphase transition and explains the historical “nuclear scaffold-like protein” terminology. The phenotype is consistent with deficient APC/C activation or integrity, although that early experiment did not itself resolve the modern subunit architecture. Hirano, Hiraoka and Yanagida, published April 1988: https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.106.4.1171. (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 9-10)

Nuc2 also shows a strong functional relationship with Apc10. In a cross between apc10-27 and nuc2-663, six of nine tetrads produced three viable spores, two produced two viable spores, and only one parental ditype produced four viable spores; the inviable progeny were inferred to be double mutants. This synthetic lethality supports complementary essential functions of Nuc2’s scaffold module and Apc10’s substrate-recognition module, but does not by itself establish direct physical binding. Kominami et al., published September 1998: https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/17.18.5388. (kominami1998apc10andste9srw1 pages 9-10)

Regulation of cytokinesis through the SIN

A particularly informative S. pombe study demonstrated that Nuc2 also suppresses the SIN after cell division. The SIN is a Spg1 GTPase-driven kinase cascade assembled on spindle pole bodies (SPBs); it activates the contractile ring and septum machinery. Loss of Nuc2 activity caused persistent SPB localization of Cdc7 and Sid1, persistent Sid2 at the division site, and formation of multiple actomyosin rings and septa. These observations indicate failure to terminate SIN signaling after cytokinesis. (chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 10-11, chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 1-2)

Conversely, Nuc2 overproduction inhibited SIN output. Actomyosin rings formed but were not maintained, and septum construction failed. Among cells containing rings, only 12/61 retained Cdc7 at SPBs and 6/71 retained Sid1, compared with at least 80% of uninduced controls. Protein abundance was not significantly reduced, implicating defective recruitment or retention rather than degradation of the SIN kinases. Time-lapse analysis found no detectable SPB Cdc7 during the division cycle under Nuc2 overproduction, followed by ring disassembly late in mitosis. (chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 6-8)

Nuc2 overproduction did not disrupt localization of the upstream SPB scaffold components Sid4 and Cdc11 or of Spg1 itself, but reduced the Cdc7–Spg1 association. The authors therefore proposed that Nuc2 influences the GTP/GDP state of Spg1, although a direct nucleotide-exchange or GTPase-activating activity was not demonstrated. In nuc2-663 dma1Δ cells, 65.3% of septated cells had segregated DNA and 34.7% had unsegregated DNA, supporting the conclusion that Nuc2 and the checkpoint protein Dma1 inhibit SIN through substantially independent routes. (chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 10-11)

Several controls suggested that this SIN function is not merely a generic consequence of altered APC/C activity. Inactivation of the APC/C components Cut9 or Lid1 did not reproduce the multiseptate phenotype, and APC/C hyperactivation through Slp1 or Ste9 did not reproduce the septation defect caused by excess Nuc2. The most accurate interpretation is therefore that Nuc2 has an APC/C-independent or partly APC/C-independent regulatory function, not that complete biochemical independence has been proven. Chew and Balasubramanian, published 25 January 2008: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.0040017. (chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 10-11, chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 1-2)

4. Cellular localization

The strongest localization assignment is nuclear/mitotic APC/C, consistent with the original nuclear scaffold-like description and with S. pombe undergoing closed mitosis. Its mitotic function is therefore executed in the nuclear compartment where chromosomes and the spindle remain enclosed. Direct retrieved evidence, however, does not provide a modern quantitative Nuc2-GFP localization map across the cell cycle. (yamano2019apcccurrentunderstanding pages 9-10)

Functionally, Nuc2 also controls signaling at spindle pole bodies, but the cited experiments primarily measured the localization of SIN components rather than demonstrating that Nuc2 itself is an SPB-resident protein. Consequently, “regulator of SPB-associated SIN signaling” is better supported than “constitutive SPB component.” Its influence extends to the division site through control of Sid2 and actomyosin-ring maintenance. (chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 10-11, chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 6-8)

5. Pathways and regulatory context

APC/C–spindle-assembly-checkpoint axis

Before all kinetochores are correctly attached, the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) inhibits APC/C through the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC). When the checkpoint is silenced, APC/C–Slp1/Cdc20 rapidly ubiquitinates securin and cyclin B, initiating anaphase and mitotic exit. Nuc2 participates as part of the APC/C scaffold on which these regulatory transitions occur. (bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 1-2, alfieri2017visualizingthecomplex pages 21-21)

A 2022 S. pombe study identified nucleolar Dnt1 as a regulator of APC/C recovery after SAC activation. Deleting dnt1 prolonged inhibitory MCC association with APC/C, reduced Slp1/Cdc20, slowed Cdc13 and Cut2 degradation, and delayed anaphase. Dnt1 associated with APC/C under checkpoint activation, although yeast two-hybrid tests did not establish direct binding to individual APC/C subunits. The experiments generally counted at least 200 cells per time point or sample over at least three replicates. This is current pathway-level evidence for the Nuc2-containing holoenzyme, not evidence of a direct Dnt1–Nuc2 interaction. Bai et al., published September 2022: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1010397. (bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 1-2, bai2022recoveryfromspindle pages 18-20)

APC/C–SIN coupling

APC/C activation and SIN signaling occur sequentially to coordinate chromosome segregation, mitotic exit, and cytokinesis. Nuc2 occupies an unusually informative position in this coupling: it is a canonical APC/C structural component but also negatively regulates SIN after cytokinesis, apparently by influencing the activation state of Spg1 and therefore recruitment of downstream kinases to SPBs. This dual behavior explains why both loss and overproduction cause cytokinetic abnormalities. (chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 1-2, chew2008nuc2pasubunit pages 10-11)

6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

No 2023–2024 primary study focused directly on S. pombe Nuc2/P10505 was identified. Accordingly, older direct organism-specific experiments remain more authoritative for its annotation than recent studies of mammalian CDC27/APC3.

The most relevant 2024 advance concerns spatial control of human APC/C. Cirillo et al. found that cyclin B1 and APC/C interact primarily at the mitotic apparatus and that both bind nucleosomes. Mutation of a positively charged cyclin-B1 nucleosome-binding element delayed cyclin-B1 degradation and produced aneuploidy. Structural analysis implicated an APC3 loop in contacting the nucleosome acidic patch, suggesting that chromatin colocalization accelerates encounters between APC/C and cyclin B1. Published August 2024: https://doi.org/10.1038/s44318-024-00194-2. This is an important current model for APC3-mediated spatial regulation, but it has not been demonstrated for fission-yeast Nuc2. (skrajna2025apccmediatedubiquitylationof pages 2-3)

Subsequent 2025 work strengthened the human mechanism by mapping an HMGN-like motif within an APC3 flexible loop and showing impaired nucleosome binding after APC3 mutation. It also found that APC/C did not detectably ubiquitinate intact nucleosomal histones under the tested conditions, despite binding nucleosomes, underscoring that recruitment and substrate catalysis are distinct. Published March 2025: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57384-7. Extrapolation to P10505 requires sequence conservation and direct mutational testing. (skrajna2025apccmediatedubiquitylationof pages 2-3)

7. Applications and real-world relevance

Nuc2 is principally a research tool and model-system target, not a direct clinical target. Temperature-sensitive nuc2 alleles permit conditional arrest around anaphase and have been used to dissect APC/C function, spindle behavior, checkpoint recovery, and cytokinesis. Overexpression and loss-of-function experiments also provide bidirectional perturbations for mapping the SIN and testing whether candidate regulators act upstream or downstream of Spg1 and SPB recruitment.

The wider APC/C has translational relevance because compromised APC/C activity can alter chromosome stability and response to antimitotic interventions. However, cancer and developmental findings involving human CDC27 cannot be assigned directly to fission-yeast Nuc2. The principal value of S. pombe nuc2 is mechanistic conservation: it offers a genetically tractable system for understanding how a TPR scaffold coordinates irreversible cell-cycle transitions.

8. Recommended functional annotation

Molecular function: TPR-repeat scaffold/regulatory subunit of the APC/C E3 ubiquitin ligase; supports complex assembly and coactivator-associated regulation. No independent enzymatic reaction should be assigned.

Biological processes: metaphase-to-anaphase transition; chromosome segregation; spindle elongation; ubiquitin-dependent proteolysis of mitotic regulators by APC/C; mitotic exit; negative regulation/termination of the SIN after cytokinesis.

Cellular location: principally nuclear APC/C during closed mitosis; functionally connected to SPB-associated SIN signaling and the division site. Direct evidence is stronger for effects on SIN-component localization than for stable localization of Nuc2 itself at SPBs.

Substrates: Nuc2 has no demonstrated autonomous substrate specificity. The Nuc2-containing APC/C targets proteins including Cut2/securin and Cdc13/cyclin B through coactivator/degron-recognition machinery. Assignment of these as “Nuc2 substrates” without the holoenzyme context would be misleading.

Confidence assessment: high confidence for identity, TPR/APC3 family membership, APC/C scaffold function, and essential mitotic role; moderate-to-high confidence for negative SIN regulation; lower confidence for a direct biochemical action on Spg1 or conserved nucleosome binding, both of which remain experimentally unresolved in P10505.

nuc2 (SPAC17C9.01c, UniProt P10505) — curation notes

Identity

  • Anaphase-promoting complex subunit 3 / Apc3; synonyms nuc2, apc3; "nuclear alteration protein 2",
    "nuclear scaffold-like protein p76". 665 aa, APC3/CDC27 family, TPR repeats (IPR019734, PF12895 ANAPC3)
    [file:SCHPO/nuc2/nuc2-uniprot.txt "Belongs to the APC3/CDC27 family."].
  • Orthologue of budding yeast Cdc27 and human ANAPC3/CDC27. Comparator reviews: genes/yeast/CDC16
    (complete), genes/human/CDC27 (complete); genes/SCHPO/cut9 is still INITIALIZED.
  • Cited as a fission yeast exemplar in modules/metaphase_anaphase_transition_and_mitotic_exit.yaml
    (APC/C TPR-subunit platform, PAINT node PTN000285286 "seeded by pombe nuc2 and budding-yeast CDC27").
  • Not present in gocams/index.tsv.

Literature timeline (cached publications; all abstract-only except PMID:15060174)

  • PMID:3283148 (Hirano, Hiraoka, Yanagida 1988, J Cell Biol). Founding paper. nuc2-663 "specifically
    blocks mitotic spindle elongation at restrictive temperature so that nuclei in arrested cells contain a
    short uniform spindle (approximately 3-micron long), which runs through a metaphase plate-like structure
    consisting of three condensed chromosomes". Gene cloned; 665-residue internally repeating protein.
    Fractionation: "p67 cofractionates with nuclei and is enriched in resistant structure that is insoluble
    in 2 M NaCl". Source of the IDA nucleus row.
  • PMID:2561424 (Yanagida 1989, review). "The roles of four representative genes, namely nda3+, nuc2+, top2+
    and dis2+ ... are discussed in regard to the mechanisms and control of chromosome separation." Source of
    one IMP mitotic sister chromatid segregation row; a review, but summarising the 1988 primary data.
  • PMID:2297790 (Hirano et al. 1990, Cell). "The nuc2+ protein contains a domain, separated from ten 34
    amino acid repeat segments, that is capable of binding AT-rich DNA in vitro." The ts mutation lies in one
    repeat. The "snap helix" model is the pre-TPR description of the repeats. Proposed chromosome-scaffold
    model: "The nuc2+ protein may in one way bind to DNA and in another way mutually associate to form a part
    of the chromosome scaffold." Source of the IDA DNA binding row.
  • PMID:7622618 (Kumada et al. 1995, J Cell Sci). "We previously showed that nuc2 is required for exit from
    the mitotic metaphase." "septation occurs in the absence of chromosome separation at the restrictive
    temperature" (cut phenotype). "The nuc2 mutant fails to arrest at the G1 phase upon nitrogen starvation at
    the permissive temperature which is a prerequisite for conjugation." "Ectopic overexpression of the nuc2+
    gene caused multiple rounds of S and M phases in the complete absence of septum formation." Three roles
    proposed: exit from mitosis, DNA replication restraint under starvation, inhibition of septation.
  • PMID:9264466 (Yamada, Kumada, Yanagida 1997, J Cell Sci). "We show here that the fission yeast gene
    products Cut9 and Nuc2 are the subunits of the 20S complex, the putative APC (anaphase promoting
    complex)/cyclosome which contains ubiquitin ligase activity required for cyclin and Cut2 destruction."
    "The assembly of Cut9 into the 20S complex requires functional Nuc2, and vice versa." Key paper for the
    APC/C identity.
  • PMID:9736616 (Kominami, Seth-Smith, Toda 1998, EMBO J; not cached, abstract read via Europe PMC). apc10,
    ste9/srw1 and rum1 mutants fail nitrogen-starvation G1 arrest and are sterile; Apc10 co-IPs with the APC;

    of the "cell cycle switching, mitotic to meiotic" IMP row as an APC/C-Ste9 G1 function.

Structural picture (from comparators and deep research)

  • TPR lobe order Cdc23(innermost)-Cdc16-Cdc27(outermost); Apc3 is the subunit whose TPR groove binds the
    C-terminal IR tails of Cdc20/Cdh1 and of Apc10 [file:SCHPO/nuc2/nuc2-deep-research-falcon.md "Repeated
    TPR-lobe binding sites engage short linear motifs, including C-box and isoleucine–arginine tails on APC/C
    coactivators or associated subunits."]. Catalysis is Apc2-Apc11; degron reading is coactivator + Apc10
    [file:SCHPO/nuc2/nuc2-deep-research-falcon.md "Catalysis must be attributed to the holoenzyme rather than
    Nuc2."].
  • Human APC3 has a flexible loop that binds the nucleosome acidic patch (Cirillo 2024; Skrajna 2025);
    untested for Nuc2 [file:SCHPO/nuc2/nuc2-deep-research-falcon.md "No direct S. pombe experiment establishes
    Nuc2 binding to nucleosomes or chromosomes through the same motif."].

description: >-
  Nuc2 (Apc3) is an essential tetratricopeptide-repeat (TPR) subunit of the Schizosaccharomyces pombe
  anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), the 20S, 13-subunit cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase that
  triggers the metaphase-to-anaphase transition and exit from mitosis. It is the fission yeast orthologue of
  budding yeast Cdc27 and human ANAPC3/CDC27. The 665-residue protein is built from ten to fourteen tandem
  34-residue TPR motifs that pack into an alpha-helical superhelix; with Cut9 (Cdc16/Apc6) and Cut23
  (Cdc23/Apc8) it forms the TPR lobe of the complex, Nuc2 being the outermost element whose incorporation
  is mutually dependent on Cut9 and stabilised by the small subunits Hcn1/Cdc26 and Apc13. Nuc2 has no
  catalytic activity: ubiquitin transfer is performed by the Apc2 cullin-Apc11 RING module, and the D-box and
  KEN-box degrons of substrates are read by the coactivators Slp1 (Cdc20) and Ste9/Srw1 (Cdh1) together with
  Apc10/Doc1. Nuc2 instead scaffolds the ligase, providing the TPR groove that docks the C-terminal
  Ile-Arg tails of the coactivators and of Apc10 and so couples the substrate-recognition module to the
  catalytic core. Temperature-sensitive nuc2-663 cells arrest in mitosis with three condensed chromosomes on
  a short (approximately 3 micron) spindle, unable to elongate the spindle or to destroy Cut2/securin and
  Cdc13/cyclin B, and at lower restrictive temperatures they septate through the undivided nucleus
  ('cut' phenotype). Nuc2 is also required, through the APC/C-Ste9 route that removes Cdc13 in G1, for
  the G1 arrest upon nitrogen starvation that precedes conjugation, so nuc2 mutants are sterile. Independently
  of the rest of the APC/C, Nuc2 switches off the septation initiation network (SIN) after cytokinesis:
  loss of Nuc2 leaves the SIN kinases Cdc7 and Sid1 on the spindle pole bodies and Sid2 at the division
  site and produces multiple septa, whereas Nuc2 overproduction prevents SIN-kinase recruitment, collapses
  the actomyosin ring and blocks septation. Nuc2 is a nuclear protein that co-fractionates with a
  salt-resistant nuclear scaffold fraction, consistent with the closed mitosis of fission yeast in which
  the APC/C acts on nuclear substrates; a bacterially expressed fragment outside the TPR region binds
  AT-rich DNA in vitro.
