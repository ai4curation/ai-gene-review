hgh1 (SCHPO, Q10498, SPAC26F1.12c): is the eEF2 folding co-chaperone assignment structurally supported?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-hgh1-eef2-chaperone-features Source: genes/SCHPO/hgh1/hgh1-ai-review.yaml, selector free-text Target: Schizosaccharomyces pombe 972h- (NCBITaxon:284812), UniProt Q10498, 356 aa, PomBase SPAC26F1.12c

Summary

Verdict: partially supported. The structural half of the seed hypothesis passes cleanly. The AlphaFold model of S. pombe hgh1 (AF-Q10498-F1, 356 aa, mean pLDDT 94.2) superposes on chain A of the S. cerevisiae Hgh1 crystal structure 6HB1 with TM-score 0.826 and a core RMSD of 1.67 Å over 297 of 330 Cα pairs at 42.4% sequence identity. The ARM-repeat solenoid is complete, with no domain loss and no insertion. Both surface patches whose substitution abolished or reduced eEF2 and TRiC binding in budding yeast (PMID: 30876804) are present in hgh1 at the equivalent positions and in the same three-dimensional arrangement: site 1 ELVEF (Sc 7-11) is ELVGF (Sp 6-10), site 2 KNSLF (Sc 236-240) is KNISF (Sp 215-219), and the centroid separation of the two patches is 45.6 Å in the crystal versus 46.6 Å in the model. Fission yeast also carries every partner the budding-yeast mechanism needs: exactly one PTHR13387 family member, one eEF2 (O14460, 842 aa) whose Hgh1-binding domain III is the most conserved part of the protein at 87.3% identity, and a cns1 ortholog (O13754).

Three findings stop this from being a clean "supported". First, the comparison the hypothesis proposes cannot be performed, because no Hgh1-eEF2 or Hgh1-eEF2-CCT structure exists anywhere. The PDB holds only apo S. cerevisiae Hgh1 (6HB1 at 2.33 Å, 6HB2 at 2.70 Å, 6HB3 at 3.00 Å), and RCSB full-text searches for eEF2-chaperonin and FAM203 complexes return nothing relevant. "Map the eEF2-contacting and CCT-contacting residues" therefore reduces to mapping two mutagenesis-defined patches, which is patch-level rather than contact-level resolution, and no CCT-contacting residues have ever been defined on any Hgh1, so the CCT half of the hypothesis has no interface to transfer at all. Second, the conservation test does not discriminate. Exposed residues of the two functional patches sit at 93.3% median identity across a 30-species fungal panel against a background median of 26.7% (Mann-Whitney one-sided, U = 592.5, p = 0.0043, n = 5 vs 140), but the patch that the same paper mutated with no functional effect (Sc 326-333) is conserved just as strongly at 95.0% median identity (U = 273.5, p = 0.0105). A positive conservation result at a candidate surface on an ARM solenoid is therefore uninformative about whether that surface binds eEF2. Third, there is no fission-yeast evidence of any kind for the molecular function: IntAct holds zero records for Q10498, all five PomBase GO lines are ISO from SGD with GO_REF:0000024, UniProt lists protein existence as "Inferred from homology", and none of the 26 FYPO phenotypes recorded for hgh1Δ assays eEF2 folding, eEF2 solubility, elongation rate or chaperone binding.

The practical reading for a curator is that homology-based transfer of the eEF2 folding co-chaperone role to hgh1 is reasonable, and is better supported for fission yeast than the equivalent transfer to human HGH1, but it stays an inference from one budding-yeast study. The structural analysis proposed in the hypothesis cannot upgrade it beyond that. The most actionable result of this run is an annotation-provenance mismatch: the five terms transferred to hgh1 by ISO are not the terms the SGD source set carries, with GO:0051083 more specific than its source evidence, GO:0044183 absent from the source set entirely, and the two IDA-supported molecular function terms (GO:0061770, GO:0030674) missing from the transfer.

Key findings

The fold transfers: hgh1 is a complete Hgh1-family ARM solenoid

AlphaFold DB model AF-Q10498-F1 (v6) covers all 356 residues at mean pLDDT 94.2, with 91.3% of residues above 90 and only the acidic C-terminal tail (334-356) falling below 70. A BLOSUM62 affine-gap global alignment against the 6HB1 chain A sequence gave 330 aligned positions at 45.8% identity of aligned pairs (42.4% of the shorter sequence). Kabsch superposition with iterative outlier pruning gave a core RMSD of 1.67 Å over 297 of 330 Cα pairs, an all-pair RMSD of 4.28 Å, and a TM-score of 0.824-0.826 across two independent runs of the calculation. TM-score 0.82 is well above the 0.5 same-fold threshold. An independent phenix.superpose_pdbs run on the same pair reported 40.9% identity over the aligned length of the fixed molecule with an essentially gap-free alignment across the full solenoid, which is consistent with the numbers computed here.

Domain annotation agrees. UniProt and InterPro assign Q10498 IPR007205 (HGH1 N-terminal, Pfam PF04063 DUF383), IPR007206 (HGH1 C-terminal, Pfam PF04064 DUF384), IPR016024 (ARM-type fold), Gene3D 1.25.10.10, SUPFAM SSF48371 (ARM repeat) and PANTHER PTHR13387. The reference structure is described by its authors as an armadillo repeat protein with a bipartite eEF2 interface (PMID: 30876804: "These experiments revealed that Hgh1 is an armadillo repeat protein that binds to the dynamic central domain III of eEF2 via a bipartite interface").

Both mutagenesis-defined patches are retained, at the same spacing

UniProt P48362 records three mutagenesis experiments from PMID: 30876804: residues 7-11 ELVEF to SAVEA causes loss of interaction with eEF2 and TRiC; residues 236-240 KNSLF to AHSLA causes decreased interaction; residues 326-333 YPLVRELH to APLVAELA has no effect. Mapping these onto hgh1 by structure-guided global alignment gives site 1 = E6-L7-V8-G9-F10 (ELVGF, 4 of 5 identical to Sc), site 2 = K215-N216-I217-S218-F219 (KNISF, 3 of 5 identical), control = Y303-H310 (YPIIRELH, 6 of 8). Both functional patches lie in well-ordered regions of the model (mean pLDDT 96.2 and 97.5) and superpose well on their budding-yeast counterparts (mean Cα deviation 1.79 Å for site 1, 1.06 Å for site 2).

The geometry is preserved as well as the sequence. The centroid separation of the two patches is 45.6 Å in the 6HB1 crystal and 46.6 Å in the hgh1 model, and the site 2-to-control separation is 22.0 Å in both. Across the 30-ortholog panel retrieved through PANTHER PTHR13387 (11 SwissProt-reviewed plus 19 fungal representatives), median patch identities are 80% for site 1, 60% for site 2 and 62% for the control, so site 1 is the most conserved of the three. The patch-versus-control contrast is not statistically separable at this sample size (Wilcoxon signed rank, site 1 vs control W = 131.5, p = 0.062; site 2 vs control p = 0.15).

The conservation argument fails its own control

Shrake-Rupley solvent accessibility on 6HB1 chain A (2854 heavy atoms, 357 residues, 147 with relative SASA above 0.25) gives mean relative SASA 0.32 for site 1, 0.22 for site 2 and 0.13 for the control patch, so site 1 is the most exposed of the three. Per-position identity across the 30-ortholog panel places the exposed residues of the functional patches in the top 6% of surface conservation: E7 at 97% identity (98th percentile of exposed positions), F11 at 93% (96th), K236 at 77% (94th), F240 at 93% (96th). Exposed patch residues versus all other exposed residues give mean 76.0% versus 32.7% identity (Mann-Whitney one-sided, U = 592.5, p = 0.0043, n = 5 vs 140).

The matched control defeats the inference. The patch whose mutation had no measurable effect on eEF2 or TRiC binding has exposed residues Y326 at 100% identity (99th percentile) and H333 at 90% (96th percentile), and as a group sits at 95.0% median identity, also significantly above background (U = 273.5, p = 0.0105). A surface patch on this fold can be in the top percentile of family conservation and contribute nothing measurable to eEF2 or TRiC binding. Interface conservation therefore supports the hypothesis but cannot establish it.

One detail cuts the other way and is worth recording. The single site-1 position that is poorly conserved across the family, Sc E10 at 20% identity and the 31st percentile of exposed positions, is exactly the position that differs in S. pombe (G9). Every site-1 residue whose substitution abolished binding and that is conserved across fungi is also conserved in hgh1.

Fission yeast is the closer ortholog; human HGH1 has drifted at both patches

Against the same reference, human HGH1 (AF-Q9BTY7, 390 aa, mean pLDDT 90.9) keeps the fold but diverges at the interface. TM-score is 0.736 with core RMSD 2.88 Å over 291 of 324 Cα pairs at 27.2% identity. Site 1 becomes EVVKL (21-25), 2 of 5 identical, with a mean Cα deviation of 3.43 Å, the largest local deviation of any patch in either species. Site 2 becomes RNCCF (225-229), 2 of 5 identical, although it superposes tightly (1.02 Å). The inert control patch is better conserved in human (YLILRELH, 5 of 8) than either functional patch.

This bears on the seed hypothesis directly. The hypothesis states that both the budding-yeast and human orthologs "are reported to be" eEF2 folding co-chaperones. Human HGH1 co-purifies with CCT subunits, TTC4 (human Cns1), HSP90AB1 and FKBP5 (PMID: 26496610 and related AP-MS studies), but EEF2 is not recovered in those datasets. The combination of 2-of-5 identity and 3.43 Å structural deviation at site 1 is consistent with that absence. Absence from an AP-MS bait list is weak negative evidence, so this does not show that human HGH1 has lost eEF2 binding, but the premise of the seed hypothesis is stronger on the human side than the evidence warrants.

The ISO transfer does not match its source

The PomBase GAF contains exactly five GO lines for SPAC26F1.12c, every one ISO with GO_REF:0000024 and with/from SGD:S000003419: GO:0005737 cytoplasm (is_active_in), GO:0005634 nucleus (is_active_in), GO:0051083 'de novo' cotranslational protein folding (involved_in), and GO:0044183 protein folding chaperone (enables, listed twice). QuickGO returns seven annotations for Q10498, adding ISS from UniProtKB:P48362 and IEA from UniProtKB-SubCell SL-0086 for cytoplasm. None is experimental.

The SGD source annotations for HGH1 are a different set: GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity (IDA, PMIDs 30876804 and 30876805), GO:0061770 translation elongation factor binding (IDA, 30876804), GO:0006458 'de novo' protein folding (IMP, both papers), GO:0005634 nucleus (HDA, 22842922) and GO:0005737 cytoplasm (HDA, PMIDs 14562095 and 22842922). GO:0051083 is defined as assisting assembly of ribosome-bound nascent chains while other parts of the protein are still being translated. It is a child of GO:0006458. PMID: 30876804 shows that Hgh1 acts on newly synthesised eEF2 and recruits TRiC to the C-terminal module ("Hgh1 binding recruits TRiC to the C-terminal eEF2 module and prevents unproductive interactions of domain III, allowing efficient folding of the N-terminal GTPase module"), which is not a demonstration of nascent-chain engagement on the ribosome.

Twenty-six fission-yeast phenotypes, none of them a test of the mechanism

The PomBase phenotype file (phenotype_annotations.pombase.phaf.gz, 183176 rows) contains 26 FYPO annotations for hgh1Δ from five primary references: viable vegetative cell population with normal morphology (FYPO:0002060, FYPO:0002177; PMIDs 20473289 and 23697806), sensitivity to hydroxyurea (23173672), decreased vegetative cell population viability plus resistance to bleomycin and to calcofluor/KCl (34984977), and 18 drug and nitrogen-source growth phenotypes from a genome-wide screen (37787768), including sensitivity to lithium, amitrole, terbinafine, itraconazole and ciclopirox olamine, resistance to caffeine, brefeldin A, diamide and tert-butyl hydroperoxide, and decreased growth on proline, serine, lysine and glutamate as nitrogen sources. Every one is a cell growth or viability readout.

Paralog confusion is ruled out. S. pombe has exactly one PTHR13387 member (Q10498), one eEF2 protein (O14460, encoded by the duplicate genes eft201 and eft202), and one Cns1 ortholog (O13754). S. cerevisiae likewise has a single HGH1. On the partner side, S. pombe and S. cerevisiae eEF2 are both 842 aa with identical Pfam domain coordinates and 78.5% identity overall, and the Hgh1-binding domain III (PF14492, residues 486-548) is the most conserved domain at 87.3% identity.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The direct activity under test is narrow. hgh1 binds the newly synthesised eEF2 polypeptide through one or two surface patches on its ARM solenoid and presents it to the TRiC/CCT chaperonin, with Cns1 and Hsp90 acting in the same route. This is an adaptor or holdase step. hgh1 performs no catalysis and uses no ATP itself, and the step ends when folded eEF2 is released.

Dashed arrows mark links that have evidence only in S. cerevisiae or only by inference. Solid arrows mark steps whose structural determinants were mapped in this run.

Everything recorded for S. pombe hgh1 sits downstream of that step, or is not connected to it at all. hgh1Δ viability, normal cell morphology, and the hydroxyurea, bleomycin, calcofluor and KCl responses (PMIDs 20473289, 23173672, 23697806, 34984977) are whole-cell growth outcomes. The 18 drug and nitrogen-source growth phenotypes from the genome-wide screen (37787768) are pleiotropic. Reduced eEF2 folding capacity would plausibly produce changes of this kind, and so would a dozen other defects; the screen does not distinguish them. The STRING associations in S. pombe (cns1 0.954, cct6 0.861, eft201 0.796) are interolog transfers of the budding-yeast data, so they restate the ortholog evidence rather than adding to it.

A curator writing a core-function statement should say that hgh1 is predicted to act in eEF2 folding, and should not attach the fission-yeast drug phenotypes to that function as supporting evidence.

Evidence base

Chaperone Function of Hgh1 in the Biogenesis of Eukaryotic Elongation Factor 2, PMID: 30876804, is the single source from which every annotation on hgh1 descends. It supplies the three apo crystal structures (6HB1, 6HB2, 6HB3), the mutagenesis that defines the two functional patches and the one inert control patch, and the mechanistic statement that "Hgh1 binding recruits TRiC to the C-terminal eEF2 module and prevents unproductive interactions of domain III, allowing efficient folding of the N-terminal GTPase module." It also establishes that the reference protein is "an armadillo repeat protein that binds to the dynamic central domain III of eEF2 via a bipartite interface", which is the architecture this run tested for in hgh1. The paper supports the hypothesis in budding yeast and simultaneously sets its limit: the deposited structures are apo, so no residue-level eEF2 or CCT contact map exists.

The Co-chaperone Cns1 and the Recruiter Protein Hgh1 Link Hsp90 to Translation Elongation via Chaperoning Elongation Factor 2, PMID: 30876805, is the independent primary study that assigns Hgh1 the eEF2-recruiting role. Its abstract states that "Chaperoning of eEF2 by Cns1 is essential for yeast viability and requires a defined subset of the Hsp90 machinery as well as the identified eEF2 recruiting factor Hgh1" and that "The in vivo folding and solubility of eEF2 depend on the presence of these proteins." This is the basis for the SGD GO:0030674 IDA and GO:0006458 IMP annotations, and it is what makes the eEF2 solubility assay the most informative discriminating test for fission yeast.

Computed structures of core eukaryotic protein complexes, PMID: 34762488, is the only large-scale resource that could in principle hold a predicted Hgh1-eEF2 model. Its abstract records that the authors "identify 1505 likely to interact, and build structure models for 106 previously unidentified assemblies and 806 that have not been structurally characterized" out of 8.3 million yeast protein pairs screened. The set covers a selected subset of pairs, and the model archive could not be queried programmatically in this run, so whether it contains Hgh1-eEF2 is unresolved. A predicted model would not be experimental interface evidence in any case.

The remaining evidence comes from database records rather than papers read in full here: the TAP-MS associations of HGH1 with EFT1, CCT subunits and CNS1 (16429126), the human HGH1 AP-MS interactor set lacking EEF2 (26496610), the high-throughput GFP localization that is the sole basis for the nucleus annotation (22842922), and the five fission-yeast phenotype references in the PomBase PHAF file.

Conflicts and alternatives

No complex structure, so no contact map. The hypothesis assumes an Hgh1-eEF2 or Hgh1-eEF2-CCT structure exists. It does not. Anything written about "eEF2-contacting residues" of hgh1 derives from two patches defined by substitution mutagenesis in a different organism. No CCT-contacting residues have ever been defined on any Hgh1, so the CCT arm of the hypothesis is unmappable.

The conservation argument is self-defeating as stated. The functionally inert control patch (Sc 326-333, YPLVRELH) is conserved in S. pombe at 6 of 8 and in human at 5 of 8, and its exposed residues are in the top percentiles of fungal surface conservation (Y326 at 100% identity, 99th percentile). Conservation of a surface patch on this fold predicts nothing about eEF2 binding.

Human HGH1 is the weaker case, which cuts both ways. Human HGH1 keeps the fold (TM 0.736) but has 2 of 5 identity at site 1 (EVVKL, mean Cα deviation 3.43 Å) and 2 of 5 at site 2 (RNCCF). Its AP-MS interactor set recovers CCT subunits, TTC4, HSP90AB1 and FKBP5, but not EEF2. If the human ortholog has drifted away from eEF2, the seed hypothesis's premise that both the budding-yeast and human orthologs are established eEF2 co-chaperones is overstated on the human side. This does not weaken the fission-yeast case, which is closer to budding yeast at every measure.

Database carry-over is the main annotation risk. GO:0051083 and GO:0044183 were transferred to hgh1 by ISO but are absent from the SGD source set, so the fission-yeast annotation is in one place more specific and in another less informative than the budding-yeast evidence it claims to copy. The nucleus call propagates one high-throughput GFP observation across a species boundary with an is_active_in qualifier.

Paralog confusion is ruled out. S. pombe has exactly one PTHR13387 member, one eEF2 protein (encoded by the duplicate genes eft201 and eft202), and one cns1. S. cerevisiae likewise has a single HGH1. There is no second family member to which the budding-yeast function could have been misassigned, and no frequency-bias explanation for the annotation.

Further limitations of the analysis itself. The structural work rests on one predicted model per species and one crystal structure, and TM-score agreement between a predicted and a crystal fold is expected for a family this well conserved, so it is confirmatory rather than discriminating. The 30-species panel was trimmed from 80 for execution-time reasons, which reduces the resolution of the conservation percentiles. The control-patch calibration uses only two exposed residues, so its p-value (0.0105) is fragile, but its direction is the point: an inert patch scores as conserved as the functional ones. The RCSB search was full-text, so a complex structure deposited without the expected keywords could have been missed. Absence of Q10498 from IntAct and absence of EEF2 from the human HGH1 AP-MS are both absences of records, and neither is positive evidence that the interaction does not occur.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Ordered by how much each would change the curation decision per unit of effort.

  1. Co-IP or AP-MS of tagged hgh1 from S. pombe, blotting or searching for Eft2 and Cct subunits. A positive result converts GO:0061770 from ISO to IPI and settles the question for this organism. This is the single highest-value experiment.
  2. eEF2 solubility and abundance in hgh1Δ. Fractionate hgh1Δ and wild-type lysates and quantify Eft2 in soluble versus pellet fractions. The budding-yeast defect is a loss of eEF2 folding and solubility, so this tests directly whether the mechanism is preserved, and would support GO:0006458 by IMP.
  3. Patch substitution in S. pombe, mutating ELVGF (6-10) and KNISF (215-219) and testing whether eEF2 binding or solubility is lost, with YPIIRELH (303-310) as the matched inert control. This is the only way to test interface conservation as a functional claim, because sequence conservation cannot.
  4. AlphaFold-Multimer prediction of Sc Hgh1-eEF2 and Sp hgh1-eEF2, reporting ipTM, interface PAE, and whether the predicted contacts land on the two mutagenesis patches. If the Sc prediction recovers the known patches, the Sp prediction becomes interpretable. It must be reported as a prediction, never as an interface measurement.
  5. Polysome profiles and elongation rate in hgh1Δ, to separate a translation-elongation consequence from the drug and nitrogen-source phenotypes already recorded.
  6. GFP-hgh1 localization in S. pombe, which would resolve the nucleus annotation at low cost.

  • Superposition: Kabsch with 12 rounds of outlier pruning (cutoff max(4.0 Å, 90th percentile)); TM-score with d0 = 1.24(L-15)^(1/3) - 1.8, normalised by the shorter chain.

The PomBase REST API and the gene web page return HTML or 404 and could not be used, so the bulk files were used instead. This is a tool limitation, stated as such, not a result.
