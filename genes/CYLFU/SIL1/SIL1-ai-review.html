Gene Description

Silaffin-1 precursor, the founding member of the silaffin family of diatom biosilica-forming polypeptides. The 265-residue precursor carries a cleaved signal peptide, an acidic N-terminal propeptide of uncertain function, and a C-terminal array of seven lysine/serine-rich repeats that is proteolytically processed at pairs of basic residues into the mature mineralization peptides silaffin-1A1 (five copies), silaffin-1A2 and silaffin-1B. The mature peptides are among the most heavily post-translationally modified polypeptides known: specific lysines carry long poly(N-methylpropylamine) chains, others are dimethylated or converted to trimethyl-delta-hydroxylysine, and in the native state essentially every serine is phosphorylated. The resulting zwitterionic peptides self-assemble and precipitate networks of silica nanospheres from monosilicic acid within seconds in vitro, and both the polyamine modification and the phosphorylation are required for that activity. In the cell the peptides are integral, HF-resistant components of the silica cell wall (frustule) and are thought to template and accelerate silica polycondensation during wall biogenesis, acting together with free long-chain polyamines to set silica sphere size and the species-specific wall pattern.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

silicic acid polycondensation activity

Definition: Binding silicic acid and promoting its condensation into solid silica (polymerized silicon dioxide), thereby accelerating silica formation and determining the size and morphology of the silica particles formed. Carriers of this function are typically not classical enzymes but polycationic, post-translationally modified polypeptides or peptide assemblies that provide a charged template surface for nucleation and growth.

Justification: SIL1 (silaffin-1) has no GO molecular function term that fits. Searching GO for "silica" returns only GO:0034021 response to silicon dioxide and GO:0071251 cellular response to silicon dioxide, both response terms; the only other silica-related function is GO:0015115 silicate transmembrane transporter activity, which is a transporter and plainly wrong for a cell-wall structural peptide. The demonstrated activity of the sil1 gene products is the rapid conversion of monosilicic acid into networks of silica nanospheres, which is a molecular function in its own right and is the single most-studied property of this protein family. Per the CLAUDE.md rule on activities with no GO term, the core function above leaves molecular_function unset and points at this proposed name rather than forcing an ill-fitting id. Note that the term is deliberately not called a catalytic or lyase-type activity: although the UniProt FUNCTION line says "Catalyzes the polymerization of silica spheres", no turnover number, active site or catalytic mechanism has been demonstrated, and the primary literature describes the effect as acceleration and templating by a charged surface.

Parent term: molecular_function

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:10550045 : A set of polycationic peptides (called silaffins) isolated from diatom cell walls were shown to generate networks of silica nanospheres within seconds when added to a solution of silicic acid.
  • PMID:11106386 : It is probably this structural element that accelerates silicic acid polymerization and promotes production of nanosphere networks.

silica deposition vesicle

Definition: A membrane-bounded, acidified intracellular vesicle of silicifying organisms, notably diatoms, within which silicic acid is concentrated and condensed into solid biosilica, and whose expansion and shaping determine the morphology of the resulting silicified cell wall element.

Justification: The silica deposition vesicle (SDV) is the defining organelle of diatom biosilicification and the compartment in which silaffins are proposed to act, but GO has no term for it: an OLS search of the go ontology for "silica deposition vesicle" returns zero classes. Without this term, the intracellular site of silica formation cannot be annotated for any diatom protein, and curators are forced either to omit the location or to misuse a generic secretory vesicle term. This review therefore annotates only the mature biosilica location (GO:0030312) and records the SDV as an ontology gap.

Parent term: cytoplasmic vesicle

Supporting Evidence:

  • file:CYLFU/SIL1/SIL1-uniprot.txt : within the silica deposition vesicle (SDV).

diatom silica cell wall

Definition: The silicified external encapsulating structure of a diatom, composed chiefly of amorphous hydrated silica together with an occluded organic matrix of silaffins, long-chain polyamines and associated proteins, and comprising two overlapping valves joined by girdle bands.

Justification: GO:0005618 cell wall carries an explicit "Not to be used for manual annotation" instruction directing curators to a lineage-specific child, but the children it offers cover only bacteria (GO:0009274), plants (GO:0009505), fungi (GO:0009277) and archaea (GO:0030115 S-layer). There is no child for the diatom frustule, which is chemically and developmentally unlike all four. As a result this review annotates the location at GO:0030312 external encapsulating structure, which is one level too general for a structure as well characterized as the diatom frustule. A diatom-specific child of GO:0005618 would let silaffins, frustulins, pleuralins and silicic acid transporters all be annotated at the right granularity.

Parent term: cell wall

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:11106386 : The silaffins are tightly associated with the biosilica so that they can only be solubilized after dissolution of the cell wall in hydrogen fluoride (HF).
  • file:CYLFU/SIL1/SIL1-uniprot.txt : formation of silica cell wall of diatoms.

Core Functions

Silaffin-1 is processed into short, hypermodified polycationic peptides that self-assemble and drive the condensation of monosilicic acid into silica nanospheres, and that remain embedded in the resulting biosilica as structural components of the diatom cell wall. The activity is a property of the modified peptide assembly rather than of a catalytic site: it requires the lysine-linked long-chain polyamines and, in the native state, near-complete serine phosphorylation.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the acidic N-terminal region of the SIL1 precursor (residues 20-107) a functional domain or only a propeptide? UniProt flags this explicitly as unknown. Given that native silaffin activity depends on a zwitterionic balance between polyanionic phosphoserine and polycationic polyamines, an acidic segment could plausibly act as an intramolecular chaperone or a self-assembly regulator that is removed on maturation, rather than being inert.

Suggested experts: Nils Kroeger, Manfred Sumper

Q: Which enzymes install the polyamine, methylation, hydroxylation and phosphorylation modifications on silaffins, and are they themselves silica deposition vesicle residents? These modifications, not the peptide backbone, carry the activity, yet none of the responsible enzymes is identified. They would be the real molecular-function targets for the polyamine-transfer and methylation chemistry; SIL1 is the substrate of those reactions, not their catalyst, so those activities must not be annotated to SIL1.

Suggested experts: Nils Kroeger, Rainer Deutzmann

Q: Should GO create a diatom-specific child of GO:0005618 cell wall, and a cellular component term for the silica deposition vesicle? Both gaps are recorded in proposed_new_terms, and both affect every silicifying organism in GO, not just this gene.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Reconstitute silica precipitation with recombinant, chemically defined SIL1 peptides in which each modification class is installed or omitted combinatorially (polyamine only, phosphate only, both, neither), and measure precipitation rate and resulting sphere size distribution. The literature establishes that both modifications are required, but the two were demonstrated in separate studies on differently extracted material (HF-extracted versus native), so their individual and joint contributions to rate versus morphology have never been separated.

Hypothesis: The lysine-linked polyamines and the serine phosphorylation make separable contributions to silaffin activity, the former driving precipitation rate and the latter controlling self-assembly and resulting silica morphology.

Type: in vitro biochemical reconstitution

Experiment: Localize SIL1 peptides in vivo through the cell cycle by immuno-electron microscopy or a genetically encoded tag, specifically asking whether they are present in the silica deposition vesicle prior to mineral deposition. The SDV is where silica formation is believed to occur, and UniProt's PTM note invokes it, but the direct evidence available for this protein shows only the mature biosilica location, which is why no intracellular location is annotated in this review.

Hypothesis: SIL1 peptides enter the silica deposition vesicle before silica condensation begins, rather than associating with biosilica only after it has formed.

Type: subcellular localization / immuno-EM

Experiment: Knock down or knock out sil1 in Cylindrotheca fusiformis and characterize frustule morphology, silica content and nanosphere size by electron microscopy. All functional evidence for silaffin-1 is currently in vitro precipitation by purified peptides; there is no loss-of-function evidence that SIL1 is required for normal wall formation in the living diatom, which is the main gap between the accepted in vitro activity and the asserted in vivo role in cell wall biogenesis.

Hypothesis: SIL1 is required in vivo for normal frustule silicification, and its loss alters silica nanosphere size or wall patterning.

Type: reverse genetics / phenotypic analysis

SIL1 (Silaffin-1), Cylindrotheca fusiformis — review notes

UniProt: Q9SE35 (SIL1_CYLFU), Swiss-Prot, 265 aa precursor, PE:1 (evidence at
protein level). NCBITaxon:2853.

Summary of what this protein is

SIL1 encodes the silaffin-1 precursor. It is not an enzyme in the usual
sense: it is a post-translationally hypermodified, highly repetitive polycationic
polypeptide that is proteolytically processed into a set of short peptides
(silaffin-1A1, -1A2, -1B) which precipitate silica from silicic acid solution
within seconds. The protein is a structural and templating component of the
diatom silica cell wall (frustule), not a catalyst with a measurable turnover
number.

Architecture from the UniProt record (Q9SE35 feature table):

  • 1–19 signal peptide (ECO:0000255)
  • 20–107 acidic propeptide (PRO_0000032597); UniProt explicitly flags that
    it is unknown whether this acidic N-terminal chain is functional or is merely
    a propeptide (CC -!- DOMAIN)
  • 108–136 silaffin-1B (PRO_0000032598)
  • 141–158 silaffin-1A2 (PRO_0000032600)
  • 163–177, 182–196, 201–215, 220–234, 239–253 — five copies of silaffin-1A1
    (PRO_0000032602, _604, _606, _608, _610)
  • seven atypical/canonical repeats R1–R7 over 108–257, with a declared
    19 X 7 AA consensus S-S-K-K-S-G-S-Y-S-G-S-K-G-S-K-R-R-[IL]-L
  • cleavage occurs on pairs of basic residues (KW Cleavage on pair of basic residues), consistent with the RR/KR motifs flanking each repeat

So one sil1 transcript yields many mineralization peptides from one
precursor — the repeats are the functional units, and the mature peptides share
the Q9SE35 accession. This is the POMC-style case the repo models with
functional_isoforms / CLEAVAGE_PRODUCT rather than separate gene folders.

The silica-precipitating activity

The founding observation PMID:10550045.
The same paper establishes that the activity depends on the lysine modifications,
not on the peptide backbone alone PMID:10550045.

The chemical structure work assigns the activity specifically to the sil1 gene
product PMID:11349130 and confirms the in vitro
activity of the purified peptides PMID:11349130. Note the verbatim typographic quirk "and-1A(2)" in the cached
abstract — quoted as-is.

Native-state extraction showed the peptides are additionally and essentially
phosphorylated PMID:12386330, giving a
zwitterionic peptide that self-assembles PMID:12386330. This matters for how the function should be described: the
active species is a self-assembled polyanionic/polycationic aggregate, and
the earlier HF-extracted ("natSil"/dephosphorylated) preparations were missing a
modification required for the native activity.

The mechanistic reading in the literature is templating/acceleration rather than
catalysis PMID:11106386. Note the hedge ("probably") — this is the authors' inference, not a
demonstrated mechanism.

Localization

Silaffins are cell wall (biosilica) components, and are so tightly held that
they require HF dissolution of the wall to release PMID:11106386. This is direct
evidence for a cell-wall / external encapsulating structure location.

In vivo, silica deposition happens in the silica deposition vesicle (SDV), an
intracellular acidic compartment; UniProt's PTM note refers to it ("may also
control the silica colloid size within the silica deposition vesicle (SDV)").
GO has no term for the SDV (OLS search in go for "silica deposition
vesicle" returns zero classes), so the SDV cannot be annotated — see
proposed_new_terms in the review. I did not annotate an SDV location, because
the cited work demonstrates the mature location (biosilica/cell wall), not a
direct SDV localization experiment for this protein.

GO annotation status — a genuine, total gap

Q9SE35 has zero GO annotations. just fetch-gene CYLFU SIL1 produced a
SIL1-goa.tsv with a header row and no data, and a direct QuickGO query
(.../annotation/search?geneProductId=Q9SE35) returns "numberOfHits":0.
So this review has no existing annotations to adjudicate; its whole content is
NEW rows plus proposed terms. This is unusual, and worth stating plainly:
it is not that curators looked at silaffin-1 and declined to annotate it, it is
that a well-characterized Swiss-Prot entry with PE:1 and four primary papers has
never been annotated at all. There is no PANTHER family for it either
(fetch-gene reported "No PANTHER family found in UniProt data"), which is
consistent — silaffins are lineage-restricted and compositionally biased, so
they sit outside the usual family-propagation machinery.

Ontology gaps that block a faithful annotation

Two terms that this protein needs do not exist, verified via OLS against go:

  1. No MF for silica polycondensation / biosilica nucleation. The only
    silica-related MF in GO is GO:0015115 silicate transmembrane transporter
    activity, which is a transporter and plainly wrong here. Searching go for
    "silica" returns only GO:0034021 response to silicon dioxide and
    GO:0071251 cellular response to silicon dioxide — both response terms, also
    wrong. Per CLAUDE.md, rather than forcing an ill-fitting id I left
    molecular_function unset on the core function and used
    proposed_molecular_function against a proposed_new_terms entry.
  2. No CC for the silica deposition vesicle, as above.

A third, softer issue: GO:0031214 biomineral tissue development is the obvious
"biomineralization" BP, but it is_a GO:0009888 tissue development and its
ancestors run through multicellular-organism development. A unicellular diatom
has no tissue, so I did not use it. GO:0042546 cell wall biogenesis is the
honest BP here. For the location I used GO:0030312 external encapsulating
structure rather than GO:0005618 cell wall, because GO:0005618 carries an
explicit "Not to be used for manual annotation" instruction directing curators
to a lineage-specific child, and the children it lists (bacterial, plant-type,
fungal-type, S-layer) have no diatom option. The missing diatom child is
recorded as a proposed term.

CMMMech cross-check (biosilica / silica precipitation)

CMMMech grounds Q9SE35 in data/records/anatase.yaml, in the
inp_silaffin_templated_anatase_graph causal graph, as node
sil1_mineralization_peptide. The source is Ping et al. 2016 (Chem. Sci. 7:6330–6336,
DOI 10.1039/C6SC02311H), an engineered E. coli surface-display construct: the
InaZ ice-nucleation anchor fused to the synthetic Sil-1 peptide
SSKKSGSYSGSKGSKRRIL, used to template amorphous titanium oxide from TALH,
which becomes anatase only after 600 °C calcination.

Two observations, recorded here rather than as source_defects (per the brief,
source_defects is for GOA/UniProt/InterPro, not for CMMMech's own fields):

  1. The CMMMech node description overstates the biology. It describes the
    SIL1 node as "C-terminal silaffin peptide domain catalyzing titanium
    precursor condensation". Nothing in the silaffin literature supports
    catalysis, and the activity demonstrated for silaffin-1 is on silicic
    acid → silica    , not on titanium. CMMMech's own evidence block is
    appropriately careful (supports: partial, non-engineered controls absent),
    and the record-level functional_role says "nucleates", which is better.
    The mismatch is confined to the node description field. UniProt's own
    CC -!- FUNCTION line unfortunately uses the same loose word ("Catalyzes the
    polymerization of silica spheres"), so CMMMech may simply have inherited it.
    I did not propagate "catalyzes" into this review.
  2. The grounded peptide is a repeat-consensus synthetic peptide, not the
    protein.     SSKKSGSYSGSKGSKRRIL is the R3–R7 consensus
    (S-S-K-K-S-G-S-Y-S-G-S-K-G-S-K-R-R-[IL]-L), chemically synthesized and
    therefore carrying none of the polyamine, methylation, hydroxylation or
    phosphorylation modifications that PMID:10550045 and PMID:12386330 show
    are required for the native silica-precipitating activity. The grounding to
    Q9SE35 is correct as a sequence provenance statement, which is all CMMMech's
    annotation_note claims. But any inference from the Ping et al. result back
    to the in vivo function of the diatom protein would be unsound in both
    directions: the construct is unmodified, heterologous, surface-displayed, and
    mineralizing a different element. Nothing from Ping et al. 2016 entered this
    review as evidence.

I did not edit CMMMech.

Review decisions

No existing annotations, so no ACCEPT/MODIFY/REMOVE calls. Three NEW rows,
kept deliberately narrow:

  • GO:0030312 external encapsulating structure (CC, IDA, PMID:11106386) — the
    HF-requirement result is direct evidence of integration into the wall.
  • GO:0042546 cell wall biogenesis (BP, IDA, PMID:10550045) — silaffin-1
    peptides themselves perform the silica precipitation step that builds the
    wall, so the participation test in CLAUDE.md is satisfied: this gene product
    does the work, it is not merely required for or consumed by the process.
  • GO:0005199 structural constituent of cell wall (MF, IDA, PMID:11106386) —
    the tight, HF-resistant association with biosilica supports a structural role
    in the wall. This is the structural half of the function; the
    precipitating half has no GO term and is left to proposed_new_terms.

I considered and rejected a NEW row for GO:0031214 biomineral tissue
development (wrong branch: tissue/multicellular, see above) and for any
polyamine-biosynthesis term (the polyamine modification is installed on SIL1 by
other, unidentified enzymes — SIL1 is the substrate of that modification, which
is exactly the non-participation case CLAUDE.md warns about).

- proposed_name: silicic acid polycondensation activity
  proposed_definition: >-
    Binding silicic acid and promoting its condensation into solid silica
    (polymerized silicon dioxide), thereby accelerating silica formation and
    determining the size and morphology of the silica particles formed. Carriers of
    this function are typically not classical enzymes but polycationic,
    post-translationally modified polypeptides or peptide assemblies that provide a
    charged template surface for nucleation and growth.
  justification: >-
    SIL1 (silaffin-1) has no GO molecular function term that fits. Searching GO for
    "silica" returns only GO:0034021 response to silicon dioxide and GO:0071251
    cellular response to silicon dioxide, both response terms; the only other
    silica-related function is GO:0015115 silicate transmembrane transporter
    activity, which is a transporter and plainly wrong for a cell-wall structural
    peptide. The demonstrated activity of the sil1 gene products is the rapid
    conversion of monosilicic acid into networks of silica nanospheres, which is a
    molecular function in its own right and is the single most-studied property of
    this protein family. Per the CLAUDE.md rule on activities with no GO term, the
    core function above leaves molecular_function unset and points at this proposed
    name rather than forcing an ill-fitting id. Note that the term is deliberately
    not called a catalytic or lyase-type activity: although the UniProt FUNCTION
    line says "Catalyzes the polymerization of silica spheres", no turnover number,
    active site or catalytic mechanism has been demonstrated, and the primary
    literature describes the effect as acceleration and templating by a charged
    surface.

- hypothesis: >-
    The lysine-linked polyamines and the serine phosphorylation make separable
    contributions to silaffin activity, the former driving precipitation rate and
    the latter controlling self-assembly and resulting silica morphology.
  description: >-
    Reconstitute silica precipitation with recombinant, chemically defined SIL1
    peptides in which each modification class is installed or omitted
    combinatorially (polyamine only, phosphate only, both, neither), and measure
    precipitation rate and resulting sphere size distribution. The literature
    establishes that both modifications are required, but the two were demonstrated
    in separate studies on differently extracted material (HF-extracted versus
    native), so their individual and joint contributions to rate versus morphology
    have never been separated.
