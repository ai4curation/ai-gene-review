# Rhodobacter OpgGIH identity and assay scope

Reviewed 2026-11-06. Primary publisher full text was accessible through the web reader; direct HTTP acquisition returned 403. The normal publication caches remain unchanged and abstract-only.

## Primary genetic source

PMID:12027885: https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1432-1033.2002.02907.x

Verbatim Table2 caption: "OPG production in various opg mutants of R. sphaeroides 2.4.1."

Table1 identifies AP5 (opgG), EG7/EG18 (opgH), EG13/EG131 (opgI), and EG238 (ndvB) as2.4.1 derivatives. The cloned cosmid pUI8166 comes from 2.4.1T. Table 2 shows cognate rescue of AP5 and EG18; EG7 produces neutral glucans after opgH-only rescue because the insertion is polar on opgC. OpgI rescue depends on upstream promoter and insertion orientation. The paper deposits AF016298. OpgH has GT2 motifs and OpgIH rescues Ecoli mdoH with linear products; this does not assign ring closure to OpgH. The authors propose, rather than demonstrate, OpgG rearrangement/cyclization. Heterologous OpgI function could include translational coupling.

## Product structure source

PMID:12027884: https://febs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1432-1033.2002.02906.x

Verbatim Figure7 caption fragment: "Primary sequences of the OPG of R. sphaeroides WS8."

The methods identify WS8 and its opgC mutant NFB4000. NMR resolves 17 beta1,2 linkages and one alpha1,6 closure in the predominant 18-residue ring. This is species-comparative chemistry, not direct2.4.1 NMR. Mixed anomeric linkage does not make the beta-glucan annotation categorically false.
