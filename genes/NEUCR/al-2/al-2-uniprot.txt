CC   -!- FUNCTION: Bifunctional enzyme that catalyzes the reactions from
CC       geranylgeranyl diphosphate to phytoene (phytoene synthase) and from
CC       lycopene to beta-carotene via the intermediate gamma-carotene and from
CC       3,4-didehydrolycopene to torulene (lycopene cyclase). Torulene is
CC       further processed to the acidic carotenoid neurosporaxanthin. The
CC       cyclase preferentially catalyzes single cyclizations at only one end of
CC       the substrate to produce monocyclic carotenoids (PubMed:11862485,
CC       PubMed:16928467). Neurosporaxanthin is synthesized from geranyl-geranyl
CC       pyrophosphate (GGPP) through several enzymatic activities. Phytoene
CC       synthase activity performed by the bifunctional enzyme al-2 first
CC       produces phytoene from geranyl-geranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP). The
CC       phytoene dehydrogenase al-1 then introduces 5 desaturations to lead to
CC       3,4-didehydrolycopene via the intermediates phytofluene, zeta-carotene,
CC       neurosporene and lycopene. Al-2 cyclase activity then converts 3,4-
CC       didehydrolycopene into torulene. Al-2 can also convet lycopene into
CC       gamma-carotene which in turn is converted to beta-carotene by an
CC       additional al-2 cyclization reaction. Torulene is the substrate of the
CC       dioxidase cao-2 that breaks the molecule, removing five carbon atoms to
CC       yield beta-apo-4'-carotenal, whereas the aldehyde dehydrogenase ylo-1
CC       mediates the last step by converting beta-apo-4'-carotenal into
CC       neurosporaxanthin (Probable). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11862485,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16928467, ECO:0000305|PubMed:11862485}.
