CC   -!- FUNCTION: Phytoene desaturase involved in the carotenoid biosynthesis
CC       pathway (PubMed:11017770, PubMed:18812228, PubMed:2144609). Converts
CC       phytoene into 3,4-didehydrolycopene via the intermediates phytofluene,
CC       zeta-carotene, neurosporene and lycopene, by introducing up to five
CC       double bonds into phytoene (PubMed:11017770, PubMed:18812228). Is also
CC       able to desaturate 1-hydroxyneurosporene into 1-hydroxylycopene and 1-
CC       hydroxylycopene into 1-hydroxy-3,4-didehydrolycopene (PubMed:11017770).
CC       Gamma-carotene and 1,19-dihydroxylycopene are not accepted as
CC       substrates (PubMed:11017770). Neurosporaxanthin is synthesized from
CC       geranyl-geranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP) through several enzymatic
CC       activities. Phytoene synthase activity performed by the bifunctional
CC       enzyme al-2 first produces phytoene from geranyl-geranyl pyrophosphate
CC       (GGPP). The phytoene dehydrogenase al-1 then introduces 5 desaturations
CC       to lead to 3,4-didehydrolycopene via the intermediates phytofluene,
CC       zeta-carotene, neurosporene and lycopene. Al-2 cyclase activity then
CC       converts 3,4-didehydrolycopene into torulene. Al-2 can also convet
CC       lycopene into gamma-carotene which in turn is converted to beta-
CC       carotene by an additional al-2 cyclization reaction. Torulene is the
CC       substrate of the dioxidase cao-2 that breaks the molecule, removing
CC       five carbon atoms to yield beta-apo-4'-carotenal, whereas the aldehyde
CC       dehydrogenase ylo-1 mediates the last step by converting beta-apo-4'-
CC       carotenal into neurosporaxanthin (Probable).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11017770, ECO:0000269|PubMed:18812228,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:2144609, ECO:0000305|PubMed:11017770}.
