# Functional annotation report: *Neurospora crassa al-1* / AL-1 (UniProt P21334)

## Executive conclusion

The identity check is positive: **the requested *al-1* is the *Neurospora crassa* carotenoid-biosynthesis gene encoding AL-1, a phytoene desaturase/dehydrogenase**, not a similarly named protein from another organism. The published P21334 sequence has been used explicitly as the *N. crassa* AL-1 reference for identifying fungal orthologs. The NCU00552 locus and specified 74-OR23-1A-derived strain identifiers are consistent with the UniProt record supplied in the question; literature independently confirms the *al-1*–phytoene-desaturase assignment (sandmann2022carotenoidsandtheir pages 13-14, strobel2009carotenoidsandcarotenogenic pages 1-2).

AL-1’s primary role is unusually broad for a carotenoid desaturase: **one enzyme executes all five sequential desaturation reactions beginning with phytoene**, producing the highly conjugated carotene branch that includes 3,4-didehydrolycopene and culminates in torulene. Torulene is then cleaved and oxidized by other enzymes to the orange carboxylic xanthophyll neurosporaxanthin. Accordingly, AL-1 is the central “polyene-generating” enzyme responsible for visible carotenoid pigmentation, but it is neither the upstream phytoene synthase nor the downstream carotenoid-cleavage enzyme (diazsanchez2011analysisofal2 pages 1-2, avalos2017carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 3-5).

## 1. Identity, nomenclature, and family assignment

The relevant identifiers are **gene *al-1***, systematic locus **NCU00552**, protein **AL-1/albino-1**, and UniProt **P21334** in *N. crassa*. The foundational cloning paper, published in October 1990, characterized the sequence and photoregulation of *al-1* as a carotenoid-biosynthesis gene: Schmidhauser et al., *Molecular and Cellular Biology* 10:5064–5070, DOI [10.1128/MCB.10.10.5064-5070.1990](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.10.10.5064-5070.1990) (sandmann2022carotenoidsandtheir pages 13-14, diazsanchez2011analysisofal2 pages 10-11).

The supplied UniProt annotation places AL-1 in the **carotenoid/retinoid oxidoreductase family** and reports Amino_oxidase/phytoene-dehydrogenase and FAD/NAD-binding-superfamily signatures (InterPro IPR002937, IPR014105, IPR036188 and IPR008150; Pfam PF01593). These assignments fit the older comparative finding that fungal and bacterial carotenoid desaturases contain regions homologous to flavoprotein disulfide oxidoreductases. They support a flavin-dependent oxidation mechanism, but a domain match alone should not be interpreted as proof of the identity of the physiological electron acceptor.

**Important nomenclature distinction:** AL-1 is the desaturase. AL-2 is the bifunctional phytoene synthase/carotene-cyclase protein, and AL-3 supplies geranylgeranyl diphosphate. Papers on *al-1* homologs in *Fusarium*, *Podospora*, chanterelles, mites, or plants are not direct studies of P21334 and are treated here only as comparative or application evidence.

## 2. Primary molecular function and reaction

### Physiological substrate and chemistry

The established physiological substrate is **phytoene**, a colorless C40 carotene formed by AL-2. AL-1 repeatedly removes pairs of hydrogen atoms and installs additional carbon–carbon double bonds. Five successive desaturations extend the conjugated π-electron system sufficiently for absorption in the visible spectrum and formation of colored carotenoids. Purified or heterologously expressed AL-1 was reported to perform all five desaturation steps, making it a **single five-step phytoene desaturase** rather than a set of separate enzymes (avalos2017carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 3-5, diazsanchez2011analysisofal2 pages 10-11).

The pathway is best summarized as:

**GGPP → phytoene —[AL-1; five desaturations]→ highly conjugated acyclic carotenes/3,4-didehydrolycopene → cyclization branch yielding torulene —[CAO-2]→ C35 apocarotenal —[YLO-1 and associated oxidation]→ neurosporaxanthin.**

The retrieved literature establishes AL-1 as responsible for all carotenoid desaturation reactions and identifies fully desaturated torulene as the substrate entering the downstream cleavage sequence. It does not support assigning CAO-2 cleavage or terminal aldehyde oxidation to AL-1 (diazsanchez2011analysisofal2 pages 1-2, sandmann2022carotenoidsandtheir pages 13-14).

### Cofactor and electron acceptor

Family/domain evidence is consistent with **FAD-dependent oxidoreductase chemistry**, but the exact native electron-transfer partner or terminal electron acceptor for P21334 was not resolved in the retrieved biochemical evidence. The broad EC annotation **EC 1.3.99.-** therefore appropriately denotes oxidation of a carbon–carbon bond with an incompletely specified physiological acceptor. Statements that AL-1 directly uses NAD(P), oxygen, quinone, or a particular membrane electron carrier should be considered unverified unless supported by a dedicated reconstitution study.

### Substrate specificity and possible promiscuity

Phytoene is the demonstrated pathway substrate. A 2011 *N. crassa* study found a trace diapolycopene-like C30 compound in *al-2* mutants but not in an *al-1* knockout, leading to the hypothesis that AL-1 can weakly desaturate **squalene** when phytoene formation is blocked. The authors explicitly noted that purified-enzyme assays were needed; thus, squalene remains a proposed promiscuous substrate, not an established physiological one. Its trace abundance may reflect poor access of AL-1 to the sterol-pathway substrate (diazsanchez2011analysisofal2 pages 4-5).

## 3. Genetic and phenotypic evidence

Classical *al-1* mutants are albino because they cannot convert colorless phytoene into conjugated colored carotenes. The albino/phytoene-accumulating phenotype, together with cloning, heterologous expression, and biochemical activity, provides mutually reinforcing genetic and enzymological evidence for the annotation. In a later targeted comparison, an *al-1* deletion also eliminated the trace diapolycopene-like material observed in *al-2* mutants, independently supporting a requirement for AL-1 in carotene desaturation (diazsanchez2011analysisofal2 pages 4-5, avalos2017carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 3-5).

The phenotype should be interpreted precisely: AL-1 loss blocks pigmentation at the desaturation stage but does not imply a general developmental defect. Carotenoid deficiency may reduce photoprotection, yet the strongest direct phenotype for this locus is the biochemical block and albino appearance.

## 4. Pathway and biological process

AL-1 functions in the **neurosporaxanthin biosynthetic pathway**, a specialized fungal carotenoid pathway. Its position is downstream of isoprenoid precursor and phytoene production and upstream of cyclization/cleavage and terminal oxidation. Neurosporaxanthin is a carboxylic xanthophyll responsible for much of the orange pigmentation of *Neurospora* conidia and illuminated mycelium (diazsanchez2011analysisofal2 pages 1-2, strobel2009carotenoidsandcarotenogenic pages 1-2).

Carotenoids generally protect fungi against photo-oxidative stress by quenching excited sensitizers and reactive oxygen species. This biological interpretation is consistent with the strong induction of the pathway by light and oxidants, but it is broader pathway-level evidence rather than proof that every pleiotropic stress phenotype is caused specifically by AL-1.

AL-1 also sits at the intersection of environmental signaling and secondary metabolism. In *N. crassa*, carotenoid formation is linked to conidiation and can occur through developmental regulation independently of light; *al-1* and *al-2* transcripts also increase under nitrogen limitation. These observations indicate that AL-1 output is controlled both by illumination and by nutritional/developmental state (avalos2017carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 10-12).

## 5. Cellular localization

A precise, experimentally validated localization for native P21334 was **not found**. The retrieved literature does not document AL-1 localization by fluorescent fusion, immunoelectron microscopy, validated membrane fractionation, or organelle proteomics. Therefore, assigning AL-1 definitively to mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, plasma membrane, or another organelle would overstate the evidence.

A membrane-associated or specialized cytoplasmic-compartment model is biologically plausible because phytoene and downstream carotenes are extremely hydrophobic. Isotope-labeling studies in related fungal systems also suggest that carotenoid synthesis is compartmentalized separately from sterol and gibberellin formation. However, those data establish pathway compartmentalization rather than direct localization of *N. crassa* AL-1 (avalos2017carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 3-5, avalos2017carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 10-12).

The UniProt “precursor” flag should likewise not be treated, by itself, as proof of a conventional secretory signal peptide or extracellular localization. AL-1’s substrates and function place its activity inside the cell; there is no evidence that it is secreted.

## 6. Regulation by light, development, oxygen, and oxidative stress

The *al-1* gene is a classical blue-light-responsive structural gene. In *N. crassa*, light is perceived by the **White Collar Complex (WCC)**, composed of WC-1 and WC-2; activated WCC binds light-responsive promoters and induces carotenoid-biosynthesis genes. This is transcriptional regulation of AL-1 abundance, not photochemical activation of the enzyme itself. Photoadaptation through VVD and broader regulators such as the RCO-1/RCM-1 and velvet systems modulates the duration and amplitude of the light response at the pathway level (sandmann2022carotenoidsandtheir pages 13-14, gmoser2017filamentousascomycetesfungi pages 10-12).

Quantitative observations summarized from *N. crassa* work include approximately **fivefold induction of carotenoid-biosynthetic gene expression by elevated oxygen** and an approximately **twofold rise in *al-1* mRNA after extracellular H₂O₂**. These values link carotenoid induction to oxidative conditions, although they are expression measurements rather than AL-1 kinetic parameters (gmoser2017filamentousascomycetesfungi pages 10-12).

Broader fungal photoinduction studies report maximal early carotenoid accumulation around 12 hours after illumination, with slower accumulation continuing for at least three days, and show dependence on oxygen and new protein synthesis. Those timing data are useful context but should not be assigned specifically to P21334 because the underlying experiments were mainly in other fungi (avalos2017carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 3-5).

## 7. Structural and evolutionary interpretation

Sequence conservation places AL-1 among fungal/bacterial-type carotenoid desaturases rather than plant-type plastid phytoene desaturases. The amino-oxidase-like and FAD/NAD-binding-superfamily annotations are consistent with a flavoprotein fold that repeatedly oxidizes a hydrophobic polyene substrate. The most functionally distinctive property is its processive or iterative catalytic breadth: one AL-1 protein can install five double bonds, whereas other carotenoid systems partition comparable chemistry among enzymes or stop at less-desaturated products.

Ortholog comparisons support conservation of the desaturase function across fungi. For example, the P21334 sequence was used to identify a *Podospora anserina* ortholog involved in lycopene/3,4-dehydrolycopene formation. Such studies strengthen the evolutionary assignment but do not substitute for direct localization or kinetic measurements on native AL-1 (strobel2009carotenoidsandcarotenogenic pages 1-2).

## 8. Recent developments and applications

### 2023–2024 research status

No 2023–2024 paper retrieved here materially changed the core native annotation of P21334; the most decisive AL-1-specific biochemical work remains older. Recent work has instead shifted toward **deploying the compact fungal pathway in synthetic biology** and defining broader transcriptional control.

A June 2023 *Molecular Plant* study installed the *Neurospora* carotenoid pathway in plants, enabling **cytosolic provitamin-A formation and sequestration in lipid droplets**: Zheng et al., DOI [10.1016/j.molp.2023.05.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molp.2023.05.003). This is a real heterologous implementation of the pathway concept, but the retrieved evidence does not isolate an AL-1-specific yield contribution or establish native localization from that engineered context (avalos2017carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 3-5).

A 2024 study examined CRZ-1 binding and regulation of *al-2*, *cao-2*, and *ylo-1* in *N. crassa*—DOI [10.1007/s11557-024-01973-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11557-024-01973-1)—but did not directly redefine AL-1 function. It is therefore relevant to pathway regulation, not direct evidence about P21334 catalysis.

### Current and prospective applications

1. **Metabolic engineering of carotenoids:** AL-1’s ability to perform five reactions with one coding sequence is attractive for compact synthetic pathways producing torulene, β-carotene/provitamin A, and derived apocarotenoids.
2. **Plant biofortification:** The 2023 cytosolic pathway demonstrates that fungal carotenoid modules can bypass normal plastid confinement and couple products to lipid-droplet storage.
3. **Fungal pigment bioprocessing:** *Neurospora* and other edible filamentous fungi are being investigated as sources of natural pigments in food, feed, cosmetics, and waste-based biorefineries. Reviews emphasize that *Neurospora* process development remains less mature than commercial yeast carotenoid systems.
4. **Functional screens and pathway diagnostics:** The visually obvious albino phenotype makes *al-1* a useful marker for carotenoid-pathway genetics, light-response studies, and testing desaturase variants.

Comparative engineering gives useful scale context but should not be confused with native AL-1 performance. In *P. anserina*, pathway engineering increased carotenoid synthesis as much as **eightfold**, whereas overexpression of *al-2*, not *al-1*, extended mycelial lifespan by up to **31%**. These numbers support the broader engineering potential of fungal carotenoid pathways but are not quantitative data for P21334 in *N. crassa* (strobel2009carotenoidsandcarotenogenic pages 1-2).

## 9. Evidence-weighted annotation

**Recommended primary annotation:**

> AL-1 (P21334; NCU00552) is an intracellular fungal phytoene desaturase/dehydrogenase in the carotenoid/retinoid oxidoreductase family. Loss of AL-1 blocks colored-carotenoid formation and causes an albino, phytoene-accumulating phenotype. Expression is induced by blue-light signaling and modulated by development, nutrient status, oxygen, and oxidative stress. Exact native subcellular localization and physiological electron acceptor remain unresolved.
