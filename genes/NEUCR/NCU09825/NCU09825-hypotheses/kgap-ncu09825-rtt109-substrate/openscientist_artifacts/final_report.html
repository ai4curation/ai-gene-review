AIGR gene hypothesis deep research: NCU09825 / RTT109 (Q7S1R7)

Organism: Neurospora crassa OR74A (NCBITaxon:367110) Gene directory / symbol: NCU09825 UniProt accession: Q7S1R7 Hypothesis slug: kgap-ncu09825-rtt109-substrate Source file: genes/NEUCR/NCU09825/NCU09825-ai-review.yaml, selector free-text Focus type: free_text

Summary

The seed hypothesis proposes that the identity of the non-H3K56 RTT109 substrate supporting PRC2-repressed chromatin in N. crassa "may be constrained by the enzyme's specificity determinants". I ran that analysis in full. The answer is that the determinants do not constrain it, and the reason they do not is specific and informative rather than a failure of method.

In S. cerevisiae, Rtt109 switches between two programmes according to which histone chaperone binds it: Asf1-bound Rtt109 makes H3K56ac, Vps75-bound Rtt109 makes H3K9ac and H3K27ac (PMID: 18458063, PMID: 21256037). The residues that implement that switch are the Vps75 interface. My alignment finds those are exactly the residues that are not conserved in N. crassa: 4 of 9 mapped interface positions are non-conservative substitutions, and across 7 fungal orthologs the interface determinants are conserved in only 0-43% of cases. Meanwhile the catalytic and acetyl-CoA determinants are near-totally conserved, which tells you the enzyme works but says nothing about what it works on, because those residues are shared by every candidate reaction. The substrate side gives nothing either: N. crassa and S. cerevisiae histone H3 are 94.9% identical with no indels, and the ±6 residue windows at K4, K9, K14, K18, K23, K27 and K36 are character-for-character identical between the two species.

Published N. crassa genetics settles the other half of the question and closes the only route the orthology argument offered. Yap, Ebot-Ojong, Ameri-Solanky and Lewis (PMID: 42832562, PLoS Genet 2026;22(10):e1012327, doi 10.1371/journal.pgen.1012327; preprint PMID: 42182108, doi 10.64898/2026.05.14.724656) separate catalysis from product by genetics. Catalytically inactive alleles rtt109 D145A and rtt109 D145A-DD304-305AA, integrated at csr-1 in a Δrtt109 background, fail to restore H3K27me3, while a wild-type rtt109-3xflag construct does. The histone point mutants hH3 K56R and hH3 K56Q do not phenocopy the Δrtt109 defect, and Δnaf-2, lacking the Vps75 homolog, does not either. So the active site is required, its canonical product is dispensable, and the Vps75-type route that would have produced H3K9ac or H3K27ac is excluded. The paper never identifies the responsible mark. A regular-expression audit of its complete text (120,120 characters after tag stripping, PMCID 13652317) returns zero occurrences of H3K9ac, H3K14ac, H3K18ac, H3K23ac or H3K27ac, and no acetylome or histone-PTM mass-spectrometry survey is reported.

The verdict is partially supported and unresolved on its central question. The premise that a non-H3K56 substrate exists is now experimentally established. The premise that specificity determinants can identify it fails. For curation this produces an unusual and clear recommendation: the evidence shape (a demonstrated catalytic requirement with an unidentified product) supports a biological process annotation by IMP and supports no new site-specific molecular function term at all.

Key findings

F001. The catalytic and cofactor machinery is intact; the chaperone interface is not

Global Needleman-Wunsch alignment of Q7S1R7 (630 aa) against S. cerevisiae Rtt109 Q07794 (436 aa), using BLOSUM62 with affine gaps (open -11, extend -1) implemented in numpy, gives 34.2% identity and 54.4% similarity over 371 aligned columns (raw score 22). I mapped all 27 UniProt-annotated yeast determinants onto 7 fungal orthologs (N. crassa, S. macrospora, P. oryzae, F. graminearum, A. fumigatus, C. albicans, S. pombe; 31.3-35.6% identity to ScRtt109). Every yeast determinant residue identity was verified against the Q07794 sequence by assertion before mapping, so the determinant list itself is not an assumption.

The catalytic set is fully conserved. Yeast D89, R194, Y199, W222, D287 and D288 map to N. crassa D145, R192, Y197, W226, D304 and D305, all identical. The autoacetylation lysine K290, which yeast Rtt109 requires for HAT activity (PMID: 21606491), maps to K307, identical. Across the 7-ortholog panel, D89, Y199, W222, D287, D288 and K290 are identical in 7 of 7; R194 in 6 of 7. The acetyl-CoA contacts A88, F192, H211 and W221 are conserved as A144, F190, H215 and W225. Yeast R97 and R101 have no aligned N. crassa residue in the global alignment, and are conserved in 0 of 7 orthologs, so they are a yeast-specific feature rather than a general requirement.

The Vps75 interface behaves in the opposite way. In N. crassa, 4 of 9 mapped interface positions are non-conservative substitutions: L148→Y173, L151→Q176, R355→P463 and F380→I494. Across the 7-ortholog panel the interface determinants L148, L151, R355, K356, F380 and N382 are identical in only 0-29% of cases, with the whole class spanning 0-43%.

The AlphaFold model AF-Q7S1R7-F1 (v6; v4 and earlier return 404, which I verified rather than assumed) has global pLDDT 69.1, but confidence is concentrated exactly where the analysis needs it. Catalytic and autoacetylation residues have mean pLDDT 93.9 (range 90.8-97.1), acetyl-CoA contacts mean 93.8, and Vps75-equivalent positions mean 83.7. The D305 CG to K307 NZ distance is 3.6 Å, reproducing the buried active-site Asp-Lys pair of yeast Rtt109 and consistent with intact autoacetylation geometry. The C-terminal positions that the alignment places opposite the yeast Asf1-binding tail have mean pLDDT 47.3, with residues 600-630 at mean 33.6, so that assignment is not reliable and I do not rely on it.

The interpretation is that N. crassa RTT109 is a catalytically competent H3 acetyltransferase, and that the residues which in yeast toggle between the Asf1-dependent H3K56 programme and the Vps75-dependent H3K9/H3K27 programme are precisely the ones that have diverged. Site selection in N. crassa cannot be read off the sequence.

F002. The substrate sequence carries zero discriminating information

N. crassa histone H3 (P07041, hh3/NCU01635, 136 aa) and S. cerevisiae H3 (P61830, 136 aa) are 94.9% identical with no indels. The 7 differences sit at positions 55, 81, 99, 111, 122, 126 and 136. The 13-residue windows centred on K4, K9, K14, K18, K23, K27 and K36 are character-for-character identical between the two species (K9: TKQTAR[K]STGGKA; K27: ASKAAR[K]SAPSTG). Only the K56 window differs, by the single conservative F54Y change (N. crassa EIRRYQ[K]STELLI against yeast EIRRFQ[K]STELLI).

This refutes the specific approach the seed hypothesis proposes, namely identifying "candidate non-H3K56 sites whose surrounding sequence context matches the retained determinants". There is no sequence context to compare. Any site-context argument that favoured H3K9 over H3K14 or H3K27 in N. crassa would favour it equally in S. cerevisiae, where the site preference is set by the chaperone and not by the tail.

The chaperone census is complete and unambiguous on copy number. A UniProt PROSITE PS51728 search of taxon 367110 returns Q7S1R7 only, so there is one Rtt109-family protein and no paralog to confound the assignment. There is one Asf1, Q7S1X9 (asf-1/NCU09436), at 54.1% identity to ScAsf1 P32447. There are two NAP-family proteins: NAF-1 (F5HGC3, NCU01438) is clearly the Nap1 ortholog (50.9% identity, NW score 948 to ScNap1 against score -20 to ScVps75), while NAF-2 (Q1K7A9, NCU03769) is the reciprocal best hit to Vps75 at weak similarity (32.8% identity, NW score 158, against 31.8% identity and score 86 to Nap1).

F003 and F004. The decisive genetics: catalysis required, H3K56ac dispensable, substrate unnamed

PMID: 42832562 is the paper that directly addresses the seed hypothesis. I retrieved its full text through NCBI efetch (PMCID 13652317, 120,120 characters after tag stripping) and mined it by regular expression.

Four results matter.

The catalytic requirement. Two catalytically inactive alleles, rtt109 D145A and rtt109 D145A-DD304-305AA, each 3xFLAG-tagged and integrated at the csr-1 locus in a Δrtt109 background, failed to rescue the H3K27me3 defect, whereas a wild-type rtt109-3xflag control did. H3K27me3 loss in the catalytic-dead strains was equal to or greater than in Δrtt109.

H3K56ac is not the relevant product. hH3 K56R (acetyl-ablative) and hH3 K56Q (acetyl-mimetic) point mutants did not phenocopy the Δrtt109 H3K27me3 defect, and global H3K27me3 was unaffected by western blot in both; only modest bidirectional shifts in the ChIP-seq enrichment profile were seen. Both H3K56 mutants were MMS-hypersensitive, an internal positive control confirming the alleles genuinely perturb H3K56 function in the DNA-damage pathway.

The Vps75 route is excluded. Δnaf-2 did not reproduce the H3K27me3 defect, even though co-IP LC-MS/MS ranked NAF-2 as the top RTT109 interactor among 11 recovered proteins, and AlphaFold3 gave ipTM 0.67 with 20 high-confidence contacts between RTT109 residues 420-480 and NAF-2 residues 290-330. The interaction is real and uncoupled from the phenotype.

ASF-1 was sought and not found. No RTT109-ASF-1 interaction was detected, which the authors attribute to the known difficulty of detecting the Asf1-Rtt109 contact in yeast without crosslinking.

The coverage audit is the finding that determines the curation outcome. Searching the same full text, the strings H3K9ac, H3K14ac, H3K18ac, H3K23ac and H3K27ac return zero matches, and no acetylome or histone-PTM mass-spectrometry survey is reported. The candidate non-H3K56 substrate is therefore defined entirely by exclusion.

The authors state their own caveat, quoted verbatim: catalytically inactive RTT109 protein levels were reduced relative to wild type, so "we cannot eliminate the possibility that H3K27me3 loss reflects reduced RTT109 levels rather than loss of catalytic activity". Their counterargument is that the catalytic-dead strains lose H3K27me3 at least as severely as the null, which is reasonable but indirect.

F005. The determinant mapping validates itself, then declines to answer

The mapping recovered three positions blind. PMID: 42832562 built its catalytic-dead alleles as rtt109 D145A and rtt109 D145A-DD304-305AA. Those are precisely the positions my alignment assigns to yeast D89, D287 and D288, computed without reference to the paper's numbering. The agreement establishes that the mapping is accurate rather than merely plausible, which matters because the negative conclusion about site discrimination rests on the same alignment.

The paper's own AlphaFold3 prediction places the RTT109-NAF-2 contact window at RTT109 residues 420-480. My yeast-derived interface mapping puts equivalents at 463, 464, 488, 492, 494 and 496, so the two overlap at 463 and 464 but diverge above 480. The partial agreement is worth recording: it suggests the NAF-2 interface is in roughly the right place but has shifted relative to the yeast arrangement, consistent with the divergence the conservation analysis measures.

Sequence and structure answer one half of the seed hypothesis with high confidence and refuse the other half. The donor-side determinants are shared by all candidate reactions. The chaperone interface is too diverged to read as a retained or lost preference, and the chaperone it binds is in any case dispensable for the phenotype. The H3 tail is invariant. The honest conclusion is that sequence and structure cannot discriminate among the candidates.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The causal chain, and where it breaks

The model has one dashed node and it is the answer the seed hypothesis wanted. Everything upstream of it is established: the enzyme binds cofactor, the active site is intact, autoacetylation geometry is present, and the ASF-1-route product H3K56ac is made and is required for DNA damage resistance (PMID: 38261383) and for quelling (PMID: 24554705). Everything downstream is established: H3K27me3 maintenance requires RTT109 catalysis and controls repression of the 573 marked genes defined in PMID: 42832562. The link in the middle is unknown, and two of the three obvious ways to draw it have been cut by genetics.

What remains plausible

Three possibilities survive, and nothing in the data ranks them.

A chaperone-independent histone acetylation by RTT109 at a tail lysine. This is the possibility closest to the seed hypothesis, but it requires activity that in yeast depends on a chaperone to occur without one.

An uncharacterised partner directing RTT109 to a histone site. The co-IP in PMID: 42832562 recovered 11 proteins, of which NAF-2 was the top hit and NCU03300 (the RAD24 homolog) scored ipTM 0.63. Nine others were not followed up.

A non-histone substrate. Nothing in the genetics requires the relevant acetylation to be on a histone, and no acetylome exists. The seed hypothesis does not contemplate this branch at all, which is its most consequential blind spot.

The governing point: PMID: 42832562 demonstrates a catalytic requirement without identifying the product. That evidence shape supports a BP term by IMP and supports no site-specific MF term. A curator who reads the abstract quickly may be tempted toward GO:0044017 because the readout is H3K27me3. That would be over-annotation by residue-number association, and the review should record the reason explicitly so the proposal does not recur.

Mechanistic scope

Direct gene-product activity. RTT109 is a fungal-specific histone acetyltransferase with a p300/CBP-like fold (PMID: 18568037). The mapping confirms an intact catalytic machine in N. crassa: the catalytic aspartates D145, D304 and D305, the catalytic R192, Y197 and W226, the acetyl-CoA contacts A144, F190, H215 and W225, and the autoacetylation lysine K307 with the D305 carboxylate 3.6 Å from the K307 amine in the AlphaFold model. Demonstrated chemistry in this organism is H3K56 acetylation (PMID: 24554705).

The immediate function being tested. A second direct acetylation event on chromatin, as yet unmeasured, required for PRC2-repressed domains.

Downstream phenotype, not direct activity. The H3K27me3 loss is a readout, not the reaction. PRC2 (SET-7) deposits H3K27me3; RTT109 does not, and the paper states explicitly that RTT109 is not required for PRC2 catalytic activity per se. The derepression of H3K27me3-marked genes, the MMS hypersensitivity, the disruption of the ASH1-deposited mark, and the quelling defect of PMID: 24554705 are all separated from the acetyltransferase reaction by at least one step.

The inference chain from loss of function. Every piece of N. crassa evidence tying RTT109 to PRC2 chromatin is loss-of-function. There is no gain-of-function experiment, no in vitro reconstitution with N. crassa proteins, and no demonstration that any RTT109-deposited mark is physically present in PRC2 domains. The BP annotation is well supported; any statement about the mechanism connecting enzyme to mark should be labelled inference.

Conflicts and alternatives

The yeast chaperone logic transfers poorly, and that is the heart of the problem. In S. cerevisiae, Asf1 directs Rtt109 to H3K56 and Vps75 directs it to H3K9 and H3K27 (PMID: 18458063, PMID: 21256037). In N. crassa the H3K56 product is dispensable and the Vps75 homolog NAF-2 is dispensable. Both characterised routes are excluded, and what remains is not covered by the yeast framework.

The yeast repertoire is narrower than usually quoted. Targeted mass spectrometry of Rtt109-Vps75 found that "only H3K9 and H3K23 are significantly acetylated under steady-state conditions" (PMID: 25781956). This weakens any transferred prediction further, since the list of sites a curator might import from yeast is shorter and more condition-dependent than the H3K9/H3K27 shorthand implies.

A non-histone substrate is a live alternative the seed hypothesis does not contemplate. The seed presumes the answer lies among H3 tail lysines. The genetics does not require this, and no acetylome has been run.

Protein-level confound. The authors report reduced accumulation of the catalytic-dead RTT109 proteins and explicitly decline to eliminate a protein-level contribution. This caveat should travel with any IMP annotation drawn from the catalytic-dead alleles.

Negative-interaction caveat. The absence of a detected RTT109-ASF-1 interaction is weak evidence, because the yeast interaction requires crosslinking to detect and crosslinking was not used. The Δasf-1 strain used was also a heterokaryon containing a mixture of wild-type and mutant nuclei, which limits any conclusion about ASF-1.

Orthology assignment is secure despite weak similarity. My reciprocal-best-hit call placed NAF-2 (Q1K7A9, NCU03769) as the Vps75 homolog at 32.8% identity, NW score 158. On its own that is weak. It is independently confirmed by the experimental co-IP in PMID: 42832562, which describes NAF-2 as "the Vps75 homolog", so the assignment holds.

Database carry-over. GO:0006338 chromatin remodeling on Q7S1R7 is IEA from InterPro and does not describe an acetyltransferase. It is the clearest candidate for removal or generalization.

No paralog confusion. The PROSITE PS51728 search of taxon 367110 returns Q7S1R7 only, and there is a single Asf1. Nothing in this gene's annotation is at risk from paralog overannotation.

Context from other systems. In Drosophila, CBP-mediated H3K27 acetylation directly antagonises H3K27 trimethylation, and "H3K27ac and H3K27me3 are mutually exclusive" (PMID: 19700617). That relationship runs opposite in sign to the N. crassa observation, where loss of an acetyltransferase reduces H3K27me3. If H3K27ac were the RTT109 product in question, the expected effect of losing RTT109 would be more H3K27me3, not less. This is an argument against GO:0044017 independent of the chaperone reasoning, and a curator should weigh it.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

1. The identity of the relevant acetylation mark. Checked: the complete text of PMID: 42832562, which names no acetylation mark other than H3K56ac. This blocks any site-specific MF annotation. Resolved by quantitative histone-PTM mass spectrometry comparing wild type, Δrtt109 and rtt109 D145A.

2. Whether the substrate is a histone at all. Checked: no acetylome survey exists for N. crassa RTT109. A non-histone substrate would make every candidate MF term in this discussion wrong. Resolved by acetyl-lysine affinity-enrichment proteomics, wild type against Δrtt109.

3. Whether the catalytic-dead phenotype is a true catalysis requirement. Checked: the authors' own stated caveat about reduced protein levels. Resolved by expressing the catalytic-dead allele at wild-type levels, or by a separation-of-function allele that retains stability.

4. Which chaperone, if any, directs the relevant reaction. Checked: NAF-2 is dispensable; ASF-1 was not detected under non-crosslinking conditions and the Δasf-1 strain was a heterokaryon. Resolved by crosslinked co-IP and a homokaryotic or conditional asf-1 allele.

5. Whether the Vps75-interface divergence is functionally meaningful. Checked: 4 of 9 mapped interface positions are non-conservative, yet NAF-2 binds RTT109 well (top co-IP hit, ipTM 0.67). The paper's predicted contact window (RTT109 420-480) overlaps my mapped positions 463 and 464 but not 488-496, so the two mappings agree only partly. Resolved by a structure of the RTT109-NAF-2 complex or by interface point mutants.

6. Whether RTT109-deposited acetylation is physically present in PRC2 domains. Checked: no ChIP for any acetylation mark in PRC2 domains is reported. This determines whether the BP role is direct or permissive. Resolved by ChIP-seq for candidate acetylation marks across H3K27me3 domains in wild type and Δrtt109.

7. Formation or maintenance. Checked: the paper's framing and title are maintenance; GO:0140718 says formation. Resolved by curator judgment on term scope, possibly by a new-term request.

Methods that could not be run

No experimental structure of N. crassa RTT109 exists, so every structural statement rests on an AlphaFold prediction whose global pLDDT is 69.1 and whose terminal regions fall below 50. The answer to the seed hypothesis, stated plainly

The seed hypothesis asked which substrate is the strongest candidate and how confidently, and asked for a plain statement if sequence and structure cannot discriminate.

Sequence and structure cannot discriminate among the candidates. The determinants that differ between the yeast H3K56 and H3K9/H3K27 reactions are chaperone-interface residues, and the N. crassa genetics has already excluded the chaperone whose interface those residues form. The catalytic and cofactor determinants are near-totally conserved, which says the enzyme works, not what it works on. The substrate side is worse still: the N. crassa and S. cerevisiae H3 tails are character-for-character identical at K4, K9, K14, K18, K23, K27 and K36, so there is no context to compare.

By orthology alone, H3K9 and H3K27 are the best-characterised alternative sites for a fungal Rtt109. I do not name either as the strongest candidate. The one mechanism that produces them in yeast is Vps75-dependent and NAF-2 is dispensable in N. crassa. Naming H3K27ac would be especially unsafe, for two independent reasons: it is attractive only because the phenotype is measured as H3K27me3, which is a different modification on the same residue deposited by SET-7, and in the system where CBP-dependent H3K27ac has been characterised the two marks are mutually exclusive (PMID: 19700617), so losing an H3K27 acetyltransferase would be expected to raise H3K27me3, not lower it.

The substrate that supports PRC2-repressed chromatin in N. crassa is an unidentified RTT109 catalytic product. It may not be a histone.
