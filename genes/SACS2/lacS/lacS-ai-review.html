Gene Description

lacS (SSO3019) encodes the thermostable beta-glycosidase of the hyperthermophilic crenarchaeon Saccharolobus (Sulfolobus) solfataricus, a glycoside hydrolase family 1 (GH1, clan GH-A) enzyme with a (beta/alpha)8-barrel fold that assembles into a homotetramer. It is a retaining exo-glycosidase that uses Glu387 as the catalytic nucleophile and Glu206 as the general acid/base, and it removes terminal non-reducing beta-linked sugars with broad specificity: beta-D-glucosides and beta-D-fucosides are the preferred substrates, beta-D-galactosides (including lactose and X-Gal) are also hydrolysed, and beta-linked glucose di- and oligosaccharides such as cellobiose and cello-oligosaccharides are cleaved from the non-reducing end. In the cell the enzyme is intracellular and is required, together with the adjacent putative major-facilitator transporter LacTr, for growth on lactose; its transcription is controlled by a catabolite-repression-like system shared with other glycoside hydrolases. The enzyme is extremely thermostable, and in vivo lysine methylation contributes to that stability. It is widely used in biotechnology for glycoside hydrolysis and transglycosylation, for example to make galacto-oligosaccharides, and as a genetic reporter and selectable marker in Sulfolobus.

GO:0008378 galactosyltransferase activity
IBA
GO_REF:0000033 		REMOVE
Summary: IBA from PAINT node PTN008498319 in PTHR10353 (GH1). The IBD at this node is seeded only by Arabidopsis SFR2 (AT3G06510), a chloroplast outer-envelope galactolipid:galactolipid galactosyltransferase. SFR2 has lost detectable hydrolase activity. The same node also carries a negated IRD for GO:0008422 beta-glucosidase activity (PTHR10353-paint.tsv). PANTHER HMM scoring puts lacS and other archaeal GH1 beta-galactosidases (P14288, P50388, O52629) in subfamily SF209, which is named after SFR2. In QuickGO, lacS is the only non-plant protein among the 58 GO:0008378 IBA rows that cite this node.
Reason: The galactosyltransferase activity placed at this node is a plant-specific neofunctionalisation. SFR2 evolved transferase activity by gaining a loop insertion, a C-terminal peptide and a hydrophobic patch, and it lost hydrolysis. The PAINT curator recorded that loss at the same node as a negated beta-glucosidase IRD. The node placement therefore claims a gain of transferase activity and a loss of hydrolase activity, and lacS contradicts both. It is experimentally a retaining hydrolase with strong beta-glucosidase, beta-fucosidase and beta-galactosidase activity, and it has no known role in galactolipid remodelling, which is a chloroplast-envelope process with no counterpart in archaeal ether-lipid membranes. Its close archaeal homologs bgaS and bglY in the same PANTHER subfamily receive beta-glucosidase, not galactosyltransferase, by TreeGrafter. That pattern suggests lacS reaches this node through subfamily assignment rather than by sharing the SFR2 innovation. In vitro, LacS (like all retaining GH1 enzymes) can transglycosylate and is used industrially to make galacto-oligosaccharides from lactose. That is a kinetic side reaction of the hydrolase mechanism under high substrate concentration, not the physiological galactosyl-transfer function the IBD asserts, so it does not rescue the annotation.

Core Functions

Broad-specificity retaining GH1 exo-beta-glycosidase. It hydrolyses terminal non-reducing beta-D-glucose, beta-D-fucose and beta-D-galactose residues from aryl glycosides and beta-linked di- and oligosaccharides at a single active site, using Glu387 as nucleophile and Glu206 as acid/base, and acts as a homotetramer.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should PAINT node PTN008498319 (PTHR10353), which carries the SFR2-seeded galactosyltransferase IBD and a negated beta-glucosidase IRD, be restricted so that archaeal GH1 beta-galactosidases (lacS P22498, bgaS P14288, bglY P50388, O52629), which PANTHER HMMs place in subfamily SF209, are excluded?

Q: Which physiological beta-glycoside substrates (lactose, cello-oligosaccharides or other glycosides) does LacS hydrolyse in vivo, given that its kinetic preference in vitro is for beta-glucosides and beta-fucosides?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Construct a clean lacS deletion in an otherwise wild-type background, leaving lacTr and xylS intact, and test growth on lactose, cellobiose and other beta-linked oligosaccharides, to define the in vivo substrate range of LacS separately from the adjacent transporter.

Identity and structure

  • GH1 (glycoside hydrolase family 1), clan GH-A, (beta/alpha)8 barrel; homotetramer
    PMID:9299327.
  • Cloned from strain MT-4; 489 aa; no homology to E. coli lacZ
    PMID:2121622.
  • Active site at centre of barrel top face; a tunnel likely binds oligosaccharides
    PMID:9299327.
  • Retaining mechanism: Glu387 nucleophile, Glu206 acid/base
    PMID:9860840; PMID:9010932.
  • In vivo lysine N-epsilon-methylation affects thermal stability and aggregation (PMID:14660666).

Activity / specificity (experimental)

  • Purified as a thermostable beta-galactosidase PMID:2105216.
  • Same protein is the beta-glucosidase, and beta-glucosides/beta-fucosides are the
    preferred substrates PMID:16348503.
  • Broad specificity, exo-acting at non-reducing end, hydrolyses beta-linked
    di/oligosaccharides including cello-oligosaccharides
    PMID:8484908;
    PMID:8484908;
    PMID:8484908.
  • Retaining GH1 enzymes also transglycosylate; LacS from P2 is used to make
    galacto-oligosaccharides from lactose in vitro (PMID:23411285). This is a
    biotechnological side reaction of the hydrolase mechanism, not a physiological
    transferase activity.
  • Engineering of specificity (M439C, E432C, W433C) (PMID:11749955).

Physiological role

  • S. solfataricus grows on lactose; a deletion mutant (GthetaW) lacking lacS (and the
    flanking lacTr/SSO3017 putative MFS transporter and xylS) cannot grow on lactose;
    complementation requires both lacS and lacTr PMID:12813089. LacS performs the hydrolytic step of lactose
    utilisation; lacTr is presumably needed for uptake.
  • lacS expression is under a catabolite-repression-like transcriptional control
    together with malA and the alpha-amylase PMID:10383958; see also PMID:10430566,
    PMID:15342498.
  • Intracellular enzyme (purified from crude cell extracts; no signal peptide in UniProt).
    Not used for a NEW CC annotation (no direct localisation experiment found).

  • GO:0005975 carbohydrate metabolic process (IEA InterPro): MODIFY to GO:0005990
    lactose catabolic process (OLS-verified). Participation test: LacS catalyses the
    glycosidic bond hydrolysis step itself. Comparator: PAINT node PTN002570652
    (mammalian lactase LCT, P09848) carries GO:0005990 IBD - the hydrolase is the
    normal bearer of this term.
  • GO:0008378 galactosyltransferase activity (IBA): OLS definition "Catalysis of the
    transfer of a galactosyl group to an acceptor molecule, typically another
    carbohydrate or a lipid." REMOVE.
  • PAINT (PTHR10353-paint.tsv): node PTN008498319 carries an IBD for GO:0008378
    seeded only by AT3G06510 (SFR2), plus a negated IRD for GO:0008422
    beta-glucosidase (i.e. PAINT asserts loss of beta-glucosidase at that node).
  • SFR2 is a plant chloroplast outer-envelope galactolipid:galactolipid
    galactosyltransferase that has lost hydrolase activity PMID:25100720; PMID:20798281.
  • PANTHER HMM scoring places lacS and the other crenarchaeal/Pyrococcus GH1
    beta-galactosidases (P14288 bgaS, P50388 bglY, O52629) in subfamily
    PTHR10353:SF209 "GALACTOLIPID GALACTOSYLTRANSFERASE SFR2, CHLOROPLASTIC"
    (PTHR10353-entries.csv). QuickGO: of 58 GO:0008378 IBA rows citing
    PTN008498319, all are land plants/algae except lacS. Sister archaeal homologs
    bgaS and bglY instead receive GO:0008422 by TreeGrafter (GO_REF:0000118,
    PTN000039748).
  • LacS is a demonstrated hydrolase (beta-glucosidase/galactosidase); target-specific
    evidence contradicts the SFR2-specific neofunctionalisation (loss of hydrolysis,
    gain of transferase). Archaea have no plant galactolipid substrate pool.
  • Note: a negated beta-glucosidase IRD at the same node would, if propagated,
    contradict direct experimental evidence for lacS (PMID:16348503, PMID:8484908).
    No such NOT row currently appears in GOA for P22498.

Archaea error-pattern candidate

sit in PTHR10353:SF209 (named after plant SFR2) and lacS inherits the plant SFR2
neofunctionalised galactolipid galactosyltransferase activity by IBA, while PAINT
simultaneously asserts loss of beta-glucosidase at that node.

    action: REMOVE
    reason: >-
      The galactosyltransferase activity placed at this node is a plant-specific
      neofunctionalisation. SFR2 evolved transferase activity by gaining a loop
      insertion, a C-terminal peptide and a hydrophobic patch, and it lost hydrolysis.
      The PAINT curator recorded that loss at the same node as a negated
      beta-glucosidase IRD. The node placement therefore claims a gain of transferase
      activity and a loss of hydrolase activity, and lacS contradicts both. It is
      experimentally a retaining hydrolase with strong beta-glucosidase,
      beta-fucosidase and beta-galactosidase activity, and it has no known role in
      galactolipid remodelling, which is a chloroplast-envelope process with no
      counterpart in archaeal ether-lipid membranes. Its close archaeal homologs bgaS
      and bglY in the same PANTHER subfamily receive beta-glucosidase, not
      galactosyltransferase, by TreeGrafter. That pattern suggests lacS reaches this
      node through subfamily assignment rather than by sharing the SFR2 innovation.
      In vitro, LacS (like all retaining GH1 enzymes) can transglycosylate and is used
      industrially to make galacto-oligosaccharides from lactose. That is a kinetic
      side reaction of the hydrolase mechanism under high substrate concentration, not
      the physiological galactosyl-transfer function the IBD asserts, so it does not
      rescue the annotation.
