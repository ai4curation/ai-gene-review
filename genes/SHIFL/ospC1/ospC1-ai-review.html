Gene Description

OspC1 is a 470-residue effector protein encoded on the large virulence plasmid of Shigella flexneri and delivered into the cytoplasm of infected epithelial cells through the type III secretion system. It is one of three paralogous OspC-family effectors (OspC1, OspC2, OspC3) and is built of two functional modules: an N-terminal catalytic region that binds NAD+ and carries the arginine ADP-riboxanase active site, and a C-terminal ankyrin-repeat region that selects the host substrate. The two modules support two biochemically distinct and genetically separable activities. As an arginine ADP-riboxanase (EC 4.3.99.-) it consumes NAD+ to convert an arginine side chain of a target protein into a cyclized ADP-riboxanated arginine, releasing nicotinamide and ammonia; in the host cell this modification is placed on the apoptotic caspases CASP3, CASP8 and CASP9 and blocks their activation, so OspC1 prevents caspase-8-dependent apoptosis of the infected cell. Unlike its paralog OspC3, whose ankyrin repeats recognize caspase-4/11, OspC1 does not modify the LPS-sensing caspases and so does not block pyroptosis. Independently of catalysis, the N-terminal region binds Ca2+-free calmodulin and holds it in that state, so calmodulin cannot load Ca2+ and cannot activate Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II; the consequence is loss of STAT1 phosphorylation and of interferon-stimulated gene expression downstream of type I, type II and type III interferons. OspC1 is also found in the nucleus of infected cells, where no activity has yet been assigned to it, and a deletion mutant shows reduced neutrophil transepithelial migration and reduced ERK1/2 phosphorylation by an unknown mechanism. Together the two activities keep the infected epithelial cell alive and interferon-blind, and loss of OspC1 together with OspC3 attenuates Shigella growth in epithelial cells and in the mouse gut.

Core Functions

Arginine ADP-riboxanase. Using NAD+ as donor, OspC1 converts an arginine side chain of a host target protein into a cyclized ADP-riboxanated arginine, releasing nicotinamide and ammonia. Its substrates in the infected cell are the apoptotic caspases CASP3, CASP8 and CASP9; the modification blocks their activation, so caspase-8-dependent apoptosis of the infected epithelial cell is prevented and the bacterium keeps its replicative niche. The chemistry is shared with the paralogs, while substrate choice rests on the C-terminal ankyrin repeats, which in OspC1 do not recognize the LPS-sensing caspases 4 and 11.

Solve the structure of the apo-calmodulin complex with the OspC1 N-terminal domain, which AlphaFold models as the two calmodulin lobes wrapped around a single N-terminal helix, and swap that helix between OspC1 and OspC2 to test whether it alone converts the non-inhibitory paralog into an inhibitor.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: The host protein whose ADP-riboxanation accounts for OspC1's anti-apoptotic effect in infected cells has not been identified. UniProt's curated function names CASP3, CASP8 and CASP9 as substrates, but the infection study that established the phenotype could not say whether OspC1 acts on caspase-8 directly or on an upstream component, and the calmodulin study describes the target as unknown. Since the ankyrin-repeat domain is what selects substrates in this family and the OspC1 domain does not recognize caspase-4/11, which protein it does recognize in the apoptotic branch is an open biochemical question.

BIOLOGY MF_DARK

What is known: Firmly established: OspC1 is an arginine ADP-riboxanase with a defined reaction and two active-site aspartates whose substitution abolishes activity; a ΔospC1 mutant fails to block caspase-8 activation and apoptosis and the defect is complemented by ospC1; OspC1 does not modify caspase-4 or caspase-11.

Significance: Substrate identity decides whether the apoptosis block is a direct caspase modification, as for OspC3 and caspase-4, or an indirect effect through an upstream regulator, and it determines what the correct molecular-level GO representation of the anti-apoptotic arm should be.

Gap: What OspC1 does in the host nucleus is unknown. The protein is reproducibly seen in the nuclei of infected cells, yet both characterized activities, calmodulin sequestration and caspase ADP-riboxanation, operate on cytosolic substrates, and no nuclear substrate, partner or import signal has been described. Related to this, the oldest reported ΔospC1 phenotypes, reduced neutrophil transepithelial migration and reduced ERK1/2 phosphorylation in infected polarized intestinal cells, have never been connected to either known activity.

BIOLOGY BP_DARK

What is known: Firmly established: OspC1-2HA delivered by infecting bacteria is detectable in both the cytoplasm and the nucleus of HeLa cells, and a ΔospC1 mutant is defective in PMN transepithelial migration and in ERK1/2 phosphorylation while invading and forming plaques normally.

Significance: A nuclear activity would make OspC1 a trifunctional effector and could explain the migration and MAPK phenotypes that neither the cell-death nor the interferon mechanism accounts for.
