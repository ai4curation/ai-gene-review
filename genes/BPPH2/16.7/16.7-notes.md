# Gene 16.7 (Early membrane-anchored DNA-binding replication protein gp16.7 / p16.7, P16517) — Curation Notes

## Summary of Function
Gene `16.7` of *Bacillus phage phi29* (`NCBITaxon:2884424`) encodes a 130-amino-acid early single-pass transmembrane DNA-binding protein (gp16.7, p16.7; UniProt `P16517`).
- **Subcellular localization**: Contains an N-terminal transmembrane helix (residues 4–22) that anchors p16.7 in the *Bacillus subtilis* host cell plasma membrane (`GO:0020002`) (`PMID:11169113`, `file:BPPH2/16.7/16.7-uniprot.txt`).
- **Molecular function & biological role**: Its C-terminal cytoplasmic domain (residues 23–130) forms dimers and higher-order multimers that bind single-stranded and double-stranded DNA (`GO:0003677`), as well as the viral terminal protein gp3 (`file:BPPH2/16.7/16.7-uniprot.txt`). During infection, p16.7 attaches displaced ssDNA stretches of viral replication intermediates to the host cell plasma membrane, spatially organizing and enhancing the efficiency of protein-primed viral DNA genome replication (`GO:0039693`), particularly at early infection times (`PMID:11169113`, `file:BPPH2/16.7/16.7-uniprot.txt`).

## Domain Architecture & Structural Features
- **InterPro / Pfam**: `InterPro:IPR009595` (Bacteriophage phi-29 DNA replication protein Gp16.7), `InterPro:IPR037211` (Phage DNA replication protein Gp16.7 superfamily), `Pfam:PF06720` (`Phi-29_GP16_7`).
- **Domains**:
  - **N-terminal transmembrane anchor (residues 4–22)**: Essential for plasma membrane localization and in vivo function (`PMID:11169113`).
  - **Central coiled-coil linker (residues 23–60)**: Mediates dimerization.
  - **C-terminal DNA-binding domain (residues 61–130)**: Dimeric helical domain (solved by NMR/X-ray in subsequent studies; PDB `1ZAE`, `2C5R`) that multimerizes upon binding ssDNA and dsDNA.

## Literature & Annotation Review
- `PMID:11169113`: Demonstrated that p16.7 is an early-expressed membrane protein whose N-terminal transmembrane domain is required for membrane localization, that p16.7 forms dimers and oligomers, and that absence of p16.7 impairs in vivo phage DNA replication and redistributes viral DNA replication intermediates away from the cell membrane.
