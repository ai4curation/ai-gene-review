# Gene 3 (Terminal protein gp3 / p3, P03681) — Curation Notes

## Summary of Function
Gene `3` of *Bacillus phage phi29* (`NCBITaxon:2884424`) encodes the 266-amino-acid terminal protein (gp3, p3; UniProt `P03681`). It plays two essential roles in the phi29 infective cycle:
1. **Protein primer for viral DNA replication (`GO:0039685`)**: Free gp3 forms a heterodimer with the viral DNA polymerase gp2 (`P03680`) and is recruited to the replication origins at both ends of the linear dsDNA genome (which already carry parental gp3 covalently linked to their 5' ends) as well as to the host bacterial nucleoid via its N-terminal DNA-binding domain (`PMID:6813861`, `PMID:6402761`, `file:BPPH2/3/3-uniprot.txt`). DNA polymerase gp2 catalyzes the covalent phosphoester linkage of 5'-dAMP to the hydroxyl group of Ser232 of gp3 (`PMID:6813861`), which then serves as the primer for strand-displacement DNA synthesis.
2. **Viral DNA genome packaging (`GO:0019073`)**: Parental gp3 remains covalently attached to both 5' termini of unit-length progeny viral dsDNA (forming the `DNA-gp3` complex) inside the mature virion (`GO:0019013`) and is essential for specific recognition and ATP-driven translocation of viral DNA into the prohead by the packaging ATPase gp16 (`P03684`) and pRNA-connector motor (`PMID:18674782`).

## Domain Architecture & Structural Features
- **InterPro / Pfam**: `InterPro:IPR008770` (DNA terminal protein Gp3), `InterPro:IPR043124` (DNA terminal protein Gp3, C-terminal), `InterPro:IPR037216` (DNA terminal protein Gp3 superfamily), `Pfam:PF05435` (`Phi-29_GP3`), `PIRSF:PIRSF004179`.
- **Three-domain structure**:
  - **N-terminal domain (residues 1–73)**: Non-sequence-specific DNA-binding domain (`GO:0003677`) that localizes gp3 and the associated DNA polymerase to the *Bacillus subtilis* bacterial nucleoid (`GO:0009295`).
  - **Intermediate domain (residues 74–172)**: Mediates extensive structural interaction with the TPR1 subdomain of phi29 DNA polymerase gp2.
  - **C-terminal priming domain (residues 173–266)**: Mimics duplex DNA in the polymerase active site cleft and presents Ser232 (`MOD_RES 232 O-(5'-phospho-DNA)-L-serine`) for covalent deoxynucleotidylation.

## Critical Evaluation of Existing GO Annotations
- **Replication terms (`GO:0006260`, `GO:0006269`, `GO:0006270`, `GO:0039693`)**: All are refined via `MODIFY` to `GO:0039685` (`protein-primed viral DNA replication`), which specifically describes the terminal protein-primed replication mechanism discovered in phi29 (`PMID:6813861`, `PMID:6402761`).
- **DNA binding (`GO:0003677`)** and **viral DNA genome packaging (`GO:0019073`, `PMID:18674782`)**: Both are `ACCEPT`ed as core functions.
- **Mis-annotated `GO:0043493` (`viral terminase complex`, `PMID:18674782`)**: Marked `REMOVE`. Unlike concatemeric dsDNA phages, phi29 replicates unit-length linear genomes with gp3 covalently linked to each 5' end and does not form a terminase complex that cleaves concatemeric DNA. Although UniProt notes that gp3 "Acts as the small terminase protein during packaging" by analogy to genome recognition, `PMID:18674782` studies the prohead/pRNA/gp16/DNA-gp3 packaging motor assembly intermediate and shows gp3 is the covalently attached terminal protein on the viral DNA substrate rather than a subunit of a terminase complex.
- **Mis-annotated `GO:0042025` (`host cell nucleus`, `PMID:23091024` and `GO_REF:0000044`)**: Marked `REMOVE`. *Bacillus phage phi29* infects the Gram-positive bacterium *Bacillus subtilis*, which is a prokaryote lacking a nucleus. In `PMID:23091024`, YFP-fused gp3 was heterologously expressed in mammalian COS-7 cells to test whether eukaryotic nuclear localization signals occur in phage terminal proteins; in its natural bacterial host, gp3 associates with the bacterial nucleoid (`GO:0009295`).
- **Mis-annotated `GO:0016787` (`hydrolase activity`), `GO:0046718`, `GO:0098932`, `GO:0098994` (`GO_REF:0000043`)**: Marked `REMOVE`. These IEA annotations stem from erroneous UniProt keyword assignments (`KW-0378 Hydrolase`, `KW-1236 Viral penetration via lysis of host cell wall peptidoglycan`) linked to the 3D virion reconstruction paper `PMID:14763988`. In phi29, peptidoglycan hydrolase / cell-wall-degrading tail knob activity resides in tail protein gp13 (`P04332`), not in the 5'-covalently linked terminal protein gp3 (`P03681`).
