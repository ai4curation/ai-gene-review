CC   -!- FUNCTION: ATPase required for the genome encapsidation reaction
CC       (PubMed:3879485). Part of the active packaging motor via the binding to
CC       the packaging RNA (pRNA), itself fixed to the head-tail connector at
CC       the unique portal vertex of the prohead (PubMed:11130079,
CC       PubMed:18674782). Binds and supercoils the pre-formed, unit-length DNA
CC       bound to gp3 to produce an initiation complex for DNA packaging
CC       (PubMed:2960820). Provides the energy to actively pump the viral DNA
CC       into the prohead. Approximately one molecule of ATP is used in the
CC       packaging of 2 bp of viral DNA (PubMed:2960820). ATP hydrolysis results
CC       in a conformational change that causes the arginine/lysine finger of
CC       one subunit to move into the active site of its neighbor, where it
CC       interacts with the negatively charged oxygens on the gamma-phosphate of
CC       ATP (Probable). After packaging, the ATPase and the pRNA are released
CC       from the prohead. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11130079,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:18674782, ECO:0000269|PubMed:2960820,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:3879485, ECO:0000305|PubMed:26904950}.

CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9086269}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The N-terminus contains the ATPase activity and the C-terminus
CC       (CTD) binds DNA (PubMed:33089330). The CTD is likely a vestigial
CC       nuclease that has lost its hydrolytic capability since Phi-29 is not a
CC       concatamer that neads cleavage (PubMed:33089330).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:33089330}.
