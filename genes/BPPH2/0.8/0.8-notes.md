# Gene 0.8 (Uracil-DNA glycosylase inhibitor protein p56, Q38503) — Curation Notes

## Summary of Function
Gene `0.8` of *Bacillus phage phi29* (`NCBITaxon:2884424`) encodes the 56-amino-acid early acidic protein p56 (UniProt `Q38503`, `P56_BPPH2`).
- **Molecular function (`GO:0004857` enzyme inhibitor activity)**: Protein p56 forms a homodimer that acts as a structural DNA mimic and directly binds the host *Bacillus subtilis* uracil-DNA glycosylase (UDG; `UniProtKB:P39615`), blocking the DNA-binding cleft of UDG and competitively inhibiting its glycosylase activity (`PMID:17698500`, `file:BPPH2/0.8/0.8-uniprot.txt`).
- **Biological role (`GO:0051701` biological process involved in interaction with host / defense against host BER)**: During infection, if uracil is misincorporated into viral DNA (or arises by cytosine deamination), excision of uracil by host UDG and subsequent cleavage by host AP endonucleases during base excision repair (BER) creates nicked or gapped templates. Because phi29 DNA polymerase gp2 performs processive strand-displacement synthesis across the entire linear genome, nicks in the parental template cause double-strand breaks and premature truncation of viral replication intermediates. By inhibiting host UDG, p56 prevents host BER-induced degradation of replicating phi29 DNA (`PMID:17698500`, `file:BPPH2/0.8/0.8-uniprot.txt`).
