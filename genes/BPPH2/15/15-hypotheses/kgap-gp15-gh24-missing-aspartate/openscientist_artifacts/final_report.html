gp15 of Bacillus phage phi29 (P11187): the missing GH24 aspartate

Hypothesis slug: kgap-gp15-gh24-missing-aspartate Source: genes/BPPH2/15/15-ai-review.yaml, focus type free_text Target: UniProtKB P11187, Bacillus phage phi29 gene 15, NCBITaxon:2884424 Iterations completed: 3. Findings recorded: 7. Papers reviewed: 12.

Summary

The seed hypothesis has three clauses, and they separate cleanly. gp15 does retain the GH24 catalytic glutamate: Glu15 is the T4 lysozyme Glu11 equivalent, placed there by every one of 12 gap-penalty settings tested and confirmed independently against a crystal structure 47.9% identical to gp15. gp15 has lost the T4 lysozyme Asp20 equivalent, and that loss is real, fixed and older than the hypothesis assumes. The third clause fails: no acidic residue, and no newly introduced polar residue, occupies the Asp20 volume in gp15. The slot holds a proline, inside a two-residue insertion in the catalytic loop, and the same substitution is present in all 45 phi29-like and c2-like endolysins scored (0/45 acidic, proline in 41/45, against 9/23 in a reviewed GH24 outgroup, Fisher exact p = 1.66e-05).

The premise built into the hypothesis also needs correcting. The Asp20 equivalent is absent from 14 of 23 reviewed GH24 entries outside this clade, including Salmonella phage P22 endolysin (P09963), Lactococcus phage phivML3 endolysin (P62693), Pseudomonas phage phiKMV (Q7Y2C0), E. coli phage T1 (Q6XQ98) and Haemophilus phage HP1 (P51728). What is conserved family-wide is the Glu11 equivalent (22/23 outgroup, 37/37 clade members long enough to cover the column) together with the Thr26 equivalent (21/23 outgroup, 45/45 clade). Calling the aspartate "the conserved GH24 catalytic aspartate" and treating its absence as a gp15 defect overstates the case in both directions.

The consequence for curation is not that gp15 is inactive. T4 lysozyme D20C retains near wild-type specific activity (PMID: 1892846), Asp20 is not essential (PMID: 10430876), and purified gp15 degrades B. subtilis cell walls (PMID: 3469652). The consequence is that the bond chemistry is open. A single-carboxylate GH24 site with a conserved alpha-face threonine is the configuration of phage lambda endolysin R, which is experimentally a lytic transglycosylase (EC 4.2.2.n2) and not a hydrolase. The one direct gp15 experiment measured turbidity loss of cell walls and never identified the cleaved product. GO:0003796 (lysozyme activity, EC 3.2.1.17) therefore asserts a hydrolysis reaction that no gp15 experiment has measured.

The most important caveat is mechanistic rather than positional. The missing carboxylate does not predict loss of activity; it predicts ambiguity between hydrolysis and lytic transglycosylation, and that ambiguity propagates directly into the molecular function term on the entry.

Two analyses prescribed in the seed could not be run, and are reported as failures rather than substituted silently. AlphaFold DB has no model for P11187, nor for any of the six other GH24 viral proteins checked (P07540, Q37896, P62692, P62693, P00720, P03706, P09963); both the files endpoint and the api/prediction endpoint return HTTP 404, because viral proteomes are excluded from AFDB. The ESMFold public API returned HTTP 504. The prescribed "superpose the gp15 AlphaFold model onto T4 lysozyme bound to peptidoglycan fragments" step was therefore replaced by two measurements that could be made directly: distances in the substrate-bound T4 lysozyme complex PDB 148L (with wild-type side chains modelled from 2LZM), and alignment of gp15 to PDB 9KBQ, a 1.52 Å GH24 structure at 47.9% identity over 142 residues. The EBI BLAST REST service returned HTTP 400 ("temporarily blocked from submitting new sequence analysis jobs"), so the homolog panel was assembled from taxonomy-restricted UniProt PF00959 queries instead.

Key findings

F1. Glu15 is the GH24 general acid of gp15, with an unambiguous alignment register

Gotoh affine global alignment of P11187 against T4 lysozyme (P00720) with BLOSUM62 places gp15 Glu15 at the T4L Glu11 column in all 12 gap-penalty settings tested (gap open -8 to -16, extension -1 or -2; provenance/gap_sensitivity_gp15.csv). The register is not a matter of alignment taste: the motif T-I-G-x-G-H, which is T4L Thr26 to His31 and gp15 Thr32 to His37, is invariant and fixes every position from gp15 residue 26 onward with no freedom. Independently, alignment of gp15 against the observed chain of PDB 9KBQ (PHAb10, an Acinetobacter baumannii prophage lysozyme solved at 1.52 Å, 47.9% identity to gp15 over 142 aligned residues) places gp15 Glu15 at 9KBQ Glu37. After superposition of 9KBQ onto the substrate-bound T4 lysozyme complex 148L, that Glu37 carboxylate sits 3.09 Å from the scissile MurNAc C1. For comparison, the wild-type T4L Glu11 carboxylate, modelled into 148L from 2LZM (162 CA atoms, RMSD 0.57 Å), is 3.86 Å from the same carbon.

In 148L itself, the engineered T26E residue forms the covalent intermediate (LINK record, Glu26 OE2 to MurNAc C1, 1.51 Å), with Asp20 OD2 at 4.09 Å and Glu11 OE1 at 4.20 Å. These numbers define the geometric volume that any candidate replacement residue would have to reach.

F2. The Asp20 slot in gp15 holds proline, and nothing acidic lies anywhere near it

The T4 lysozyme Asp20 column maps to gp15 Pro26 in all 12 gap-penalty settings, and independently to Pro26 when gp15 is aligned to 9KBQ (template residue Asp46). In 9KBQ only three acidic side chains lie within 10 Å of the catalytic Glu37 carboxylate: Glu37 itself (0.60 Å), Asp84 (8.33 Å) and Asp46 (8.61 Å, the GH24 aspartate). The gp15 equivalents are Glu15, Asp60 and Pro26. Reading the full 26-residue shell within 10 Å of the catalytic glutamate, gp15 introduces no acidic residue at any position that is non-acidic in the template.

The other candidate carboxylates fail on distance. In the substrate-bound T4L frame, the Glu22 equivalent (gp15 Glu28) sits 8.0 to 10.0 Å from the scissile MurNAc C1 and 6.3 Å from the Asp20 carboxylate. The Lys35 and Asp70 equivalents (gp15 Asp41 and Asp60) sit 9.6 to 9.9 Å from C1. gp15 Asp41 is a charge reversal relative to T4L Lys35 and is the only new carboxylate gained near the cleft, which makes it the obvious candidate, but it is not conserved: B103 has a gap at that position, and Lactococcus phages c2 and vML3 have Ala42.

The nearest conserved polar group that does sit on the alpha face is Thr32, the T4L Thr26 equivalent, whose wild-type OG1 is 5.15 Å from MurNAc C1. That residue is ancestral, not a replacement.

F3. The loss is clade-wide and predates the phi29 and c2 split

Semi-global affine alignment (Gotoh, BLOSUM62, gap -11/-1, free terminal gaps) of 45 PF00959 endolysins from Salasmaviridae, Ceduovirus and Picovirinae to gp15, spanning 30.0% to 100% identity, gives the following occupancy at the gp15 position 26 column (T4L Asp20):

The Bacillus phi29-like phages and the Lactococcus c2-like phages share only about 30% to 37% identity with gp15 and infect different hosts, so a substitution fixed in both groups is an ancestral state of the clade, not a phi29-specific degeneration and not a sequencing artefact. The proline that occupies the slot in 41 of 45 members is the opposite of a carboxylate substitute: it removes a backbone amide and rigidifies the catalytic loop.

F4. A single-carboxylate GH24 site is catalytically complete, and lambda R shows what it does

Phage lambda endolysin R (P03706) is a GH24 enzyme with a single annotated active site, Glu19, and it is annotated EC 4.2.2.n2 with GO:0008933 (IDA) and GO:0008932 (EXP): it cleaves the MurNAc-GlcNAc bond with formation of a 1,6-anhydro bond. Measured on its crystal structure (PDB 1AM7, chain A), no acidic residue lies within 8 Å of the Glu19 carboxylate; the closest side chains are Ser126 (3.53 Å), Ala125 (4.11 Å), Gln68 (4.72 Å), Tyr67 (4.97 Å) and Ser18 (5.14 Å). That is the same active-site composition as gp15.

Mutagenesis on T4 lysozyme makes the same point from the other direction. D20C retains nearly wild-type specific activity with an unchanged pH-activity profile (PMID: 1892846), and in the T26H background Glu11 is required as general acid while Asp20 is not essential (PMID: 10430876). That second paper also shows that reengineering the T4 lysozyme active site converts the enzyme into a transglycosidase, so the choice between hydrolysis and transglycosylation inside this fold turns on a small number of residues.

F5. The current GO record asserts a chemistry that was never measured

QuickGO, queried live, returns 12 annotations on UniProtKB:P11187. Two are experimental, both from CACAO: GO:0003796 (lysozyme activity) with ECO:0000315 IMP citing PMID: 3469652, and GO:0044659 (viral release from host cell by cytolysis) with ECO:0007161 IMP citing 820555. The other ten are IEA pipeline annotations (GO:0003824, GO:0016787, GO:0016798, GO:0009253, GO:0016998 from InterPro, GO:0031640, GO:0042742, GO:0030430, plus duplicate IEA versions of GO:0003796 and GO:0044659).

Two separable problems sit in that record. The evidence code on GO:0003796 is wrong in kind: 3469652 is Saedi, Garvey and Ito 1987 PNAS, Cloning and purification of a unique lysozyme produced by Bacillus phage phi 29, a purified-protein assay, which is IDA (ECO:0000314), not IMP. The term itself is more specific than the assay supports, because EC 3.2.1.17 names a hydrolysis reaction while the assay measured cell-wall turbidity loss. QuickGO confirms that GO:0061783 (peptidoglycan muralytic activity) is an is_a ancestor of both GO:0003796 and GO:0008933 (7 ancestors each), which makes it the exact term for what was measured.

Mechanistic model

The active site of gp15 contains one carboxylate and one threonine. That is the lambda R configuration, and it leaves two chemistries open that the existing data cannot separate.

Everything downstream of the dashed links is identical in the two branches: both produce cell-wall cleavage, both produce lysis, and both give the same turbidity curve. The 1987 assay sits at the node marked "measured". Assigning GO:0003796 picks one branch above that node without evidence.

gp15 is modular. The GH24 catalytic domain occupies roughly residues 1 to 150, followed by two LysM peptidoglycan-binding domains (PF01476) at roughly 165-210 and 215-258. The LysM modules are substrate-targeting and not catalytic, so they do not affect the chemistry question.

Mechanistic scope

The activity under test is direct and molecular: cleavage of the glycan backbone of bacterial peptidoglycan by the N-terminal GH24 domain of gp15.

Direct gene-product activity: degradation of B. subtilis cell wall by purified gp15 (PMID: 3469652). The catalytic apparatus is Glu15 plus Thr32, with no second carboxylate.

Downstream and distinct from the molecular function: host cell lysis and phage release, captured by GO:0044659 and GO:0009253 and evidenced by phage-mutant characterisation (820555). Phenotype-level evidence of this kind cannot settle the active-site question, because both candidate chemistries give the same lysis phenotype.

Separate protein, separate annotation: phi29 also carries gp13, a tail-associated cell-wall-degrading enzyme with a lysozyme-like N-terminal domain and a metallo-endopeptidase C-terminal domain, solved at 1.8 Å and located at the distal end of the tail knob (PMID: 18606992). gp13 acts during entry; gp15 is the endolysin acting at lysis. Evidence about one must not be transferred to the other.

Conflicts and alternatives

Lambda R is the closer analogy than T4 lysozyme. Lambda endolysin R is GH24, has a single catalytic Glu19, has no acidic residue within 8 Å of that carboxylate in PDB 1AM7 (measured here), and is experimentally a lytic transglycosylase. gp15 matches this composition. Any claim that gp15 is a hydrolase inherits T4's two-carboxylate site by family label without a residue match.

The missing Asp is not a defect, and the slot is not empty. D20C retains near wild-type activity (PMID: 1892846) and Asp20 is not essential (PMID: 10430876). In gp15 the slot holds proline in 41 of 45 clade members, which is a positive structural feature.

gp15 Asp41 is a false candidate on three counts: its T4L-frame equivalent is about 9.9 Å from the scissile C1; it is absent from B103 (gap) and from c2 and vML3 (Ala42); and it occurs in only 10 of 45 clade members, all Bacillus.

Database carry-over risk. The "muramidase" label on the c2-like lysins, the closest characterised gp15 relatives, is motif-based (PMID: 15043867). Using it to confirm hydrolase chemistry for gp15 is circular, because the motif in question is the GH24 signature being examined. The same applies to older homology-based lysozyme calls, such as the Haemophilus somnus orf7 product described as having extensive homology with the lysozymes of phages PZA, phi29, phi-vML3, T4 and others (PMID: 9068631), which was assayed for lysozyme activity in cell lysates without product analysis.

Inconsistent curation inside UniProt. 21 of 45 clade members carry the same non-acidic substitution without the caution that phi29, PZA, B103, Nf, c2 and bIL67 carry. Any action on P11187 should be propagated.

Alignment ambiguity, stated plainly. The clade carries a 2-residue insertion in the catalytic loop (gp15 Ala24, Val25), so the exact residue occupying the Asp20 position could be Ala24, Val25 or Pro26 depending on the alignment. Twelve gap-penalty settings all returned Glu15, Pro26 and Thr32, and none of the three candidates is acidic, so the conclusion is insensitive to this ambiguity.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

1. The reaction product of gp15 has never been identified. Checked: 3469652 and PubMed searches for phi29 lysin product chemistry. This is the single fact separating GO:0003796 from GO:0008933. Resolved by mass spectrometry of gp15 digests of B. subtilis sacculi, looking for the 1,6-anhydro-MurNAc signature.

2. No experimental or predicted structure of gp15 exists. Checked: AlphaFold DB (404 for P11187 and six other GH24 viral entries), ESMFold API (504), RCSB search. The Asp20 volume in gp15 is defined here only by transfer from 9KBQ at 47.9% identity. Resolved by a crystal structure, or by a local AlphaFold2 or ESMFold run, which the executor could not reach.

3. No mutagenesis has been done on gp15 itself. Glu15 is inferred as the general acid entirely from homology.

4. Whether Thr32 supports the alpha-side water network. Thr is 45/45 in the clade and 21/23 in the outgroup, and T4L Thr26 OG1 is 5.15 Å from the scissile C1. The T26H substitution converts T4 lysozyme to a transglycosidase (PMID: 10430876), so this position is mechanistically loaded.

5. Whether the proline at the Asp20 position is functional or merely tolerated. It is present in 41 of 45 clade members, which is strong conservation, but no experiment addresses it.

6. The provenance of the CACAO IMP annotations. Both experimental annotations on P11187 are assignedBy CACAO, and the GO:0003796 code is IMP where the cited paper reports a purified-enzyme assay.

Methodological limits: every structural statement about gp15 is transferred from 9KBQ or from T4 lysozyme by alignment. The 45-member homolog panel was assembled by taxonomy-restricted Pfam queries rather than by BLAST, because the EBI BLAST REST service blocked submissions, so it reflects UniProt's current taxonomy assignments. The alignment is pairwise to gp15 rather than a full multiple-sequence alignment, which is adequate because the active-site register is anchored on an invariant motif but would be less reliable for distant positions. No wet-lab evidence was generated.

Discriminating tests and proposed follow-up

Ranked by how much each would move the curation decision.

  1. Product identification by LC-MS on gp15 digests. Digest B. subtilis peptidoglycan with purified gp15 and look for 1,6-anhydro muropeptides against reducing-end muropeptides, with lambda R and T4 lysozyme as controls for the two outcomes. This is the method that established lytic transglycosylase activity for the Neisseria AtlA endolysin homolog, where purified enzyme "was found to degrade macromolecular peptidoglycan and to produce 1,6-anhydro peptidoglycan monomers, characteristic of lytic transglycosylase activity" (PMID: 17526702). One experiment decides GO:0003796 against GO:0008933.
  2. Reducing-sugar assay in parallel with the turbidity assay. A transglycosylase lyses the wall without releasing new reducing ends. Cheaper than MS and nearly as decisive.
  3. E15A and E15Q point mutants with the 3469652 lysis assay, confirming the general acid and validating the alignment-based active-site call.
  4. P26D, A24D and V25D variants to test whether a carboxylate can be reintroduced into the Asp20 volume, and what that does to pH optimum and product.
  5. A structure of the gp15 GH24 domain, ideally with a bound chitooligosaccharide or muropeptide, which would replace every transfer-by-alignment step in this report with measurement.
  6. Comparative product assay on c2 endolysin (P62692) and phivML3 endolysin (P62693). Both lack the Asp. If the Asp-less clade is uniformly transglycosylase, the result propagates to all 45 members.
