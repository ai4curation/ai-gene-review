Gene Description

Wnt8 is a secreted, lipid-modified Wnt-family ligand (358 aa, single Wnt domain with the family-conserved cysteine pattern) of the purple sea urchin embryo. It is the earliest zygotic intercellular signal of the vegetal pole: transcription begins at the 16-cell stage in the four micromeres, under control of the maternally nuclearized beta-catenin/Tcf system, and then spreads at the 60-cell stage to the adjacent veg2 tier of macromere descendants. Reception of Wnt8 by neighbouring cells drives beta-catenin into their nuclei, and because the wnt8 cis-regulatory system itself depends on a beta-catenin/Tcf input (together, in one module, with the zinc-finger factor Blimp1), each Wnt8-producing cell recruits its neighbours to produce Wnt8 in turn. This intercellular positive feedback, coupled to blimp1 autorepression that later extinguishes expression at the centre, generates an expanding torus of wnt8 and blimp1 expression that sweeps outward from the skeletogenic micromere lineage through the non-skeletogenic mesoderm and into the veg2 endoderm, carrying with it the beta-catenin/Tcf input on which most endomesodermal regulatory genes depend, and setting where the complementary Delta/Notch signal is deployed. Blocking Wnt8 translation prevents specification of both the mesoderm (primary mesenchyme, pigment and muscle cells) and the endoderm and abolishes gastrulation, while overexpressed Wnt8 induces ectopic endoderm and multiple invagination sites. Later, from the blastula stage, wnt8 expression moves into veg1 and posterior ectoderm, where Wnt8 together with Wnt1, acting through the Frizzled5/8 receptor and JNK rather than beta-catenin, restricts the anterior neuroectoderm to the anterior pole.

Core Functions

Secreted Wnt ligand of the micromere and veg2 lineages that activates canonical Wnt/beta-catenin signaling in adjacent vegetal cells, driving the self-propagating Blimp1/Wnt8 feedback torus that delivers the beta-catenin/Tcf input for endoderm and mesoderm specification across the vegetal plate.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should frizzled binding remain an IBA-only statement, or is there (or should there be) direct evidence that Sp-Wnt8 binds a specific sea urchin Frizzled (Fzl1/2/7 or Fzl5/8)? The endomesoderm role is beta-catenin-dependent and the later ectodermal role runs through Fzl5/8-JNK, suggesting Wnt8 engages more than one receptor.

Q: The later A/P neuroectoderm role is beta-catenin-independent (Wnt/JNK through Fzl5/8). Should a non-canonical Wnt signaling pathway term be added for Wnt8, given that the PLoS Biol study attributes the JNK step jointly to Wnt1 and Wnt8 and does not separate their receptor engagement?

Q: Is Blimp1 a necessary in vivo input into wnt8? The reporter-module analysis says yes, but the whole-locus BAC experiment in the endoderm GRN paper says the Blimp1-site mutation has no effect and that wnt8 turns on in veg2 before blimp1b; this affects how the torus subcircuit should be modelled but not the annotations here.

What the gene is

Wnt8 is a secreted Wnt-family ligand, the zygotic "motor" of the sea urchin endomesoderm gene
regulatory network. It is not a transcription factor: it is the intercellular signal whose
reception drives beta-catenin into the nucleus of the receiving cell, and it is itself
transcribed under control of beta-catenin/Tcf, so that it forms a self-propagating intercellular
positive feedback loop.
