Gene Description

GataE is the sea urchin ortholog of the vertebrate GATA4/5/6 transcription factors, a 567-residue protein with two class IV (GATA-type) zinc fingers. It is zygotically expressed from the 15 h blastula, first in a ring of prospective non-skeletogenic mesoderm cells in the vegetal plate, where it is a direct target of Delta/Notch signalling (through Su(H) sites in its early intronic cis-regulatory module) and of the Gcm transcription factor; its expression then spreads to the endoderm of the veg2 lineage under Otx control, and in the gastrula and pluteus it marks the midgut, hindgut and coelomic pouches. In the endoderm GataE is a direct activator of the endodermal regulatory genes otx (beta1/2 transcript), foxA and brachyury, and it forms a positive cross-regulatory feedback loop with otx (and blimp1b) that locks down the endoderm regulatory state; the otx-gatae loop is conserved between sea urchins and starfish. In the aboral non-skeletogenic mesoderm GataE is a linchpin of the pigment cell gene regulatory network, directly activating the pigment biosynthesis gene polyketide synthase together with Gcm and Krl, and its knockdown eliminates pigment cells and allows the oral mesoderm regulatory state to expand. GataE also binds a cis-regulatory element of the aboral ectoderm gene spec2a and physically interacts with the Friend-of-GATA cofactor SpFog1.

Core Functions

GATA4/5/6-class zinc-finger transcriptional activator of the endoderm gene regulatory network: in the veg2 endoderm it directly activates the regulatory genes otx (beta1/2), foxA and brachyury through GATA sites in their cis-regulatory modules and forms a positive feedback loop with otx that locks down the endoderm regulatory state before gastrulation.

Identity of the UniProt entry

  • Q64HK6 (Q64HK6_STRPU, unreviewed/TrEMBL, 567 aa) is the S. purpuratus GATA
    transcription factor deposited by the Davidson lab (EMBL AAU21562.1, gene name
    gatae); its single RX line is PMID:15567710 (Lee & Davidson 2004), the
    expression paper for Spgatae. The cDNA in that paper is GenBank AY623814, which
    is also the cDNA used to define the exon structure in Lee, Nam & Davidson 2007
    PMID:17570356. So the accession is the GRN gene, not a
    paralogue; there is no identity doubt. The sea urchin has a second GATA gene,
    gatac (GATA1/2/3 class), which is a different node of the mesoderm GRN.
  • Orthology: PMID:15567710; also
    PMID:17570356. PANTHER PTHR10071 (family) / PTHR10071:SF337
    (subfamily), labels looked up in interpro/panther/panther.obo, not from memory.
  • Domain architecture (UniProt FT): two GATA-type zinc fingers at 256-311 and
    311-364 (PROSITE PS50114); PMID:17570356. Long disordered N- and C-terminal regions.

description: >-
  GataE is the sea urchin ortholog of the vertebrate GATA4/5/6 transcription
  factors, a 567-residue protein with two class IV (GATA-type) zinc fingers.
  It is zygotically expressed from the 15 h blastula, first in a ring of
  prospective non-skeletogenic mesoderm cells in the vegetal plate, where it
  is a direct target of Delta/Notch signalling (through Su(H) sites in its
  early intronic cis-regulatory module) and of the Gcm transcription factor;
  its expression then spreads to the endoderm of the veg2 lineage under Otx
  control, and in the gastrula and pluteus it marks the midgut, hindgut and
  coelomic pouches. In the endoderm GataE is a direct activator of the
  endodermal regulatory genes otx (beta1/2 transcript), foxA and brachyury,
  and it forms a positive cross-regulatory feedback loop with otx (and
  blimp1b) that locks down the endoderm regulatory state; the otx-gatae loop
  is conserved between sea urchins and starfish. In the aboral
  non-skeletogenic mesoderm GataE is a linchpin of the pigment cell gene
  regulatory network, directly activating the pigment biosynthesis gene
  polyketide synthase together with Gcm and Krl, and its knockdown eliminates
  pigment cells and allows the oral mesoderm regulatory state to expand. GataE
  also binds a cis-regulatory element of the aboral ectoderm gene spec2a and
  physically interacts with the Friend-of-GATA cofactor SpFog1.
