Gene Description

Hex is the single sea urchin orthologue of the vertebrate hematopoietically-expressed homeobox protein HHEX, a 317-residue transcription factor with one homeodomain flanked by disordered N- and C-terminal regions. In the embryo it is zygotically activated in the large micromere-derived skeletogenic mesenchyme lineage (the primary mesenchyme cells), downstream of nuclear beta-catenin and pmar1 and of the lineage-founding regulators ets1 and alx1. There it is the hub of a dynamic state-stabilization subcircuit, forming interlocking positive feedback loops with the ETS factor Erg (erg-hex) and the TALE homeobox factor Tgif (hex-tgif), so that knockdown of hex depresses erg and tgif transcripts and vice versa; this transcriptional lockdown converts the transient initiating inputs into a stable skeletogenic regulatory state. Hex outputs also go to foxo and, as a feed-forward driver together with Alx1, Ets1, Tbr, Tel, Erg, FoxB and Dri, to the battery of differentiation genes that build the larval calcite endoskeleton. Morpholino knockdown of hex yields embryos with a full complement of ingressed skeletogenic cells that nevertheless cannot form the biomineral skeleton. After skeletogenic cell ingression hex is switched on a second time in the oral non-skeletogenic mesoderm, together with erg and ets1/2. In Lytechinus variegatus the hex orthologue is additionally required for the apical constriction and motility steps of the skeletogenic epithelial-mesenchymal transition.

Core Functions

Homeodomain sequence-specific transcription factor that, in the skeletogenic mesenchyme (primary mesenchyme cell) lineage, forms positive feedback loops with Erg and Tgif to lock down the lineage regulatory state and acts as a feed-forward driver of the skeletogenic differentiation (biomineralization) gene battery; its knockdown yields ingressed skeletogenic cells that cannot build the larval skeleton.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is Hex a direct activator of erg, tgif and of the skeletogenic differentiation genes (which would justify GO:0001228 / GO:0000978 with IDA), or does it act through intermediates? No cis-regulatory dissection of a Hex-dependent module has been reported for S. purpuratus.

Q: Why do hex morphants ingress normally in S. purpuratus (Oliveri 2008) but fail apical constriction and motility during EMT in L. variegatus (Saunders and McClay 2014)? Is this a species difference, a difference in knockdown efficiency, or a difference in scoring?

Q: Does Ets1 provide a direct input into hex? Oliveri et al. 2008 reported loss of hex expression after ets1 knockdown, whereas Rafiq et al. 2014 did not detect such a change.

Q: What is the function of the later oral non-skeletogenic mesoderm expression of hex (with erg and ets1/2), and does the same hex-erg loop operate there, as the vertebrate hematopoietic parallels suggest?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: ChIP-qPCR/ChIP-seq of tagged Sp-Hex in primary mesenchyme cells, combined with GFP reporter analysis of the erg and tgif cis-regulatory regions with mutated homeodomain (TAAT-core) sites, to test direct Hex input into its feedback-loop partners and into the differentiation genes.

Experiment: Side-by-side morpholino and CRISPR knockdown of hex in S. purpuratus and L. variegatus with time-lapse imaging of ingression, and QPCR of erg, tgif and biomineralization genes at matched time points, to resolve the ingression-phenotype discrepancy.

Hex (Strongylocentrotus purpuratus, UniProt A0A7M7R9J9) - curation notes

Identity

  • UniProt A0A7M7R9J9 (A0A7M7R9J9_STRPU, unreviewed/TrEMBL, 317 aa) is an
    EnsemblMetazoa-derived "Homeobox domain-containing protein" with no literature
    RX line. Cross-references: RefSeq XP_781292.3 / XP_030841717.1, GeneID 575826
    and GeneID 115918645 (two loci in the current assembly, both named by NCBI
    "hematopoietically-expressed homeobox protein HHEX homolog"; checked via NCBI
    gene esummary, 2026-10-03), PANTHER PTHR24324:SF5 (official label, from
    interpro/panther/panther.obo: "HEMATOPOIETICALLY-EXPRESSED HOMEOBOX PROTEIN
    HHEX"), FunFam 1.10.10.60:FF:000721 "Hematopoietically-expressed homeobox
    protein HHEX". Single homeodomain at 186-246 (PROSITE PS50071) flanked by
    disordered N- and C-terminal regions, as in vertebrate HHEX.
  • The GRN gene hex (Oliveri, Tu & Davidson 2008, where it is cited to the
    homeobox survey of Howard-Ashby et al. 2006, ref. 5) is the sea urchin HHEX
    orthologue: Saunders & McClay state that of the 13 PMC EMT transcription
    factors they studied, "12 of the 13 TFs are the sole member of their subfamily
    represented in the sea urchin genome" and PMID:24598159, i.e. hex is the
    single HHEX-class gene. Since A0A7M7R9J9 is the only HHEX-subfamily homeodomain
    protein in the S. purpuratus proteome, the accession and the GRN gene are the
    same gene. Confidence: high. The only residual caveat is that the two NCBI
    GeneIDs (575826, 115918645) with identical descriptions may be assembly
    duplicates (haplotypes) rather than two genes; the UniProt entry maps to both.
  • Community symbol: Hex (hex, Sp-Hex, SpHex in the GRN literature).
  • The homeobox survey that first characterized the S. purpuratus homeobox
    complement (including hex) is abstract-only in the cache:
    PMID:17055477. Its hex-specific data (WMISH in the micromere lineage) are not
    visible in the cached abstract.

    listed among the genes expressed selectively in the large micromere-PMC
    lineage: PMID:30264451.
  • Second, later domain in the oral non-skeletogenic mesoderm (NSM) after PMC
    ingression: PMID:23261933 PMID:23261933 The same cohort (gataC, ets1/2, hex, erg) characterizes sea star
    embryonic mesoderm PMID:23261933.

Place in the skeletogenic micromere GRN (Oliveri, Tu & Davidson 2008)

  • Upstream: hex lies downstream of nuclear beta-catenin and pmar1 (the
    double-negative gate) and of the first-tier regulators, especially ets1 and
    alx1: PMID:30264451;
    PMID:30264451 (so the ets1 ->
    hex input is reported by one lab and not reproduced by the other).
  • Position: the dynamic state-stabilization subcircuit immediately downstream
    of alx1/ets1/tbr/tel: PMID:18413610.
  • Feedback: PMID:18413610 Hex is the hub of both loops.
  • Outputs: PMID:18413610
  • Differentiation genes: hex is one of the feed-forward drivers of the
    biomineralization gene battery PMID:18413610.
  • Phenotype of hex MASO (the key loss-of-function result): skeleton-minus with
    normal ingression, i.e. a differentiation rather than an EMT defect in S.
    purpuratus: PMID:18413610; PMID:18413610
  • Interpretation of the loop by Ettensohn's group: PMID:30264451 PMID:30264451

Lytechinus variegatus EMT data (Saunders & McClay 2014; not S. purpuratus)

  • LvHex was cloned from the Sp annotation and knocked down with two MASOs;
    hex is one of the 10 downstream PMC TFs required for EMT: PMID:24598159 Specifically it is a terminal node
    of the apical-constriction and motility sub-circuits: PMID:24598159 PMID:24598159 In the Lv PMC GRN "snail
    regulates erg and erg regulates hex".
  • This contrasts with the S. purpuratus result that hex morphants ingress
    normally (Oliveri 2008, Fig. 3C-E). Because of the species difference and the
    conflicting ingression phenotype, no EMT annotation is proposed for the Sp
    gene product; recorded as a question.
