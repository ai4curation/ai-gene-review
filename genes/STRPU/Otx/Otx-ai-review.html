Gene Description

Otx (SpOtx) is the single orthodenticle-class (paired/bicoid-type, K50) homeodomain transcription factor of the purple sea urchin. One gene produces an alpha form and several beta forms (otx-beta1/2) by alternative promoter use and splicing; the forms share the homeodomain and C-terminus but differ in their N-termini. Otx is supplied maternally, is transcribed in all cells of the cleaving embryo, and its transcripts later become restricted to endoderm, oral ectoderm and the ciliated band. The protein is initially held in the cytoplasm, entering micromere nuclei precociously at the 16-cell stage and the nuclei of all other cells around the 60-120-cell stage. Otx binds TAATCC/T motifs and acts as a transcriptional activator with N- and C-terminal transactivation domains. In the micromeres it cooperates with nuclear beta-catenin/Tcf to switch on pmar1. In the vegetal-plate endoderm the otx-beta1/2 cis-regulatory module integrates Gatae, Blimp1 (Krox) and Otx inputs as an AND gate, and Otx in turn directly activates blimp1b, gatae, foxa, brachyury and endo16, forming the positive feedback loops that lock down the endoderm regulatory state. In the ectoderm Otx activates the aboral-ectoderm differentiation gene spec2a through multiple Otx sites, and dominant-negative Engrailed-Otx blocks both endoderm and aboral ectoderm formation while Otx overexpression converts the embryo into aboral ectoderm.

Core Functions

Sequence-specific transcriptional activation of endoderm regulatory genes in the endomesoderm gene regulatory network. Acting in the nucleus of vegetal-plate endoderm cells, Otx (chiefly the beta1/2 forms) binds TAATCC/T sites and, together with Gatae and Blimp1/Krox, drives its own beta1/2 module and directly activates blimp1b, gatae, foxa, brachyury and endo16, forming the positive feedback loops that lock down the endoderm regulatory state; dominant-negative Engrailed-Otx blocks endoderm formation.

Otx (Q26417, Strongylocentrotus purpuratus) — curation notes

Identity

  • UniProt Q26417 (Swiss-Prot, OTX_STRPU, "Homeobox protein OTX", SpOTX) is the
    original SpOtx cDNA (EMBL S76899 / AAB33568) reported by Gan et al. 1995
    PMID:7875376. 371 aa; homeodomain at residues 131-190
    (PROSITE PS50071); paired/bicoid-class (K50) homeodomain; PANTHER PTHR45793 /
    SF5 (id not asserted in the review; see CLAUDE.md on PANTHER labels).
  • The otx gene produces several isoforms. Li et al. 1997 showed two protein forms,
    alpha and beta, from one gene: "SpOtx exists in two forms (alpha and beta) that are
    generated by alternative RNA splicing from a single SpOtx gene. SpOtx(alpha) and
    SpOtx(beta) had identical C-termini and homeoboxes but were entirely different in
    their N-terminal domains." PMID:9242422. "three distinct SpOtx(beta) mRNAs
    resulted from two separate transcriptional initiation events" PMID:9242422; the
    GRN literature refers to these as otx-beta1/2 (Yuh 2004, Peter & Davidson 2010).
    Swiss-Prot Q26417 carries no "ALTERNATIVE PRODUCTS" section and no isoform
    accessions (Q26417-1 etc.), so the schema isoform field cannot be populated with a
    real identifier. The Gan 1995 clone accumulates in all cells during cleavage, the
    behaviour Li 1997 attributes to SpOtx(alpha) ("SpOtx(alpha) mRNAs were transcribed
    from a single start site and accumulated in all cells during cleavage"
    PMID:9242422), so Q26417 most likely represents the alpha form; the beta forms,
    which carry the GRN-critical beta1/2 cis-regulatory module, share the homeodomain
    and C-terminus. All annotations below are made on the gene product as a whole and
    isoform specificity is recorded in the review text.

DNA binding and activator function (Klein lab, 1994-1996)

  • Otx-class proteins bind TAATCC/T; the aboral-ectoderm Spec2a enhancer carries
    several such sites and SpOtx binds them in bandshifts PMID:7875376.
  • Three Otx sites in the 188-bp RSR enhancer are needed for activity, and a
    76-bp fragment with two Otx sites suffices for aboral ectoderm expression
    [PMID:8088441 "Using site-directed mutagenesis, we showed that three Otx consensus
    binding sites were responsible for the activity of the enhancer, acting in a
    non-cooperative manner to yield full activity." ... "Bandshift analysis revealed
    that the Otx sites were able to bind SpOtx, suggesting that this transcription
    factor mediates positive control at these sites."].
  • SpOtx has two transactivation domains, and its overexpression phenotype depends
    on them: "These same N-terminal and C-terminal regions were shown to be
    transactivation domains in a yeast transactivation assay, indicating that the
    biological effects of overexpressing SpOtx were due to its action as a
    transcription factor." PMID:8625836. Consensus Otx sites fused to basal
    promoters activate reporters in all cell types, with enhanced endomesoderm
    expression PMID:11401403.
  • Later cis-regulatory work still treats SpOtx as "a broadly distributed
    transcriptional activator" PMID:15882584, with the oral-ectoderm repressor Gsc
    and the endomesoderm factor GataE competing for the same TAATCC/T elements.

Subcellular localization: cytoplasmic retention, then nuclear

  • "SpOtx was found in the cytoplasm of early cleavage stage embryos and was
    translocated into nuclei between the 60- and 120-cell stage, coincident with Spec
    gene activation." PMID:8625836. In contrast, at the 16-cell stage the protein is
    nuclear specifically in micromeres: "SpOtx appears transiently in nuclei of
    micromeres but not in nuclei of mesomeres and macromeres. At later stages of
    development, SpOtx was translocated into nuclei of all cells." PMID:8952065;
    cytoplasmic retention is proposed to be mediated by an SH3-like interaction with
    alpha-actinin PMID:8952065. Gan 1995: "SpOtx protein was found in nuclei of
    all cells at both blastula and pluteus stages." PMID:7875376.
  • The precocious micromere nuclearization is used as one of the three micromere
    inputs in the skeletogenic GRN PMID:18413610.

Cell-fate requirement (Li et al. 1999; Mao et al. 1996)

  • Dominant-negative Engrailed-SpOtx blocks endoderm and aboral ectoderm:
    "We show here that the homeodomain transcription factor SpOtx is required for
    endoderm and aboral ectoderm formation during sea urchin embryogenesis."
    ... "The Engrailed-SpOtx fusion protein reduced the expression of endoderm- and
    aboral ectoderm-specific genes and inhibited the formation of endoderm and aboral
    ectoderm cell types." PMID:10433832. SpOtx acts downstream of or parallel to
    nuclear beta-catenin and rescues aboral ectoderm (but not endoderm) in
    C-cadherin embryos PMID:10433832.
  • Overexpression converts the embryo to aboral ectoderm: "Eggs injected with SpOtx
    mRNA developed into epithelial balls of aboral ectoderm suggesting that SpOtx
    redirected nonaboral ectoderm cells to an aboral ectoderm fate." PMID:8625836.

Place in the endomesoderm GRN (Davidson lab)

  • Micromeres: Otx plus beta-catenin/Tcf activate pmar1 [PMID:18413610 "The pmar1
    genes are activated by the β-catenin/Tcf transcription complex plus Otx, i.e., by
    two of the micromere-specific inputs just enumerated ( 25 , 30 )."; PMID:12027443
    "It is initially activated as soon as the micromeres are formed, in response to Otx
    and beta-Catenin/Tcf inputs."].
  • Endoderm: the otx beta1/2 cis-regulatory module is an AND-logic node requiring
    Gatae, Otx and Krox(Blimp1) inputs [PMID:15110718 "It requires gatae, otx, and
    krox inputs, as predicted, and it operates as an "AND" logic processor in that
    removal of any one of these inputs essentially destroys activity."; "For spatial
    expression in the endoderm, one particular pair of Gata sites is essential and
    these function synergistically with an adjacent Otx site."]. Gatae and beta1/2-otx
    cross-regulate in a positive feedback loop that locks down endoderm specification
    PMID:17570356. Otx directly regulates blimp1b, and provides inputs into foxA and
    brachyury [PMID:19895806 "Another key function in the early endoderm regulatory
    network is provided by the homeodomain transcription factor Otx."; "An earlier
    report showed that Otx directly regulates the expression of blimp1b (Smith et al."
    ; "Recent results also indicate that Otx provides direct regulatory inputs into the
    cis-regulatory apparatus of both the foxA and brachyury genes"]. Peter & Davidson
    2011 retain the "positive feedback circuit between blimp1b, otx and gatae"
    PMID:21623371. The otx/wnt8/blimp1 subcircuit drives the expanding torus of
    endomesodermal gene expression [PMID:17975065; PMID:19104065 "the blimp1 gene
    requires inputs from the same Wnt8/Tcf signaling system as well as from another
    then ubiquitous factor, Otx"]. foxa also receives gatae and otx inputs
    PMID:17038513.
  • endo16: SpOtx is required for endo16 activation in the vegetal plate
    PMID:11401403.
  • Oral ectoderm / ciliated band: otx-beta1/2 is among the nodal-independent early
    oral ectoderm genes and later a ciliated band gene PMID:19268450.
  • Morpholino perturbation of otx is confounded by the isoforms: "The perturbation
    of Otx expression by morpholinos is complicated by the fact that otx is expressed
    in various splice isoforms (Li et al." PMID:19895806; a homeodomain-exon
    splice-blocking MASO gave only a weak foxA decrease.

  their N-termini. Otx is supplied maternally, is transcribed in all cells of the

  nuclei of all other cells around the 60-120-cell stage. Otx binds TAATCC/T

  transactivation domains. In the micromeres it cooperates with nuclear
  beta-catenin/Tcf to switch on pmar1. In the vegetal-plate endoderm the
  otx-beta1/2 cis-regulatory module integrates Gatae, Blimp1 (Krox) and Otx
