Gene Description

Ets1 (Sp-Ets1/2) is the single sea urchin orthologue of the vertebrate ETS1/ETS2 subfamily of ETS-domain transcription factors, with an N-terminal PNT (pointed/SAM) domain and a C-terminal winged-helix ETS DNA-binding domain. Its transcript is maternally supplied and ubiquitous through cleavage; zygotic transcription is confined to the large micromere lineage, the founder cells of the skeletogenic primary mesenchyme cells (PMCs), because ets1 is one of the direct targets of the Pmar1/HesC double-negative gate, and it later extends to the non-skeletogenic mesoderm. The protein is activated in the micromere lineage by Raf/MEK/ERK phosphorylation of a conserved threonine (T107) that lies in a P-P-T-P MAP-kinase consensus motif. Ets1 is a transcriptional activator that helps lock in the skeletogenic regulatory state (feeding into alx1, tbr and foxN2/3) and is the most widely used positive driver of the skeletogenic differentiation gene batteries, acting directly through ETS sites in cis-regulatory modules of effector genes such as cyclophilin-1 and the spicule matrix gene SM50. Loss of Ets1 function gives skeleton-less embryos whose PMCs fail to ingress, whereas ectopic ets1 mRNA converts non-PMC cells into migratory mesenchyme, so Ets1 is required both for specification of the skeletogenic mesenchyme and for its epithelial-mesenchymal transition. Across echinoderms Ets1/2 is a conserved larval mesoderm regulator whose skeletogenic target set was co-opted in the sea urchin lineage.

Core Functions

ERK-activated ETS-domain transcriptional activator of the skeletogenic (primary mesenchyme cell) regulatory state in the large micromere lineage: turned on by the Pmar1/HesC double-negative gate, it maintains alx1, activates tbr and foxN2/3, and is the principal positive driver of the differentiation gene batteries required for PMC ingression and calcite biomineralization.

description: >-
  Ets1 (Sp-Ets1/2) is the single sea urchin orthologue of the vertebrate
  ETS1/ETS2 subfamily of ETS-domain transcription factors, with an N-terminal
  PNT (pointed/SAM) domain and a C-terminal winged-helix ETS DNA-binding domain.
  Its transcript is maternally supplied and ubiquitous through cleavage; zygotic
  transcription is confined to the large micromere lineage, the founder cells of
  the skeletogenic primary mesenchyme cells (PMCs), because ets1 is one of the
  direct targets of the Pmar1/HesC double-negative gate, and it later extends to
  the non-skeletogenic mesoderm. The protein is activated in the micromere
  lineage by Raf/MEK/ERK phosphorylation of a conserved threonine (T107) that
  lies in a P-P-T-P MAP-kinase consensus motif. Ets1 is a transcriptional
  activator that helps lock in the skeletogenic regulatory state (feeding into
  alx1, tbr and foxN2/3) and is the most widely used positive driver of the
  skeletogenic differentiation gene batteries, acting directly through ETS sites
  in cis-regulatory modules of effector genes such as cyclophilin-1 and the
  spicule matrix gene SM50. Loss of Ets1 function gives skeleton-less embryos
  whose PMCs fail to ingress, whereas ectopic ets1 mRNA converts non-PMC cells
  into migratory mesenchyme, so Ets1 is required both for specification of the
  skeletogenic mesenchyme and for its epithelial-mesenchymal transition. Across
  echinoderms Ets1/2 is a conserved larval mesoderm regulator whose skeletogenic
  target set was co-opted in the sea urchin lineage.
