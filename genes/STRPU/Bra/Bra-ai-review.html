Gene Description

Bra (SpBra) is the single sea urchin orthologue of the vertebrate Brachyury (T/TBXT) transcription factor, a 503-residue protein with an N-terminal T-box DNA-binding domain. It is a node of the endoderm gene regulatory network of the embryo. Zygotic transcription begins in the vegetal plate, where it is confined to the presumptive endoderm of the veg2 lineage and depends on nuclear beta-catenin/Tcf together with Otx and Hox11/13b inputs; by 24 h the expression domain shifts into the veg1 (future hindgut) endoderm and, at gastrulation, forms a ring of cells around the blastopore through which invaginating endoderm cells transit. A second expression domain arises in the oral ectoderm and becomes restricted to the stomodeum, the site of mouth invagination. After the embryonic phase the gene is silenced and is re-expressed in the larval vestibule of the adult rudiment and in the hydrocoel mesoderm. Bra binds genomic cis-regulatory elements and acts mainly as a terminal regulator of the endoderm network: loss of function does not abolish endoderm or mesoderm specification but blocks the morphogenetic movements of gastrulation, and the genes that depend on Bra in the endoderm are effector genes, including cytoskeletal modulators implicated in gut morphogenesis, gut enzymes and the Hedgehog ligand, rather than further regulatory genes. Bra also feeds back positively on the otx-beta transcript of the otx/blimp1b/gatae endoderm kernel, and genome-wide binding studies place it in an ancient Brachyury-FoxA-Wnt feedback loop that predates the cnidarian-bilaterian split.

Core Functions

T-box transcription factor of the endoderm gene regulatory network that binds genomic cis-regulatory elements and activates the morphogenetic effector programme of the invaginating endoderm: expressed in the veg2 and then veg1 endoderm downstream of Tcf/beta-catenin, Otx and Hox11/13b, it drives cytoskeletal modulators, gut effector genes and the Hedgehog ligand, feeds back positively on the otx-beta transcript of the endoderm kernel, and is required for the cell movements of gastrulation.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is Brachyury a direct transcriptional activator of its endodermal effector targets (cytoskeletal modulators, hh) and of the otx-beta module, which would justify GO:0001228 and confirm the otx-brachyury positive feedback as a direct linkage in the endoderm kernel?

Q: Does Brachyury perform the same morphogenetic function in the oral ectoderm of S. purpuratus as it does in Lytechinus, that is, is stomodeal invagination and mouth formation dependent on Brachyury, and which effector genes does it control there?

Q: The hindgut (veg1) phase of brachyury expression is driven by Hox11/13b; does Brachyury contribute to hindgut/anus morphogenesis at the pluteus stage, and is the later re-expression in the adult rudiment vestibule and hydrocoel mesoderm functional?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Site-directed mutagenesis of T-box (Brachyury) binding sites in the cis-regulatory modules of S. purpuratus endodermal effector genes identified by ChIP-seq and by the macroarray screen, assayed as reporter constructs in Brachyury morpholino and misexpression backgrounds, to establish direct activation.

Experiment: Oral-ectoderm-targeted Brachyury perturbation in S. purpuratus (for example a photoactivatable or late-injected dominant-negative construct) combined with live imaging of stomodeal invagination, to test the mouth-formation function described in Lytechinus.

    Its xrefs are RefSeq XP_030850405.1 / XM_030994545.1 (transcript variant X2) and

    XP_030850404.1 / XM_030994544.1 (transcript variant X1) of the same GeneID 576774, so
    the two accessions are two predicted isoforms of one locus, not two genes.
  • NCBI Gene 576774 is annotated "T-box transcription factor T homolog | brachyury protein
    homolog" on scaffold NW_022145607.1. A GenBank nuccore search for S. purpuratus T-box
    genes finds only one "T-box transcription factor T homolog" locus (LOC576774); the other
    T-box loci in the genome are Tbx2/3, TBX3 (LOC592389), TBX1 (LOC585491) and H15
    (LOC100889015). S. purpuratus EST clones (e.g. CX697415, CX691184) from the same locus
    were annotated "similar to SW:BRAC_HEMPU Q25113 BRACHYURY PROTEIN HOMOLOG" (the
    Hemicentrotus HpTa protein).
  • Domain evidence on the record itself: InterPro IPR002070 "TF_Brachyury", PRINTS
    PR00938 BRACHYURY, CDD cd20192 T-box_TBXT_TBX19-like, FunFam 2.60.40.820:FF:000002
    "T-box transcription factor Brachyury".
  • The sea urchin brachyury gene is single copy PMID:7555703, so the one T-homolog locus in the S. purpuratus
    genome must be SpBra, the gene cloned by Peterson et al. 1999 PMID:10068473 and used for the target-gene screen
    of Rast et al. 2002 PMID:12027442.
  • Confidence: HIGH. I could not locate the GenBank accession of the 1999 SpBra cDNA to
    do a direct sequence comparison (the nearest hit in the protein database, AAD20328, is
    the SpNot cDNA from the same paper), but a single-copy gene, a single genomic locus
    and Brachyury-specific signatures on the record leave no realistic alternative.

What the gene is

Bra encodes the sea urchin orthologue of vertebrate Brachyury (T/TBXT), the founding
member of the T-box family of DNA-binding transcription factors. The protein has an
N-terminal T-box DNA-binding domain (residues 45-220 on A0A7M7PDZ1) and a
low-complexity C-terminal region. Sea urchin Brachyury was first cloned from
Hemicentrotus pulcherrimus (HpTa) PMID:7555703.

    the secondary mesenchyme founder cells, then extinguished; re-expressed a week later
    in the larval vestibule of the adult rudiment and in the mesoderm of both hydrocoels
    [PMID:10068473 "this gene is expressed during embryogenesis in the embryonic vegetal

    "SpBra is also expressed in the mesoderm of both left and right hydrocoels, and it

    PMID:12027442.
  • Endoderm GRN timing (Peter & Davidson 2010, 2011): bra is part of the veg2 endoderm
    regulatory state by 18 h, together with foxA, blimp1b and hox11/13b PMID:19895806,
    and by 24 h it is transcribed instead in the veg1 (future hindgut) endoderm
    PMID:21623371.
  • A second, oral-ectoderm domain around the future mouth is seen in every sea urchin
    examined: Lytechinus PMID:11784024, Paracentrotus

  • Lytechinus protein data (anti-LvBrac serum): the vegetal domain is a torus of

description: >-
  Bra (SpBra) is the single sea urchin orthologue of the vertebrate Brachyury
  (T/TBXT) transcription factor, a 503-residue protein with an N-terminal T-box
  DNA-binding domain. It is a node of the endoderm gene regulatory network of
  the embryo. Zygotic transcription begins in the vegetal plate, where it is
  confined to the presumptive endoderm of the veg2 lineage and depends on
  nuclear beta-catenin/Tcf together with Otx and Hox11/13b inputs; by 24 h the
  expression domain shifts into the veg1 (future hindgut) endoderm and, at
  gastrulation, forms a ring of cells around the blastopore through which
  invaginating endoderm cells transit. A second expression domain arises in the
  oral ectoderm and becomes restricted to the stomodeum, the site of mouth
  invagination. After the embryonic phase the gene is silenced and is
  re-expressed in the larval vestibule of the adult rudiment and in the
  hydrocoel mesoderm. Bra binds genomic cis-regulatory elements and acts mainly
  as a terminal regulator of the endoderm network: loss of function does not
  abolish endoderm or mesoderm specification but blocks the morphogenetic
  movements of gastrulation, and the genes that depend on Bra in the endoderm
  are effector genes, including cytoskeletal modulators implicated in gut
  morphogenesis, gut enzymes and the Hedgehog ligand, rather than further
  regulatory genes. Bra also feeds back positively on the otx-beta transcript of
  the otx/blimp1b/gatae endoderm kernel, and genome-wide binding studies place
  it in an ancient Brachyury-FoxA-Wnt feedback loop that predates the
  cnidarian-bilaterian split.
