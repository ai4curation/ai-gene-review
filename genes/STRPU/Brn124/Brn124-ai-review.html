Gene Description

Sp-Brn1/2/4 is the single class III POU-domain (Brn1/Brn2/Brn4/Oct6-related) homeodomain transcription factor of the purple sea urchin, with a POU-specific domain and a homeodomain that together confer sequence-specific DNA binding. It was cloned as the predicted gut-specific regulator of the endoderm differentiation gene endo16: zygotic brn1/2/4 transcription begins at low level in the 20-h blastula endoderm, rises abruptly about ten-fold as gastrulation starts, and is confined to the gut endoderm (described as midgut by Yuh et al. and as foregut by later fluorescent in situ hybridization), coincident with endo16. In the endoderm gene regulatory network it is activated in veg2-derived anterior endoderm cells by Blimp1 in the last hours before gastrulation, downstream of the post-gastrular gut specification genes, and it drives the late, high-rate phase of endo16 transcription through endo16 cis-regulatory Module B; blocking its translation abolishes Module B-driven expression while leaving Module A intact. Predicted Brn1/2/4 sites also occur in the midgut/hindgut cis-regulatory module of blimp1/krox1a. Independently of its gut role, Brn1/2/4 is expressed in scattered ectodermal neural precursors under SoxC control, is required for the differentiation of serotonergic and all synaptotagmin B-expressing neurons, and at low dose its misexpression produces excess neurons, placing it as a neurogenic regulator downstream of SoxC, a role shared with its vertebrate class III POU relatives.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the endodermal expression domain of brn1/2/4 the midgut (Yuh et al. 2005, WMISH and endo16 coincidence) or the foregut (Cole and Arnone 2009, FISH)? The answer decides whether a midgut development or foregut-specific term should replace the organ-level digestive tract development annotation.

Q: Which endoderm GRN genes does Brn1/2/4 regulate besides endo16? Perturbation data in Peter and Davidson 2011 (Fig. 4 and supplementary tables) may define outputs such as gatae or foxa, but the cached text does not state them, so no such linkages were annotated.

Q: Is the Brn1/2/4 input into endo16 Module B direct (site mutation in Module B) or mediated by another Module B factor? The morpholino result is module-specific, consistent with the predicted site, but a Module B site mutation was not quotable from the abstract.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Mutate the predicted Brn1/2/4 site in endo16 Module B in a GFP reporter and compare with the Brn1/2/4 morpholino phenotype to show direct activation.

Type: cis-regulatory analysis

Experiment: Double FISH of brn1/2/4 with foregut (foxa) and midgut (endo16, blimp1/krox1a) markers at gastrula and prism stages to settle the midgut versus foregut discrepancy.

Type: expression analysis

Experiment: Brn1/2/4 morpholino followed by NanoString or RNA-seq of the endoderm regulatory gene set at 27-48 h to define its outputs in the gut GRN and separate them from its neural targets.

Brn1/2/4 (Sp-Brn1/2/4, brn1/2/4) — curation notes

UniProt A0A7M7GMA5 (unreviewed, "POU domain protein", 467 aa), GeneID 577601 (NCBI gene
description "brain 1/2/4", alias brn1/2/4, other designation "POU domain, class 3,
transcription factor 4"), PANTHER PTHR11636:SF89. Organism Strongylocentrotus purpuratus
(NCBITaxon:7668).

Identity of the UniProt entry

  • The UniProt entry carries no literature RX lines; it is a genome-project translation
    (RefSeq XP_003729178.1 / LOC577601). Identity with the GRN gene rests on (i) the NCBI
    Gene record for 577601, whose description is "brain 1/2/4" with alias "brn1/2/4" and
    designation "POU domain, class 3, transcription factor 4", retrieved via eutils; (ii) the
    PANTHER subfamily PTHR11636:SF89 (a class III POU / Brn-related subfamily) and PROSITE
    POU-specific + homeobox domains, matching the Yuh et al. description of a POU factor
    "related closely to the mammalian factors Brain-1, -2, and -4"; and (iii) the single
    class III POU gene in the sea urchin genome, for which the human Brn1/Brn2/Brn4/Oct6 are
    equal co-orthologs PMID:30450080.
  • Confidence that A0A7M7GMA5 is the brn1/2/4 of the Davidson endoderm GRN: HIGH.

What the gene is

  • Cloned as the predicted midgut regulator of endo16 PMID:15893979.
  • Class III POU homeodomain TF (POU-specific domain 219-293, homeodomain 309-369 in the
    UniProt entry); in vertebrates the class III POU genes are neural regulators
    PMID:26657764.

Expression

  • Maternal transcripts decay to undetectable at blastula PMID:26657764.
  • Zygotic activation in the endoderm: first detectable in the 20-h blastula at ~100
    molecules/embryo, then an abrupt 10-fold rise at the onset of gastrulation; late endo16
    rise follows that of brn1/2/4 PMID:15893979.
  • Spatial domain in the gut: Yuh et al. described it as confined to the midgut
    PMID:15893979, whereas a
    later FISH study assigns the endodermal domain to the foregut and adds two ectodermal
    domains PMID:19250980. The discrepancy
    (midgut vs foregut) is unresolved in the cached literature; it may reflect stage or
    probe/method differences. Both studies agree that the gene is a gut-endoderm regulatory
    gene.
  • In the Peter & Davidson 2011 endoderm GRN, brn1/2/4 is one of the regulatory genes that
    come on in veg2-derived (anterior endoderm) cells in the last hours before gastrulation
    PMID:21623371.

Place in the endoderm GRN

  • Input: Blimp1 (veg2 endoderm) activates brn1/2/4 PMID:21623371. The veg1 regulator Eve affects it only
    indirectly, through a proposed veg1-to-veg2 signal PMID:21623371.
  • Upstream of it sit the post-gastrular midgut specification genes PMID:15893979.
  • Output 1, endo16 (the midgut differentiation gene whose cis-regulatory analysis predicted
    this factor): Brn1/2/4 MASO blocks the late phase of endo16 transcription and abolishes
    expression driven by cis-regulatory Module B, leaving Module A untouched PMID:15893979. This is a module-specific, sign-
    positive effect, i.e. Brn1/2/4 is an activator acting through Module B.
  • Output 2 (predicted site): the blimp1/krox 1a midgut/hindgut cis-regulatory module
    carries Brn1/2/4 sites PMID:16798107.
  • The brief for this review mentioned brn1/2/4 inputs into foxa and gatae; the cached
    Peter & Davidson 2011 text does not state these linkages explicitly (they would be in
    Fig. 4 / the supplementary perturbation tables), so they are not asserted here.
  • Not present in Smith & Davidson 2008 (PMID:19104065) or Peter & Davidson 2010
    (PMID:19895806, pre-mid-blastula GRN): consistent with its late (>20 h) activation.

Second role: neurogenesis

  • Wei, Angerer & Angerer 2016 placed Brn1/2/4 downstream of SoxC in the neurogenic pathway
    PMID:26657764 and PMID:26657764.
  • SoxC MASO removes the scattered ectodermal (neural precursor) Brn1/2/4 expression but
    not the foregut expression PMID:26657764, so the endodermal and neural inputs are
    separate.
  • Later larval neurons co-expressing SpLox and Brn1/2/4 are lateral ganglion neurons with a
    "pre-pancreatic" signature PMID:30450080.
