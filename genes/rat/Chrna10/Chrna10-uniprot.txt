CC   -!- FUNCTION: Component of neuronal acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) that
CC       function as pentameric, ligand-gated cation channels with high calcium
CC       permeability among other activities. nAChRs are excitatory
CC       neurotrasnmitter receptors formed by a collection of nAChR subunits
CC       known to mediate synaptic transmission in the nervous system and the
CC       neuromuscular junction. Each nAchR subunit confers differential
CC       attributes to channel properties, including activation, deactivation
CC       and desensitization kinetics, pH sensitivity, cation permeability, and
CC       binding to allosteric modulators (PubMed:11248107, PubMed:12117536,
CC       PubMed:14742688, PubMed:16306403). Forms heteropentamers with CHRNA9.
CC       Expressed in the inner ear, in sympathetic neurons and in other non-
CC       neuronal cells, such as skin keratinocytes and lymphocytes
CC       (PubMed:14742688, PubMed:16306403). nAChR formed by CHRNA9:CHRNA10
CC       mediate central nervous system control of auditory and vestibular
CC       sensory processing. The channel is permeable to a range of divalent
CC       cations including calcium, the influx of which may activate a potassium
CC       current which hyperpolarizes the cell membrane (PubMed:11248107,
CC       PubMed:12117536). In the ear, mediates synaptic transmission between
CC       efferent olivocochlear fibers and hair cells of the cochlea, this may
CC       lead to a reduction in basilar membrane motion, altering the activity
CC       of auditory nerve fibers and reducing the range of dynamic hearing.
CC       This may protect against acoustic trauma (PubMed:11248107). May also
CC       regulate keratinocyte adhesion (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q9GZZ6, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12117536, ECO:0000269|PubMed:14742688,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16306403}.

CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: Activated by a myriad of ligands such as
CC       acetylcholine (PubMed:12117536). AChR activity is inhibited by the
CC       antagonist alpha-conotoxins RgIA and GeXXA, small disulfide-constrained
CC       peptides from cone snails (PubMed:25740413).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12117536, ECO:0000269|PubMed:25740413}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Forms homo- or heterooligomeric channels in conjunction with
CC       CHRNA10. The native outer hair cell receptor may be composed of
CC       CHRNA9:CHRNA10 heterooligomers. Found in the stoichiometric form
CC       (CHRNA9)2:(CHRNA10)3 (PubMed:25740413). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12117536, ECO:0000269|PubMed:14742688,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25740413}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Synaptic cell membrane {ECO:0000305}; Multi-pass
CC       membrane protein {ECO:0000255}. Cell membrane
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107, ECO:0000269|PubMed:12117536}; Multi-pass
CC       membrane protein {ECO:0000255}.
CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Expressed in the outer hair cells of the cochlea
CC       and the neurons of dorsal root ganglia. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12401316}.
CC   -!- DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE: Expression in the inner hair cells of the ear is
CC       lost at the onset of hearing, around P12. This correlates with a loss
CC       of sensitivity of these cells to cholinergic stimuli.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15356192}.
CC   -!- MISCELLANEOUS: The heterooligomeric receptor composed of CHRNA9 and
CC       CHRNA10 has an atypical pharmacological profile, binding several non-
CC       nicotinic ligands including strychnine (a glycine receptor antagonist)
CC       and atropine (a muscarinic acetylcholine receptor antagonist).
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the ligand-gated ion channel (TC 1.A.9) family.
CC       Acetylcholine receptor (TC 1.A.9.1) subfamily. Alpha-10/CHRNA10 sub-
CC       subfamily. {ECO:0000305}.
