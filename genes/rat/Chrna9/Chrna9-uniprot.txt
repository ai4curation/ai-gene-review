CC   -!- FUNCTION: Component of neuronal acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) that
CC       function as pentameric, ligand-gated cation channels with high calcium
CC       permeability among other activities. nAChRs are excitatory
CC       neurotrasnmitter receptors formed by a collection of nAChR subunits
CC       known to mediate synaptic transmission in the nervous system and the
CC       neuromuscular junction. Each nAchR subunit confers differential
CC       attributes to channel properties, including activation, deactivation
CC       and desensitization kinetics, pH sensitivity, cation permeability, and
CC       binding to allosteric modulators (PubMed:7954834). Forms either
CC       homopentamers or heteropentamers with CHRNA10. Expressed in the inner
CC       ear, in sympathetic neurons and in other non-neuronal cells, such as
CC       skin keratinocytes and lymphocytes (PubMed:11044723, PubMed:14742688).
CC       nAChR formed by CHRNA9:CHRNA10 mediate central nervous system control
CC       of auditory and vestibular sensory processing. The channel is permeable
CC       to a range of divalent cations including calcium, the influx of which
CC       may activate a potassium current which hyperpolarizes the cell membrane
CC       (PubMed:10713500, PubMed:11248107, PubMed:12117536). In the ear,
CC       mediates synaptic transmission between efferent olivocochlear fibers
CC       and hair cells of the cochlea, this may lead to a reduction in basilar
CC       membrane motion, altering the activity of auditory nerve fibers and
CC       reducing the range of dynamic hearing. This may protect against
CC       acoustic trauma (PubMed:11248107). May also regulate keratinocyte
CC       adhesion (By similarity). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q9UGM1,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:10713500, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11044723,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107, ECO:0000269|PubMed:12117536,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:14742688, ECO:0000269|PubMed:7954834}.

CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: Activated by a myriad of ligands such as
CC       acetylcholine (PubMed:10713500, PubMed:12117536). AChR activity is
CC       inhibited by the antagonist alpha-conotoxins RgIA and GeXXA, small
CC       disulfide-constrained peptides from cone snails (PubMed:22774872,
CC       PubMed:25740413). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10713500,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12117536, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22774872,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25740413}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Forms homo- or heterooligomeric channels in conjunction with
CC       CHRNA10. The native outer hair cell receptor may be composed of
CC       CHRNA9:CHRNA10 heterooligomers. Found in the stoichiometric form
CC       (CHRNA9)2:(CHRNA10)3 (PubMed:25740413). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12117536, ECO:0000269|PubMed:14742688,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25740413}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Synaptic cell membrane {ECO:0000305}; Multi-pass
CC       membrane protein {ECO:0000255}. Cell membrane
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10713500, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12117536, ECO:0000269|PubMed:7954834}; Multi-pass
CC       membrane protein {ECO:0000255}.
CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Detected in the nasal epithelium, in the outer hair
CC       cells of the cochlea, in the pars tuberalis of the hypophysis, and in
CC       the developing muscle of the tongue. Also expressed in the neurons of
CC       dorsal root ganglia. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11248107,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:12401316, ECO:0000269|PubMed:7954834}.
CC   -!- MISCELLANEOUS: The heterooligomeric receptor composed of CHRNA9 and
CC       CHRNA10 has an atypical pharmacological profile, binding several non-
CC       nicotinic ligands including strychnine (a glycine receptor antagonist)
CC       and atropine (a muscarinic acetylcholine receptor antagonist).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:7954834}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the ligand-gated ion channel (TC 1.A.9) family.
CC       Acetylcholine receptor (TC 1.A.9.1) subfamily. Alpha-9/CHRNA9 sub-
CC       subfamily. {ECO:0000305}.
