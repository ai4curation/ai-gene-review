CC   -!- FUNCTION: Inward rectifier potassium channel that forms the pore of
CC       ATP-sensitive potassium channels (KATP), regulating potassium
CC       permeability as a function of cytoplasmic ATP and ADP concentrations in
CC       many different cells (By similarity). Inward rectifier potassium
CC       channels are characterized by a greater tendency to allow potassium to
CC       flow into the cell rather than out of it. Their voltage dependence is
CC       regulated by the concentration of extracellular potassium; as external
CC       potassium is raised, the voltage range of the channel opening shifts to
CC       more positive voltages. The inward rectification is mainly due to the
CC       blockage of outward current by internal magnesium. Can be blocked by
CC       extracellular barium (By similarity). In pancreatic cells, it forms
CC       KATP channels with ABCC8/SUR1 (By similarity). Can form cardiac and
CC       smooth muscle-type KATP channels with ABCC9 (By similarity).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q14654}.
CC   -!- CATALYTIC ACTIVITY:
CC       Reaction=K(+)(in) = K(+)(out); Xref=Rhea:RHEA:29463, ChEBI:CHEBI:29103;
CC         Evidence={ECO:0000269|PubMed:8607800};
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: KATP channels are regulated by cytoplasmic ATP/ADP
CC       ratios; ATP inhibits the channel by closing the pore, while ADP
CC       activates the channel (By similarity). Activated by
CC       phosphatidylinositol 4,5-biphosphate (PtdIns(4,5)P2) (PubMed:38509107).
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q14654, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38509107}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homotetramer; the homotetramer binds four ATP molecules (one
CC       ATP per subunit). Forms an heterooctamer with ABCC8/SUR1; one KCNJ11
CC       homotetramer interacts with four ABCC8/SUR1 molecules
CC       (PubMed:38509107). Interacts with ABCC9/SUR2.
CC       {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q14654, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38509107}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Membrane {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q14654}; Multi-
CC       pass membrane protein {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:Q14654}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: There are two PtdIns(4,5)P2 binding sites: A canonical site
CC       where the phosphate groups of one PtdIns(4,5)P2 molecule are
CC       coordinated at least by residues Lys-67, Trp-68 and Arg-176; a non-
CC       canonical site where the second PtdIns(4,5)P2 molecule is coordinated
CC       by both KCNJ11 and ABCC8/SUR1 residues. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:38509107}.
CC   -!- PTM: Phosphorylation by MAPK1 results in changes in channel gating that
CC       destabilize the closed states and reduce the ATP sensitivity.
CC       {ECO:0000250}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the inward rectifier-type potassium channel
CC       (TC 1.A.2.1) family. KCNJ11 subfamily. {ECO:0000305}.
