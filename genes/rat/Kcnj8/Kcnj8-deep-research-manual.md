# Kcnj8 (rat, Q63664) - manual literature research

No deep-research API was available for this session. This summary was compiled by the
reviewer from cached publications in `publications/` and PubMed searches. Every claim
carries its source.

## Identity

- Rat Kcnj8 encodes Kir6.1 (originally cloned as uKATP-1), a 424-residue member of the
  inward rectifier K+ channel family, KCNJ8 subfamily (UniProt Q63664; RGD:2960; GeneID 25472).
- Two transmembrane helices (M1, M2) flank a pore loop with the K+ selectivity filter;
  large cytoplasmic N- and C-terminal domains (UniProt features, by similarity).

## Molecular function: pore subunit of the vascular KATP channel

- Cloning: "uKATP-1 closes in response to 1 mM ATP and has a single channel conductance
  of 70 +/- 2 picosiemens", and "is a weak rectifier and is blocked with Ba2+ ions"
  [PMID:7890693]. Activated by diazoxide in that study.
- With SUR2B, Kir6.1 forms a channel that "is not a classical ATP-sensitive K+ channel but
  closely resembles the nucleotide diphosphate-dependent K+ channel in vascular smooth
  muscle cells" [PMID:9130167]; UDP/GDP activate it and glibenclamide blocks it.
- Cryo-EM of rat Kir6.1 with SUR2B: "Vascular tone is dependent on smooth muscle KATP
  channels comprising pore-forming Kir6.1 and regulatory SUR2B subunits"; channels
  "lack spontaneous activity and require Mg-nucleotides for activation" [PMID:34711681].
  Channels were "purified from COSm6 cells coexpressing rat Kir6.1 and SUR2B".
- Nucleotide binding: "We show direct binding of TNP-ATP to the COOH termini of all three
  known K(ATP) channels" (Kir1.1, Kir6.1, Kir6.2) [PMID:11956191]; a high-affinity
  site needs "unique N- and C-terminal interactions from adjacent subunits" [PMID:15775962].
- Cantu syndrome mutation V65M in Kir6.1 "results in increased open state stability and
  hence significantly reduced ATP sensitivity" [PMID:28842488].

## Physiology

- Mouse Kcnj8 knockout: "high rate of sudden death associated with spontaneous ST
  elevation"; pinacidil "did not induce K(+) currents in vascular smooth-muscle cells of
  Kir6.1-null mice, and there was no vasodilation response to pinacidil"; "The
  Kir6.1-containing K(+) channel is critical in the regulation of vascular tonus,
  especially in the coronary arteries" [PMID:11984590].
- Rat descending vasa recta pericytes have KATP conductance contributing to basal K+
  conductance; Kir6.1, Kir6.2 and SUR2B immunostained [PMID:16048905].
- Rat ventricular myocytes: two KATP channel types; "western blotting, immunochemistry,
  and RT-PCR revealed two types of Kir6.X channels, i.e., Kir6.1 and Kir6.2"
  [PMID:17097686]. Kir6.1 found "to express strongly at the sarcolemma" and in coronary
  vascular smooth muscle and endothelium [PMID:15647111].
- Hippocampus: "functional Kir6.1 channels are located at excitatory pre-synaptic
  terminals as a complex with type-1 Sulfonylurea receptors (SUR1)"; deletion "enhances
  glutamate release at CA3 synapses" [PMID:17883401].
- Developing kidney: Kir6.1/SUR2 highly expressed in early ureteric bud and nephron
  epithelia, and opener pinacidil increases tubulogenesis [PMID:12466933].
- Interaction with connexin 43 phosphorylated at S262 (pull-down/co-IP) [PMID:22960107].

## Notes on specific GOA sources

- PMID:20123112 (RGD IPI, sulfonylurea receptor binding) studies SUR2A with Kir6.2 in
  H9C2 cells; Kir6.1 appears only in the introduction. The co-IP is of Kir6.2 with SUR2.
- PMID:28842488 supports Kir6.1 channel activity, not an ATPase-coupled transporter
  activity or a potassium-transporting ATPase complex.
