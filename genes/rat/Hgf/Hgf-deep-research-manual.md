# Hgf (rat, UniProt P17945) - manual literature synthesis

No deep-research provider was available for this review. This file is a manual
synthesis written by the reviewer from cached publications (`publications/`) and
the UniProt record. Every claim carries inline provenance.

## Identity and structure

- Rat HGF is a 728-residue pre-pro-protein; signal and pro sequences are removed and
  the mature disulfide-linked alpha/beta heterodimer is generated by proteolytic
  cleavage of a single-chain precursor
  [PMID:2139229 "The mature heterodimer structure is derived proteolytically from this single pre-pro precursor polypeptide"].
- The alpha chain carries an N-terminal PAN domain and four kringles; the beta chain is a
  serine-protease-like domain
  [PMID:2139229 "The alpha subunit of HGF contains four \"kringle\" structures, and the beta subunit has 37% amino acid identity with the serine protease domain of plasmin"].
- The protease-like domain is catalytically dead
  [file:rat/Hgf/Hgf-uniprot.txt "Has lost two of the three essential catalytic residues and so probably has no enzymatic activity"].
- Activation of the single-chain precursor is carried out by other proteases (HGFAC,
  TMPRSS13), not by HGF itself
  [file:rat/Hgf/Hgf-uniprot.txt "The single-chain precursor undergoes proteolytic processing by HGFAC resulting in an active two-chain form"].

## Molecular function: ligand of the MET receptor tyrosine kinase

- HGF is the ligand for the MET receptor tyrosine kinase, established by cross-linking
  of labelled HGF to MET [PMID:1846706 "Covalent cross-linking of 125I-labeled ligand to cellular proteins of appropriate size that were recognized by antibodies to c-met directly established the c-met product as the cell-surface receptor for HGF"].
- UniProt (by similarity to human P14210) summarises it as an activating MET ligand
  [file:rat/Hgf/Hgf-uniprot.txt "Activating ligand for the receptor tyrosine kinase MET by binding to it and promoting its dimerization"].
- Rat HGF purified from injured liver is a hepatocyte mitogen
  [PMID:1826679 "A hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)-like substance that strongly stimulated DNA synthesis of adult rat hepatocytes in primary culture"].

## Secretion / extracellular location

- Serum HGF rises during liver regeneration [PMID:19638569 "These effects were also accompanied for a long-lasting increase of serum and liver levels of HGF"].
- Rat cardiomyocytes and Stro-1 cells secrete HGF into the medium, increased by a
  hyaluronan-retinoate-butyrate ester [PMID:20097747 "The HBR treatment enhanced the secretion of VEGF and HGF in the culture medium from both RCm and Stro-1 cells."].
  This paper treats HGF as a readout (expression/secretion), not as an actor; it supports
  only secretion of rat HGF.
- In muscle, HGF is tethered in the extracellular matrix and released by stretch
  [PMID:12181355 "HGF is apparently released from its tethering in the extracellular domain by stretching and becomes available for interaction with the c-met receptor on the satellite cell"].

## Biological roles (downstream of MET activation)

- Satellite-cell activation by stretch-conditioned medium is HGF dependent
  [PMID:12181355 "Stretch-conditioned medium plus anti-HGF antibody (d) did not stimulate activation relative to the control"].
- Anti-apoptotic in ovarian granulosa cells [PMID:16885537 "Results showed that HGF suppressed apoptosis in GC and follicle cultures"].
- Neurotrophic/angiogenic after spinal cord injury (HSV-vector overexpression)
  [PMID:17549731 "This treatment resulted in the significant promotion of neuron and oligodendrocyte survival, angiogenesis, axonal regrowth, and functional recovery after SCI"].
- Liver injury induces hepatic Hgf mRNA [PMID:2146117 "The mRNA levels increased in the liver and spleen but not in the kidney after administration of carbon tetrachloride."].
- Mouse knockout: essential for liver and placenta development
  [PMID:7854452 "mice lacking SF/HGF fail to complete development and die in utero"].

## Interpretation

The single core activity is extracellular MET-receptor ligand / growth factor activity.
Proliferation, survival, migration, angiogenesis and morphogenesis are cellular outcomes of
MET signalling in target cells and are kept as non-core process annotations.
