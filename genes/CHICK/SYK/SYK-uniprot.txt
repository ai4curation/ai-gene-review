CC   -!- FUNCTION: Non-receptor tyrosine kinase which mediates signal
CC       transduction downstream of a variety of transmembrane receptors
CC       including classical immunoreceptors like the B-cell receptor (BCR) (By
CC       similarity). Regulates several biological processes including innate
CC       and adaptive immunity, cell adhesion, osteoclast maturation, platelet
CC       activation and vascular development (By similarity). Assembles into
CC       signaling complexes with activated receptors at the plasma membrane via
CC       interaction between its SH2 domains and the receptor tyrosine-
CC       phosphorylated ITAM domains (By similarity). The association with the
CC       receptor can also be indirect and mediated by adapter proteins
CC       containing ITAM or partial hemITAM domains (By similarity). The
CC       phosphorylation of the ITAM domains is generally mediated by SRC
CC       subfamily kinases upon engagement of the receptor (By similarity). More
CC       rarely signal transduction via SYK could be ITAM-independent (By
CC       similarity). Direct downstream effectors phosphorylated by SYK include
CC       DEPTOR, MYO1F, VAV1, PLCG1, PI-3-kinase, LCP2 and BLNK
CC       (PubMed:9697839). Initially identified as essential in B-cell receptor
CC       (BCR) signaling, it is necessary for the maturation of B-cells most
CC       probably at the pro-B to pre-B transition (By similarity). Activated
CC       upon BCR engagement, it phosphorylates and activates BLNK an adapter
CC       linking the activated BCR to downstream signaling adapters and
CC       effectors (PubMed:9697839). {ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P43405,
CC       ECO:0000250|UniProtKB:P48025, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9697839}.
CC   -!- CATALYTIC ACTIVITY:

CC         ChEBI:CHEBI:61978, ChEBI:CHEBI:456216; EC=2.7.10.2;
CC         Evidence={ECO:0000255|PROSITE-ProRule:PRU10028};
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: Autoinhibited. Intramolecular binding of the
CC       interdomains A and B (also called linker region) to parts of the
CC       catalytic domain keep the catalytic center in an inactive conformation.
CC       The phosphorylation of the interdomains or the binding of the SH2
CC       domains with dually phosphorylated ITAM domains on transmembrane
CC       proteins disrupt those intramolecular interactions allowing the kinase
CC       domain to adopt an active conformation. The phosphorylation of SYK and
CC       of the ITAM domains which is responsible for SYK activation is
CC       essentially mediated by SRC subfamily kinases, like LYN, upon
CC       transmembrane receptors engagement. Downstream signaling adapters and
CC       intermediates may mediate positive and/or negative feedback regulation
CC       (By similarity). {ECO:0000250}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cell membrane {ECO:0000305}. Cytoplasm, cytosol
CC       {ECO:0000305}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: The SH2 domains mediate the interaction of SYK with the
CC       phosphorylated ITAM domains of transmembrane proteins. {ECO:0000250}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the protein kinase superfamily. Tyr protein
CC       kinase family. SYK/ZAP-70 subfamily. {ECO:0000255|PROSITE-
