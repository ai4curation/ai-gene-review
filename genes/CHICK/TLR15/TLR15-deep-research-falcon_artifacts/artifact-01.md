| Property | Current annotation and evidence | Evidence strength / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | **TLR15 (Toll-like receptor 15)** from *Gallus gallus*; UniProt accession **A0A8V0Z0H8**. The literature describes the same chicken TLR15 protein family and domain architecture specified by UniProt. | Identity is well supported; no conflicting same-symbol protein was identified. |
| Protein class and primary function | Single-pass Toll-like pattern-recognition receptor specialized for detecting **extracellular microbial proteolytic activity** and initiating innate inflammatory signaling. It is a signaling receptor, not an enzyme or transporter. (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002) | High confidence for protease sensing; broader claims of direct recognition of lipoproteins or yeast products are less mechanistically established. |
| Domain architecture | Canonical type-I TLR organization: N-terminal signal peptide, extracellular leucine-rich-repeat (**LRR**) ectodomain, one transmembrane helix, and a cytoplasmic Toll/IL-1 receptor (**TIR**) domain. This agrees with the supplied InterPro assignments: IPR001611, IPR003591, IPR032675, IPR000157 and IPR035897. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000011) | Strong family-level and sequence-domain support; the complete full-length ectodomain has not been structurally resolved. |
| TIR-domain structure | The crystallized chicken TIR construct encompassed residues 699–868; resolved structures contained residues 704–788 and 793–850. The domain has a central five-stranded β-sheet surrounded by α-helices and an unusual BB loop, DD loop and C2 helix. Structures were solved at **1.90 Å** and **1.80 Å** resolution (PDB **7YLF** and **7YLG**). (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000044) | High-confidence atomic structural evidence for the intracellular domain. |
| Receptor oligomerization | TLR15 can form homodimers. The crystallographic TIR dimer buries about **1,110 Å² per monomer**; the C2 region contributes about **59%** and the BB region about **30%** of the interface. Ile753, Tyr779 and Phe813 contribute to dimerization; Y779D eliminated the detectable dimer peak. (pqac-00000042, pqac-00000045) | Strong structural and mutational support for a plausible signaling dimer, although the isolated TIR domain is predominantly monomeric in solution. |
| Cellular localization | Chicken TLR15 is principally a **cell-surface/plasma-membrane receptor**, positioning its LRR ectodomain to encounter extracellular pathogen proteases. This contrasts with endosomal avian TLR3, TLR7 and TLR21 and with reported intracellular localization of some reptilian TLR15 orthologs. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007, pqac-00000021) | Good experimental and review support; trafficking dynamics after cleavage remain incompletely defined. |
| Best-established activating signal | Proteases from bacteria and fungi cleave the extracellular LRR region, generating an approximately **70-kDa C-terminal fragment**. Cleavage is inhibited by PMSF, consistent with serine-protease involvement. Removal of inhibitory ectodomain elements is proposed to permit receptor self-activation. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000028) | High confidence that proteolysis activates the receptor; the precise cleavage site and complete conformational sequence remain unresolved. |
| Candidate non-protease stimuli | Yeast-associated lipoprotein or lysate and a *Mycoplasma synoviae* diacylated lipopeptide have been associated with TLR15 responses. *M. synoviae* stimulation induced TLR15, iNOS and nitric oxide, and these responses declined after TLR15-targeted siRNA. (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000026) | Functional association is supported, but direct physical binding and receptor exclusivity are not established; these stimuli may involve receptor cooperation or indirect induction. |
| Parasite-derived candidate ligand | A 2025 study identified *Eimeria tenella* aspartyl protease (**EteASP**) as a candidate TLR15-interacting ligand by co-immunoprecipitation. Recombinant EteASP activated chicken macrophages and bone-marrow-derived dendritic cells. (pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051) | Important recent finding, but the authors describe TLR15 as a **potential** receptor; contributions from other PRRs remain possible. |
| Proximal signaling | Proteolytic activation and TIR-domain dimerization support recruitment of the adaptor **MyD88**, followed by activation of **NF-κB**. The conserved, solvent-exposed BB-loop proline is structurally compatible with MyD88-dependent signaling. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000046) | MyD88 dependence is well supported; a direct atomic structure of the TLR15–MyD88 complex is unavailable. |
| Extended signaling pathways | In *E. tenella* models, TLR15 activation is coupled to **NF-κB → NLRP3 → caspase-1 → IL-1β/IL-18** signaling. EteASP also activates **MAPK** signaling. Overexpression of TLR15 enhanced, and knockdown reduced, pathway activation in DF-1 cells exposed to sporozoites. (pqac-00000050, pqac-00000052, pqac-00000053) | Strong pathway-level evidence in infection and cell models; not every downstream branch has been proven to arise from direct TLR15 activation in all cell types. |
| Downstream effectors | Documented or closely associated outputs include NF-κB activation, NLRP3 and caspase-1 activity, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18 and TNF-α production, iNOS induction, nitric oxide and reactive oxygen species. EteASP additionally increased macrophage phagocytosis and dendritic-cell CD11c and MHC-II expression. (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000050) | Cytokine specificity varies by stimulus and model; IL-6, TNF-α, ROS and antigen-presentation effects derive particularly from the candidate EteASP study. |
| Baseline and inducible tissue distribution | Expression has been reported in spleen, bursa of Fabricius, bone marrow and intestine. Infection-associated expression occurs in cecum, ileum and spleen; demonstrated responding cells include chicken embryo fibroblasts, HD11 macrophages, primary chondrocytes, macrophages and bone-marrow-derived dendritic cells. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017) | Broad tissue expression is supported, but quantitative baseline protein-level maps and single-cell localization remain limited. |
| *Eimeria tenella* response | Cecal TLR15, MyD88, NF-κB, NLRP3, caspase-1, IL-1β and IL-18 increased significantly at **4, 12 and 24 hours** after infection; sporozoite-stimulated DF-1 cells reproduced the pathway response. A later study found cecal TLR15 negatively correlated with oocyst output, whereas splenic expression correlated positively. (pqac-00000035, pqac-00000052) | Mechanistic evidence supports inflammatory involvement; expression correlations do not by themselves establish protection or causation. |
| Bacterial-pathogen responses | TLR15 is induced in the cecum during *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium infection and in fibroblasts exposed to heat-killed bacteria. It is also induced by *M. synoviae* or its diacylated lipopeptide and is upregulated in ileum and spleen during *Clostridium perfringens*-associated necrotic enteritis. (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000036, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000041) | These findings establish responsiveness, not necessarily direct recognition of a pathogen-specific molecule in every case. Salmonella and *C. perfringens* should not automatically be treated as proven direct TLR15 ligands. |
| Viral associations | TLR15 expression changes have been reported during some avian viral infections, including splenic upregulation with fowl adenovirus 8b; however, TLR15 is not established as a direct viral nucleic-acid sensor. (pqac-00000035) | Primarily expression-level association; direct viral ligand recognition and antiviral mechanism remain uncertain. |
| Evolutionary distribution | TLR15 is absent from mammals but retained broadly in birds and some reptiles. Comparative genomic work extends its origin to the common ancestor of jawed vertebrates more than **450 million years ago**, with orthologs in lungfish and holocephalan cartilaginous fishes and extensive lineage-specific loss or pseudogenization elsewhere. (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000022) | The older description “avian-specific” is operationally useful in poultry immunology but phylogenetically incomplete; current evidence supports an ancient, patchily retained vertebrate lineage. |
| Functional interpretation | The most defensible annotation is: **cell-surface innate immune receptor activated by pathogen-associated extracellular proteolysis, signaling mainly through TIR–MyD88–NF-κB and, in relevant infection contexts, MAPK and NLRP3-inflammasome pathways**. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000052) | High-confidence core annotation; exact protease specificity, cleavage site, co-receptors and stimulus-dependent pathway branching remain research gaps. |
