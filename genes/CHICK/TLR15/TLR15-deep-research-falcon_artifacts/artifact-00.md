| Pathogen | Disease / challenge | Tissue or cell model | TLR15 change or activation | Associated immune response | Functional / clinical significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Eimeria tenella* | Cecal coccidiosis | Cecum; DF-1 chicken fibroblasts exposed to sporozoites | TLR15 mRNA and protein increased significantly at 4, 12, and 24 h after infection; overexpression enhanced, whereas knockdown suppressed, downstream signaling | MyD88–NF-κB activation followed by NLRP3–caspase-1 signaling and increased IL-1β and IL-18; establishes a causal contribution to the early inflammatory response (pqac-00000052, pqac-00000053) | Strongest pathogen-specific mechanistic evidence for TLR15. Cecal TLR15 expression was also negatively correlated with oocyst output, consistent with—but not proving—a locally protective role (pqac-00000035) |
| *E. tenella* aspartyl protease (EteASP) | Parasite-derived molecular challenge relevant to coccidiosis | Chicken macrophages and bone-marrow-derived dendritic cells | EteASP was recovered as a candidate TLR15-interacting ligand by co-immunoprecipitation; recombinant EteASP also increased TLR15 transcription in dendritic cells | Activated NF-κB and MAPK; increased IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, nitric oxide, reactive oxygen species, macrophage phagocytosis, CD11c and MHC-II expression, and naïve-T-cell activation (pqac-00000050, pqac-00000051) | Identifies a possible parasite-derived TLR15 agonist and prospective immunostimulant, but direct and exclusive receptor dependence remains unproven because other pattern-recognition receptors may contribute |
| *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium | Salmonellosis / intestinal bacterial challenge | Cecum in infected chickens; chicken embryo fibroblasts exposed to heat-killed bacteria | TLR15 transcription was significantly upregulated in infected cecum and induced by heat-killed bacteria; these data show responsiveness but do not establish *Salmonella* as a direct TLR15 ligand | Consistent with cell-surface protease sensing and MyD88-dependent NF-κB inflammatory signaling; the precise TLR15-specific cytokine profile was not resolved (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000034, pqac-00000039) | Supports TLR15 as an inducible component of intestinal antibacterial defense; functional effects on bacterial clearance or disease severity remain to be demonstrated |
| *Clostridium perfringens* | Necrotic enteritis | Ileum and spleen | Infection increased TLR15 expression together with TLR2-family genes and TLR21 | Associated with TLR/NF-κB inflammatory signaling and TNF-α production, although these outputs were not isolated experimentally as TLR15-specific (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000041) | Implicates TLR15 in local intestinal and systemic responses to necrotic enteritis; whether activation is protective or contributes to damaging inflammation is unresolved |
| *Mycoplasma synoviae* and its diacylated lipopeptide | Infectious synovitis / mycoplasmosis | HD11 chicken macrophages and primary chondrocytes | Infection or diacylated-lipopeptide exposure induced TLR15; TLR15-targeting siRNA reduced associated responses | Increased inducible nitric-oxide synthase and nitric-oxide production; knockdown evidence supports a functional TLR15 contribution (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000031) | Provides receptor-dependence evidence for antimicrobial inflammatory signaling in immune cells and joint-associated chondrocytes |
| Fowl adenovirus serotype 8b | Inclusion body hepatitis–associated viral infection | Spleen | Splenic TLR15 was reported to be significantly upregulated alongside TLR2 | Indicates participation in the innate response, but no direct viral ligand, TLR15-specific signaling mechanism, or causal antiviral effect was established (pqac-00000035) | TLR15 may serve as an infection-responsive marker; current evidence is transcriptional and does not establish it as a direct viral sensor |
| Viral infections more broadly | Newcastle disease and other avian viral challenges | Multiple tissues, depending on model | Some studies report altered TLR15 expression, but available evidence does not establish direct recognition of viral nucleic acids or proteins | Antiviral associations are likely indirect or context-dependent; canonical viral sensing in chickens is more firmly assigned to receptors such as TLR3 and TLR7 | TLR15 should not presently be annotated as a primary antiviral receptor; pathogen-associated protease sensing remains its best-supported molecular function (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002) |
