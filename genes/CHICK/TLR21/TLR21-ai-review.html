Gene Description

Intracellular Toll-like receptor of birds that senses unmethylated CpG DNA and is the functional counterpart of mammalian TLR9, which is absent from avian genomes. It has a leucine-rich-repeat ectodomain, a transmembrane segment and a cytoplasmic TIR domain, resides in the endoplasmic reticulum in resting cells and signals from endolysosomal compartments, its activation being blocked by agents that prevent endosomal maturation. Expression of the receptor in cells that otherwise lack a CpG sensor confers NF-kappa-B activation in response to synthetic CpG oligodeoxynucleotides and to bacterial genomic DNA, with sequence preferences of its own, and reducing receptor expression in avian macrophages reduces their response to the same ligands, leading to induction of inflammatory mediators. Signalling from the receptor has two arms in chicken macrophages, an NF-kappa-B arm that induces inflammatory cytokines and chemokines and an interferon arm that induces interferon beta; interferon-beta transcription downstream of the receptor requires both NF-kappa-B and IRF7 and, unlike mammalian TLR9 signalling, does not require TBK1. Two of its leucine-rich repeats resemble a repeat implicated in ligand binding in mammalian TLR9, but the receptor is not a TLR9 orthologue: it belongs to the TLR21/TLR22 branch that also contains mouse TLR13 and has homologues in fish and amphibians, so its similarity to TLR9 is convergent in ligand rather than inherited. Transcripts are found across tissues.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does chicken TLR21 bind CpG DNA directly? All the evidence is functional (heterologous expression, knockdown, pharmacological blockade of endosomal maturation). A direct binding assay would license GO:0045322 unmethylated CpG binding, which is currently held even by human TLR9 only by ISS.

Q: Should GO record the relationship between TLR21 and TLR9 as functional analogy? Both receptors sense the same ligand class from different branches of the family, and annotation transfer between them would be wrong while a note of the analogy would be informative.

Q: Which UniProt entry should carry chicken TLR21 annotations? Several Ensembl models of the same gene exist in TrEMBL, this one has no RefSeq or GeneID cross-reference, and none is reviewed, so annotations risk being split across accessions.

Q: Does the avian receptor depend on UNC93B1 for trafficking, as the zebrafish and mammalian nucleic-acid-sensing receptors do? That dependence is established for zebrafish TLR21 and for mouse TLR13 but has not been tested in chicken.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure direct binding of defined CpG and GpC oligodeoxynucleotides to the purified chicken TLR21 ectodomain at endolysosomal and neutral pH, by fluorescence polarisation or surface plasmon resonance, with methylated ligands as controls.

Hypothesis: The receptor binds unmethylated CpG DNA directly and in a pH-dependent manner, which would convert the current functional annotations into a molecular binding annotation.

Experiment: Test the trafficking requirement by co-expressing chicken TLR21 with and without chicken UNC93B1 in avian cells, following localisation and CpG responsiveness, and by disrupting UNC93B1 in a chicken macrophage line.

Hypothesis: The CpG motif preferences differ systematically between receptors, which matters both for adjuvant design in poultry and for deciding how far ligand-level annotations may be shared across these receptors.

Comprehensive Research Report: TLR21 (Gallus gallus)

Key Concepts and Definitions

TLR21 (Toll-like receptor 21) is an avian pattern-recognition receptor (PRR) found in chickens (Gallus gallus), acting as the principal functional counterpart to mammalian TLR9 for the recognition of microbial DNA. According to UniProt (A0A8V0ZKW5), TLR21 possesses a leucine-rich repeat (LRR) ectodomain typical of PRRs that detect microbial patterns, and a cytoplasmic Toll/Interleukin-1 Receptor (TIR) domain needed for signal transduction (rehman2021thepotentialof pages 2-3, guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 4-7). Overwhelming recent evidence confirms that the gene nomenclature, organism, and predicted domain structure match the supplied UniProt record (guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 7-10).

Recent Developments and Latest Research (2023–2024)

The primary ligand for chicken TLR21 is unmethylated CpG DNA motifs commonly found in bacterial and some viral genomes, as well as synthetic CpG-containing oligonucleotides (ODNs). High-quality 2023–2024 studies, especially Guabiraba et al. (2024), used CRISPR-Cas9 knockout cells and dose-response experiments to show that TLR21 is necessary and sufficient for CpG-DNA recognition and downstream immune activation in chickens (guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 1-4, guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 7-10). TLR21 is localized in the endosome, consistent with its role in distinguishing foreign nucleic acids from self-DNA (mehrzad2024avianinnateand pages 6-8, magor2022evolutionofrna pages 4-5, neerukonda2020avianpatternrecognition pages 3-6).

Upon ligand binding, TLR21 triggers two major cooperating signaling arms:
- An NF-κB-dependent inflammatory pathway, which induces key cytokines (IL1B) and chemokines (CCL4).
- An IRF7-dependent antiviral pathway, which induces interferon-beta (IFNB) and the interferon-stimulated gene ISG12.2. Full activation of IFNB transcription requires both NF-κB and IRF7. Notably, this pathway operates independently of TBK1, which distinguishes it from cGAS/STING-mediated cytoplasmic DNA sensing (guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 7-10, guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 10-17, guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 17-27, guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 1-4). MyD88 is likely involved as an adaptor by analogy with TLR9 biology, but direct MyD88 loss-of-function evidence was not reported for TLR21 in the latest studies.

Current Applications and Real-World Implementations

Recent translational studies (
2023–2024) demonstrate several experimental applications for TLR21 biology in chicken health:
- Vaccine adjuvantation: In ovo administration of CpG ODNs at 5 or 10 μg/egg alongside herpesvirus-of-turkey–laryngotracheitis vaccine increased abundance of macrophages and diverse T-cell subsets without reducing hatchability or vaccine viability (tachibana2024effectsofsynthetic pages 7-8).
- Mucosal immunostimulation: Intravaginal CpG ODN administration modulated TLR21 expression, cytokine response, and beta-defensin gene expression, showing promise for preventing Salmonella Enteritidis infection in poultry reproductive tracts (suryohastari2024intravaginallycpgodnand pages 7-7).
- Whole-animal immunity: Synthetic CpG ODNs (K3) induced broad physiological and metabolic changes in vivo, including cytokine upregulation in spleen and symptom-like anorexia in male chicks, showing the systemic impact of TLR21 activation (tachibana2024effectsofsynthetic pages 7-8).

While these studies do not yet show established production-scale implementation or full field trial results, they highlight active development and proof-of-principle for TLR21-targeted immunotherapies and adjuvants in poultry.

Expert Opinions, Analysis, and Quantitative Data

Mechanistic studies (guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 1-4, guabiraba2024mechanismsoftype pages 7-10) provide strong experimental evidence, using CRISPR-generated knockout lines, dose-dependent ligand stimulation (e.g., 1–10 µg/mL CpG ODN), qPCR-normalized mRNA output, and statistical rigor (Welch-corrected t-tests, biological replicates). The endosomal localization and ligand specificity are also reinforced by authoritative 2024 review syntheses (mehrzad2024avianinnateand pages 6-8). Sample sizes for in vivo chicken studies (Suryohastari et al. 2024) were moderate (n=12/treatment group, n=3/control).

Recent studies consistently characterize TLR21 as the dominant avian sensor for microbial CpG DNA, essential for optimal innate immune activation, and a promising molecular target for next-generation poultry vaccine and immunomodulation strategies.

TLR21 (chicken, UniProt A0A8V0ZKW5) — curation notes

These notes rest on the UniProt record, the GOA rows and the cached publications;
the Falcon deep-research report arrived after the review was written and is
cross-checked at the end of this file.

Entry identity (TrEMBL, unreviewed)

A0A8V0ZKW5_CHICK is an unreviewed, Ensembl-derived entry of 1083 aa, submitter
name "Toll like receptor 21", gene name TLR21, whose only cross-reference is
Ensembl ENSGALG00010023066 / ENSGALP00010034014. Ensembl gives that gene as
"TLR21 toll like receptor 21 [Source:NCBI gene; Acc:415623]" on chromosome 11, so
the entry is the right gene even though it carries no RefSeq or GeneID link of its
own. (The project's candidate table listed another accession, A0A8V0ZYL3; both are
Ensembl models of chicken TLR21.) The entry has a TIR domain (915..1055), five
LRR_8 repeats and one predicted transmembrane segment; its PANTHER subfamily is
PTHR24365:SF545, the same subfamily as zebrafish tlr21, which is consistent with
the TLR21/TLR22/TLR13 branch rather than with any mammalian TLR9 orthology.

Caveat worth recording: this Ensembl model is longer than the cDNAs used in the
functional literature, and the functional work was done on cloned chicken TLR21
rather than on this accession; the review therefore treats the published work as
evidence about the chicken TLR21 gene, which is what GO annotations are made to.

Established biology

  • Chicken TLR21 is a receptor for unmethylated CpG DNA and the functional
    counterpart of mammalian TLR9, which birds lack:
    PMID:20498358
    PMID:20498358, and it also
    responded to bacterial genomic DNA. Independently,
    PMID:19573927
    PMID:19573927
    PMID:19573927, with the response reduced by RNA interference

  • It acts intracellularly, in the same compartments as human TLR9:
    PMID:20498358
    PMID:20498358
    PMID:20498358; and in resting cells
    PMID:19573927
    PMID:19573927.
  • Keestra notes that chTLR21 is absent from human, has homologues in fish and frog,

Annotation decisions (summary)

GOA has only five electronic rows for this entry; none is experimental, and none
mentions the CpG-DNA function.

  • GO:0002224 (IBA) modified to GO:0035682 toll-like receptor 21 signaling pathway:
    the receptor-specific term exists and two independent studies show
    NF-kappa-B-dependent signalling initiated at this receptor by CpG DNA.
  • GO:0038023 (IBA) modified to GO:0038187 pattern recognition receptor activity.
    Unmethylated CpG DNA is a pathogen-associated pattern; heterologous expression of
    the receptor confers responsiveness to it and knockdown removes it in chicken
    cells. GO:0045322 unmethylated CpG binding was considered and not proposed: no
    direct binding assay exists for the chicken receptor, and human TLR9 itself holds
    that term only by ISS/IEA, so proposing it here would be less well supported than
    the existing annotation on the mammalian receptor.
  • GO:0005886 plasma membrane (IBA) modified to endosome: the ancestral node's donors
    are surface TLRs, whereas this receptor is ER-resident when resting and functions
    in endolysosomes, its activation being blocked by inhibitors of endosomal
    maturation.
  • GO:0007165 and GO:0016020 kept as non-core.

Ligand-specificity question for the project: the transfer that would have been wrong
here — a TLR9 pathway or ligand term — is absent from GOA, and the correct
receptor-specific term (GO:0035682) exists and is now proposed. The general pattern
across the batch is that the numbered pathway terms are safe to use where the
receptor identity is unambiguous, whereas donor-specific ligand terms
(triacyl lipopeptide binding on chicken TLR15) are not.

Deep-research cross-check (2026-09-30)

Compared TLR21-deep-research-falcon.md against the finished review. The report is about the
right gene and the right accession, and it agrees with the review on everything the review
asserted: unmethylated CpG DNA as the ligand class, endosomal site of action, functional rather
than orthologous relationship to mammalian TLR9, pattern recognition receptor rather than enzyme,
and the reservation that MyD88 dependence for this receptor is inferred rather than directly
demonstrated.

  • The receptor drives an interferon-beta response, not only an NF-kappa-B one. The report
    cited a preprint; the published version was fetched as publications/PMID_37951324.md
    (Dev Comp Immunol, abstract-only in the cache, but the claims used are in the abstract).
    Chicken TLR21 PMID:37951324
    in chicken macrophages, and interferon-beta transcription downstream of it
    PMID:37951324
    TBK1. Added as NEW GO:0032728 positive regulation of interferon-beta production, and to
    core_functions.directly_involved_in; the two-arm signalling output is now in description.

The two tests in CLAUDE.md were run before proposing it. Participation: the receptor performs
the sensing step that switches the output on, rather than being a substrate of it. Comparator:
the mammalian receptors in the same role already carry this exact term from equivalent
experiments — human TLR9 and mouse Tlr9 hold GO:0032728 by IDA (PMID:16286015 and
PMID:15356140 respectively, checked in QuickGO), and human TLR3 holds it by IDA and IMP — so
the absence of an interferon term on the avian sensor is a gap in this species' annotation
rather than a convention. GO:0032728 is neither an ancestor nor a descendant of the proposed
GO:0035682, so it is not redundant with it.

Not taken up: the CpG-adjuvant and in-ovo vaccination work, the intravaginal CpG study and the
CpG-K3 whole-animal study are all applications of the ligand rather than evidence about the
gene product's own activity, and the behavioural and metabolic readouts are far downstream. The
report also repeats the (correct) point that no new primary localisation study exists, so the
endosome call still rests on the 2009-2010 papers.

description, core_functions.directly_involved_in, two new references (PMID:37951324 and the
