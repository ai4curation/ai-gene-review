| Protein/gene identity and structure | **TLR21** is a chicken (*Gallus gallus*) Toll-like receptor and the principal avian functional counterpart—not a one-to-one ortholog—of mammalian TLR9 for CpG-DNA sensing. The supplied UniProt target **A0A8V0ZKW5 / ENSGALP00010034014.1** has the canonical TLR architecture: an LRR-rich luminal ectodomain, a transmembrane segment, and a cytoplasmic TIR signaling domain. Recent literature confirms the chicken gene identity and function but does not independently map the domain boundaries of this specific accession (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000001, pqac-00000005). | Guabiraba et al., *Mechanisms of type I interferon production by chicken TLR21* (bioRxiv, September 2024; preprint first posted September 20, 2023). | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.09.20.558228) |
| Ligand/substrate and main function | TLR21 is a **pattern-recognition receptor, not an enzyme or transporter**. Its principal ligand class is microbial DNA containing **unmethylated CpG motifs**, including synthetic CpG oligodeoxynucleotides such as class-B ODN2006. Its primary function is to convert endosomal CpG-DNA recognition into inflammatory and type-I-interferon responses. In chicken HD11 macrophages, TLR21 knockout abolished CpG-induced transcription of **IFNB, ISG12.2/IFI6, CCL4, and IL1B**, while responses to cytosolic DNA remained, distinguishing TLR21 from cGAS–STING sensing (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009, pqac-00000023). | Guabiraba et al. (September 2024), CRISPR knockout and pathway-dissection study in chicken macrophage-like cells. | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.09.20.558228) |
| Subcellular localization | Current consensus places chicken TLR21 on the **endosomal membrane**, with the LRR ectodomain exposed to the endosomal lumen and the TIR domain facing the cytosol. This compartmentalization supports detection of internalized microbial DNA while limiting inappropriate self-DNA sensing. A 2024 review classifies TLR21 with the endosomal nucleic-acid sensors TLR3 and TLR7; no new 2023–2024 microscopy or fractionation study directly validating localization was identified (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017). | No direct 2023–2024 primary localization study identified; Mehrzad et al. (January 2024) provides the latest review-level synthesis. | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.61838/kman.jpsad.2.3.7) |
| Main signaling pathways | CpG–TLR21 engagement activates an **NF-κB inflammatory arm** promoting **CCL4** and **IL1B**, plus an **IRF7-associated antiviral arm** promoting **ISG12.2** and contributing to **IFNB** transcription. Full IFNB induction requires NF-κB and IRF7 but was **TBK1-independent** in HD11 cells. Canonical MyD88–IRAK4–TRAF6 signaling is strongly inferred from TLR9-like biology, but the 2024 study did not directly test MyD88 loss of function; MyD88 dependence therefore remains probable rather than conclusively demonstrated in that experiment (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021). | Guabiraba et al. (September 2024), using TLR21- and IRF7-knockout cells, NF-κB reporters, and pathway inhibitors. | [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.09.20.558228) |
| Recent experimental or translational applications | **Vaccine adjuvantation:** in ovo CpG ODN at **5 or 10 µg/egg** was tested with recombinant herpesvirus-of-turkey–laryngotracheitis vaccine; selected doses reportedly did not impair hatchability or vaccine-virus replication, while 10 µg increased macrophage, γδ T-cell, CD4 T-cell, and activated CD4⁺CD44⁺ populations. **Mucosal immunostimulation:** intravaginal CpG ODN was evaluated against *Salmonella Enteritidis*, with modulation of TLR21-associated AvBD10 and cytokine responses. **Whole-animal immunomodulation:** CpG K3 induced splenic inflammatory cytokine transcripts and physiological sickness responses. These remain experimental applications rather than established commercial TLR21-targeted products (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000025). | Gaghan et al., *Vaccines* (January 2023); Suryohastari et al., *Tropical Biomedicine* (June 2024); Tachibana et al., *Journal of Poultry Science* (December 2024). | [Gaghan et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/vaccines11020294)<br>[Suryohastari et al.](https://doi.org/10.47665/tb.41.2.002)<br>[Tachibana et al.](https://doi.org/10.2141/jpsa.2024025) |
