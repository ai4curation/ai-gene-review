Gene Description

Chicken BTK is a Tec-family non-receptor protein tyrosine kinase with PH, SH3, SH2 and catalytic domains. It couples B-cell receptor activation to PLCgamma-dependent phosphoinositide and calcium signaling and contributes to downstream NF-kappaB activation. Its conserved PH-domain recruitment mechanism supports movement from a cytoplasmic pool to membrane signaling assemblies. DT40 genetic experiments establish native avian pathway dependence, while mammalian rescue and purified-domain studies provide complementary mechanistic evidence. Nuclear, raft and non-B-cell roles transferred from mammals retain narrower comparative support.

Full BCR-panel reassessment (2026-10-26): CHICK/BTK

All 21 baseline rows were reviewed across B-cell and non-B-cell functions. Original row payloads and raw UniProt/GOA files are preserved; no new annotation was proposed. The shared acquisition ran actual Falcon/fallback attempts and normal publication retrieval; failed providers were not replaced with fabricated reports.

The molecular core is non-receptor tyrosine phosphorylation; PH-domain PIP3 recognition supplies recruitment rather than lipid synthesis. PLCgamma activation by covalent phosphorylation does not establish the independent noncovalent phospholipase-activator MF. Native BCR, mast-cell and myeloid contexts remain distinct; full PMID:34554188 supports NLRP3 substrate phosphorylation and assembly under tested priming/activation conditions, not universal positive inflammatory action. PMID:23544144 establishes negative cytokine regulation during mouse Listeria infection. PMID:31431692 supports a contextual native mouse T-cell role and prevents dismissal of the TCR IBA merely because ITK/TXK are more familiar T-cell kinases.

Native DT40 BTK loss establishes avian BCR pathway dependence; mammalian BTK complementation or purified-domain experiments keep their original construct species. Nuclear and raft locations are contextual mammalian orthology inferences, not native chicken imaging claims. The mouse NLRP3 assembly mechanism cannot be transferred solely from a shared catalytic domain and remains unresolved at the avian substrate/complex level. No evolutionary loss is asserted.
