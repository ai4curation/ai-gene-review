Gene Description

Cas12a (Cpf1) is the single-protein effector of the class 2, type V-A CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune system of Francisella tularensis subsp. novicida U112, and the founding member of the Cas12a family. It is a 1300-residue bilobed enzyme in which an alpha-helical recognition lobe and a nuclease lobe, joined by a discontinuous wedge domain, enclose a central channel that holds the CRISPR RNA (crRNA) and the crRNA-target DNA heteroduplex. The protein combines in one polypeptide the two activities that other CRISPR-Cas types distribute across several proteins. First, it matures its own guide, cleaving the precursor crRNA transcribed from the CRISPR array just upstream of a hairpin formed by the repeat, requiring no tracrRNA and no dedicated Cas6-type ribonuclease, by an intramolecular transesterification in which the 2'-hydroxyl of the upstream ribonucleotide attacks the scissile phosphate to leave a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate. Second, loaded with the mature crRNA, it searches duplex DNA for a 5' T-rich protospacer adjacent motif (TTN in this organism), unwinds the target, forms an R-loop in which the crRNA base-pairs with the target strand, and cleaves both strands to leave a staggered double-strand break with a 5-nucleotide 5' overhang. Both strands are cut in a single RuvC-type active site assembled from three dispersed sequence motifs; the protein has no HNH domain, the second nuclease domain used by Cas9. Cleavage requires a divalent cation, Mg2+ or Ca2+. The biological output is sequence-programmed destruction of invading DNA, so that a cell carrying the locus with a matching spacer resists transformation by the corresponding plasmid. Because recognition is programmed entirely by the spacer content of the array, the specificity of the enzyme is heritable and adaptive rather than intrinsic to the protein sequence.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

guide RNA-dependent nucleic acid endonuclease activity

Definition: Catalysis of the cleavage of ester linkages within a nucleic acid molecule by creating internal breaks, where the position of cleavage is determined by base pairing between the substrate and a separate guide RNA bound non-covalently by the enzyme. The guide RNA is neither substrate nor product; it programs the sequence specificity of an enzyme whose active site is otherwise sequence-non-specific, so that the specificity of the catalysis is a property of the bound guide rather than of the protein.

Justification: GO has no molecular-function term for guide-RNA-programmed target recognition or cleavage. Checked against the current GO release: no molecular-function label contains "RNA-guided", "guide RNA" or "CRISPR". The consequence is that every CRISPR effector, and every other guide-dependent nuclease, can only be annotated with a term that describes its chemistry (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238) and is therefore indistinguishable in GO from an unguided nuclease of the same chemistry. For Cas12a this is the single most important thing about the protein, since the sequence it cuts is set by the spacer content of the CRISPR array, is heritable, and changes without any change to the protein. Two children are needed, guide RNA-dependent DNA endonuclease activity (Cas9 and Cas12 families) and guide RNA-dependent RNA endonuclease activity (Cas13 and type III effectors), and the parent would also accommodate class 1 multi-subunit effectors through contributes_to. This request is deliberately scoped to the nuclease function rather than to recognition in general, so that catalytically dead guide-loaded effectors are handled by a binding term instead.

Parent term: endonuclease activity

Core Functions

Matures its own CRISPR guide, cleaving the precursor crRNA transcribed from the CRISPR array immediately upstream of the hairpin formed within the repeat, in a reaction that needs no tracrRNA, no Cas6-type ribonuclease and no other element of the locus. Catalysis is an intramolecular transesterification in which the 2'-hydroxyl of the upstream ribonucleotide attacks the scissile phosphate, leaving a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate on the 5' product; the site is separate from the DNA-cleaving RuvC site and recognition depends on the structure of the repeat hairpin rather than on the spacer sequence. This is the activity UniProt records as EC 4.6.1.22.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Are the spacers in the native Francisella novicida U112 CRISPR array derived from phage, from plasmids, or from both? All interference data for this protein come from heterologous reconstitution in E. coli with engineered spacers, so the invaders this system actually defends against in its own host are unknown.

Q: Does FnCas12a show the target-activated, substrate-non-specific trans-ssDNase reported for the Acidaminococcus, Lachnospiraceae and Moraxella orthologs, and if so does trans cleavage contribute anything to immunity in a native host, or is it an in vitro consequence of an exposed RuvC site?

Q: Is the divalent cation required for pre-crRNA processing catalytic or structural? PMID:27096362 reports cleavage in Mg2+ or Ca2+ and UniProt records a cation requirement, whereas PMID:28431230 finds the catalytic step metal-independent and attributes the cation requirement to ordering of the crRNA pseudoknot.

Q: Should GO's molecular-function hierarchy acquire a guide-RNA-dependent branch, and if so should the guide dependence be modelled as a distinct function or as an extension on an existing nuclease term?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Delete cas12a and the CRISPR array in Francisella novicida U112 itself and challenge with phages and conjugative plasmids isolated from the same environment, to establish what the native system defends against rather than what it can be programmed to cut.

Experiment: Assay purified FnCas12a for target-activated trans cleavage of a fluorophore-quencher ssDNA reporter, side by side with AsCas12a as a positive control, to settle whether the collateral activity is general to the family or ortholog-specific.

Experiment: Small-RNA sequencing of Francisella novicida U112 across growth conditions and under phage challenge, to measure whether crRNA maturation by Cas12a is constitutive or induced, and whether the 5' and 3' ends match those produced in vitro.

Experiment: Single-molecule FRET on the FnCas12a R-loop with wild-type, E1006Q and Nuc-domain mutants, to resolve directly whether target- and non-target-strand cleavage occur sequentially in one RuvC site or in two sites, which is the disagreement between PMID:27114038 and PMID:28431230.

1. What the protein is

FnCas12a is the single-protein effector of a class 2, type V-A CRISPR-Cas system. It is the
founding Cpf1 ortholog: the system was first shown to be a functional defence system in
PMID:26422227.

Architecture: bilobed, REC lobe (25–591) plus NUC lobe (662–1300) joined by a discontinuous WED
domain; the crRNA and the crRNA–target DNA heteroduplex sit in the channel between the lobes
(UniProt DOMAIN, from PMID:28431230 and PMID:28562584). The catalytic machinery is a single
RuvC-like domain, assembled from three dispersed motifs, plus an inserted Nuc domain. Unlike
Cas9 it has no HNH domain: PMID:26422227.

2. Three distinguishable activities

2a. pre-crRNA processing (endoribonuclease; EC 4.6.1.22)

Cas12a needs no tracrRNA and no Cas6: it matures its own guide.
PMID:26422227. Established as a second, separable nuclease activity by
PMID:27096362 and
PMID:27096362.

The chemistry is not hydrolysis. Swarts et al. showed the reaction is an intramolecular
transesterification that leaves a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate:
PMID:28431230. That is exactly why UniProt carries
EC 4.6.1.22 (a phosphorus–oxygen lyase, reaction RNA = a 5'-hydroxy-ribonucleotide + n nucleoside-2',3'-cyclophosphates) in addition to the DNase EC — the two ECs are two different
activities of one protein, not two descriptions of one activity.

Note a literature conflict on the metal requirement: PMID:27096362 reports cleavage "in the presence
of magnesium or calcium", and UniProt's COFACTOR note says a divalent cation (bound by the crRNA) is
required, while PMID:28431230 reports the catalytic step itself to be metal-independent. The
resolution offered in PMID:28431230 is that the cation orders the crRNA pseudoknot rather than
participating in catalysis. Both readings agree the chemistry yields a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate.

Recognition is structure- rather than sequence-driven:
PMID:27096362

2b. cis crRNA-guided cleavage of double-stranded target DNA (EC 3.1.21.1 in UniProt)

Requires a 5' T-rich PAM (TTN for FnCas12a) and makes a staggered, not blunt, break:
PMID:26422227
and PMID:26422227. Sufficiency of protein + crRNA shown in vitro:
PMID:26422227.

How many active sites? The literature disagrees, and the disagreement matters for how many
molecular functions to assert.
- Yamano et al., on the Acidaminococcus ortholog, proposed two: PMID:27114038
- Swarts et al., on FnCas12a itself and with mutagenesis, concluded one:
PMID:28431230 and
PMID:28431230
- The single-RuvC reading is now the standard one:
PMID:30936531

Curation consequence: one DNase molecular function, not one per strand.

2c. trans (collateral) ssDNA cleavage — same RuvC site

Target binding switches the RuvC site into a substrate-nonspecific ssDNase:
PMID:29449511 and
PMID:29449511. That paper is
abstract-only in the cache, and its named orthologs are not FnCas12a; the activity is therefore
asserted here for the Acidaminococcus entry (where PMID:30936531 provides full-text, As-specific
evidence) and left unasserted for FnCas12a. See §5.

3. Native biological role

Plasmid immunity, formally demonstrated by heterologous reconstitution in E. coli:
PMID:26422227. UniProt's FUNCTION block
records the same ("When this protein is expressed in E.coli it prevents plasmids homologous to the
first CRISPR spacer from transforming, formally showing it is responsible for plasmid immunity").

No phage-challenge data for FnCas12a is present in the cached literature. Anti-phage immunity is
documented for the Acidaminococcus ortholog (PMID:38261981, see that gene's notes), so for this
entry the defensible process term is the system-level GO:0099048 CRISPR-cas system rather than
GO:0051607 defense response to virus.

4. GOA adjudication

GOA holds two rows, both GO:0004530 deoxyribonuclease I activity:

evidence reference basis
EXP PMID:28431230 the dsDNase activity measured by Swarts et al.
IEA GO_REF:0000003 EC 3.1.21.1 → GO mapping

GO:0004530 is the GO term for EC 3.1.21.1, deoxyribonuclease I — a non-specific, secreted,
metazoan-type nuclease. Its definition is the generic EC reaction: "Catalysis of the endonucleolytic
cleavage of DNA to 5'-phosphodinucleotide and 5'-phosphooligonucleotide end products." Nothing
about that term says double-stranded, programmed, PAM-dependent, or staggered, and the
dinucleotide-product clause is an artefact of the DNase I reaction rather than a property of Cas12a.
The underlying biology — endonucleolytic cleavage of both strands of a duplex DNA target — is
captured exactly by GO:1990238 double-stranded DNA endonuclease activity ("Catalysis of the
hydrolysis of ester linkages within a double-stranded DNA molecule by creating internal breaks").
Both rows are therefore MODIFY → GO:1990238.

The EXP row's own reference supports the replacement, not the DNase I framing:
PMID:28431230.

The second UniProt EC, 4.6.1.22, has produced no GOA row at all — a real gap, since it is the
crRNA-maturation activity that makes type V-A "the most minimalistic of the CRISPR-Cas systems so
far described" (PMID:27096362). Added as NEW with GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity, which is
the right level: GO:0004521 is defined mechanism-agnostically ("Catalysis of the cleavage of ester
linkages within ribonucleic acid by creating internal breaks") and sits under
GO:0004519 endonuclease activity → GO:0004518 nuclease activity → GO:0140640, not under
hydrolase. Its children GO:0016891/GO:0016892 would be wrong, since both stipulate a "hydrolytic
mechanism" and the Cas12a reaction is a metal-independent transesterification.

5. What is deliberately NOT asserted

  • Genome editing / biotechnology. UniProt has a long BIOTECHNOLOGY block, and most of the
    literature on this protein is about editing rice, tobacco and human cells. None of it is a
    biological function of the gene in F. novicida; it is excluded from description and
    core_functions entirely.
  • trans-ssDNase for this ortholog. See §2c. Raised in suggested_experiments instead.
  • A guide-RNA-recognition molecular function. GO has no such term; checked against the local GO
    release, where no MF label contains "RNA-guided", "guide RNA" or "CRISPR". Raised as a
    proposed_new_terms entry and in suggested_questions.
  • GO:0003677 DNA binding / GO:0003723 RNA binding. UniProt carries both keywords and several
    DNA_BIND features. They are true but uninformative — they do not distinguish Cas12a from any
    generic nucleic-acid-binding protein, and the project guidance is to prefer a term that says what
    the protein does. The informative version of this claim is precisely the missing guide-recognition
    term.
  • GO:0051607 defense response to virus. No phage data for FnCas12a in the cached literature.

6. Open questions recorded in the review

  • Is the native F. novicida CRISPR array ever loaded with phage-derived spacers, or is this system
    predominantly anti-plasmid? The only interference data are heterologous.
  • Does FnCas12a show the target-activated trans-ssDNase that As/Lb/Mb Cas12a show, and does trans
    cleavage contribute to immunity in a native host at all, or is it only detectable in vitro?
  • The metal-dependence conflict in §2a is unresolved between PMID:27096362 and PMID:28431230.

  dedicated Cas6-type ribonuclease, by an intramolecular transesterification in which
  the 2'-hydroxyl of the upstream ribonucleotide attacks the scissile phosphate to
  leave a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate. Second, loaded with the mature crRNA, it searches
  duplex DNA for a 5' T-rich protospacer adjacent motif (TTN in this organism), unwinds
  the target, forms an R-loop in which the crRNA base-pairs with the target strand,
  and cleaves both strands to leave a staggered double-strand break with a 5-nucleotide
  5' overhang. Both strands are cut in a single RuvC-type active site assembled from
  three dispersed sequence motifs; the protein has no HNH domain, the second nuclease
  domain used by Cas9. Cleavage requires a divalent cation, Mg2+ or Ca2+. The
  biological output is sequence-programmed destruction of invading DNA, so that a cell
  carrying the locus with a matching spacer resists transformation by the corresponding
  plasmid. Because recognition is programmed entirely by the spacer content of the
  array, the specificity of the enzyme is heritable and adaptive rather than intrinsic
  to the protein sequence.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: guide RNA-dependent nucleic acid endonuclease activity
  proposed_definition: >-
    Catalysis of the cleavage of ester linkages within a nucleic acid molecule by
    creating internal breaks, where the position of cleavage is determined by base
    pairing between the substrate and a separate guide RNA bound non-covalently by the
    enzyme. The guide RNA is neither substrate nor product; it programs the sequence
    specificity of an enzyme whose active site is otherwise sequence-non-specific, so
    that the specificity of the catalysis is a property of the bound guide rather than of
    the protein.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:0004519
    label: endonuclease activity
  justification: >-
    GO has no molecular-function term for guide-RNA-programmed target recognition or
    cleavage. Checked against the current GO release: no molecular-function label
    contains "RNA-guided", "guide RNA" or "CRISPR". The consequence is that every CRISPR
    effector, and every other guide-dependent nuclease, can only be annotated with a term
    that describes its chemistry (GO:0004520, GO:0004521, GO:0004530, GO:1990238) and is
    therefore indistinguishable in GO from an unguided nuclease of the same chemistry.
    For Cas12a this is the single most important thing about the protein, since the
    sequence it cuts is set by the spacer content of the CRISPR array, is heritable, and
    changes without any change to the protein. Two children are needed, guide
    RNA-dependent DNA endonuclease activity (Cas9 and Cas12 families) and guide
    RNA-dependent RNA endonuclease activity (Cas13 and type III effectors), and the
    parent would also accommodate class 1 multi-subunit effectors through contributes_to.
    This request is deliberately scoped to the nuclease function rather than to
    recognition in general, so that catalytically dead guide-loaded effectors are handled
    by a binding term instead.
