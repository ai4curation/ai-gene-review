Gene Description

rmt2 encodes the Aspergillus fumigatus ortholog of fungal Rmt2 type IV protein arginine methyltransferases. The protein has an N-terminal ankyrin-repeat region and a C-terminal RMT2 SAM-dependent methyltransferase domain, and is inferred from characterized fungal Rmt2 enzymes to catalyze N5/delta methylation of peptidyl-arginine residues. Its best-established fungal homolog, Saccharomyces cerevisiae Rmt2, monomethylates ribosomal protein L12/Rpl12.

Core Functions

A. fumigatus rmt2 is inferred to catalyze S-adenosyl-L-methionine-dependent N5/delta methylation of peptidyl-arginine residues. It belongs to the fungal Rmt2 clade that inherited protein arginine N5-methyltransferase activity from the experimentally characterized budding-yeast enzyme, and is not in the vertebrate GAMT branch of PTHR32379.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does A. fumigatus rmt2 monomethylate Aspergillus ribosomal protein L12 at the conserved arginine residue, and are any additional protein substrates present in filamentous fungi?

Q: Does rmt2 deletion perturb A. fumigatus 60S subunit maturation, translational fidelity, stress resistance, or virulence-associated growth?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Delete or catalytically inactivate rmt2 in Af293, assay Rpl12 arginine methylation by targeted mass spectrometry, and complement the mutant with wild-type and SAM-binding-site mutant alleles.

Hypothesis: AFUA_2G09080/rmt2 is the methyltransferase for the conserved Rpl12 N5-methylarginine mark in A. fumigatus.

Experiment: Compare wild-type, rmt2-null and complemented strains in polysome profiling, stop-codon readthrough and programmed frameshifting reporters, oxidative and heat-stress growth, and in vivo infection assays.

Hypothesis: Loss of Rpl12 methylation perturbs ribosome performance in A. fumigatus.

rmt2 curation notes

Automated deep research was attempted with
just deep-research-falcon ASPFU rmt2 --fallback perplexity-lite, but Falcon was
not available in this runner and the Perplexity fallback was unavailable as well.
This review therefore uses the local UniProt record, the local PTHR32379 PAINT
slice, the S. cerevisiae RMT2 biochemical seed paper, PubMed searches, and
manual web searches for AFUA_2G09080, Q4X1R1, and A. fumigatus rmt2.

AFUA_2G09080/Q4X1R1 is the A. fumigatus member of PTHR32379. The official
PANTHER family name is GUANIDINOACETATE N-METHYLTRANSFERASE, but PAINT separates
the fungal Rmt2 node from the vertebrate GAMT node. PANTHER:PTN001285370
carries GO:0019702 protein arginine N5-methyltransferase activity from the
experimentally characterized S. cerevisiae RMT2 seed; the vertebrate
PANTHER:PTN001285367 node carries guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase
activity and creatine biosynthesis.

No direct A. fumigatus rmt2 biochemical or genetics paper turned up in PubMed
for Afu2g09080, Q4X1R1, AFUA_2G09080, or Aspergillus rmt2. Web search
on 2026-11-06 likewise found product and genome-catalog records for
AFUA_2G09080 rather than direct functional literature. The review therefore
treats protein arginine N5-methyltransferase activity as the inherited fungal
core activity and leaves the precise Aspergillus Rpl12 substrate and ribosome
phenotypes as questions.
