erg13A (Q4WBQ3) and erg13B (Q4WXT8): can the two A. fumigatus HMG-CoA synthase paralogues be distinguished?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-erg13a-paralog-division Gene: ASPFU / erg13A / Q4WBQ3 / AFUA_8G07210 / Afu8g07210 Partner paralogue: erg13B / Q4WXT8 / AFUA_3G10660 / Afu3g10660 Focus type: free text. Source: genes/ASPFU/erg13A/erg13A-ai-review.yaml

Summary

The two Aspergillus fumigatus HMG-CoA synthase paralogues cannot be distinguished functionally on current evidence, and no GO term assigning a branch of isoprenoid biosynthesis to one of them is supportable. All three axes the seed hypothesis names fail to separate them, each for a different reason. Catalytic integrity fails because both proteins are intact: every active-site and substrate-binding residue of the HMG-CoA synthase family is present in both, in register with the characterised yeast and human enzymes, so neither is a degenerate copy. Measured expression fails because exactly one published dataset resolves both loci and it moves them in the same direction. Requirement fails because it is empty: neither gene has ever been deleted, repressed or called essential, and no double perturbation has been attempted.

What this run does establish is an asymmetry of rank, supported by three independent sequence-level lines that the seed hypothesis did not name. erg13B is the principal copy and erg13A is the accessory one. The duplication is an ancestral Eurotiomycete split into two reciprocally monophyletic clades; the erg13B clade is retained in all seven Eurotiomycetes sampled while the erg13A clade is absent from A. nidulans and Talaromyces marneffei (erg13B closer to every outgroup, 6 of 6, sign test p = 0.016). erg13A sits 63,094 bp from the right end of chromosome 8 (1,833,124 bp), inside the sub-telomeric 100 kb, while erg13B sits 1,316,463 bp from the nearest end of chromosome 3 (4,079,167 bp), 20.9-fold further in. erg13B carries markedly stronger codon adaptation than erg13A (CAI 0.587 at the 96.2nd percentile against 0.454 at the 86.7th, paired codon bootstrap p = 0.0003, 1.42 background standard deviations), which survives chromosome, GC3 and gene-length confound checks.

Rank is not the same as division of labour, and the distinction governs the curation decision. Codon bias indexes how much a gene is translated on average. Chromosomal address predicts dispensability at the population level. Clade retention predicts that an erg13A-type gene is dispensable somewhere in Eurotiomycetes. None of the three says which reaction either protein performs, and the reaction is identical in both. The single experiment that would separate them is the double-perturbation design already used in this organism for the ERG11A/ERG11B pair (PMID: 17352532): delete one paralogue, place the other under a repressible promoter, and score growth, ergosterol content and triacetylfusarinine C output under iron starvation, hypoxia and azole challenge. The run supplies a falsifiable prediction for that experiment: the erg13B single deletion, not the erg13A one, should carry the phenotype.

Executive judgment

Unresolved for the division of labour. Partially supported for an asymmetry of rank: erg13B is the principal copy, erg13A the accessory copy.

The two statements are separate and both matter for curation.

Why the division-of-labour question is unresolved

Axis 1, catalytic integrity, does not separate them. Both proteins carry the complete catalytic set. erg13A has Glu86, Cys118 (the residue forming the acyl-thioester intermediate) and His259; erg13B has Glu86, Cys120 and His263. All three align one to one with each other and with S. cerevisiae ERG13 Glu127/Cys159/His296 and human HMGCS1 Glu95/Cys129/His264. All five further HMG-CoA binding residues are present and identical in both: Thr160/162, Ser209/212, Lys268/272, Asn334/340, Ser368/374. The two proteins share 61.0% identity and 75.8% similarity over 454 aligned columns, with a largest internal gap of 13 aa. Neither is truncated: 467 and 460 aa against 491 (yeast) and 520 (human HMGCS1). A degenerate copy would have answered the question outright, and that answer is excluded.

Axis 2, measured expression, does not separate them qualitatively. Exactly one published dataset resolves both loci, the oxygen-controlled fermenter transcriptome and proteome time course of Barker et al. (PMID: 22309491), and it moves both genes in the same direction under hypoxia, with a magnitude difference only. Everything else in the literature footprint is a whole-genome survey table that mentions one locus ID in passing. A permutation-controlled promoter scan added nothing: no CCAAT, GATA or sterol element difference survived FDR correction.

Axis 3, requirement, is empty. Targeted Europe PMC searches combining each Af293 locus ID with essential, deletion, knockout and null mutant return no genetic study for either gene. No single deletion, no conditional repression, no double perturbation.

Because no axis assigns a branch, the hypothesis that one paralogue serves ergosterol biosynthesis and the other siderophore biosynthesis remains untested, not refuted. It is also untestable from sequence alone.

Why the asymmetry of rank is partially supported

Three independent lines converge: evolutionary retention (p = 0.016), chromosomal position (20.9-fold difference in distance to a telomere) and codon adaptation (p = 0.0003). Each is computed from a different property of the sequence and each ranks erg13B above erg13A.

Most important caveats

Codon adaptation is an evolutionary proxy for average translation, not a measurement in any condition. A gene induced strongly in one rare condition carries no codon signature, so erg13A could still be the dominant copy under iron starvation or azole stress without contradicting the result.

The phylogenetic result rests on a single distance method without bootstrap support and on UniProt EC-2.3.3.10 annotation completeness per proteome. An unannotated third copy in a species scored as single-copy would weaken it.

The sub-telomeric inference is correlative. The coordinates are exact; the step from sub-telomeric address to accessory status rests on the published enrichment of lineage-variable genes in A. fumigatus sub-telomeres, not on a test run here.

Resources that could not be queried are named rather than substituted for: FungiDB's expression service returned HTTP 401 and needs an API key, EBI Expression Atlas returned no Aspergillus experiments, PaxDb exposed no usable API, and Ensembl REST would not resolve the AFUA_ identifiers (coordinates were taken from NCBI instead).

Finding 3: one paralogue-resolving measurement exists, and it moves both genes together

Europe PMC full-text search of the Af293 locus identifiers returns 3 papers for Afu8g07210 (erg13A) and 6 for Afu3g10660 (erg13B); only 2 papers mention both. Barker et al. 2012 (PMID: 22309491) is the single study that resolves both loci, and its full text states verbatim: "Next, erg13A (Afu8g07210) and erg13B (Afu3g10660) are significantly decreased, with erg13B more so than erg13A." Both move in the same direction under hypoxia. The difference is in magnitude only, and no per-gene fold change or significance value was recoverable from the retrieved text.

The remaining mentions are whole-genome survey tables, one locus ID at a time: erg13B in AcuM-dependent iron acquisition and gluconeogenesis (PMID: 21062375), conidial dormancy and germination (PMID: 27185182), amphotericin B exposure (PMID: 18838595) and HOG MAPK signalling (PMID: 31134001); erg13A in sub-telomere directed expression during initiation of invasive aspergillosis (PMID: 18787699). Single-locus presence in a survey table reflects which genes passed that study's filter, not biology.

Axis 3 is empty. Searches restricting either locus ID to essential, deletion, knockout or null mutant return no genetic study.

STRING-DB for Af293 (taxid 330879) gives the two proteins near-identical association neighbourhoods: Jaccard 0.935 over the top 30 partners, the only difference being each other. The coexpression subscores are numerically identical per shared partner (0.560, 0.428, 0.578), which shows the coexpression evidence is homology-transferred rather than measured in A. fumigatus. This is a demonstration of paralogue-blind annotation transfer and should not be read as biological co-regulation.

Finding 5: promoter regulatory motif content does not separate the paralogues

A negative result that closes the computationally testable part of axis 2.

The 1,500 bp upstream regions of erg13A, erg13B and eight controls (ergosterol branch hmg1, hmg2, cyp51A, cyp51B; siderophore branch sidA, sidI; housekeeping act1, tubA) were retrieved from NCBI and scanned on both strands for CCAAT boxes (CBC, HapB-HapC-HapE), HGATAR GATA elements (SreA iron regulation) and the TCACNCCAC sterol regulatory element (SREBP, SrbA). Significance was assessed against 2,000 mononucleotide shuffles per promoter, two tails, with Benjamini-Hochberg correction over all 60 tests.

Nothing survived correction: every q value was 1.0. On z-scored motif densities, erg13B lies 1.17 from the ergosterol-branch centroid and erg13A 1.69, a difference well inside the spread of the controls themselves. Two caveats limit how far the negative goes. Consensus-string scanning of 1,500 bp has low power for short degenerate motifs, since 5-mers and 6-mers occur near chance frequency in any sequence of this length, so only a large difference would have been detectable. The TCACNCCAC consensus is absent from all ten promoters, including the four ergosterol-branch genes that SrbA is known to control, which shows the motif model rather than the biology is the limiting factor here. The correct experiment is measured occupancy, meaning SrbA, HapX and SreA ChIP-seq with the two loci resolved.

Finding 6: codon adaptation separates the paralogues

The first quantified separation of the two genes on axis 2.

All RefSeq coding sequences from 7 Af293 chromosomes were retrieved from NCBI (8,456 CDS, 8,307 scored at 80 codons or more). With a chromosome-balanced self-consistent reference set (the 126 lowest-Nc genes, 18 per chromosome, 41,547 codons), erg13B reaches CAI 0.587 at the 96.2nd percentile and erg13A 0.454 at the 86.7th. The difference is +0.133, which is 1.42 background standard deviations (background mean 0.366, sd 0.094). A paired codon bootstrap over 3,000 replicates gives a 95% CI of +0.070 to +0.195 and a two-sided p = 0.0003.

Retain the molecular function term on both. GO:0004421 is the one annotation the evidence supports without qualification, and it belongs on both entries at equal strength. The reaction is identical, the residues are identical, and the identity between the two proteins (61.0%) is comfortably within the range where catalytic function transfers.

Keep the biological process term general. Do not add a branch-specific term to either paralogue. No evidence assigns ergosterol biosynthesis to one and siderophore biosynthesis to the other, and the one dataset that resolves both loci regulates them together.

Guard against any CC term implying compartmental specialisation. The companion erg10A run already excluded mitochondrial targeting for both. The siderophore branch's own compartmental step is peroxisomal and downstream (SidI, SidH, SidF; PMID: 23617799), so compartment cannot be repurposed as a division-of-labour argument for the two synthases.

Do not annotate erg13A as a reduced, degenerate or pseudo-enzyme copy. The sequence evidence is against it. The single UniProt feature difference, erg13A Ala35 against erg13B Glu35, sits at a position that is not conserved in the family (yeast Gln76).

Treat the asymmetry of rank as a note, not an annotation. The three convergent lines warrant a free-text comment on both entries. None is a basis for differential GO terms: chromosomal address predicts dispensability at the population level, codon bias predicts how much a gene is translated on average, and clade retention predicts dispensability somewhere in Eurotiomycetes. Do not infer differential regulation from promoter sequence. The 1,500 bp upstream regions give no CCAAT, GATA or sterol-element difference that survives correction, so there is no sequence-level warrant for an annotation separating the two on regulation.

Mechanistic scope

The immediate molecular function under test is a single reaction, EC 2.3.3.10, RHEA:10188: acetoacetyl-CoA plus acetyl-CoA plus H2O gives (3S)-HMG-CoA plus CoA plus a proton. It is the second committed step of the mevalonate pathway, downstream of the acetoacetyl-CoA thiolase and upstream of HMG-CoA reductase. Both Af293 proteins are predicted to perform this reaction and nothing in this run challenges that.

What the seed hypothesis asks about sits one level above the reaction: which downstream consumer draws on each protein's product. In A. fumigatus the mevalonate pathway feeds two branches. The ergosterol branch runs through Hmg1 and Hmg2 (HMG-CoA reductase) to Cyp51A and Cyp51B. The siderophore branch runs to triacetylfusarinine C through SidI (mevalonyl-CoA ligase), SidH (mevalonyl-CoA hydratase) and SidF (anhydromevalonyl-CoA transferase), all three of which are peroxisomal (PMID: 23617799). The branch point lies downstream of HMG-CoA synthase, so neither erg13 protein acts at it.

The division-of-labour hypothesis would require a mechanism that routes the product of one synthase preferentially to one branch. Three such mechanisms are conceivable and all three are either excluded or untested here. Compartmental segregation is excluded: the companion erg10A run showed neither protein is mitochondrial, and the branch-specific compartmental step (peroxisomal SidI/SidH/SidF) is downstream. Transcriptional segregation is untested in measurement and negative in sequence: the one resolving dataset co-regulates them and the promoters show no motif difference. Kinetic segregation, meaning different Km or acyl-CoA preference between the two enzymes, has never been assayed for either protein and remains the one molecular mechanism that no analysis in this run could see.

The asymmetry this run does establish operates at the level of gene usage, not reaction chemistry. Codon adaptation reflects the cumulative translational demand a gene has experienced over evolutionary time. Sub-telomeric position and clade loss reflect how dispensable a gene has been at the population and lineage level. Both describe how much a cell relies on a gene, and neither describes what the gene's product does. That distinction is why the asymmetry supports a free-text note and not a differential GO term.

Evidence base

Nine papers were reviewed. Their bearing on the question varies widely, and the distinction matters because only one of them resolves the two loci.

Transcriptomic and proteomic analyses of the Aspergillus fumigatus hypoxia response using an oxygen-controlled fermenter (PMID: 22309491) is the single study that names both locus identifiers and compares them. Its abstract reports that "A concomitant reduction in transcripts was observed with ribosome and terpenoid backbone biosynthesis, TCA cycle, amino acid metabolism and RNA degradation", which is the summary of the full-text sentence naming erg13A and erg13B as both significantly decreased. It supports the finding that measured expression does not separate the paralogues qualitatively, and it is the sole basis for that axis.

Essential gene identification and drug target prioritization in Aspergillus fumigatus (PMID: 17352532) supplies the methodological precedent. It states that "the ERG11 gene family (ERG11A and ERG11B) is essential in A. fumigatus despite neither member being essential individually", which is exactly the situation erg13A and erg13B are predicted to be in. It defines the decisive experiment and supplies no data on erg13.

Sterol Biosynthesis and Azole Tolerance Is Governed by the Opposing Actions of SrbA and the CCAAT Binding Complex (PMID: 27438727) shows that CBC inactivation causes "transcriptional derepression of multiple ergosterol biosynthetic genes including those coding for HMG-CoA-synthase, HMG-CoA-reductase and sterol C14-demethylase". It establishes that HMG-CoA synthase is under CBC control in this organism and contributes nothing to the paralogue question, because it names the enzyme and not the locus. The same limitation applies to ChIP-seq and in vivo transcriptome analyses of the Aspergillus fumigatus SREBP SrbA (PMID: 25375670), which confirms "direct regulation of ergosterol biosynthesis and iron uptake by SrbA in hypoxia" without a retrievable locus-level peak table, and to A sterol-regulatory element binding protein is required for cell polarity, hypoxia adaptation, azole drug resistance, and virulence (PMID: 18989462), which identifies 87 SrbA-dependent genes without naming either paralogue.

Fungal siderophore biosynthesis is partially localized in peroxisomes (PMID: 23617799) closes the compartmental route from the other end. It reports "peroxisomal localization of the TAFC biosynthetic enzymes SidI (mevalonyl-CoA ligase), SidH (mevalonyl-CoA hydratase) and SidF (anhydromevalonyl-CoA transferase)", and that eliminating the peroxisomal targeting signal impaired both targeting and triacetylfusarinine C biosynthesis. Because the compartment-specific step belongs to the siderophore branch and sits downstream of HMG-CoA synthase, compartment cannot carry a division-of-labour argument between the two erg13 genes.

Deciphering metabolic traits of the fungal pathogen Aspergillus fumigatus: redundancy vs. essentiality (PMID: 23264772) is a review. It states that "the plethora of genes that encode underlying effectors has hampered their in-depth analysis with respect to pathogenesis" and that recent developments "allow conditional gene expression or comprehensive targeting of gene families to cope with redundancy". It frames the method and supplies no erg13 data, so it is orientation only.

Two further papers were read for context on the pathway and are not used as evidence for any finding. Systems-wide analysis of A. fumigatus using kinetic modeling of metabolic pathways to identify putative drug targets (PMID: 37334711) nominates HMG-CoA reductase, not synthase, as a target from kinetic modelling. Exposure of Aspergillus fumigatus to Atorvastatin Leads to Altered Membrane Permeability and Induction of an Oxidative Stress Response (PMID: 32225059) shows that inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase with atorvastatin reduces ergosterol content and increases membrane permeability, which confirms the pathway matters for viability without addressing either synthase paralogue.

The remaining locus-ID mentions recovered by full-text search (PMID: 21062375, PMID: 18787699, PMID: 27185182, PMID: 18838595, PMID: 31134001) are genome-wide survey tables that each name one locus. They are listed in the evidence matrix as neutral because single-locus presence in such a table reflects that study's significance filter.

Conflicts and alternatives

Database carry-over is the largest source of apparent agreement between the paralogues, and it is not evidence. The STRING-DB result for taxid 330879 gives the two proteins top-30 partner sets that are identical except for each other (Jaccard 0.935), with coexpression subscores numerically identical per shared partner (0.560, 0.428, 0.578). Identical scores cannot arise from measurement. They arise from homology transfer. Any apparent co-regulation read off STRING for this pair is an artifact of paralogue-blind annotation, and a curator should not treat it as support for either redundancy or division of labour.

The one piece of evidence that looks like branch assignment does not survive inspection. erg13B appears in the AcuM iron-acquisition and gluconeogenesis study (PMID: 21062375) and erg13A does not. Read naively, that is a siderophore-branch link for erg13B. Read properly, it is a survey-table mention whose direction and magnitude were not recoverable, and the asymmetry is explained by which genes passed that study's filter. The same asymmetry runs the other way in PMID: 18787699, where erg13A appears and erg13B does not.

The codon-bias result could be read as supporting either redundancy or specialisation, and it supports neither. A higher CAI for erg13B is consistent with erg13B carrying most of the flux under most conditions, with erg13A as a backup. It is equally consistent with erg13A being a specialist induced only under a condition rare enough to leave no codon signature. Codon bias cannot distinguish these, because it integrates over evolutionary time and is blind to condition.

Paralogue confusion in the literature is a real risk for a curator working from gene names. The gene symbols erg13A and erg13B are used inconsistently, and most papers that discuss HMG-CoA synthase in A. fumigatus name the enzyme without a locus identifier. Any citation added to either entry should be checked for whether it resolves Afu8g07210 from Afu3g10660, and only 22309491 does.

An alternative explanation for the whole asymmetry is non-functionalisation in progress. If erg13A were drifting toward pseudogene status, it would show exactly this profile: lineage-specific loss, sub-telomeric position and relaxed codon bias. The sequence evidence argues against it, because all eight catalytic and binding residues are intact and the protein is not truncated, but relaxed selection on translation with intact catalysis is a coherent intermediate state. This alternative predicts erg13A presence/absence variation within the A. fumigatus species, which is testable against the sequenced population panels.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

  1. No measured, paralogue-resolved expression compendium was obtained. Checked: Europe PMC full text for both locus IDs, STRING, EBI Expression Atlas (returned no Aspergillus experiments), PaxDb (no usable API), FungiDB (HTTP 401, API key required). This matters because the measured part of axis 2 rests on one sentence in one paper. The codon-adaptation result predicts what a compendium would show, that erg13B carries the higher steady-state abundance across most conditions, and that prediction is the sharpest thing in this report that a dataset could confirm or kill. Resolved by: FungiDB's RNA-seq compendium queried with an API key, or re-analysis of the hypoxia RNA-seq raw data at locus resolution.

  2. Whether either locus is a direct SrbA or CBC target. Narrowed, still open. Checked: abstracts of 25375670, 27438727 and 18989462, none of which names a locus ID; and the promoter sequences themselves, which gave no CBC, GATA or SREBP difference at FDR q < 0.05. The sequence route is exhausted and should not be re-attempted. Direct SrbA binding at one promoter and not the other would be the cleanest transcriptional separation available. Resolved by: the ChIP-seq peak table from 25375670, which was not retrievable here, or fresh SrbA, HapX and SreA ChIP-seq scored at both loci.

  3. Whether either gene is essential. Checked: targeted Europe PMC searches combining each locus ID with essential, deletion, knockout and null mutant. Nothing for either. This is the gap that keeps the question unresolved, because a genetic result on axis 3 would settle it outright. Resolved by: single deletions in a clean background scored on minimal medium.

  4. Whether the two enzymes have different kinetics or acyl-CoA preference. Checked: no enzymology exists for either protein. A substrate-preference difference would be a genuine molecular division of labour invisible to every analysis run here. Resolved by: recombinant expression and steady-state kinetics on both.

  5. Whether erg13A shows presence/absence variation within A. fumigatus. The chromosomal context gap from the first iteration is resolved: erg13A is sub-telomeric, erg13B is chromosome-internal. What remains open is whether erg13A is absent or pseudogenised in any clinical or environmental isolate. Resolved by: scoring presence and intactness of AFUA_8G07210 across the sequenced A. fumigatus population panels. A positive result would turn a correlative architectural argument into a population-genetic one and would discriminate between the accessory-copy and non-functionalisation readings.

  6. Methodological limits of the computational work. The phylogeny used a single distance method with no bootstrap. The promoter scan used consensus strings, which have low power for degenerate 5-mers and 6-mers in 1,500 bp, and the SREBP consensus used was the mammalian TCACNCCAC, which is absent from all four SrbA-controlled ergosterol controls as well, so the motif model limits that test. The codon-adaptation reference set was built self-consistently from low-Nc genes, which is standard practice but circular in principle; the reference-free Nc measure was computed as a check and agrees.

Discriminating tests

Ranked by how efficiently each separates redundancy from a real division of labour.

  1. The single decisive experiment: a double perturbation on the ERG11 template. Delete one paralogue and place the other under a repressible promoter (pNiiA, as in PMID: 17352532, or a tetracycline-off system). Score growth, ergosterol content and triacetylfusarinine C output under repressing and permissive conditions. If the pair is essential while each single mutant is viable, the answer is dosage redundancy and the division-of-labour hypothesis is closed. If one single mutant carries a branch-specific defect, that settles the division.

  2. Branch-specific phenotyping of the single mutants, run under the conditions that stress each branch separately: iron starvation (siderophore demand) and voriconazole challenge plus hypoxia (ergosterol demand). A single mutant that is normal on one and defective on the other is the only clean branch assignment available short of flux work.

  3. Reciprocal promoter swap. Place the erg13A ORF under the erg13B promoter and vice versa. If each coding sequence rescues the other's loss when expressed in the right place and amount, the paralogues differ in regulation and not in protein function, and the GO molecular function must stay identical on both.

  4. Locus-resolved re-analysis of the existing SrbA ChIP-seq and hypoxia RNA-seq (PMID: 25375670, PMID: 22309491). Cheapest of the five, needs no bench work, and would either confirm co-regulation or produce the first real transcriptional separation.

  5. Steady-state kinetics on both recombinant enzymes (kcat, Km for acetoacetyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA, pH optimum). Would detect a molecular difference that no in-vivo assay above can see, and it is the only test that addresses the kinetic-segregation mechanism.

The codon-adaptation result makes tests 1 and 3 sharper by supplying a prediction to falsify. If erg13B is the principal copy, the erg13B single deletion is the one that should show a growth or sterol phenotype, and the erg13A deletion should be silent. The prediction is directional and cheap to kill. Test 3 gains a second reason to be run: codon bias is a property of the coding sequence, so a promoter swap alone will not equalise translation between the two ORFs. If a swapped erg13A ORF driven by the erg13B promoter still fails to complement, the deficit lies in the coding sequence, in translational efficiency or in protein-level properties, and not in transcription.
