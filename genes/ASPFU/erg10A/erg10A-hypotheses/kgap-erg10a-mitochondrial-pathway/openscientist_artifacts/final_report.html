Which mitochondrial pathway consumes the acetoacetyl-CoA handled by A. fumigatus Erg10A?

Gene: erg10A, Aspergillus fumigatus Af293 (NCBITaxon:330879), UniProt Q4WLA8, locus AFUA_1G13490 (A1163 orthologue AFUB_000550). Hypothesis slug: kgap-erg10a-mitochondrial-pathway. Source: genes/ASPFU/erg10A/erg10A-ai-review.yaml, selector free-text. Focus type: free_text. Three investigation iterations, 9 recorded findings, 26 papers reviewed, 3 computed figures and 4 provenance tables.

Summary

The seed hypothesis proposed to settle the pathway question by subtraction: ask what the A. fumigatus metabolic inventory permits, and in particular test whether the organism lacks a succinyl-CoA:3-oxoacid CoA-transferase (SCOT), because without one, acetoacetate cannot be activated and the mammalian ketone-body analogy collapses. The inventory does not deliver that elimination. A. fumigatus Af293 encodes three full-length family I 3-oxoacid CoA-transferases, Q4WLU8 (AFUA_6G12250, 518 aa), Q4WK09 (AFUA_1G04120, 504 aa) and Q4WEZ2 (AFUA_3G04030, 526 aa), at 56.6, 54.0 and 48.6 percent identity to human OXCT1 (P55809) by Smith-Waterman, all carrying InterPro IPR014388 and Pfam PF01144 and all with mitochondrial-type N-terminal presequences. Acetoacetate activation is metabolically permitted in this organism. The "organism lacks the enzyme" argument is unavailable and must be dropped from the review.

What the inventory does settle is the direction of acetoacetate supply and the exclusion of two candidate pathways. There is no mitochondrial-type HMG-CoA synthase: both paralogues, Erg13A (Q4WBQ3, N-terminal net charge 0) and Erg13B (Q4WXT8, net -2), sit inside the cytosolic control range, against a mitochondrial control median of +5 (Mann-Whitney U = 34.5, one-sided p = 0.0031, n = 7 versus 5). There is no credible 3-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase: the sole EC 1.1.1.30 candidate Q4WUQ4 is 22.3 percent identical to human BDH1 and is a generic short-chain dehydrogenase/reductase. So A. fumigatus has an acetoacetate module and not a mammalian ketone-body system. The acetoacetate it can make comes from leucine, through the clustered loci AFUA_5G08910 (MccA, net +7), AFUA_5G08930 (IvdA) and AFUA_5G08940 (MccB, net +8) and mitochondrial-type HMG-CoA lyase Q4X0S6 (net +3). Across 15 fungal reference proteomes, SCOT presence and the leucine catabolism module co-occur in 14 of 15 (Fisher exact two-sided p = 0.0088), and SCOT-positive proteomes carry a larger thiolase complement (median 6 versus 1, Mann-Whitney one-sided p = 0.0053).

Terminal beta-oxidation is ruled out for Erg10A on two independent grounds. Erg10A is in the ACAT clade (50.1 percent identity to human ACAT1 P24752, 47.4 percent to yeast Erg10, but 36.1 percent to ACAA2 and 35.6 percent to ACAA1), and A. fumigatus maintains four separate ACAA-clade thiolases for the 3-ketoacyl-CoA step. A controlled PTS2 analysis separates their compartments: E9RBL5 and Q4WPY9 carry PTS2 nonapeptides and are peroxisomal, while Q4WG79 (AFUA_7G04080) has no PTS2 and net charge +6, the mitochondrial pattern, making it the plausible counterpart of the A. nidulans mitochondrial ketoacyl-CoA thiolase of PMID: 18245820. The mitochondrial beta-oxidation thiolysis step therefore has a dedicated enzyme that is not Erg10A. Two questions stay open. Isoleucine catabolism cannot be separated from the acetoacetate loop, because activity on 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA, the one measurement that discriminates them, has never been run on Erg10A. Essentiality supports no pathway assignment at all, because every candidate route is dispensable in fungi.

Verdict: partially supported, with the pathway assignment unresolved and one premise of the seed hypothesis refuted.

Key findings

The organism has three genuine SCOT orthologues, which overturns the seed premise

A UniProt proteome query of UP000002530 for EC 2.8.3.5 returns exactly three entries: Q4WLU8 (518 aa), Q4WEZ2 (526 aa) and Q4WK09 (504 aa). No other EC 2.8.3.- enzyme is annotated in the proteome. Smith-Waterman local alignment against human OXCT1 (520 aa, mitochondrial SCOT), run here with BLOSUM62 and affine gaps of 11 and 1, gives 56.6, 48.6 and 54.0 percent identity respectively, and against OXCT2 (Q9BYC2) 54.4, 47.9 and 51.6 percent. The internal calibration matters: OXCT1 against OXCT2, a genuine human paralogue pair, is 74.6 percent, while the A. fumigatus beta-oxidation thiolases against their human counterpart reach only 49 to 54 percent. The three candidates therefore sit in the same identity band that other confidently assigned fungal-human orthologue pairs occupy, and their lengths match OXCT1 almost exactly, consistent with full-length orthologues rather than partial domain hits.

InterPro assignment through the EBI API returns IPR014388 (3-oxoacid CoA-transferase), IPR004165 (coenzyme A transferase family I) and Pfam PF01144 for all three, with IPR012791 and IPR012792 (3-oxoacid CoA-transferase subunits B and A) on two of them, matching human OXCT1 domain for domain. The obvious confounder is the Ach1-type acetyl-CoA hydrolase/transferase that performs propionyl-CoA detoxification in Aspergillus (PMID: 18331473). That enzyme is a separate, single, non-homologous gene in A. fumigatus, Q4WC63 (AFUA_8G05580, Pfam PF02550 and PF13336), so the SCOT assignment is not family confusion. All three SCOT candidates are unreviewed TrEMBL entries with no experimental characterisation, so this is sequence evidence that the chemistry is possible, not evidence that it happens.

Erg10A is an ACAT-clade enzyme and A. fumigatus has four ACAA-clade thiolases for beta-oxidation

Aligning all A. fumigatus PF00108 thiolases against reference enzymes gives a clean two-clade separation. Erg10A (Q4WLA8) best-hits human ACAT1 at 50.1 percent, then ACAT2 at 45.4 percent and S. cerevisiae Erg10 at 47.4 percent, but only 36.1 percent to human ACAA2 (mitochondrial beta-oxidation 3-ketoacyl-CoA thiolase) and 35.6 percent to ACAA1 (peroxisomal). Erg10B (Q4WCL5) best-hits yeast Erg10 at 61.7 percent, which fits the curated UniProt statement that the two A. fumigatus paralogues have no overlapping function and that Erg10A is not in ergosterol biosynthesis. The other four thiolases, Q4WPY9 (AFUA_4G10950), Q4WG79 (AFUA_7G04080), Q4X135 (Kat1, AFUA_2G11350) and E9RBL5 (AFUA_1G12650), all best-hit ACAA1 at 49.5, 54.4, 51.0 and 49.2 percent and reach only 31 to 35 percent to ACAT1. The gap between clades is 14 to 19 percentage points.

A. fumigatus therefore maintains a dedicated four-member ACAA-type thiolase set for the degradative 3-ketoacyl-CoA step, so Erg10A is not needed for the general terminal beta-oxidation reaction. Its substrate is the unsubstituted C4 acetoacetyl-CoA that the ACAT subfamily handles. The caveat is explicit: mammalian ACAT1 uses both acetoacetyl-CoA and 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA, so clade placement alone does not exclude isoleucine catabolism.

A controlled PTS2 test finds a second mitochondrial thiolase that is not Erg10A

N-terminal net charge alone cannot separate mitochondrial presequences from peroxisomal PTS2 nonapeptides, which are also basic. Testing both signals together across all seven A. fumigatus PF00108 thiolases, with the Gould/Petriv PTS2 consensus [RK]-[LVIQ]-x(5)-[HQ]-[LAF] scanned within residues 1 to 35, the three experimental positive controls all match: human ACAA1 at position 3 (RLQVVLGHL), yeast Pot1 at position 4 (RLQSIKDHL) and human PHYH at position 9 (RLQIVLGHL), each with low N-terminal net charge of +1 to +2. The mitochondrial controls ACAT1 (+5) and ACAA2 (+1) and the cytosolic controls ACAT2 (-1) and yeast Erg10 (+1) produce no PTS2 match, so the test does not fire spuriously on either control class.

Applied to A. fumigatus, E9RBL5 matches PTS2 at position 5 (RLSSLLNHL, net 0) and Q4WPY9 at position 7 (RLSQVSSHF, net +1), placing both in peroxisomes. Q4WG79 has no PTS2 and net charge +6, the mitochondrial pattern, and is in the ACAA clade (54.4 percent to ACAA1, 34.8 percent to ACAT1). Erg10A has no PTS2 and net +7. Erg10B is at net +1, the cytosolic pattern. Q4X135 (net +2) and the SCPx-type Q4WNN6 (net +3) are unresolved by this test. The reading is that A. fumigatus has at least two mitochondrial thiolases with different clade assignments, and that Q4WG79 is the plausible A. fumigatus counterpart of the A. nidulans mitochondrial ketoacyl-CoA thiolase whose mutation blocks growth on short-chain but not long-chain fatty acids (PMID: 18245820).

Both HMG-CoA synthases look cytosolic, so mitochondrial acetoacetate comes from leucine

The presequence heuristic was calibrated on seven experimentally mitochondrial-matrix proteins with cleavable presequences (human ACAT1, OXCT1, HMGCS2, HMGCL, BDH1; yeast Cit1, Aco1) and five cytosolic or peroxisomal controls (human ACAT2, HMGCS1; yeast Erg10, Cit2, Pgk1). N-terminal net charge over the first 40 residues separates them: mitochondrial median +5 (range +3 to +7), cytosolic median 0 (range -5 to +3), Mann-Whitney U = 34.5, one-sided p = 0.0031.

Scored against that calibration, Erg10A is +5, which agrees with its experimentally demonstrated mitochondrial localisation and its UniProt-annotated 34-residue transit peptide. Erg10B is +2. All three SCOT candidates are +6. The leucine catabolism carboxylase subunits MccA (Q4WUL8) and MccB (Q4WUM1) are +7 and +8. HMG-CoA lyase Q4X0S6 is +3 and the second lyase Q4WAL4 is 0. The two HMG-CoA synthases, Erg13A (Q4WBQ3) at 0 and Erg13B (Q4WXT8) at -2, both fall in the cytosolic control range. The three leucine-catabolism genes AFUA_5G08910, AFUA_5G08930 and AFUA_5G08940 are consecutive loci, a physical gene cluster.

The mammalian liver ketogenic route, which runs acetoacetyl-CoA plus acetyl-CoA to HMG-CoA to acetoacetate driven by HMGCS2, is therefore not available in A. fumigatus. What is available is leucine catabolism into mitochondrial HMG-CoA, with HMG-CoA lyase releasing free acetoacetate plus acetyl-CoA, three mitochondrial-type SCOTs to re-activate it, and Erg10A to perform the final thiolysis. This is the single pathway that the A. fumigatus inventory most specifically permits.

The fungal CoA-transferase travels with leucine catabolism across 15 proteomes

Counting genes per family in 15 fungal UniProt reference proteomes gives the distribution below. SCOT presence (EC 2.8.3.5) and the leucine catabolism module (HMG-CoA lyase EC 4.1.3.4 together with 3-methylcrotonyl-CoA carboxylase EC 6.4.1.4) agree in 14 of 15 proteomes: 11 double-positive, 3 double-negative, 1 discordant (C. albicans, SCOT-positive and leucine-module-negative). Fisher exact two-sided p = 0.0088 with an infinite odds ratio. The three double-negatives are S. cerevisiae (0, 0, 0), S. pombe (0, 0, 0) and U. maydis (0, 0, 0). The PF00108 thiolase complement is larger in SCOT-positive proteomes, median 6 (n = 12) against median 1 (n = 3), Mann-Whitney U = 36.0, one-sided p = 0.0053.

The acetoacetate module in fungi is tied to leucine catabolism, which is the one fungal pathway that releases free acetoacetate. Species that dropped leucine catabolism dropped the CoA-transferase, and those same species retain a single cytosolic ACAT thiolase with no mitochondrial paralogue. S. pombe survives on one thiolase in total. EC annotation in these proteomes is largely electronic, and U. maydis returning zero PF00108 thiolases is biologically implausible, so the three negatives are partly undercounted and the p values should be read as support for a trend rather than as exact.

Neither the kinetics nor the active-site sequence distinguishes acetoacetyl-CoA from 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA

The seed hypothesis asks whether the measured substrate preference separates acetoacetyl-CoA from the 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA of isoleucine catabolism. It does not. UniProt's biophysicochemical properties for Q4WLA8, sourced from PMID: 32005728, list Km values for three substrates only: CoA 26 uM, acetoacetyl-CoA 43 uM, acetyl-CoA 232 uM. No 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA measurement exists. Haapalainen et al. (PMID: 17371050) established that human T2/ACAT1 degrades acetoacetyl-CoA and 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA with similar catalytic efficiencies while other structurally characterised thiolases cannot use 2-methylated compounds, so this is the discriminating assay, and it has not been run on Erg10A.

Mapping all 14 UniProt-annotated functional residues of human ACAT1 onto the fungal thiolases by Smith-Waterman residue projection gives Erg10A 9 of 14 (64 percent), Erg10B 9 of 14, cytosolic yeast Erg10 12 of 14 (86 percent), human ACAT2 6 of 14 and human ACAA2 6 of 14. The potassium-site tyrosine Tyr219 is conserved in Erg10A (Tyr219), Erg10B (Tyr187) and yeast Erg10 (Tyr186) and replaced by Gln in both ACAT2 and ACAA2. The annotated residue set, including the K+ site, is a clade-wide feature that identifies the chemistry and not the pathway, and the cytosolic yeast enzyme scores higher than Erg10A on it. The 2-methyl capability that makes mammalian ACAT1 the isoleucine-catabolism thiolase depends on the Phe325-Pro326 dipeptide near the catalytic cavity (PMID: 17371050) and not on the annotated set, so residue mapping has no discriminating power here.

Essentiality is not explained by any candidate pathway

Loss of mitochondrial beta-oxidation function in Aspergillus gives a conditional carbon-source phenotype and not lethality. The A. nidulans deltaechA mutant is viable and is required for growth on short-chain fatty acids and on isoleucine and valine (PMID: 15554960). A mutation affecting a mitochondrial ketoacyl-CoA thiolase, with disruption of a mitochondrial hydroxy-acyl-CoA dehydrogenase, prevented growth on short-chain but not long-chain fatty acids, again without loss of viability (PMID: 18245820). Branched-chain amino acid catabolism is active in A. fumigatus and generates propionyl-CoA from valine, isoleucine and methionine (PMID: 16008561), and it is a dispensable catabolic convenience. Against this, both knockout and inducible-promoter strategies show that Aferg10A is essential for survival (PMID: 32005728).

Lethality therefore cannot be used as evidence for any of the candidate pathway assignments, because all of them are dispensable in laboratory media and the organism keeps four ACAA-clade thiolases available for beta-oxidation. The essentiality is better explained by a requirement that is not pathway-specific, for example mitochondrial acetyl-CoA and free CoA homeostasis, or by a function other than the thiolase reaction. The reported phenotype of reduced expression, severe morphological defects and increased susceptibility to oxidative and cell wall stresses (PMID: 32005728), is as consistent with a general mitochondrial metabolic requirement as with loss of a specific catabolic route.

Mechanistic model

The inventory supports a closed intramitochondrial loop in which leucine, and possibly isoleucine, feeds carbon back into the mitochondrial acetyl-CoA pool, with Erg10A performing the final thiolytic step. The link from Erg10A to the acetoacetate branch is drawn dashed, because it rests on compartment and clade inference rather than on flux data. The isoleucine branch is drawn dashed for the same reason, with the extra problem that the discriminating kinetic measurement has never been made.

Mechanistic scope

Direct activity, established experimentally: thiolytic cleavage of acetoacetyl-CoA into two acetyl-CoA, and the reverse Claisen condensation, by a mitochondrially localised enzyme. The Km for acetoacetyl-CoA (43 uM) against acetyl-CoA (232 uM) is consistent with the degradative direction being the physiologically favoured one, which is what an ACAT-clade enzyme in a mitochondrion would be expected to do.

Inference and not measurement: the identity of the metabolite flux that feeds the reaction. The inventory marks out what is possible, namely acetoacetate from leucine through HMG-CoA lyase and re-activation by one of three SCOTs, or 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA from isoleucine, without showing which actually carries flux in a growing hypha.

Downstream phenotype, which must be kept separate from the above: the lethality of the deletion, with severe morphological defects and increased susceptibility to oxidative and cell wall stresses in the knockdown (PMID: 32005728). Mapping that phenotype onto the thiolase reaction requires an assumption that no candidate pathway supports, because every candidate is dispensable in fungi. Lethality should be treated as a separate claim about mitochondrial acetyl-CoA or free CoA balance, or about a non-catalytic requirement, and should not be used as evidence for any particular pathway assignment.

Conflicts and alternatives

The UniProt entry contradicts itself. The PATHWAY line places Erg10A at mevalonate step 1 while the FUNCTION comment on the same entry says the protein is not involved in ergosterol biosynthesis, and both cite the same paper. STRING reinforces the error, since its highest-scoring partners are the two HMG-CoA synthases at combined score 0.987 with an experimental score of 0.000, which is annotation carry-over from exactly that PATHWAY assignment. A curator reading STRING as independent support would be reading the UniProt PATHWAY line twice.

The branch assignment PTHR18919:SF156 (ACAT1-like) is suggestive and nothing more. Human ACAT1 works in ketone body utilisation and in isoleucine catabolism at the same time (PMID: 17371050), so inheriting the branch inherits an ambiguity. The PANTHER API returned no mapped genes for any of the six accessions queried here, so the branch assignment was taken as supplied in the seed context and was not independently confirmed.

Two competing hypotheses survive. Erg10A could be primarily an isoleucine-pathway 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA thiolase with acetoacetyl-CoA activity as a side reaction, and nothing measured so far excludes it. Separately, Erg10A could be essential for a reason unrelated to its thiolase chemistry, with the enzymatic activity being real but not the essential property.

One alternative was considered and set aside. A mitochondrial polyketide or siderophore route drawing on acetoacetyl-CoA would fit the biosynthetic direction of the reaction. A. fumigatus siderophore biosynthesis is linked to mevalonate through SidI and SidH (PMID: 22106303), but that route runs on mevalonate and acyl-CoA intermediates in other compartments, and no mitochondrial acetoacetyl-CoA-consuming synthase appeared in the inventory. This is a weak negative, because secondary-metabolite enzymes are poorly annotated in this genome.

A paralog-confusion risk was checked and cleared. The three PF01144 SCOTs are not the Ach1-type acetyl-CoA hydrolase/transferase used for propionyl-CoA detoxification in Aspergillus (PMID: 18331473), which is the separate non-homologous gene Q4WC63 (PF02550 and PF13336).

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Every localisation claim except Erg10A's own rests on an N-terminal charge and amphipathicity heuristic. The heuristic was calibrated on experimental controls and gives a real separation (mitochondrial median +5 against cytosolic 0, p = 0.0031), and the PTS2 confounder was controlled with three experimentally peroxisomal positives, but it is still a prediction and not an import assay. A mitochondrial Erg13A would reopen the ketogenesis option that the analysis currently closes.

No substrate panel beyond acetoacetyl-CoA, acetyl-CoA and CoA has been run on Erg10A. Without 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA, the isoleucine route can be neither confirmed nor excluded, and this single missing number is what keeps the verdict unresolved.

No A. fumigatus SCOT has been assayed. Three orthologues is a strong sequence signal and no signal at all about flux. All three are unreviewed TrEMBL entries.

The basis of essentiality is untested. There is no conditional allele reported beyond the inducible promoter, no catalytically dead point mutant, and no metabolite profile of a depleted strain, so the distinction between a catalytic and a non-catalytic requirement cannot be made from published data.

Q4WG79, the predicted mitochondrial beta-oxidation thiolase, has not been localised or deleted, so the division of labour proposed here between it and Erg10A is inferred from clade membership plus a motif scan.

The comparative genomics result rests on electronic EC annotation. U. maydis returning zero PF00108 thiolases is biologically implausible and shows that the SCOT-negative proteomes are partly undercounted, so the Fisher and Mann-Whitney p values support the trend without being exact.

PANTHER could not be queried programmatically, with all six accession requests failing, so the PTHR18919:SF156 assignment in the seed context was not independently verified here.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

1. Measure kcat/Km of purified Erg10A on 2-methylacetoacetyl-CoA against acetoacetyl-CoA. This is the single measurement that separates the two surviving routes, and it is the established discriminator for human T2 (PMID: 17371050). A clear preference for the 2-methyl substrate assigns isoleucine catabolism. Near-indifference, or a strong preference for the unbranched substrate, assigns the acetoacetate loop. The existing 6L2C coordinates, which have CoA bound, would help interpret the result against the Phe325-Pro326 position.

2. Build a catalytically dead knock-in at the native locus, replacing the active-site cysteine with serine or alanine. If the point mutant is viable while the deletion is lethal, essentiality is non-catalytic, and the pathway question becomes separable from the essentiality question.

3. Trace 13C-labelled leucine and isoleucine into mitochondrial acetyl-CoA, with and without Erg10A depletion. This gives direct flux assignment and simultaneously tests whether the three SCOTs carry acetoacetate.

4. Delete the three SCOTs singly and in combination, testing growth on leucine as sole carbon or nitrogen source. If the leucine-to-acetoacetate loop operates, the triple mutant should fail where the wild type grows.

5. Localise Q4WG79, Erg13A and Erg13B by tagging, to replace the charge heuristic with data at the three points where the heuristic currently does the most work.
