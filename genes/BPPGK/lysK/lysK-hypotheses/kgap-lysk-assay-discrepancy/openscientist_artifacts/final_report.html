LysK (Q6Y7T6, Kayvirus kay): why the CHAP-only truncation lyses cells in three assays but gives no MIC

Hypothesis slug: kgap-lysk-assay-discrepancy. Source: genes/BPPGK/lysK/lysK-ai-review.yaml, free-text focus. Target: UniProt Q6Y7T6, endolysin LysK, Kayvirus kay (NCBITaxon:221915), 495 residues.

Summary

Verdict: partially supported. Assay format does explain the discrepancy, and the seed hypothesis is right that the answer lies in what the formats demand. The two specific mechanisms it names are not the answer. Calcium starvation of the MIC medium is refuted. The stability leg cannot be resolved from the published record, and the specific version of it in which the truncation misfolds is excluded by the crystal structure. The property the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) assay demands and the other three do not is the endpoint itself.

Zymogram, plate lysis and turbidity all score a zone or a rate. Any non-zero lysis rate is detectable, and a 10-fold weaker enzyme reads as 10-fold slower, which stays inside the measured dynamic range of the assay (in the homolog PlyTW, 0.21 against 0.024 dOD600/min, both reported as values). The MIC scores a threshold: the specific lysis rate has to exceed the growth rate of a dividing population for 18-24 h, or the well goes turbid and the result is written down as no detectable activity. Integrating dN/dt = mu·N - k·E(t)·N with mu = 1.386 /h, a 5x10^5 CFU/mL inoculum and a 10^7 CFU/mL visible threshold, the MIC demands an initial specific lysis rate of at least 1.24 /h from a non-decaying enzyme, rising to 2.60 /h at an 8 h functional half-life and 8.58 /h at 2 h. Potency loss and stability loss therefore multiply in this format and in no other. At the 8.6-10 fold penalty measured for the homologous SH3b cell wall binding domain, the CHAP-only truncation needs 8.6-10.0x the full-length MIC with no stability loss at all, which a 2-fold dilution series topping at 8x cannot report.

The MIC-negative result carries almost no information about whether the CHAP domain is catalytically competent, and it should not be curated as though it did. Three independent lines of primary evidence agree that the CHAP-only construct lyses live staphylococci: it clears live clinical MRSA isolates in a turbidity assay at 3,690 U/mg (PMID: 19047377), the same laboratory that reported the LysK MIC result concluded in the homolog that "the CHAP domain alone is necessary and sufficient for lysis of live S. aureus" (PMID: 25790497), and the isolated domain crystallises as a complete papain-like fold with an assembled catalytic triad (PMID: 25064136).

The most important caveat is retrieval. The full methods of Becker et al. 2009 (PMID: 19493008), the source of the discrepancy, are paywalled. Unpaywall returns is_oa: false, the article is absent from PMC, and Europe PMC fullTextXML returns HTTP 500. The MIC medium, the top concentration tested, the inoculum and the incubation time are not stated in the abstract. The published methods are not reported in enough detail to separate potency from stability for LysK itself. The protocol lineage was reconstructed from the same laboratory's open-access papers.

Executive judgment

The seed hypothesis proposes that assay format, specifically stability or accessibility, explains why the LysK CHAP-only truncation lyses Staphylococcus aureus in zymogram, plate-lysis and turbidity assays yet gives no MIC, while the CHAP-SH3b fusion works in all four (PMID: 19493008). Leg by leg:

Key findings

What each of the four assay formats demands

Methods text was retrieved from the primary papers wherever the source was open access. Becker 2009 itself is paywalled, so the MIC row is reconstructed from the same laboratory's later open papers, which name the protocol explicitly: "a classical microdilution broth method for determination of the MIC was used (Jones et al. 1985) with modifications as described previously (Becker et al. 2009)" (PMID: 23413880, body text).

Turbidity reduction Mid-log cells frozen in saline lysis buffer with 30% glycerol, thawed, washed 3x Non-growing, de-energized by freeze-thaw and washing Saline lysis buffer; cations added only when deliberately tested; enzyme equilibrated 10 min 5-10 min, room temperature, OD600 every 20 s, maximum rate scored PMID: 25790497; PMID: 23413880

Two things separate the MIC from the zymogram and the turbidity assay: live, dividing, membrane-energized cells in nutrient-rich broth, and an 18-24 h window during which the enzyme must hold a growing population at zero. Plate lysis shares the overnight incubation and the live cells, but it delivers up to 100 uM enzyme locally onto a dense dried lawn and scores graded thinning, so it does not impose the same stringency.

F1. Losing SH3b costs about 10-fold, which a short 2-fold dilution series cannot report

In the closest quantified homolog, Twort endolysin PlyTW, which has the same CHAP-amidase2-SH3b_5 architecture as LysK, deleting the SH3b cell wall binding domain reduced turbidity-reduction activity 8.6-fold (0.21 +/- 0.02 to 0.024 +/- 0.006 dOD600/min at 0.5 uM, n >= 6) and plate-lysis activity 10-fold (lawn clearing at 10 pmol per spot for full length against 100 pmol per spot for CHAP alone) (PMID: 25790497, methods and Fig. 1D retrieved from PMC4811206). The authors state it directly: "Loss of the CBD results in ∼10X reduction of enzymatic activity in both turbidity reduction and plate lysis assays compared to the full length protein."

The censoring arithmetic computed in this run is simple. A 2-fold broth microdilution series whose top concentration is 2x, 4x or 8x the full-length MIC cannot return any value for a variant carrying a 9-10 fold penalty. Only a series reaching at least 16x the full-length MIC would resolve it. The plate-lysis spot used by the same laboratory delivers up to 1000 pmol in 10 uL, which is 100 uM local enzyme, 10 to 100 times above the concentrations a broth MIC typically tests.

F2. The MIC is the only threshold-scored format, and potency and stability multiply in it

This is the central result. The three short assays score a rate or a zone, so the readout is monotonic in the lysis rate and any non-zero activity is detectable. The MIC scores a binary outcome after the enzyme has competed against bacterial growth for most of a day.

Writing the competition explicitly, with dN/dt = mu·N - k·E(t)·N and first-order enzyme decay E(t) = E0·2^(-t/t_half), integration gives ln N(T) = ln N0 + mu·T - K·(t_half/ln2)·(1 - 2^(-T/t_half)) where K = k·E0 is the initial specific lysis rate. With mu = 1.386 /h (30 min doubling in rich broth), T = 20 h, N0 = 5x10^5 CFU/mL and a visible-growth threshold of 10^7 CFU/mL, unchecked growth over 20 h is 27.7 e-folds and the enzyme is permitted only 3.0. The required initial lysis rate is then:

Expressing the CHAP-only requirement as fold of the full-length LysK MIC, with the full-length half-life fixed at 8 h: at the 8.6-10 fold potency penalty and no stability loss at all, CHAP-only needs 8.6-10.0x; with a 4 h half-life, 14.6-17.0x; with a 2 h half-life, 28.3-33.0x. A pure stability loss with no potency loss needs 3.3x at a 2 h half-life.

The three surviving explanations are therefore factors that multiply in this one format, and the measured potency penalty alone already clears a standard dilution ceiling. Any stability loss and any growth-dependent tolerance push the construct further out of range.

F3. Calcium starvation is refuted, twice over

Computed from the CLSI cation-adjustment specification for Mueller-Hinton broth (Ca2+ 20-25 mg/L, Mg2+ 10-12.5 mg/L): Ca2+ = 0.499-0.624 mM and Mg2+ = 0.411-0.514 mM. The primary cation titration for PlyTW started at 0.5 mM Ca2+ and found Ca2+ increased activity 4-fold (full length), 6.5-fold (CHAP only), 8.2-fold and 2.8-fold (internal deletions), with Mg2+ and Mn2+ having no significant effect, and EDTA or Zn2+ inhibiting all constructs (PMID: 25790497, Fig. 4 and text from PMC4811206). Mueller-Hinton broth contains no added chelator. The MIC medium therefore sits at or above the activating calcium concentration.

The structure adds an independent argument. The only two experimental structures mapping to Q6Y7T6 are PDB 4CSH (native, 1.79 A) and 4CT3 (methylmercury derivative, 1.69 A), retrieved through the PDBe SIFTS best_structures API, and both cover exactly residues 1-165. Computed from the 4CSH coordinates, the Ca2+ is held by seven protein oxygen ligands, identical in all four chains: Asp45 OD1 (2.29-2.41 A), Asp47 OD1 (2.32-2.39 A), Tyr49 main-chain O (2.27-2.40 A), His51 main-chain O (2.42-2.46 A) and bidentate Asp56 OD1/OD2 (2.37-2.76 A), plus one ordered water. All four ions refine at occupancy 1.00 with a mean B-factor of 10.8 A^2 against a protein mean of 17.4 A^2 (ratio 0.62), and each is enclosed by 98 protein heavy atoms within 8 A. The deposited crystallisation liquor contains neither calcium nor zinc: 25 mM Tris-HCl, 22% (w/v) PEG 8000, 0.1 M MES-NaOH pH 6.5. That ion was carried through expression in E. coli, purification and crystallisation. A site of that affinity is not emptied by a broth supplying 0.5-0.6 mM Ca2+, and the CHAP-SH3b fusion carries the same site, so the effect could not be construct-selective in any case.

The computed coordination sphere reproduces the residue list the authors published, which is the correctness check on that code path: "CHAP(K) contains an additional lobe in its N-terminal domain, with a structural calcium ion, coordinated by residues Asp45, Asp47, Tyr49, H..." (PMID: 25064136), and "The presence of calcium and zinc ions in the structure was confirmed by X-ray fluorescence emission spectroscopy."

The zinc behaves oppositely and should not be read as a cofactor. It has a single protein ligand, Cys54 SG at 2.15-2.26 A, with 2-3 waters, refines at occupancy 0.67 with a B-factor of 21.9 A^2 (1.26x the protein mean) and sits more exposed (84 heavy atoms within 8 A). One thiolate bond to the catalytic nucleophile at sub-stoichiometric occupancy is the geometry of an inhibitor. Zn2+ inhibits every PlyTW construct tested.

F4. The isolated CHAP domain folds autonomously, so misfolding is excluded

In 4CSH there are 4 independent copies in the asymmetric unit, 656 modelled residues in total, and chain A is modelled continuously from residue 2 to 165 with zero internal gaps. Mean B-factor for chain A is 15.4 A^2 (whole entry 17.4 A^2), the last 10 modelled residues average 18.0 A^2, and the catalytic triad Cys54/His117/Glu134 averages 9.7 A^2. The engineered C-terminus shows no disorder. Neither structure contains the amidase or SH3b domain, so SH3b is not required for CHAP to fold. The authors reach the same conclusion from the experiment: "The structure of CHAP(K) revealed a papain-like topology with a hydrophobic cleft, where the catalytic triad is located" (PMID: 25064136).

This does not measure thermodynamic or kinetic stability. Crystal order at 1.79 A reports on the ordered fraction of the sample, not on the fraction that aggregates in broth over 18-24 h. The stability leg stays untested rather than refuted.

F5. LysK inactivates by aggregation on the MIC timescale, but the discriminating comparison has never been made

Filatova et al. 2010 report that below 40 C LysK stability depends on concentration, pH and additives; that "Results of electrophoresis under native and denaturing conditions as well as sedimentation analysis strongly suggest that aggregation is behind LysK inactivation"; that "Decrease in the enzyme concentration, as well as addition of low molecular mass polyols (glycerol, sorbitol, sucrose, trehalose) and Ca(2+) cations resulted in an enhanced (more than 100 times) stability of LysK"; and that a sharp stability decline occurs over the narrow 40-42 C range with circular-dichroism-detectable secondary structure change (PMID: 20144680).

A MIC incubates dilute enzyme for 18-24 h at 35-37 C with no stabilising additive, which is exactly that regime. The zymogram (~1 h in water) and the turbidity assay (5-10 min, enzyme pre-equilibrated 10 min) are not. No differential scanning fluorimetry, circular dichroism melting curve, or aggregation measurement comparing the isolated CHAP domain with the CHAP-SH3b fusion was found in any retrieved primary paper. Two PubMed searches for staphylococcal protease inactivation of exogenous cell wall hydrolases returned no papers. The half-life axis of the threshold model is therefore a sensitivity analysis over a parameter nobody has measured for either construct.

The cationic bulk of LysK sits in the Lys-rich linker and the amidase-2 region (+10.9 over 209 residues), not in the cell wall binding domain. Appending SH3b to CHAP raises net charge from +2.9 to +4.6 and leaves charge density unchanged at 0.16 per kDa. SH3b is also the least hydrophilic segment and the poorest in basic residues. Non-specific attraction to the anionic wall teichoic acid surface cannot be how SH3b improves CHAP activity. The 8.6-10 fold benefit has to come from specific, saturable peptidoglycan binding, which is what the co-sedimentation data show directly: the SH3b domain of the Kayvirus endolysin LysF1 "was able to bind to three types of purified staphylococcal peptidoglycan 11.2, 11.3, and 11.8 that differ in their peptide bridge, but also to the peptidoglycan type 11.5 of Streptococcus uberis" (PMID: 28852930).

F7. Ionic strength is not the MIC-specific discriminator

Debye screening lengths were computed as kappa^-1 = 0.304 nm / sqrt(I) at 25 C, with I = 0.5·sum(c·z^2) over the stated composition of each assay buffer.

CHAP-only lyses S. aureus in the saline lysis buffer, where the Debye length is 0.77 nm, shorter than the 0.99 nm of the MIC broth. Electrostatic screening in the MIC medium is milder than in a condition where the truncation plainly works, so screening cannot be what the MIC demands and the others do not. The ionic-strength argument survives only in its narrow form, separating Horgan's acetate from Donovan's saline lysis buffer by 1.76x, where it reconciles the two conflicting primary datasets.

Mechanistic scope

The immediate molecular activity under test is hydrolysis of the D-Ala-Gly bond of the staphylococcal peptidoglycan cross-bridge by the CHAP domain, using a Cys54/His117/Glu134 triad with a structural Ca2+ site and an adventitious active-site Zn2+ (PMID: 25064136), together with hydrolysis of the MurNAc-L-Ala bond by the amidase-2 domain (PMID: 19493008). The SH3b domain's immediate activity is non-catalytic binding to peptidoglycan (PMID: 28852930), which raises the local enzyme concentration at the substrate.

Separable from the above, and downstream of it: osmotic lysis of the cell, the fall in culture OD600, the clearing of a lawn, and the failure of a culture to reach visible turbidity in 18-24 h. Only the last depends on the enzyme holding a dividing population at zero growth for most of a day against active cell wall synthesis and repair. That is a population-level outcome and a property of the assay endpoint, not a property of the catalytic domain. Curating the MIC result as a molecular-function observation conflates the two.

Conflicts and alternatives

  1. Direct conflict between primary papers. Horgan et al. (PMID: 19047377) measured CHAP-only C165 at 3,690 U/mg against 621 U/mg for full-length LysK on live MRSA, a ~2-fold per-mole advantage for the truncation, in 50 mM sodium acetate pH 6.5. Becker et al. and the PlyTW work find a 9-10 fold penalty in 150 mM NaCl with 10 mM Tris pH 7.5. The most economical reconciliation is ionic strength, and the computed Debye lengths support it for this contrast (1.36 nm against 0.77 nm, a 1.76x difference): a wall-binding module earns its keep where electrostatic enzyme-wall association is screened and is more dispensable in low-salt acetate. The same calculation removes this argument from the MIC question, since the MIC broth sits at 0.99 nm, between the two. A confounder is tag status: C165 was untagged and LysK was His-tagged in the Horgan work.
  2. The MIC format itself may be the artifact. The same laboratory later reported that the Jones et al. microdilution method "failed to yield a clear MIC" for two LysK-containing chimeras and switched to a plate-lysis checkerboard (PMID: 22286996, body text). A format that fails to give a clear endpoint for active constructs is weak evidence of absence for a 10-fold weaker one.
  3. Zinc, not calcium. Zn2+ inhibits all PlyTW constructs and sits in the CHAPk active site next to Cys54 (PMID: 25790497, PMID: 25064136). Free Zn2+ in a rich undefined broth is not controlled in a MIC and was not measured. This is a live alternative to the calcium leg and it is untested.
  4. Protein loss by adsorption and proteolysis. An 18-24 h incubation in a plastic microplate at low enzyme concentration permits surface adsorption and attack by staphylococcal extracellular proteases, which accumulate in late growth. Neither was controlled in any retrieved method, and two PubMed searches for protease inactivation of exogenous staphylolytic enzymes returned no papers. This is a stability-class alternative distinct from thermal unfolding.
  5. Homolog carry-over. Most of the quantitative SH3b-contribution data come from PlyTW (Twort) and LysF1, not from LysK itself. They are close homologs within the same group of staphylococcal phage endolysins, and the numbers should be curated as homolog evidence, not as LysK measurements.
  6. Downstream conflicts with "CHAP-only is non-functional". CHAPk alone eliminated S. aureus from the nares of mice (PMID: 21468207) and removed staphylococcal biofilms (PMID: 23431312). These are again different formats, not MICs, but they make a general statement of inactivity untenable.

Further limitations on what was computed. The quantitative SH3b-contribution numbers come from the homolog PlyTW, not from LysK. The growth-tolerance numbers come from Lys11. The threshold model is a model: its parameters (30 min doubling, 5x10^5 inoculum, 10^7 visible threshold, 20 h read) are standard but were not measured in the Becker experiment, and first-order enzyme decay with a single half-life is a simplification of aggregation, adsorption and proteolysis. What the model establishes is the structural point that potency and stability enter the MIC multiplicatively and the three short assays additively, which does not depend on the exact parameter values. The structural work settles folding and metal occupancy in the crystal, which is a different question from how much enzyme is still active after 18-24 h at 37 C in broth. Charge and Debye calculations are predictions from sequence and buffer composition, and the CAMHB ionic strength rests on an assumed composition because the Becker 2009 medium is not stated.

Discriminating tests

  1. Re-run the MIC with a wider ceiling. A 2-fold series extending to at least 32x the full-length LysK MIC. If CHAP-only yields a MIC 8-16x that of CHAP-SH3b, the discrepancy is potency plus censoring and nothing more. This is the single highest-value experiment and it is cheap.
  2. Split medium from growth state. Compare CHAP-only and CHAP-SH3b in (a) CAMHB with growing cells, (b) CAMHB with chloramphenicol-arrested cells, (c) CAMHB with cells de-energized by gramicidin or CCCP, and (d) saline lysis buffer. If arresting growth or collapsing the proton motive force rescues CHAP-only, tolerance is the mechanism. The design is shown to work for staphylococcal endolysins in PMID: 35075218.
  3. Separate stability from everything else. Pre-incubate both constructs in CAMHB at 37 C for 0, 2, 6, 12 and 24 h, then assay residual activity by the 10 min turbidity assay. If CHAP-only loses activity faster, the stability leg is supported, and the measurement is independent of the cells. This also supplies the half-life parameter the threshold model currently has to vary.
  4. Ionic-strength titration. Measure both constructs' specific activity across 0-300 mM NaCl in acetate and Tris buffers. This directly tests the reconciliation proposed for the Horgan against PlyTW conflict and predicts that the cell wall binding domain benefit grows with ionic strength.
  5. Cation add-back. MIC in CAMHB supplemented with 0.5-2 mM CaCl2, and separately with TPEN. This refutes or rescues the cation leg in one plate and tests the free-zinc alternative at the same time.
  6. Wall-composition genetics. MIC and turbidity on tarO and oatA deletion mutants. If tarO rescues CHAP-only in the MIC but oatA does not, accessibility through wall teichoic acid is confirmed and O-acetylation is excluded.
  7. Binding-dead SH3b control. A CHAP-SH3b fusion carrying a non-binding SH3b point mutant distinguishes a binding contribution from a stability or solubility contribution of the appended domain. This is the only clean way to answer the seed hypothesis question about whether SH3b contributes stability as distinct from binding.
