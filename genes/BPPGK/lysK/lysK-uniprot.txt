CC   -!- FUNCTION: Endolysin that degrades host peptidoglycans and participates
CC       in the sequential events which lead to the programmed host cell lysis
CC       releasing the mature viral particles (Probable). Exhibits lytic
CC       activity against Staphylococcus aureus (PubMed:19493008,
CC       PubMed:25064136). The CHAP activity cleaves the peptidic bond between
CC       the D-alanine of the tetra-peptide stem and the first glycine of the
CC       penta-glycine cross-bridge (PubMed:19493008, PubMed:25064136). The N-
CC       acetyl-muramidase activity cleaves between N-acetylmuramic acid and N-
CC       acetylglucosamine bonds (PubMed:19493008).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:19493008, ECO:0000269|PubMed:25064136,
CC       ECO:0000305}.
CC   -!- CATALYTIC ACTIVITY:
CC       Reaction=Hydrolyzes the link between N-acetylmuramoyl residues and L-
CC         amino acid residues in certain cell-wall glycopeptides.; EC=3.5.1.28;
CC         Evidence={ECO:0000269|PubMed:19493008};

CC   -!- DOMAIN: Contains a cysteine-histidine dependent amido-
CC       hydrolase/peptidase domain (peptidase C51/CHAP domain) at the N-
CC       terminus, a central amidase domain and a SH3b cell wall-binding domain
CC       at the C-terminus (PubMed:19493008, PubMed:25064136). The CHAP domain
CC       contains a tightly bound structural Ca(2+) ion and it is present at the
CC       N-terminus and is associated with the endopeptidase activity
CC       (PubMed:25064136). The SH3b domain increases the CHAP domain activity
CC       (PubMed:19493008). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:19493008,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25064136}.
CC   -!- SIMILARITY: Belongs to the N-acetylmuramoyl-L-alanine amidase 2 family.
