Gene Description

Uncharacterised metal-dependent enzyme of the phosphoenolpyruvate mutase/isocitrate lyase superfamily (Pfam PF13714) from the brown-rot basidiomycete Coniophora puteana, encoded by locus CONPUDRAFT_105298 and the only member of this superfamily in the reference proteome. Members of the superfamily act on alpha-oxycarboxylate substrates, cleaving or forming C-C or P-C bonds in an (alpha/beta)8 barrel whose active site is covered by a mobile gating loop and requires Mg2+ or Mn2+. The protein retains the complete catalytic set of the lyase/hydrolase branch, including the gating-loop lysine and the catalytic cysteine, together with the serine that marks oxaloacetate acetylhydrolase rather than 2,3-dimethylmalate lyase or 2-methylisocitrate lyase. Its closest relative with a known physiological context is the equivalent single-copy protein of another brown-rot fungus, Gloeophyllum trabeum, whose oxaloacetase gene is upregulated at the advancing hyphal front during wood decay. The substrate of this protein has not been determined experimentally, and no basidiomycete enzyme of this branch has been characterised as a purified protein.

Core Functions

Metal-dependent cleavage of an alpha-oxycarboxylate substrate by the lyase/hydrolase branch of the phosphoenolpyruvate mutase/isocitrate lyase superfamily. The reaction is inferred from the complete conserved active site; the substrate is not established, so no more specific molecular function is asserted.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the single-copy PF13714 protein of brown-rot basidiomycetes the oxaloacetase whose expression tracks the hyphal front in Postia placenta and Gloeophyllum trabeum? This protein is 78.8% identical to the G. trabeum protein, so one characterisation would settle the family.

Q: Does the marker serine retain its predictive value outside the ascomycete and plant proteins in which it was derived and tested? The Penicillium paralog Pc18g05100 carries the serine but conferred no oxalate production on yeast, which is a counterexample within the ascomycetes.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express and purify the protein and measure activity against oxaloacetate, (2R,3S)-dimethylmalate, 2-methylisocitrate and D-threo-isocitrate with Mg2+ and Mn2+. This is the experiment that would replace the removed EC 4.1.3.1 with a real assignment.

Experiment: Delete or silence CONPUDRAFT_105298 in Coniophora puteana and measure secreted oxalate during wood or mineral-substrate colonisation; loss of oxalate would identify the gene as the basidiomycete oxaloacetase and would also test the pathway that CMMMech's humboldtine and malachite records assume.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: Which reaction this enzyme catalyses is unknown. It is the single PEP mutase/isocitrate lyase superfamily protein of Coniophora puteana and carries the serine that marks oxaloacetate acetylhydrolase, yet it is more similar overall to 2,3-dimethylmalate lyase than to any characterised oxaloacetase, and no basidiomycete member of this branch has been purified and assayed. Only enzymology on the protein can settle it.

A0A5M3MM75 (Coniophora puteana CONPUDRAFT_105298) — curation notes

Identity

UniProtKB:A0A5M3MM75, 364 aa, locus CONPUDRAFT_105298 of Coniophora puteana
RWD-64-598-SS2 (brown-rot fungus), NCBITaxon:741705, reference proteome
UP000053558. Pfam PF13714 (PEP_mutase) only; no gene symbol, so the folder is
named by accession. It is the only PF13714 protein in the reference proteome.

The "Isocitrate lyase (EC 4.1.3.1)" name is wrong

UniProt's name and EC come from PROSITE-ProRule PRU10119 alone. PS00161 is the
six-residue pattern K-[KR]-C-G-H-[LMQR] around the catalytic cysteine shared by
the whole PEP mutase/isocitrate lyase superfamily; in the analysis it matched
2,3-dimethylmalate lyase, E. coli 2-methylisocitrate lyase and the petal death
protein as readily as it matched the characterised oxaloacetases, so it cannot
discriminate. The FT ACT_SITE 186 that the rule places on this entry is the
cysteine of that motif.

Three independent observations exclude isocitrate lyase:

  • No ICL domain. Genuine ICLs carry Pfam PF00463 and are 428-590 aa; this
    protein has only PF13714 and is 364 aa.
  • C. puteana's own isocitrate lyases are elsewhere: A0A5M3N7P6 (552 aa) and
    A0A5M3MQD7 (539 aa) carry PF00463.
  • Identity to real ICLs is background-level (31.0% to A. nidulans AcuD, 30.3%
    to M. tuberculosis Icl1), against 43.5-44.1% to characterised OAHs.

See [file:CONPW/A0A5M3MM75/A0A5M3MM75-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md] for the method
and the full residue table.

What it probably is, and why the review stops short of saying so

The protein has the OAH specificity serine aligned to Ser-308 of C. parasitica
OAH, the position where dimethylmalate lyase and methylisocitrate lyase carry a
proline PMID:20558740,
and whose mutation swaps the two activities in B. cinerea OAH
PMID:20558740.
Joosten et al. proposed that same serine as the superfamily marker for OAH
activity PMID:17654546.
The whole metal-binding and catalytic set (Asp-126/155/157, Glu-184, Lys-190,
Cys-192, Glu-259, Asn-282, the Tyr-111/His-182 pair) is conserved, so this is a
functional superfamily enzyme, not a pseudoenzyme.

Brown-rot fungi do run this route: oxalate biosynthesis in the wood-rotting
basidiomycete Fomitopsis palustris assigns oxaloacetase a "pivotal role in
yielding oxalate from oxaloacetate via the acetate-recycling" routes
PMID:11553780,
and in Postia placenta and Gloeophyllum trabeum the oxaloacetase gene is
expressed distinctly from the "paralogous of isocitrate lyases"
PMID:27543342 and is upregulated at the
advancing hyphal front PMID:27543342.
Consistent with that, this protein's closest relative in the comparison set is
the single OAH-branch PF13714 protein of G. trabeum (S7Q6Y6, 78.8% identical).

Against calling it OAH outright: it is more similar to A. niger
2,3-dimethylmalate lyase (51.5%) than to any characterised OAH (43.5-44.1%),
several active-site neighbourhoods differ from the ascomycete OAHs (Thr-113 to
Ser; Glu-259 window VCFIEGVKT against VGLLEGFRS), and no basidiomycete
oxaloacetase has ever been characterised at the protein level — the Fomitopsis
work is enzymology on cell extracts, not on a cloned gene. So the review removes
the isocitrate lyase claim, keeps the superfamily-level catalytic activity, and
asserts no EC 3.7.1.1: writing EC 3.7.1.1 here would repeat the mistake that
PRU10119 made, with a different guess.

CMMMech context

CMMMech (humboldtine.yaml, malachite.yaml) grounds C. puteana oxalate
production to malate dehydrogenase and pyruvate carboxylase, and states in
annotation_note that the basidiomycete "oahA homologs" A0A5M3MM75 and F8PSW8
are auto-annotated as isocitrate lyase. That note is accurate about the
auto-annotation. The implied identification of these two as oahA homologs is
reasonable but not established: it is a sequence inference about the branch of
the superfamily, and no basidiomycete OAH protein has been characterised. The
CMMMech evidence itself (Vlasov et al. 2023) is mineralogical, with no enzyme
assay or gene perturbation, so it cannot bear on this protein's substrate.

and RCSB; run with uv run --with biopython python oah_vs_icl_markers.py).
Raw output: results.tsv. Nothing below is hard-coded in the script except the
accessions and the reference residue positions taken from PMID:20558740.

1. The PROSITE signature behind the name is not diagnostic for ICL

PS00161 (ISOCITRATE_LYASE, ProRule PRU10119) is the six-residue pattern
K-[KR]-C-G-H-[LMQR], whose third position PROSITE itself flags as
active_site(?). It matches the catalytic-cysteine loop shared by the whole
PEP mutase/isocitrate lyase superfamily. In this run it matched, besides the
three query proteins and three characterised OAHs, also:

protein PS00161 match
A. niger 2,3-dimethylmalate lyase (PDB 3FA3) KRCGHL
E. coli 2-methylisocitrate lyase PrpB (P77541) KRCGHR
Dianthus petal death protein (Q05957) KKCGHM

So a PS00161 hit cannot distinguish isocitrate lyase from oxaloacetase,
dimethylmalate lyase or methylisocitrate lyase. The FT ACT_SITE that the rule
places on each query entry (186 in A0A5M3MM75, 180 in F8PSW8, 150 in B6HR98) is
exactly the cysteine of this motif, i.e. the superfamily catalytic Cys
(Cys-192 in C. parasitica OAH numbering), not an ICL-specific feature.

2. None of the three has an isocitrate lyase domain, and real ICLs sit beside them

True isocitrate lyases carry Pfam PF00463 (ICL) and are 428-590 residues long.
All three queries carry only PF13714 (PEP_mutase) and are 326-364 residues.
Each of the three reference proteomes contains separate, full-length PF00463
proteins (UniProt query proteome:<id> AND xref:pfam-PF00463):

proteome PF00463 proteins (length)
UP000053558 C. puteana A0A5M3N7P6 (552), A0A5M3MQD7 (539)
UP000008063 S. lacrymans F8QF23 (547), F8PZU2 (539)
UP000000724 P. rubens B6HRS2 (538), B6HVG1 (590)

The glyoxylate-cycle ICL of each organism is therefore a different gene; the
EC 4.1.3.1 on the PF13714-only entries is a second, redundant assignment.

3. Global identity places all three in the OAH/DMML branch, not with ICL

Percent identity (global Needleman-Wunsch, BLOSUM62, from results.tsv):

query C. parasitica OAH1 D5LIR7 A. niger OahA (UPI00001AA410) B. cinerea OahA Q6PNM8 A. niger DMML 3FA3 G. trabeum S7Q6Y6 A. nidulans ICL P28298 M. tuberculosis ICL P9WKK7 E. coli MICL P77541
A0A5M3MM75 44.1 43.5 43.7 51.5 78.8 31.0 30.3 33.6
F8PSW8 44.4 46.9 44.6 51.9 82.1 28.2 29.5 37.1
B6HR98 68.4 81.6 70.4 58.2 45.1 32.6 28.2 33.3

Identity to genuine isocitrate lyases is at or below the ~30% background for the
superfamily, while identity to characterised OAHs is 44-82%.

4. The OAH active-site machinery is complete, including the specificity serine

Chen et al. (PMID:20558740) identify the residue that distinguishes OAH from its
closest relative, 2,3-dimethylmalate lyase: OAH has Ser-308, which hydrogen-bonds
the pro-S C(2) hydroxyl of the oxaloacetate gem-diol, where DMML and
2-methylisocitrate lyase have a proline that packs against a methyl group. The
B. cinerea S260P mutant (the counterpart of Ser-308) lost 40-fold OAH activity and
gained 15-fold dimethylmalate lyase activity. Joosten et al. (PMID:17654546)
proposed the same serine as the sequence marker for OAH activity within the
superfamily.

Aligned to D5LIR7, all three queries have:

  • Ser at the 308 position (DMML 3FA3 and E. coli PrpB have Pro; A. nidulans ICL has Asp)
  • the Tyr-111/His-182 pair (replaced by Phe-Phe in the broad-specificity petal death protein)
  • the metal ligands Asp-126, Asp-155, Asp-157, Glu-184
  • the invariant gating-loop Lys-190/Arg-191 and the catalytic Cys-192
  • the shared-proton Glu-259 and Asn-282

The one named residue that is not conserved is Arg-221; the ±4 windows in
results.tsv show the alignment there is sound and that the position is
variable across the branch (Arg in A. niger OahA, Asn in B. cinerea OahA, Gly in
DMML), so it is not a usable marker.

5. Per-protein conclusion

B6HR98 (Pc22g24830) is oxaloacetate acetylhydrolase. It is 81.6% identical to
A. niger OahA and 68-70% to the two OAHs with enzymology, carries the Ser marker,
and is the gene whose heterologous expression conferred oxalate production on
S. cerevisiae and whose deletion abolished oxalate formation in P. chrysogenum
(PMID:21549851). Its paralog B6HBX9/Pc18g05100, which did not confer oxalate
production in that experiment, is only 57.7% identical to it, so sequence
similarity within this family is not by itself sufficient - the experimental
result is what settles B6HR98.

A0A5M3MM75 and F8PSW8 are OAH-branch lyase/hydrolase enzymes of unresolved
substrate specificity. They are not isocitrate lyases, and the Ser marker is
present, but they are closer to A. niger DMML (51.5-51.9%) than to any
characterised OAH (43.5-46.9%), and their nearest relative in this set is the
single OAH-branch PF13714 protein of another brown-rot fungus, Gloeophyllum
trabeum (S7Q6Y6, 78.8-82.1%); each is also the only PF13714 protein in its own
reference proteome. Several active-site neighbourhoods differ from the
ascomycete OAHs (e.g. Thr-113 to Ser, and the Glu-259 window VCFIEGVKT against
VGLLEGFRS). No basidiomycete OAH has been characterised at the protein level,
so "oxaloacetase candidate" is as far as sequence takes these two; assigning
EC 3.7.1.1 would be as unsupported as the EC 4.1.3.1 now on them.
