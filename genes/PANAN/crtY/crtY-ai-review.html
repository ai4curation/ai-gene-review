Gene Description

CrtY is the lycopene beta-cyclase of the carotenoid-producing enterobacterium Pantoea ananatis (historically Erwinia uredovora), a 382-residue FAD-dependent enzyme of the lycopene cyclase family. It catalyses the double cyclization that converts the acyclic C40 hydrocarbon all-trans-lycopene into bicyclic all-trans-beta-carotene, forming one beta-ionone ring at each acyclic psi end by way of the monocyclic intermediate gamma-carotene. The reaction is a cycloisomerization (EC 5.5.1.19): despite the flavoprotein-like FAD/NAD(P)-binding fold, no net oxidation or reduction of the carotenoid takes place and no molecular oxygen is used or incorporated. Reduced FAD is the essential cofactor; it is proposed to stabilize a positively charged transition state or carbocation intermediate, while proton attack from water at C-2 of the folded psi end group initiates ring closure. NAD(P)H stimulates activity but is not incorporated and acts indirectly, most likely by maintaining FAD in its reduced state or as an allosteric activator. The enzyme is promiscuous at the ring-precursor level, also cyclizing neurosporene to 7,8-dihydro-beta-carotene via beta-zeacarotene and zeta-carotene to a tetrahydro-beta-carotene. CrtY is a membrane-associated peripheral enzyme of the inner (cytoplasmic) membrane, acting on its extremely hydrophobic membrane-embedded substrate. In the Pantoea crt gene cluster it acts on the lycopene supplied by the CrtE/CrtB/CrtI segment and hands beta-carotene to the hydroxylase CrtZ and glucosyltransferase CrtX, and it is a standard tool for reconstructing beta-carotene biosynthesis in heterologous hosts.

Core Functions

Lycopene beta-cyclase activity: CrtY binds the extremely hydrophobic all-trans-lycopene at the inner-membrane surface and folds each acyclic psi end into a beta-ionone ring, converting lycopene to bicyclic all-trans-beta-carotene through the monocyclic intermediate gamma-carotene. The beta-carotene product is the precursor handed to the downstream hydroxylase CrtZ, so this activity sets entry into the cyclic (provitamin A) carotenoid branch of the pathway.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: How does a reduced FAD cofactor promote a reaction with no net redox change in the carotenoid; does it stabilize a carbocation intermediate as proposed, and which active-site residues complete the acid-base machinery alongside Glu-199?

Q: What is the structural basis for the two-end double cyclization; does CrtY process both psi ends in a single active site sequentially via gamma-carotene, and what determines the strict beta-ring (versus epsilon-ring) outcome?

Q: How is CrtY positioned at the inner membrane and how does it acquire its membrane-embedded lycopene substrate and release beta-carotene to CrtZ; is there a physical or kinetic coupling among the crt-cluster enzymes?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine the structure of CrtY with bound FAD and a non-reactive lycopene analogue by crystallography or cryo-EM, identify the FAD and substrate pockets, and test candidate catalytic residues (including the region around Glu-199) by alanine substitution, measuring the effect on activity, FAD binding and product ring stereochemistry.

Hypothesis: CrtY uses a flavoprotein fold to house an acid-base cycloisomerase active site in which reduced FAD stabilizes a positively charged intermediate while a network including Glu-199 provides the proton chemistry, and no residue performs oxygen-dependent redox catalysis.

Type: structure determination and site-directed mutagenesis

Experiment: Reconstitute purified CrtY with defined FAD redox states and with flavin analogues, quantify turnover and any change in flavin oxidation state during catalysis by spectroscopy and mass spectrometry, and test isotope incorporation from labelled NAD(P)H and from solvent water into the product to confirm the proton source and the absence of net redox chemistry.

Hypothesis: Reduced FAD is required for catalysis but is not consumed and effects no net oxidation or reduction of the carotenoid, and NAD(P)H acts only to regenerate or maintain reduced FAD rather than participating in the cyclization.

Type: cofactor and mechanism assay

Experiment: Fractionate Pantoea ananatis into soluble and membrane fractions with validated marker controls, locate CrtY by immunoblot, probe the strength and orientation of the membrane association by salt and carbonate extraction and protease-accessibility assays on sealed vesicles, and image a functional tagged allele in the native organism.

Hypothesis: CrtY is a peripheral enzyme of the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane rather than a soluble cytoplasmic protein or an integral membrane protein.

Type: subcellular fractionation and imaging

Experiment: Assay purified CrtY against a graded panel of acyclic carotenes including lycopene, neurosporene and zeta-carotene, determine steady-state kinetics for each, and identify the mono- and bicyclic products by HPLC and mass spectrometry to map the tolerance for reduced terminal structures already reported qualitatively.

Hypothesis: CrtY cyclizes a range of psi and 7,8-dihydro-psi end groups but favours all-trans-lycopene as its physiological substrate.

Gap: No experimental three-dimensional structure of CrtY has been determined, so the residues that bind FAD and lycopene and the geometry that positions the psi end group for proton-initiated ring closure are not directly known.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: The reaction, substrate, product, EC classification, FAD requirement and the non-redox mechanism are established by direct biochemistry, and one catalytic residue is identified (the E199A mutant loses activity while still binding FAD). Functional annotation report: crtY / UniProt P21687

Executive conclusion

The target is correctly identified as CrtY, the lycopene β-cyclase of Pantoea ananatis (historically Erwinia uredovora) strain 20D3/ATCC 19321. It is not a plant LCYB, fungal bifunctional synthase–cyclase, or one of the unrelated proteins sometimes annotated with similar symbols. The original gene was ORF-C of a 6,918-bp chromosomal carotenoid cluster; ORF-C occupies nucleotides 2422–3570 and was predicted to encode a 43,047-Da protein. These data agree with the supplied P21687 identity and lycopene-cyclase family/domain annotations (https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.172.12.6704-6712.1990; published December 1990). (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2, misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 6-7)

Its primary function is the two-end β-cyclization of all-trans-lycopene to all-trans-β-carotene:

all-trans-lycopene → γ-carotene → all-trans-β-carotene

The enzyme creates one β-ionone ring at each acyclic ψ-end of lycopene. The reaction is classified as EC 5.5.1.19 and is a cycloisomerization: no net oxidation or reduction of the carotenoid occurs. Reduced FAD is nevertheless required for catalysis, most plausibly to facilitate or stabilize proton-initiated carbocation chemistry. The product β-carotene is then available to CrtZ for hydroxylation to zeaxanthin and subsequently to CrtX for glycosylation. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 6-7, misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2, schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2)

1. Mandatory identity verification

Gene symbol and protein description

The symbol crtY matches the experimentally assigned lycopene-cyclase gene in the original E. uredovora carotenoid cluster. Misawa and colleagues named six ORFs crtE, crtX, crtY, crtI, crtB, and crtZ after sequencing and reconstructing their functions in Escherichia coli. ORF-C was assigned as crtY because deleting it caused lycopene accumulation, whereas retaining it allowed formation of cyclic β-carotene. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2, misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 6-7)

Organism

The source organism in the foundational study was Erwinia uredovora 20D3, ATCC 19321, subsequently classified as Pantoea ananatis. Thus, literature using either organism name can concern this protein, but strain and sequence provenance remain essential because crtY is widespread among carotenogenic bacteria. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2)

Family and domains

The supplied assignments—lycopene-cyclase family, PF05834/Lycopene_cycl, Lycopene_cyclase_b/e, CrtY_pseudo, and an FAD/NAD-binding-like superfamily—are functionally coherent. They describe a bacterial carotenoid cyclase with flavoprotein-like architecture and internal pseudo-repetition. Importantly, an FAD/NAD-binding-like fold does not mean that NAD(P)H is incorporated into β-carotene; reducing equivalents maintain FAD in its catalytically active reduced state, while the carotenoid reaction itself has no net redox change. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2)

2. Primary biochemical function and experimental evidence

The strongest direct evidence is the 1990 deletion/reconstitution analysis. E. coli carrying the intact pathway produced β-carotene, whereas constructs lacking ORF-C/crtY accumulated approximately 2.0 mg lycopene per gram dry cell weight. A construct retaining ORF-C with the required upstream genes produced β-carotene; the isolated products were identified by combinations of TLC, UV-visible spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, and NMR. The predicted CrtY polypeptide was 43,047 Da. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 6-7)

The pathway reconstructed from those experiments was:

GGPP —CrtB→ phytoene —CrtI→ lycopene —CrtY→ β-carotene —CrtZ→ zeaxanthin —CrtX→ zeaxanthin β-diglucoside.

The complete E. coli reconstruction yielded 1.1 mg/g dry weight zeaxanthin β-diglucoside. A crtX-deficient construct accumulated 2.2 mg/g zeaxanthin, while CrtY-containing constructs accumulated approximately 2.0 mg/g all-trans-β-carotene. These measurements are pathway-level yields, not purified-enzyme kinetic constants. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2, misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 6-7)

Mechanistically, cyclization is understood to occur one end at a time. Protonation of a terminal double bond initiates ring closure and produces a β-ring; the monocyclic intermediate is γ-carotene, which undergoes an equivalent second cyclization to β-carotene. γ-Carotene should therefore be annotated as a catalytic intermediate, although it was not reported as an accumulating metabolite in the retrieved 1990 deletion study.

3. Cofactor and catalytic mechanism

Yu and colleagues’ 2010 biochemical work identified the P. ananatis CrtY reaction as FADred-dependent but non-redox (Journal of Biological Chemistry 285:12109–12120; published April 2010; https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M109.091843). This resolves the otherwise unusual coexistence of flavin-binding architecture and an isomerization reaction. Reduced FAD is required, but there is no net hydrogenation or dehydrogenation of lycopene. Comparative cyclase studies support a role for FADred in enabling proton-driven catalysis or stabilizing the carbocation intermediate rather than serving as a consumed reductant. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2)

Accordingly, the most defensible annotation is:

  • Cofactor: FAD, catalytically active in the reduced state.
  • External reductant: NADH or NADPH can support regeneration of FADred in biochemical systems, depending on assay context.
  • Net reaction: cycloisomerization with no net electron transfer to β-carotene.
  • Likely chemical logic: protonation, carbocation formation/stabilization, ring closure, and deprotonation.

No experimentally solved three-dimensional structure of P21687 was identified in the retrieved literature. Structural details inferred from other flavoproteins or modeled domains should therefore not be represented as a solved P21687 structure.

4. Substrate specificity

Physiological substrate

The best-established physiological substrate is all-trans-lycopene, and the principal product is all-trans-β-carotene. The enzyme recognizes both ψ-end groups and normally cyclizes both, distinguishing it from lycopene β-monocyclases that stop predominantly at γ-carotene. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 6-7, misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 7-8)

Broader specificity

Historical work reported that the E. uredovora cyclase can cyclize a 7,8-dihydro-ψ end group, demonstrating some tolerance for reduced terminal structures (Takaichi et al., 1996, European Journal of Biochemistry 241:291–296; https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1432-1033.1996.0291t.x). This indicates that strict, fully conjugated lycopene is not the only chemically acceptable substrate, although lycopene remains the physiological reference substrate.

Engineered bacterial pathways have also used CrtY-type cyclases to create cyclic products from altered carotenoid backbones. For example, a crtY-containing E. coli pathway formed β-carotene from a C40 pathway and generated a cyclic diaponeurosporene derivative in a C30 context. Because the retrieved report does not unambiguously tie every such construct to P21687, these results establish CrtY-family engineering potential, not necessarily the complete native substrate spectrum of P21687. Evolved CrtY variants must likewise be kept separate from wild-type P21687. (lee2003biosynthesisofstructurally pages 3-4, lee2003biosynthesisofstructurally pages 5-8)

5. Cellular localization

CrtY acts inside the cell at the cytoplasmic-membrane interface. Lycopene and β-carotene are extremely hydrophobic and partition into lipid membranes; early work also noted that carotenoid-biosynthetic enzymes lose activity when removed from their membrane environment. The most appropriate functional description is therefore a membrane-associated/peripheral enzyme acting on a membrane-embedded substrate, rather than an integral transporter or secreted enzyme. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2)

This localization is strongly supported biochemically but should be distinguished from direct imaging: the retrieved evidence did not include P21687-specific fluorescence microscopy, quantitative cell-fractionation data, or a demonstrated transmembrane topology. Consequently, “cytoplasmic membrane-associated” is preferable to asserting a particular membrane orientation.

6. Biological process and pathway role

CrtY occupies the branch-defining cyclization step of bacterial carotenoid biosynthesis. CrtE supplies GGPP, CrtB forms phytoene, and CrtI introduces the conjugated double-bond system to produce lycopene. CrtY then converts the acyclic red carotenoid into cyclic orange β-carotene. CrtZ hydroxylates the β-rings to zeaxanthin, and CrtX glycosylates zeaxanthin. Deletion experiments showed that hydroxylation follows cyclization: CrtZ did not hydroxylate accumulated lycopene in the absence of functional CrtY. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2, misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 6-7)

CrtY therefore controls flux between lycopene accumulation and production of β-ring-containing carotenoids, including β-carotene and downstream zeaxanthin derivatives. This is its precise biochemical role; broad claims concerning virulence, plant colonization, oxidative-stress resistance, or ecological fitness cannot be assigned specifically to P21687 without targeted crtY perturbation in the native strain.

7. Applications and real-world implementation

Pathway reconstruction and modular synthetic biology

P. ananatis carotenoid genes are widely used as modular pathway components because products have readily distinguishable colors. A 2014 serine-integrase assembly study explicitly cloned P. ananatis crtB, crtE, crtI, and crtY modules to construct and optimize carotenoid pathways (published online 12 November 2013; https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkt1101). CrtY provides a convenient metabolic switch: without it, red lycopene accumulates; with it, flux proceeds to orange β-carotene. (colloms2014rapidmetabolicpathway pages 1-2)

Nutritional and industrial carotenoids

β-Carotene is used as a colorant, antioxidant, and provitamin-A ingredient in food, feed, cosmetics, and related products. CrtY is consequently useful in microbial β-carotene factories and in pathways supplying downstream xanthophylls. Conversely, suppressing or delaying CrtY is useful when lycopene itself is the desired product.

Recent 2024 developments

A 2024 dual-inducible system independently controlled a multistep β-carotene pathway across Gram-negative chassis. Improved translational control allowed lycopene to accumulate first and then be converted after induction of crtY. Under unoptimized conditions, the reported maximum was 0.46 mg/L β-carotene. The system was evaluated in E. coli, Pseudomonas putida, and Vibrio natriegens, demonstrating portable, temporal control of carotenoid flux (published November 2024; https://doi.org/10.1186/s13036-024-00462-z). This is strong evidence for present-day CrtY pathway utility, but exact attribution to P21687 requires sequence provenance from the construct. (rincon2024adualinduciblecontrol pages 1-3, rincon2024adualinduciblecontrol pages 9-11)

Another 2024 study represented the canonical heterologous pathway as CrtE→CrtB→CrtI→CrtY and identified host cAMP regulation as an engineering lever. Whole-genome sequencing of an improved E. coli producer identified 55 mutations, including reduced cyaA copy number; targeted cyaA perturbation supported a host-regulatory contribution to β-carotene production (published 28 November 2024; https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms252312796). This modifies precursor supply and global regulation rather than changing the intrinsic catalytic function of CrtY. (kwon2024genomicinsightsinto pages 1-3)

8. Current expert assessment and research gaps

The authoritative mechanistic interpretation is that CrtY is not merely a generic “carotenoid enzyme,” but a flavin-dependent, two-end lycopene β-cyclase whose net chemistry is non-redox. Its key functional consequence is conversion of membrane-embedded lycopene into β-carotene and commitment of carotenoid flux to β-ring-containing products.

The exact protein is well supported at the levels of genetic assignment, pathway reconstruction, product chemistry, and cofactor mechanism. Important remaining limitations are:

  1. No P21687 experimental three-dimensional structure was identified.
  2. Native-cell localization has not been resolved at high spatial precision.
  3. A comprehensive purified-enzyme substrate panel and modern kinetic constants were not available in the retrieved evidence.
  4. Most 2023–2024 studies concern engineered CrtY pathways or homologs rather than new mechanistic work on P21687 itself.
  5. Ecological effects of native crtY disruption in P. ananatis should not be inferred solely from the general antioxidant roles of carotenoids.

Final annotation

crtY/P21687 encodes a cytoplasmic-membrane-associated lycopene β-cyclase of Pantoea ananatis (formerly Erwinia uredovora). It uses reduced FAD to catalyze the net-nonredox, sequential cyclization of both ψ-ends of all-trans-lycopene, through γ-carotene, to all-trans-β-carotene. It acts after CrtI and before CrtZ/CrtX in the carotenoid pathway and is a major control point determining whether cells accumulate lycopene or synthesize β-carotene and downstream β-ring xanthophylls.

  carbocation intermediate, while proton attack from water at C-2 of the folded
  psi end group initiates ring closure. NAD(P)H stimulates activity but is not
  incorporated and acts indirectly, most likely by maintaining FAD in its reduced
  state or as an allosteric activator. The enzyme is promiscuous at the
  ring-precursor level, also cyclizing neurosporene to 7,8-dihydro-beta-carotene via
