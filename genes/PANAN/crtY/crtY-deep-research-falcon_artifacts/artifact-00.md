| Identity and source | **crtY/P21687 is the lycopene β-cyclase from *Pantoea ananatis*, historically named *Erwinia uredovora*, strain 20D3 (ATCC 19321).** The original locus was ORF-C in a chromosomal 6,918-bp carotenoid-gene cluster (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010). | Exact-gene cloning, sequencing, and heterologous functional analysis | **High.** The primary paper predates the UniProt accession but its organism, gene, locus, and function match P21687. It must not be confused with plant LCYB, fungal bifunctional cyclases, or other bacterial CrtY homologs. |
| Predicted size | ORF-C spans nucleotides 2422–3570 and was predicted to encode a **43,047-Da** polypeptide, corresponding to approximately 382 amino acids (pqac-00000010). | Exact-gene nucleotide sequence and predicted translation | **High** for the originally annotated ORF. Later work reported alternative translation initiation during expression in *E. coli*, so recombinant product size can depend on construct context. |
| Primary reaction | CrtY converts **all-trans-lycopene into all-trans-β-carotene** by forming a β-ionone ring at each ψ-end of lycopene. Removing ORF-C caused lycopene accumulation, whereas constructs containing ORF-C produced β-carotene (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000010). | Exact-gene deletion/reconstitution in *E. coli*; products identified by chromatographic and spectroscopic methods | **High.** This is direct pathway-level experimental evidence for the target protein. |
| Reaction intermediate | Cyclization occurs sequentially: one lycopene end is cyclized first to **γ-carotene (β,ψ-carotene)**, followed by cyclization of the second end to β-carotene. | Exact-protein biochemical model supported by the established two-end β-cyclase activity; general cyclase chemistry | **Moderate.** γ-Carotene was not explicitly resolved in the retrieved 1990 deletion experiment, so it should be annotated as a catalytic intermediate rather than a demonstrated accumulating product in that study. |
| Pathway position | CrtY acts after CrtI-mediated formation of lycopene and before CrtZ-mediated hydroxylation of β-carotene to zeaxanthin; CrtX subsequently glycosylates zeaxanthin. The original cluster is ordered **crtE–crtX–crtY–crtI–crtB**, with oppositely oriented **crtZ** (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000009). | Exact-cluster sequencing and combinatorial expression | **High.** Deletion constructs also showed that hydroxylation occurs only after ring formation (pqac-00000010). |
| FAD-dependent mechanism | The target CrtY is an **FAD-requiring cyclase** whose catalytically relevant state is reduced FAD (**FADred**); cyclization nevertheless produces **no net redox change** in the carotenoid. FADred is proposed to assist proton-initiated carbocation formation or stabilization rather than act as a conventional terminal reductant (pqac-00000003). | Exact-protein biochemical study, supported by comparative flavoprotein-cyclase mutagenesis | **High** for FADred dependence and net-nonredox chemistry; **moderate** for the detailed carbocation-stabilization model. NAD(P)H primarily supplies reducing equivalents to generate FADred and is not incorporated into β-carotene. |
| Cellular localization | CrtY functions **intracellularly at the cytoplasmic-membrane interface**, where the extremely hydrophobic lycopene substrate resides. It is best treated as a membrane-associated or membrane-peripheral enzyme, not as a transporter or integral multipass membrane protein (pqac-00000009). | Biochemical behavior of the exact pathway plus physicochemical and family-based inference | **Moderate.** Membrane dependence is consistent with purification behavior and substrate location, but the retrieved evidence does not include exact-protein microscopy, fractionation quantitation, or a solved CrtY structure. |
| Protein-family/domain interpretation | The supplied Lycopene_cycl/PF05834, Lycopene_cyclase_b/e, CrtY_pseudo, and FAD/NAD-binding-superfamily annotations agree with a bacterial β-cyclase containing duplicated or pseudo-repeated catalytic architecture and a flavin-binding fold. | Curated domain/family annotation interpreted against exact-protein biochemistry | **Moderate–high.** Domains support—not replace—the direct functional evidence. An FAD/NAD-binding-like fold does not imply that NAD(P)H is a stoichiometric carotenoid substrate. |
| Substrate breadth | Lycopene is the principal physiological substrate. Historical work on the *E. uredovora* enzyme indicates tolerance for related ψ-end groups, including a **7,8-dihydro-ψ end**, while engineered bacterial pathways show that some CrtY enzymes can cyclize noncanonical carotenoid backbones (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000007). | Exact-enzyme historical substrate testing plus broader bacterial-pathway engineering | **Moderate.** Non-C40 or strongly altered substrates should not be assigned to P21687 solely from homolog studies; evidence from evolved CrtY variants must be labeled separately. |
| 2024 applications | CrtY-controlled pathways are used as modular metabolic-engineering systems: inducible CrtY expression can first permit lycopene accumulation and then drive β-carotene formation. A 2024 dual-control study reported up to **0.46 mg/L β-carotene** without media optimization and demonstrated portable pathway control in Gram-negative chassis (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012). Another 2024 study used the canonical CrtE–CrtB–CrtI–CrtY pathway while identifying **cyaA/cAMP regulation** as a host-level lever for β-carotene production (pqac-00000014). | Recent synthetic-biology and host-engineering studies | **High** as CrtY-pathway application context; **lower for exact P21687 attribution** unless the construct’s sequence or provenance explicitly identifies the *P. ananatis* allele. These studies validate present-day utility but do not redefine P21687’s biochemical function. |
