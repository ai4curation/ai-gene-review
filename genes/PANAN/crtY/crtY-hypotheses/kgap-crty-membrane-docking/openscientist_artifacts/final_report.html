CrtY (P21687) peripheral membrane docking: hypothesis evaluation

Target: crtY, Pantoea ananatis (NCBITaxon:553), UniProt P21687, 382 aa Hypothesis slug: kgap-crty-membrane-docking Source: genes/PANAN/crtY/crtY-ai-review.yaml, free-text Verdict: Partially supported

Summary

The seed hypothesis asks whether the way CrtY docks onto a membrane to reach lycopene can be predicted from its sequence and AlphaFold model. The answer is partly yes and partly no, and the two halves separate cleanly along methodological lines. The evolutionary half works. The structural half does not.

One element survives every test applied to it: a bipartite amphipathic face built from helix 311-321 (QGFFRMLNRML) and helix 331-338 (WRVMQRFY). These two segments carry the highest hydrophobic moments in the protein (μH 0.798 and 0.962 for the best windows), are placed against the protein core with full model confidence (mean predicted aligned error 3.86 Å for 331-338 against 3.47 Å within the core itself), hold their amphipathicity under selection across 200 bacterial lycopene cyclases (median μH 0.820 and 0.767 against a background of 0.333 across 4,606 windows, Mann-Whitney p = 1.8e-88 and 1.6e-93), lose that signal in the fused plant and fungal enzymes (median 0.346 for the 331-338 equivalent, p = 8.0e-46 against bacteria), and donate Tyr338 and Tyr349 to the wall of the single enclosed cavity that holds Glu199. Buried-core control positions at 140-150 and 236-246 give medians of 0.191 and 0.177 with zero windows above the background 95th percentile, so the conservation signal is specific to the exposed amphipathic face and is not an artifact of the alignment method.

The structural half returns no usable answer. An implicit-membrane docking scan over 27,006 poses gives a best pose at -31.2 kcal/mol, which normalises to -1.70 kcal/mol per 1000 Å² of surface. That value sits between monotopic cyclooxygenase-1 (-2.27) and soluble glutathione reductase (-1.55), so it does not discriminate. The best pose is driven by residues 353-382, and the AlphaFold predicted aligned error places that segment at 13.46 Å from the core, meaning the model has no confident opinion about where it sits. Delete it and the best pose jumps to an unrelated face built from Trp109 and Tyr5. CrtY also fails the aromatic penetration test that separates COX-1, fatty acid amide hydrolase and squalene-hopene cyclase (inserted Trp and Tyr at 14-15 Å) from soluble flavoproteins (4-7 Å): the CrtY model reaches 0.77 Å. Two NDH-2 controls stop that failure being a refutation, because 5NA1 inserts no aromatic at all and still binds membrane. There is no basic patch above 173 Å² anywhere on the surface, so the anionic-headgroup-reading mechanism proposed in the seed hypothesis is refuted outright. The single active-site cavity (217 Å³) is closed in the static model and ends 9.6 Å short of the fitted membrane plane.

Stated plainly, as the seed hypothesis demands: the model cannot distinguish a genuine membrane-binding element from an ordinary hydrophobic surface, and in this case it does not even give a stable answer about which surface to consider. The positive case for 311-338 rests entirely on two lines that never use the fitted membrane: conservation of the hydrophobic moment across bacterial genomes, and contact between Tyr338/Tyr349 and the active-site cavity wall. The discriminating experiment is the triple mutant W331A/M334A/F337A combined with F313A/F314A/M320A, assayed for lycopene cyclisation and liposome co-sedimentation, with SEC-MALS to exclude the competing reading that the conserved face is an oligomer interface.

Executive judgment

Partially supported. The hypothesis has three separable claims, and they receive different verdicts.

A specific candidate element exists and can be named. Helices 311-321 and 331-338 form one contiguous amphipathic face. The evidence is evolutionary and is strong: the hydrophobic moment at these positions is held above background in 200 bacterial lycopene cyclases, beats the protein's own exposed control helix 95-104 in a paired test within the same proteins (median difference +0.239, Wilcoxon p = 6.0e-27, dz = 1.11 for 331-338; +0.313, p = 1.2e-27, dz = 1.20 for 311-321), and collapses in the fused eukaryotic enzymes that carry their own transmembrane segments.

The basic-patch mechanism is refuted. The five largest exposed charged patches are 173, 150, 145, 134 and 131 Å², each built from 2-3 residues, and the three largest sit 12-29 Å outside the fitted membrane plane. A PH, C2 or BAR domain presents several hundred square angstroms of contiguous basic surface. CrtY presents nothing of the sort. Its interfacial arginines (R319, R332, R336, R347, R358, R360, R382, K365) are spread along the C-terminal region rather than clustered, which is the geometry of snorkelling residues that set insertion depth.

The structure cannot demonstrate membrane binding. The docking score falls in the overlap zone between monotopic and soluble controls, the best pose is steered by an unplaceable segment, the aromatic penetration signature is absent, and the active-site cavity does not open onto the bilayer.

The most important caveat is the competing interpretation that is not excluded by any of this work: a conserved exposed amphipathic helix pair is also exactly what a protein-protein or oligomer interface looks like. The oligomeric state of CrtY is not known. Conservation of amphipathicity is necessary evidence for an interfacial membrane element and it is not sufficient.

A second caveat concerns the segment 353-382. It dominated the raw docking score in the first pass of this analysis, and an earlier reading of the model would have named it as the docking element. Three independent signals retire it: mean pLDDT 81.0 against 95.2 for residues 1-349, 44% of its heavy atoms making no contact with the core within 5 Å, and the structurally equivalent region in human squalene epoxidase being buried core (mean relative solvent accessibility 0.104 with 80% of residues below 0.20, against 0.408 and 23% in the CrtY model). The predicted aligned error settles it. Any report that names 353-382 as a membrane anchor is reading a modelling artifact.

Key findings

F1. The whole docking signal in the model comes from one C-terminal region

Shrake-Rupley solvent accessible surface area was computed on AF-P21687-F1-model_v6 (382 residues, mean pLDDT 94.1) with 256 sphere points per atom and a 1.4 Å probe, giving 17,333 Å² of total surface. An implicit monotopic docking scan sampled 500 orientations by 54 plane positions, 27,006 poses in total, under a half-space insertion model with per-atom transfer coefficients of -0.030 kcal/(mol·Å²) for nonpolar atoms, +0.012 for polar, +0.060 for charged, and -0.012 for cationic nitrogen in the 0-5 Å headgroup band.

The scan gives a single best pose at ΔG = -31.2 kcal/mol, z = -5.07 against the distribution of all sampled poses (mean -2.19, SD 5.72). It buries 3,281 Å² (18.9% of the surface) across 58 residues in contiguous segments 79-82, 309-347 and 353-382. The deepest residues are Leu371 (15.0 Å), Val370 (14.1 Å), Leu374 (13.9 Å), Lys365 (13.4 Å), Leu359 (12.6 Å) and Arg360 (11.8 Å). Deleting residues 350-382 collapses the normalised score from -1.70 to -0.89 kcal/mol per 1000 Å² and moves the best plane to a different surface that buries 47.7% of the model, which is not a physical membrane interface.

No other surface of the model is a credible alternative under this scan. The model therefore offers exactly one candidate region, and the question becomes whether that region is real.

CrtY ranks above two of the three soluble controls and is indistinguishable from glutathione reductase (-1.55, z = -4.23), a definitively soluble enzyme. All three soluble controls were scored as isolated chains taken from obligate homodimers, so their exposed subunit interfaces inflate their scores. The same confound applies in reverse to CrtY, whose oligomeric state is unknown. The useful output of this calculation is the location of the candidate element, not a verdict on whether it binds membrane.

F3. The Glu199 cavity is closed and ends 9.6 Å short of the fitted membrane

Grid cavity detection (1.0 Å grid, 1.5 Å probe clearance, 30-ray burial fraction ≥ 0.85) finds one dominant enclosed cavity of 217 Å³ (287 Å³ at burial ≥ 0.80). Its nearest point is 4.0 Å from the Glu199 carboxylate and 11.3 Å from the centroid of the UniProt dinucleotide-binding region 6-36, so the catalytic glutamate and the FAD site share a single continuous chamber. Lining residues include Gly11-Ala12-Gly13-Leu14-Ala15-Asn16 and Gly42-Gly43 from the Rossmann motif, Leu197, Glu199, Thr201, Tyr203, and at the far end Tyr338 and Tyr349.

Measured along the fitted membrane normal, the cavity spans -29.6 to -9.6 Å, entirely outside the bilayer, with its shallowest point 9.6 Å above the membrane surface. Glu199 sits at -17.8 Å (relative solvent accessibility 0.099, pLDDT 98.5) and the FAD motif centroid at -27.7 Å.

The static model therefore does not place a substrate-entry channel at the membrane surface. This is not evidence against docking. Squalene-hopene cyclase, the reference monotopic enzyme acting on a lipophilic substrate, has the same arrangement, with a mobile region between the active-centre cavity and the membrane that must melt to open a passage (PMID: 15113001). What matters for the hypothesis is the direction: the CrtY cavity points at the candidate element, and the 9.6 Å of intervening protein is contributed by the 331-349 helical hairpin whose Tyr338 and Tyr349 form the cavity wall.

F5. The scan localises the interface correctly on a known monotopic flavoprotein

The same scan (300 directions, 1.0 Å plane step) was applied to three type II NADH dehydrogenase structures, a class of FAD Rossmann flavoproteins known to dock monotopically through C-terminal amphipathic helices (PMID: 42315832).

5NA1 chain A (Caldalkalibacillus thermarum, residues 5-402) scores -1.10 per 1000 Å², z = -2.42, burying 3,379 Å², of which 2,670 Å² (79%) comes from the last 55 residues. The 15 deepest residues are Lys368 (12.0 Å), Lys374 (11.9), Phe397 (10.5), Phe377 (9.6), Phe366 (9.2), Ile393 (8.8), Lys373 (8.2), Met378 (8.1), Phe402 (7.1), Val381 (7.0), Phe388 (6.7), Arg385 (6.6), Val365 (6.0), Ile370 (6.0) and Gly394 (5.7). Truncating the last 56 residues collapses the score to -0.26. Ndi1 from S. cerevisiae (4NWZ) scores -1.37 with its deep set at 355-397, and 4G73 scores -1.76 with its deep set at 473-513.

Where the answer is known independently, the scan puts the interface on the right residues. That validates localisation. It does not validate detection, because the three NDH-2 scores (-1.10 to -1.76) overlap the soluble controls measured in F2. Localisation and detection are different questions and only localisation passes.

F6. The nearest solved structures anchor at positions CrtY does not have

A Foldseek 3Di+AA search of the model against pdb100 and afdb50 returned 1,000 afdb50 and 719 pdb100 hits. The top afdb50 hits are all lycopene cyclases, the best being AF-A0A7X3SHA5 from Pantoea sp. Nvir (E = 1.9e-59, 58.1% identity over 382 residues). The top pdb100 hits are geranylgeranyl reductase from Sulfolobus acidocaldarius (3ATR, E = 1.3e-15, 12.5% identity over 445 residues), a geranylgeranyl bacteriochlorophyll reductase homologue (3OZ2, E = 3.9e-13), the human squalene epoxidase catalytic domain (6C6P, E = 6.5e-13, 11.9% identity over 459 residues) and p-hydroxybenzoate hydroxylase (1CC4, E = 7.7e-13).

Running the membrane scan on these relatives: 6C6P chain B scores -3.22 per 1000 Å² (z = -3.64, 8,019 Å² buried) with its interface on residues 456-547, while the region structurally equivalent to CrtY 309-382 (SQLE 335-428) supplies only 156 Å², 2% of the buried surface, and the equivalent of CrtY 331-338 (SQLE 363-373) sits 8.1 Å outside the fitted bilayer. 3ATR chain A scores -0.38 (z = -1.47, 1,692 Å²) with a weak interface at 138-143 and 257-264, and the equivalent of CrtY 309-382 contributes 0 Å².

In squalene epoxidase the membrane-binding device is an N-terminal amphipathic helix in the regulatory region that interconverts with a soluble degradation-prone segment (PMID: 29222193), and that region was removed before crystallisation, since 6C6P contains only the catalytic domain (PMID: 30626872). The surface the catalytic domain presents to the membrane in my fit lies beyond residue 428, past the end of the 382-residue CrtY chain. CrtY has no counterpart of the SQLE anchor. The mechanistic class, an amphipathic helix grafted onto a soluble FAD fold, is precedented in both SQLE and NDH-2, but the graft point differs in each. Homology cannot confirm or refute the CrtY element.

In 6C6P the assembly and isolated-chain RSA values are identical for all three regions, so none lies at the crystallographic dimer interface. The CrtY 353-382 segment has mean pLDDT 81.0 against 95.2 for the rest of the chain and 44% of its atoms make no contact with the protein core.

Three independent signals converge: low model confidence, no packing against the core, and an equivalent region that is buried core in the closest solved structure. The simplest explanation is that AlphaFold left this C-terminal stretch under-packed. The amphipathic helix 331-338 survives the same test, because its exposure in the model matches what two solved structures show at the same position.

F8. No basic patch, one strong amphipathic helix

Patch detection by single-linkage clustering (5 Å) of exposed atoms (atom ASA > 5 Å²) gives five largest exposed nonpolar patches of 2,172, 549, 519, 409 and 383 Å². The largest spans 61 residues including 313-329 and the 331-338 helix and reaches 14.1 Å into the fitted bilayer. The five largest exposed charged patches measure 173, 150, 145, 134 and 131 Å², each built from only 2-3 residues, and the three largest sit 12-29 Å outside the fitted membrane plane. The only membrane-proximal charged cluster is 134 Å² (residues 343, 347, 357).

F10. Amphipathicity at 311-321 and 331-338 is under selection in bacteria and lost in the fused eukaryotic enzymes

Within the same proteins, both candidate helices beat the exposed control helix 95-104 by a paired test: 331-338 median difference +0.239 (Wilcoxon p = 6.0e-27, dz = 1.11) and 311-321 median difference +0.313 (p = 1.2e-27, dz = 1.20). 89% of bacterial homologues keep at least one of the two helices above the background 95th percentile and 47% keep both. P. ananatis CrtY itself sits at 0.859 and 0.798.

Sequence identity alone cannot do this test, because an amphipathic helix tolerates substitution as long as the hydrophobic face is preserved. Measuring the moment instead of the identity shows that bacterial lycopene cyclases hold a bipartite amphipathic face at 311-321 and 331-338 across 200 genomes, well above the amphipathicity of their own exposed helices elsewhere. The candidate element should be stated as 311-338, not 331-338 alone and not 309-382.

The loss of the signal in the fused plant and fungal enzymes fits their different membrane context. Those proteins carry their own N-terminal targeting and transmembrane segments and, in the fungal and algal cases, a fused phytoene synthase domain. PMID: 42943443 and PMID: 42825966 both describe the bifunctional phytoene synthase/lycopene cyclase architecture in Rhodotorula, and PMID: 41092119 reports that in rice OsLCYb the N-terminal 181 amino acids are critical for low-temperature sensitivity while the C-terminal extension modifies this response, which is consistent with the eukaryotic enzymes having reorganised their membrane-facing elements.

The only inserted aromatic in the CrtY full model is Trp331 at 1.1 Å, sitting at the plane rather than in the acyl region. The energetic and geometric half of the analysis therefore collapses to a null. The docking scan placed the membrane against the 311-338 face only because residues 353-382 dragged the plane there, and those residues have no defined position in the model. The two NDH-2 controls limit how far the aromatic metric can be pushed, since 5NA1 inserts no Trp or Tyr at all and still binds membrane, so failing the test is not a refutation of membrane binding.

Mechanistic model

The model that survives all eleven analyses is interfacial rather than inserted, and its support is evolutionary rather than geometric.

The chemistry constrains the geometry. Reduced FAD is the essential CrtY cofactor and the reaction is non-redox, with the flavin stabilising a positively charged transition state or intermediate through charge transfer while acid-base catalysis supplies the catalytic principle (PMID: 20178989). That means the flavin and the catalytic glutamate must sit in one chamber with the lycopene end group, which is what the 217 Å³ cavity holding Glu199 at 4.0 Å and the FAD motif at 11.3 Å provides. Lycopene is a C40 polyene that does not exist free in the cytoplasm, so the substrate has to be reached in the bilayer. The enzyme has no signal peptide and no transmembrane span, so an integral topology is excluded by the premises of the hypothesis.

What the analysis adds is the position of the interfacial element and the reason the geometric argument cannot carry weight. The 311-338 face lies on the same side of the protein as the cavity mouth, and the 9.6 Å of protein between them is the 331-349 hairpin contributing Tyr338 and Tyr349 to the cavity wall. A mobile hairpin that both forms the cavity wall and sits at the interface is the arrangement squalene-hopene cyclase uses, where a mobile region between the active-centre cavity and the membrane melts to open a passage (PMID: 15113001), and where the cyclase is a monotopic membrane protein whose membrane-binding interactions resemble those of prostaglandin H2 synthase (PMID: 9931258). Molecular dynamics on the same enzyme shows it keeps its secondary and tertiary structure outside its native membrane while the ligand entrance channel shows dynamical features of functional importance (PMID: 18247576), which is the reason a static model cannot show an open channel even when one exists.

The broader class has precedent. An amphipathic helix added to an otherwise soluble fold is how squalene epoxidase anchors (PMID: 29222193) and how type II NADH dehydrogenases reach the quinone pool (PMID: 42315832). The same device appears in chloroplast fibrillins, where an amphipathic helical insert at the lip of the beta-barrel is necessary for plastoglobule association and interacts with membranes rich in lipid-packing defects (PMID: 41980065). The position of the graft is protein-specific in every one of these cases, which is why no homologue can validate the CrtY position and why conservation within the lycopene cyclase family was the only informative comparative test available.

Mechanistic scope

The immediate molecular activity tested here is a protein-lipid interaction: whether a defined surface of CrtY inserts into or adsorbs onto the cytoplasmic leaflet of the inner membrane. That is separable from three other things the gene review touches.

The catalytic activity, lycopene beta-cyclisation using reduced FAD with acid-base catalysis, is established by direct assay on the P. ananatis protein (PMID: 20178989) and is not under test. The membrane-docking question is about substrate access, which is upstream of the chemistry.

The localisation, which the seed hypothesis takes as experimentally supported, is a statement about where the protein is found. It does not specify which surface contacts the lipid, and a protein can be membrane-associated through a partner rather than through its own surface. Nothing in this report distinguishes those two routes.

The pathway-level consequences are further downstream and are not evidence for docking. Plant and fungal lycopene cyclase manipulations produce large phenotypes: β-LCY knockout in tomato raises fruit lycopene and increases cadmium stress susceptibility (PMID: 42051075), LCYB overexpression in tobacco and tomato changes root growth, mycorrhizal colonisation and Striga seed germination through abscisic acid and strigolactone levels (PMID: 41102519), and the rice oslsz1 V232M missense mutation impairs chloroplast development at low temperature (PMID: 41092119). These are pathway and developmental outcomes of altered carotenoid flux in multicellular eukaryotes. They tell us nothing about how a 382-residue bacterial enzyme contacts a bilayer, and they should not be imported into the CrtY review as support for any membrane claim.

Conflicts and alternatives

The conserved face could be a protein interface. A conserved, exposed, strongly amphipathic helix pair is as consistent with an oligomerisation interface or a partner-binding site as with a lipid interface. The oligomeric state of P. ananatis CrtY is not reported in anything I found. Three of the soluble controls used in the benchmark (1PBE, 3GRS, 1FOH) are obligate homodimers scored as isolated chains, and their exposed subunit interfaces produced docking scores of -0.67 to -1.55, overlapping CrtY at -1.70. This is the single most important alternative and it is fully testable by SEC-MALS or native mass spectrometry.

The docking scan is not an independent witness. The best pose over the full model is produced by residues the model cannot place. After removing them, the scan prefers a face centred on Trp109 and Tyr5 that has no conservation behind it. The scan therefore votes for two different surfaces depending on an arbitrary preprocessing choice, which means its vote should be discounted entirely rather than weighted.

The deletion control has its own artifact. Removing 353-382 exposes new surface at the truncation point, and some of the shift to the Trp109/Tyr5 face could be driven by that newly exposed surface rather than by the removal of the biasing segment. A cleaner control would mask the segment rather than delete it.

The eukaryotic signal loss has two readings. The fused plant and fungal enzymes lose the amphipathic moment at the 331-338 equivalent. One reading is that they have replaced a bacterial interfacial helix with their own N-terminal transmembrane and targeting segments. The other is that the pairwise alignment at 31.2% mean identity misplaces the equivalent window. The buried-core controls give 0% above background in both groups, which argues the method is not generating spurious signal, but it does not rule out spurious signal loss.

The implicit-membrane model is a half-space approximation. It has no explicit lipids, no acyl-chain entropy, no curvature, and a single flat plane. It cannot model the lipid-packing defects that the fibrillin work identifies as the recognition feature for an amphipathic helical insert (PMID: 41980065). A flat-plane transfer-energy model is structurally blind to the thing that may be doing the recognising.

Database carry-over risk. Several of the lycopene cyclase entries pulled from UniProt for the conservation analysis are themselves computational annotations. The conservation result does not depend on the annotations being correct, because it measures a physical property of the sequence, but any curation claim built on "all lycopene cyclases have this element" inherits whatever misannotation is present in the 400-sequence set.

Does CrtY bind liposomes at all? Checked: no direct binding assay was found in PubMed searches covering lycopene cyclase, CrtY and membrane binding. This matters because the entire peripheral-docking framing rests on inference from substrate hydrophobicity and from localisation. Resolved by a liposome co-sedimentation or flotation assay with purified CrtY against defined vesicles.

What is the oligomeric state? Checked: not reported in the literature retrieved; the AlphaFold entry is a monomer model and I did not run a multimer prediction. This matters because it is the main competing explanation for a conserved exposed amphipathic face. Resolved by SEC-MALS, native MS, or an AlphaFold-Multimer run on the homodimer.

What is the real structure of residues 309-382? Checked: no experimental structure of any bacterial CrtY exists in the PDB; the closest solved relatives are at 11.9-12.5% identity. This matters because the whole geometric argument depends on a region the model places with PAE 13.46 Å. Resolved by a crystal or cryo-EM structure, or by hydrogen-deuterium exchange mapping of the membrane-protected surface.

Is the lipid dependence hydrophobic or electrostatic? Checked: the patch analysis refutes a headgroup-reading basic surface, but it cannot exclude a weak preference for anionic lipids through the scattered arginines. This matters because the two models predict different salt dependence. Resolved by comparing binding to pure phosphatidylcholine vesicles against phosphatidylglycerol and cardiolipin vesicles at varying ionic strength.

Does the 331-349 hairpin open? Checked: the static model shows a closed 217 Å³ cavity ending 9.6 Å short of the plane, and the SHC precedent says a mobile region must melt (PMID: 15113001, PMID: 18247576). This matters because a permanently closed cavity would mean the substrate arrives by some other route. Resolved by molecular dynamics in an explicit bilayer, or by the approach used on small monotopic phosphoglycosyl transferases, where single-molecule FRET in styrene-maleic acid liponanoparticles reports ligand-dependent conformational change in a native-like lipid environment (PMID: 41131924).

Does the element matter for catalysis in vivo? Checked: no mutational data exists for any residue in 311-338 of any lycopene cyclase that I found. This matters because a mutation that abolishes colour in an E. coli carotenoid reconstitution would be the cheapest discriminating result available. Resolved by the mutant panel below.

Discriminating tests

1. The mutant panel, ranked by information per experiment. Build three constructs in the standard E. coli crtEBI plus crtY colour complementation system: the hydrophobic-face mutant W331A/M334A/F337A, the second-helix mutant F313A/F314A/M320A, and the double construct combining both. The prediction that separates the hypotheses is specific. If 311-338 is an interfacial docking element, the single mutants reduce colour partially and the double abolishes it, while purified protein retains cyclase activity on detergent-solubilised lycopene. If the face is an oligomer interface, the mutants disrupt assembly and lose activity in both assays equally. Include R332A/R336A as a separate charge-pair construct to test the snorkelling interpretation of the scattered arginines; the hydrophobic model predicts a smaller effect from the charge pair than from the aromatic triple.

2. Liposome co-sedimentation with the mutant panel. Purified wild-type and mutant CrtY against vesicles of defined composition, scoring the pelleted fraction. This is the assay that converts the question from inference to measurement, and it is the one piece of evidence that would let a curator add a peripheral membrane CC term with IDA. Use the deletion design that worked for the fibrillin amphipathic inserts, where removing the insert disrupted plastoglobule localisation (PMID: 41980065).

3. SEC-MALS or native mass spectrometry on wild-type CrtY. This is cheap and it eliminates the most serious alternative interpretation. Run it before the mutant panel, because a homodimer result changes how every mutant phenotype should be read.

4. Tryptophan fluorescence titration against vesicles. Trp331 is the only aromatic the docking fit places at the interface. If it inserts, its emission blue-shifts on vesicle binding. A W331F control distinguishes a genuine environmental change from a global conformational one. This directly tests the one residue the structure-based analysis names.

5. Cross-species segment swaps. Replace CrtY 311-338 with the corresponding region from a homologue that lost the moment signal (one of the 11% of bacterial sequences below background) and test colour complementation. A conservation signal that is functional should produce a measurable deficit; one that is a statistical pattern should not.

6. Explicit-bilayer molecular dynamics. Start from the full model with 353-382 either removed or modelled as disordered, place the 311-338 face at the interface, and measure the depth distribution of Trp331, Phe313, Phe314 and Phe337 over a microsecond. This is the computational test that would replace the flat-plane approximation that failed here, and it can model the lipid-packing defects the half-space model cannot see.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Every structural number in this report comes from a single AlphaFold model. There is no experimental structure of any bacterial lycopene cyclase, and the closest solved relatives align at 11.9-12.5% identity.

The implicit membrane model is a flat half-space with per-atom transfer coefficients. It omits explicit lipids, curvature, acyl-chain entropy, protein flexibility and the membrane dipole potential. Its validation here is limited to showing that it localises a known interface correctly on three NDH-2 structures, and its failure to discriminate CrtY from glutathione reductase is a property of the method as well as of CrtY.

The conservation analysis used 400 pairwise Needleman-Wunsch alignments, not a multiple sequence alignment, because the EBI Clustal Omega REST endpoint returned HTTP 400 ("temporarily blocked") throughout the run. Pairwise alignment to a single reference is more vulnerable to local misalignment than a profile method. The buried-core negative controls at 140-150 and 236-246 argue the method is not fabricating signal, since they return 0% above background with p = 1.0.

The soluble FAD controls were scored as isolated chains extracted from obligate homodimers, which inflates their docking scores by exposing subunit interfaces. The same confound applies to the CrtY monomer model if CrtY is an oligomer.

No wet-lab data on CrtY membrane binding exists in anything retrieved. Every supporting statement about docking in this report is inference from sequence, model and comparison.

The literature search returned 45 papers, of which roughly a third concern carotenoid metabolic engineering in yeast and plants and are orientation only. The directly relevant primary literature on this protein amounts to one biochemical paper (PMID: 20178989), and the monotopic comparator literature is about other proteins.

Lead 1: narrow the hypothesis text to residues 311-338. The review text should name helices 311-321 (QGFFRMLNRML) and 331-338 (WRVMQRFY) as the candidate peripheral docking element and should drop any reference to a C-terminal region beyond residue 352.

Lead 3: flag residues 353-382 as low-confidence. Mean predicted aligned error to the core is 13.46 Å against 3.47 Å within the core. Any existing or proposed annotation that cites this region as a membrane anchor, a hydrophobic insertion segment, or a structured C-terminal extension should be marked as unsupported by the model that generated it.

Lead 4: remove the anionic-headgroup element from the hypothesis. The largest charged patch on the surface is 173 Å² built from 2-3 residues, and the three largest point away from any plausible membrane plane. There is no basis for a phospholipid-binding MF term.

Lead 5: record the competing oligomer-interface interpretation. The review should state that a conserved exposed amphipathic helix pair is equally consistent with a homo-oligomer interface, that the oligomeric state of CrtY is unknown, and that SEC-MALS would resolve it.

Lead 6: record the methodological finding. A docking scan over an AlphaFold model must be run against the predicted aligned error matrix, because a segment the model cannot place will dominate the fit and steer the plane. This applies to every other gene in the collection where a membrane element has been proposed from an AlphaFold model.

Evidence base

The directly relevant primary literature on P21687 is small. PMID: 20178989, The lycopene cyclase CrtY from Pantoea ananatis catalyzes an FADred-dependent non-redox reaction, is the one biochemical study of this exact protein. It establishes reduced FAD as the essential cofactor and acid-base catalysis as the principle, which fixes the geometric requirement that the flavin, Glu199 and the substrate end group share one chamber. It says nothing about membrane docking, so it supports the mechanistic frame without testing the hypothesis.

The monotopic comparator literature is about other proteins and supplies the reference behaviours. PMID: 9931258 and PMID: 9295270 establish squalene-hopene cyclase as a monotopic membrane protein with membrane-binding interactions comparable to prostaglandin H2 synthase, and describe an active site in a large central cavity lined by aromatic residues. PMID: 15113001 supplies the finding that most directly rescues the CrtY cavity result: a mobile region between the active centre cavity and the membrane that must melt to open a passage. PMID: 12031670 shows the inhibitor Ro48-8071 bound in the SHC active-centre cavity and extending into the channel that connects the cavity with the membrane, which is the structural proof that such a channel exists even when a given static structure does not show it open. PMID: 18247576 adds that SHC keeps its secondary and tertiary structure outside its native membrane while the ligand entrance channel shows dynamical features of functional importance.

The flavoprotein comparators challenge the detection claim. PMID: 42315832 states that type II NADH:quinone oxidoreductases are typically monotopic flavoproteins making direct contact with the membrane to access the quinone pool. That makes them the correct positive control, and running the scan on them produced the result that undermines the metric: scores of -1.10 to -1.76, overlapping soluble glutathione reductase at -1.55, and in the 5NA1 case no inserted aromatic at all.

The structural relatives found by Foldseek argue against positional homology. PMID: 29222193 describes the N-terminal domain of squalene monooxygenase interconverting between a membrane-binding amphipathic helix and a soluble degradation-prone segment, and PMID: 30626872 confirms that the crystal structures are of the catalytic domain only. Together they show that the nearest solved monotopic lipophilic-substrate enzyme anchors at a position that has no counterpart in the 382-residue CrtY, so the SQLE comparison cannot validate the CrtY element.

PMID: 41980065 is the closest thing in the retrieved literature to the experiment CrtY needs. Deleting the amphipathic helical inserts of two plastoglobule-targeted fibrillins disrupted their localisation, and molecular dynamics supported interaction with membranes rich in lipid-packing defects. The protein family is different and the target is a monolayer, but the experimental logic transfers directly to CrtY 311-338.

PMID: 41131924 supplies a method for the conformational question: single-molecule FRET on dual-labelled protein in styrene-maleic acid liponanoparticles, applied to monotopic phosphoglycosyl transferases, reporting ligand-dependent conformational change in a native-like lipid environment.

The eukaryotic lycopene cyclase literature explains why the conservation signal disappears outside bacteria. PMID: 42943443 and PMID: 42825966 describe the bifunctional phytoene synthase/lycopene cyclase CrtYB and Car2 in Rhodotorula, and PMID: 41092119 shows that in rice OsLCYb the N-terminal 181 residues carry the low-temperature sensitivity while the C-terminal extension modifies it. These are different proteins with different membrane contexts, which is consistent with the amphipathic moment at the bacterial 331-338 position being dispensable in them.

The remaining retrieved papers, covering carotenoid engineering in Lipomyces starkeyi (PMID: 42121163), Komagataella phaffii (PMID: 42201412), tomato (PMID: 42051075) and tobacco (PMID: 41102519), are orientation only and carry no weight on the docking question.
