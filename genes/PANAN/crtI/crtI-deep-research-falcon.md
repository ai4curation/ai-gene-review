# Functional Annotation Report: **crtI / PaCrtI (UniProt P21685)**

## Executive summary

The identity check is positive. **P21685 is the bacterial gene product CrtI from *Pantoea ananatis* (historically *Erwinia uredovora*)**, not an unrelated protein sharing the symbol *crtI*. A 2024 study explicitly identifies P21685 as PaCrtI, while the foundational *E. uredovora* genetics and later *P. ananatis* biochemical work converge on the same function: a lycopene-forming, four-step phytoene desaturase. Its experimentally determined amino-oxidase-like flavoprotein structure also agrees with the supplied InterPro/Pfam assignments. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2, castanocerezo2024combiningsystemsand pages 4-5)

PaCrtI is a **peripheral membrane-associated, FAD-dependent oxidase/isomerase**. It converts membrane-embedded 15-cis-phytoene through phytofluene, ζ-carotene, and neurosporene into all-trans-lycopene, introducing four additional conjugated double bonds and accomplishing the necessary cis–trans isomerization. Oxygen is the principal terminal electron acceptor in the purified aerobic assay; suitable quinones can replace oxygen anaerobically. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 2-3, schaub2012onthestructure pages 3-5, schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2, misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2)

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

### Gene and organism

The symbol ***crtI*** matches the supplied protein description: bacterial/fungal-type, lycopene-forming phytoene desaturase, also called phytoene dehydrogenase or four-step phytoene desaturase. The original functional-genetics study analyzed *crtI* in *Erwinia uredovora* strain 20D3/ATCC 19321. Later biochemical and structural work identifies the organism under its accepted name, ***Pantoea ananatis***, and the 2024 in-vivo enzymology study explicitly identifies **P21685 as PaCrtI**. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2, castanocerezo2024combiningsystemsand pages 4-5)

This distinction matters because *crtI* is a generic pathway-gene symbol used for many bacterial and fungal phytoene desaturases. Results concerning *Pantoea agglomerans*, *Rhodobacter*, *Rubrivivax*, fungi, or other CrtI homologs should not automatically be assigned to P21685. For example, heterologous studies show that CrtI homologs can stop at neurosporene, extend desaturation beyond lycopene, or change product distributions with pathway context. Those findings illustrate family diversity but do not redefine P21685. (song2013heterologouscarotenoidbiosyntheticenzymes pages 1-2, song2013heterologouscarotenoidbiosyntheticenzymes pages 3-4)

### Family and domain consistency

The supplied Amino_oxidase/PF01593, carotenoid-retinoid oxidoreductase, and FAD/NAD-binding-superfamily annotations agree with crystallography. Apo-PaCrtI has an amino-oxidase-like flavoprotein fold organized into an FAD-binding region, a substrate-binding region, and a predominantly helical region implicated in membrane interaction. The structure contains 19 β-strands, 12 α-helices, and three 3₁₀-helices; PDB 4DGK was refined to 2.35 Å in the reported structural analysis. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 8-9, schaub2012onthestructure pages 5-6, schaub2012onthestructure pages 13-14)

Thus, the domain evidence supports—not merely predicts—the supplied functional annotation.

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### Catalyzed transformation

PaCrtI converts the colorless C40 carotene **15-cis-phytoene** to red **all-trans-lycopene**. Functional reconstruction in *E. coli* originally showed that one CrtI gene product conducts four sequential desaturations through:

**15-cis-phytoene → phytofluene → ζ-carotene → neurosporene → all-trans-lycopene.** (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2)

Purified-protein assays subsequently confirmed production of all-trans-lycopene from 15-cis-phytoene in phosphatidylcholine liposomes. Appreciable quantities of the intermediates did not accumulate in that assay, consistent with efficient repeated processing by the same enzyme. The overall transformation includes four dehydrogenations and a cis–trans isomerization. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 2-3, schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2)

### Substrate specificity

The best-supported physiological substrate is **15-cis-phytoene embedded in a lipid bilayer**. The requirement for a membrane-like presentation is biologically sensible because phytoene and later carotene intermediates are extremely hydrophobic. Purified soluble enzyme became highly active when presented with phytoene-containing phosphatidylcholine liposomes. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 2-3, schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2)

PaCrtI also has a conditional isomerase activity. Under anaerobic, reducing conditions, reduced-FAD enzyme isomerized tetra-cis-lycopene/prolycopene to tri-cis- and smaller amounts of other cis-lycopene species. It did not comparably isomerize 15-cis-phytoene under the tested CRTISO-like conditions. This is secondary mechanistic activity rather than evidence that cis-lycopene is its principal physiological substrate. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 3-5, schaub2012onthestructure pages 8-9)

Reported apparent parameters in the liposome assay were a phytoene **Kₘ below 16.7 ± 1.9 µM**, FAD **Kₘ of 50.5 ± 2.9 µM**, and **Vmax below 226 ± 19 pmol·min⁻¹** under the specified experimental conditions. The “below” qualification should be retained because substrate availability in a two-phase membrane system limits interpretation as conventional solution kinetics. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 2-3)

## 3. Cofactor, electron acceptor, and proposed mechanism

### FAD has catalytic and structural functions

FAD is the sole demonstrated redox-active cofactor; no additional metal or cofactor was required. Electrons removed during carotene desaturation pass to enzyme-associated FAD and then to a terminal acceptor. FAD is also required during productive membrane association: enzyme associated with membranes without FAD was inactive and could not simply be rescued later by adding FAD. This led the structural investigators to characterize FAD as both catalytic and architectural. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2, schaub2012onthestructure pages 6-8)

### Electron acceptors

Under aerobic assay conditions, molecular oxygen serves as the terminal acceptor, making PaCrtI operationally a **phytoene:oxygen oxidoreductase/oxidase**. Under anaerobic conditions, quinones can substitute. Benzoquinones generally performed better than naphthoquinones, with optimal midpoint potentials around 0–100 mV. Duroquinone and oxygen supported similar reported activity—approximately 2.2 versus 2.4 pmol lycopene·mg⁻¹·min⁻¹ in the cited comparison. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 3-5, schaub2012onthestructure pages 8-9, schaub2012onthestructure pages 6-8)

Whether oxygen or a membrane quinone is the dominant acceptor in native *P. ananatis* cells has not been established as firmly as the purified-enzyme result. The defensible annotation is therefore: oxygen is directly demonstrated in vitro, and quinones are competent alternatives under oxygen limitation.

### Catalytic interpretation

Structural docking places FAD’s isoalloxazine ring and the carotene chain within a shared tunnel-like cavity. Conserved residues including Asp149 and nearby arginines have been proposed to participate in base-assisted dehydrogenation or substrate polarization, while reduced FAD may stabilize a carbocation during isomerization. Histidine substitutions changed the extent/product specificity of desaturation without abolishing all activity. These residue-level mechanisms remain a combination of structural modeling and mutagenic inference rather than a fully resolved catalytic sequence. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 6-8, schaub2012onthestructure pages 13-14)

## 4. Cellular localization

PaCrtI is best annotated as a **cytoplasmic-side peripheral membrane protein**, not an integral transmembrane transporter or secreted enzyme. Purified CrtI is soluble and approximately 56.04 kDa as a monomer, but catalysis requires association with a lipid bilayer carrying its hydrophobic carotene substrate. Liposome reconstitution, membrane washing, FAD-dependent holoenzyme formation, and the structure’s helical membrane-interaction region collectively support this assignment. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 2-3, schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2, schaub2012onthestructure pages 5-6)

The precise native bacterial membrane leaflet and quantitative partitioning between soluble and membrane-associated pools have not been resolved in the retrieved studies. “Peripheral inner-membrane associated” is therefore a biologically likely interpretation, while “membrane-peripheral” is the directly demonstrated statement.

## 5. Pathway and biological role

### Position in carotenoid biosynthesis

CrtB first condenses geranylgeranyl diphosphate-derived precursors to phytoene. CrtI then creates lycopene’s extended conjugated system; CrtY can cyclize lycopene to β-carotene, after which hydroxylation and glycosylation generate downstream xanthophylls. Reconstruction experiments support this ordering: *crtE-B* produced phytoene, *crtE-B-I* produced lycopene, and *crtE-B-I-Y* produced β-carotene. (misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2, choi2021pantoeaananatiscarotenoid pages 8-9)

In *P. ananatis* PA13, carotenoid genes occur in a plasmid-borne **crtE–X–Y–I–B–Z** region. Cluster-level carotenoid synthesis is governed by an Hfq/quorum-sensing/RpoS network: AHL signaling relieves EanR repression, Hfq supports quorum signaling, and RpoS promotes carotenoid production. These are pathway-regulatory findings, not evidence that CrtI itself acts as a signaling protein. (choi2021pantoeaananatiscarotenoid pages 2-4, choi2021pantoeaananatiscarotenoid pages 8-9)

### Native physiological significance

Native *P. ananatis* carotenoid production confers tolerance to UV radiation and toxoflavin, consistent with carotenoids functioning as membrane-associated antioxidant and photoprotective pigments. However, those phenotypes were established at the carotenoid-pathway level; they should not be interpreted as isolated, CrtI-specific pleiotropic activities. CrtI’s precise contribution is catalytic: without phytoene desaturation, the conjugated colored carotenoids required for these protective effects cannot be formed. (choi2021pantoeaananatiscarotenoid pages 2-4, choi2021pantoeaananatiscarotenoid pages 8-9)

## 6. Recent developments, especially 2023–2024

The most important accession-specific recent development is Castaño-Cerezo et al., published in **September 2024** in *The EMBO Journal*. The investigators explicitly studied **P21685/PaCrtI** in engineered *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, alongside fungal desaturases, using strains spanning intracellular phytoene concentrations of **4–1,641 µM**, a 430-fold range. The work demonstrates a systems/synthetic-biology route to characterize hydrophobic, membrane-associated enzymes under intracellular conditions where conventional purified-enzyme parameters may not reflect physiology. (castanocerezo2024combiningsystemsand pages 4-5)

The accessible evidence does not supply a PaCrtI-specific in-vivo Kₘ, Vmax, or kcat from that paper, so such values should not be inferred. Its major current contribution is methodological: it places P21685 in a quantitative cellular context and highlights the importance of substrate availability, expression burden, membrane storage, and product toxicity when interpreting carotenoid-enzyme performance. (castanocerezo2024combiningsystemsand pages 4-5)

A **May 2024** biofortification review confirms continued importance of bacterial CrtI in Golden Rice. Golden Rice 2, combining bacterial CrtI with maize PSY, reportedly achieved a **23-fold increase in total carotenoids** relative to the original Golden Rice. This is a combined-pathway outcome and cannot be attributed to CrtI alone. [DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e30595](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e30595). (naik2024biofortificationasa pages 4-5)

Recent carotenoid-production research increasingly treats membrane capacity as an engineering variable. A 2024 *E. coli* study reported that chromosomal installation of an upstream isopentenol-utilization pathway increased specific lycopene yield approximately 2.0–2.1-fold, while membrane-lipid engineering raised lipid content by 31–33% but had context-dependent effects on lycopene. These data are relevant to PaCrtI deployment because its substrate, enzyme complex, and product are membrane-associated, but that study is production-system evidence rather than direct characterization of P21685.

## 7. Applications and real-world implementation

### Crop biofortification

Plastid-targeted bacterial CrtI replaces the plant requirement for separate PDS, ZDS, Z-ISO, and CRTISO activities between phytoene and lycopene. This compact function has enabled provitamin-A engineering in rice and has also been deployed experimentally in maize, tomato, potato, and tobacco. Its best-known implementation is Golden Rice, where CrtI operates with phytoene synthase and endogenous downstream cyclization to produce β-carotene in endosperm. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2, naik2024biofortificationasa pages 4-5)

### Transient pathway engineering

A January 2017 viral-vector study expressed the *P. ananatis* **crtE, crtB, and crtI** pathway in tobacco leaves and obtained approximately **150 µg lycopene·g⁻¹ dry weight**. This demonstrates rapid, transient rewiring of plant metabolism, although the yield is attributable to the three-enzyme pathway rather than CrtI in isolation. [DOI: 10.1038/srep41645](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep41645). (majer2017rewiringcarotenoidbiosynthesis pages 6-6)

### Microbial production and pathway design

PaCrtI is widely useful as a synthetic-biology module because one enzyme supplies all four phytoene desaturation steps. In engineered bacteria and yeast it links phytoene production to lycopene and downstream β-carotene/xanthophyll synthesis. Nevertheless, product distributions cannot always be predicted from the isolated enzyme alone: enzyme expression level, membrane environment, competing lycopene cyclases, precursor supply, and heterologous partner enzymes can alter intermediate accumulation and even promote over-desaturated products in related systems. (castanocerezo2024combiningsystemsand pages 4-5, song2013heterologouscarotenoidbiosyntheticenzymes pages 1-2)

### Selectable-marker concept

Bacterial CrtI can provide resistance to bleaching herbicides that inhibit plant/algal PDS. A 2019 microalgal study using a codon-optimized bacterial *crtI* reported an average transformation efficiency of approximately 550 colonies·µg⁻¹ DNA under norflurazon selection. This supports the enzyme-class application, but the available evidence does not establish that the construct was sequence-identical to P21685; it should therefore be regarded as CrtI-family rather than accession-specific implementation.

## 8. Expert assessment and annotation confidence

**High-confidence annotation:** P21685 is a four-step, lycopene-forming phytoene desaturase/oxidase-isomerase. This conclusion rests on historical gene-by-gene pathway reconstruction, purified-protein catalysis, liposome reconstitution, cofactor and electron-acceptor experiments, and crystallography. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 2-3, schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2, schaub2012onthestructure pages 5-6, misawa1990elucidationofthe pages 1-2)

**High-confidence localization:** peripheral association with carotenoid-containing membranes. The evidence does not support secretion or an integral multipass-membrane topology. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 2-3, schaub2012onthestructure pages 1-2)

**Moderate-confidence native electron-transfer annotation:** oxygen is directly established in vitro, and quinones are competent anaerobic acceptors; their relative contributions in native *P. ananatis* remain uncertain. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 3-5, schaub2012onthestructure pages 8-9)

**Moderate-to-low-confidence residue mechanism:** the tunnel architecture, docking, isotope chemistry, and mutagenesis support acid–base/FAD-mediated desaturation and reduced-FAD-assisted isomerization, but individual catalytic assignments remain provisional. (schaub2012onthestructure pages 6-8, schaub2012onthestructure pages 13-14)

**Important evidence boundary:** stress tolerance, Golden Rice carotenoid levels, and engineered lycopene titers are pathway-level phenotypes. They demonstrate the practical value of CrtI but do not measure the isolated contribution of P21685 independently of precursor supply and downstream enzymes.
