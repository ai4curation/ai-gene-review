Gene Description

bop encodes bacterio-opsin, the apoprotein of bacteriorhodopsin, the light-driven outward proton pump of the extremely halophilic archaeon Halobacterium salinarum. The protein is made as a precursor with a 13-residue N-terminal propeptide and folds into seven transmembrane helices. Retinal is bound covalently as a protonated Schiff base to Lys216 (mature numbering). Bacteriorhodopsin is the only protein of the purple membrane, a specialised patch of the cell membrane in which homotrimers form a two-dimensional crystalline lattice with archaeal lipids. Light isomerises the all-trans retinal to 13-cis and drives a photocycle in which the Schiff base proton is passed to Asp85 and released to the outside, and the Schiff base is then reprotonated from the cytoplasm through Asp96. Each photocycle moves one proton out of the cell. The resulting proton motive force drives ATP synthesis by the cell's ATP synthase and lets cells grow phototrophically when oxygen is scarce; bop expression is induced by low oxygen and light through the Bat activator. The light-dependent change in proton motive force can also be sensed by the cell, so bacteriorhodopsin contributes a secondary photoreceptor input to phototaxis. Bacteriorhodopsin is among the most intensively studied membrane proteins, with more than a hundred crystal and cryo-EM structures, including time-resolved structures of photocycle intermediates.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

light-driven proton transmembrane transporter activity

Definition: Primary active transport of a proton across a membrane against its electrochemical gradient, driven by the energy of absorbed light, for example by a retinal-containing microbial rhodopsin.

Justification: GO:0015454 light-driven active monoatomic ion transmembrane transporter activity has no children, so the proton pumps (bacteriorhodopsin, proteorhodopsin, xanthorhodopsin) cannot be distinguished from the chloride pump halorhodopsin or the sodium-pumping rhodopsins. The activity of bacteriorhodopsin is fully characterised; currently the ion specificity has to be expressed by a second term (GO:0015078). A proton-specific child of GO:0015454 (and of GO:0015078) would also be the natural target for InterPro2GO mappings of the proton-pumping rhodopsin families, replacing the incorrect channel mapping.

Parent term: light-driven active monoatomic ion transmembrane transporter activity

Core Functions

Light-driven outward proton pump of the cell membrane. Absorption of a photon by the covalently bound retinal (Schiff base on Lys216) starts a photocycle in which the Schiff base proton is transferred to Asp85 and released to the outside, and the Schiff base is reprotonated from the cytoplasm via Asp96. One proton is moved out per cycle. The resulting proton motive force drives ATP synthesis by ATP synthase and supports phototrophic growth when oxygen is low.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should the InterPro2GO mapping of IPR018229 (rhodopsin retinal-binding site) to GO:0005216 monoatomic ion channel activity be withdrawn? It places channel activity on bacteriorhodopsin, halorhodopsins, sensory rhodopsins and proteorhodopsin, none of which is a channel; only the eukaryotic channelrhodopsins are.

Q: Should GO have a cellular component term for the purple membrane, the crystalline bacteriorhodopsin-lipid domain of the haloarchaeal cell membrane, or is plasma membrane the intended level for such membrane domains?

Q: Should a light-driven proton pump that generates the proton motive force be annotated to GO:0042777 proton motive force-driven plasma membrane ATP synthesis? The term definition includes the proton transport that builds the gradient, but experimental annotations are on ATP synthase subunits (and Pyrococcus Mbh), so the convention appears to restrict the term to the synthase.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Compare phototrophic growth and ATP levels of an in-frame bop deletion in H. salinarum NRC-1 with the parent strain under low oxygen and illumination, to give a genetic (IMP) basis for the physiological role that is so far supported mainly by biochemistry and by older strain comparisons.

- description: >-
    Light-driven outward proton pump of the cell membrane. Absorption of a photon by

    Schiff base is reprotonated from the cytoplasm via Asp96. One proton is moved out
    per cycle. The resulting proton motive force drives ATP synthesis by ATP synthase
    and supports phototrophic growth when oxygen is low.
