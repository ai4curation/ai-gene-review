CC   -!- FUNCTION: Energy-dependent efflux pump responsible for decreased drug
CC       accumulation in multidrug-resistant cells (PubMed:35507548,
CC       PubMed:37527341). Transports lumefantrine, mefloquine, chloroquine,
CC       quinine, quinidine, amodiaquine, piperaquine, dihydroartemisinin and
CC       quinacrine (PubMed:35507548). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:35507548,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:37527341}.

CC         ECO:0000269|PubMed:37527341};

CC   -!- DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE: Expressed throughout the erythrocytic asexual life
CC       cycle with increasing amount at mature stages (at protein level)
CC       (PubMed:1674943). Detected in gametocyte stages (at protein level)
CC       (PubMed:1674943). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:1674943}.
CC   -!- POLYMORPHISM: Protein variants from field strains differ significantly
CC       in their capacities for antimalarial drug transport in vitro: variants
CC       prevalent in chloroquine-resistant isolates exhibit reduced capacities
CC       for chloroquine, lumefantrine and mefloquine transport
CC       (PubMed:35507548). Transporter from 7G8 strain shows significantly
CC       lower levels of transport activity for lumefantrine, mefloquine,
CC       chloroquine, quinine, quinidine, amodiaquine, piperaquine,
CC       dihydroartemisinin and quinacrine compared to 3D7 strain
CC       (PubMed:35507548). Reduced transporter activity for lumefantrine,
CC       mefloquine, chloroquine, quinine, quinidine, amodiaquine, piperaquine,
CC       dihydroartemisinin and quinacrine is observed for the proteins from Dd2
CC       and GB4 isolates (PubMed:35507548). Increased transporter activity for
CC       quinine and quinidine and reduced activity for lumefantrine,
CC       mefloquine, chloroquine, amodiaquine, piperaquine, dihydroartemisinin
CC       and quinacrine is observed for HB3 strain transporter compared to 3D7
CC       strain (PubMed:35507548). Strong positive correlation between
CC       lumefantrine and mefloquine transport rates by the protein and parasite
CC       drug resistance indices for these antimalarials is observed
CC       (PubMed:35507548). Strong negative correlation between chloroquine
CC       transport rates by the protein and parasite drug resistance indices for
CC       this antimalarial is observed (PubMed:35507548). No correlation between
CC       quinine transport rates by the protein and parasite drug resistance
CC       indices for this antimalarial is observed (PubMed:35507548).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:35507548}.
CC   -!- MISCELLANEOUS: Can transport human ABCB1 substrates rhodamine B and
CC       vinblastine (PubMed:35507548). Does not transport amantadine
CC       (PubMed:35507548). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:35507548}.
