CC   -!- FUNCTION: During the asexual blood stage, involved in merozoite egress
CC       from host erythrocytes possibly via its interaction with the host
CC       cytoskeleton protein spectrin resulting in the destabilization of the
CC       host cytoskeleton and thus leading to erythrocyte cell membrane rupture
CC       (PubMed:26468747). Involved in the binding to host erythrocytes and is
CC       required for host erythrocyte invasion (PubMed:12692305,
CC       PubMed:21175202, PubMed:25778531, PubMed:36202833).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12692305, ECO:0000269|PubMed:21175202,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25778531, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26468747,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:36202833}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [p33 subunit]: By binding to host proinflammatory cytokine
CC       S100P may interfere with host immune responses.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:22431641}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: [p19 subunit]: Involved in merozoite invasion of host
CC       erythrocytes (PubMed:1694225, PubMed:29511044). May play a role in the
CC       biogenesis and/or function of the food vacuole during the
CC       intraerythrocytic development (Probable). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:1694225,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29511044, ECO:0000305|PubMed:18769730}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Forms a complex composed of subunits p83, p30, p38, and p42
CC       which remain non-covalently associated; the complex is formed at the
CC       merozoite surface prior to egress from host erythrocytes
CC       (PubMed:16940297, PubMed:17097159, PubMed:34078606). Forms a complex
CC       composed of processed MSP1 subunits, MSP6 subunit p36 and MSP7; the
CC       complex is formed at the merozoite surface prior to egress from host
CC       erythrocytes (PubMed:16940297). Within the complex, interacts (via
CC       subunit p38) with MSP6 subunit p36 and (via subunits p83, p30 and p38)
CC       with MSP7 (via subunit p22) (PubMed:16940297, PubMed:17097159,
CC       PubMed:18769730). Forms a complex composed of MSP1, MSP6, DBLMSP1 and
CC       DBLMSP2 (PubMed:25074930). Within the complex, interacts (via subunit
CC       p38) with DBLMSP1 and DBLMSP2 (PubMed:25074930). Forms a complex
CC       composed of MSP1, and rhoptry proteins RhopH3, RAP1 and CLAG9/RhopH3
CC       (PubMed:21175202). Within the complex, interacts (via subunits p42 and
CC       p19) with RhopH3 (via C-terminus) (PubMed:21175202). Forms a complex
CC       composed of MSP1, MSP6, MSP7, MSP9 and MSP3; within the complex, MSP6
CC       and MSP9 mediate the binding to the host erythrocyte (PubMed:29511044).
CC       Interacts (via subunits p19 and p42) with MSP9; the interaction is
CC       direct; MSP1 subunits p19 or p42, and MSP9 form a co-ligand complex
CC       that interacts with host SLC4A1/Band 3 protein (PubMed:14630931). May
CC       interact with PFD6 (PubMed:33145997). Interacts with host spectrin
CC       (PubMed:26468747, PubMed:34078606). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:14630931,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16940297, ECO:0000269|PubMed:17097159,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:18769730, ECO:0000269|PubMed:21175202,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25074930, ECO:0000269|PubMed:26468747,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:29511044, ECO:0000269|PubMed:33145997,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:34078606}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: [p83 subunit]: Interacts with host glycophorin GYPA in a
CC       sialic acid-independent manner. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:25778531}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: [p33 subunit]: Interacts with host proinflammatory cytokine
CC       S100P; the interaction blocks S100P inflammatory and chemotactic
CC       activities. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:22431641}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: [p42 subunit]: Interacts with host SLC4A1/Band 3 (via 5ABC
CC       region) on the host erythrocyte surface in a sialic acid-independent
CC       manner. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:12692305}.

CC   -!- PTM: The p190 precursor is cleaved by SUB1 prior to merozoite egress
CC       into 4 subunits p83, p30, p38, and p42 which remain non-covalently
CC       associated (PubMed:16940297, PubMed:17097159, PubMed:19214190,
CC       PubMed:20735778, PubMed:26468747, PubMed:34078606). SUB1-mediated
CC       proteolytic cleavage occurs in an orderly manner; the first cleavage
CC       occurs at the p30/p38 site, followed by cleavage at the p83/p30 site,
CC       in the 3D7 strain a second cleavage occurs at the N-terminus of p83,
CC       the last cleavage occurs at the p38/p42 site (PubMed:20735778). The
CC       order of cleavage is essential for parasite viability
CC       (PubMed:20735778). SUB1-mediated processing is essential for merozoite
CC       egress (PubMed:19214190, PubMed:26468747). In a second processing step
CC       during erythrocyte invasion, p42 is cleaved by SUB2 into p33 and p19;
CC       the latter remains attached to the merozoite surface via its GPI-anchor
CC       and is endocytosed during the subsequent ring stage (PubMed:16322767,
CC       PubMed:1694225, PubMed:18769730). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:16322767,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16940297, ECO:0000269|PubMed:1694225,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:17097159, ECO:0000269|PubMed:18769730,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:19214190, ECO:0000269|PubMed:20735778,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:26468747, ECO:0000269|PubMed:34078606}.
CC   -!- POLYMORPHISM: The sequence varies across Plasmodium strains
CC       (PubMed:34078606). There are two major dimorphic forms of MSP1,
CC       typified by those expressed by the 3D7 and Wellcome P.falciparum
CC       isolates (PubMed:20735778). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:20735778,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:34078606}.
CC   -!- BIOTECHNOLOGY: [p42 subunit]: Potential target for heparin-based
CC       inhibitors. Binding of heparin-like molecules to p42 subunit inhibits
CC       parasite invasion of host erythrocytes. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:20220119}.
CC   -!- BIOTECHNOLOGY: [p19 subunit]: Potential candidate for the development
CC       of parasite blood stage vaccines (PubMed:29511044, PubMed:36202833). In
CC       vitro, neutralizing antibodies against strain-conserved regions of the
CC       p19 subunit are capable of inhibiting parasite growth in host
CC       erythrocytes (PubMed:36202833). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:29511044,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:36202833}.
