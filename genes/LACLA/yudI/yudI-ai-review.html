Gene Description

YudI is the only tRNA-dihydrouridine synthase (Dus, COG0042) of Lactococcus lactis, a cytoplasmic FMN-dependent flavoenzyme of the DusB subfamily that reduces the C5-C6 double bond of specific uridines in the tRNA D-loop to dihydrouridine, using NADPH or NADH as the electron source. It keeps the catalytic cysteine (Cys111, equivalent to E. coli DusB Cys100) and all FMN-binding residues of E. coli DusB. Among Firmicute Dus proteins it is closest to Bacillus subtilis Dus1 (YacF), and so probably belongs to the DusB1 subgroup that Mollicutes also retained. In E. coli DusB makes D17 only, whereas the single DusB of Mycoplasma capricolum makes D17, D20 and D20a; because L. lactis has no other Dus paralog, YudI may likewise modify several D-loop positions, but the positions it modifies have not been determined. Expression of the KF147 orthologue correlates positively with heat-stress robustness across L. lactis strains, and a Lactobacillus paracasei transposon mutant in the orthologue was picked up in a heat-stress screen, but no direct physiological role has been shown.

Core Functions

FMN-dependent, NAD(P)H-driven reduction of specific uridines in the tRNA D-loop to dihydrouridine. As the only Dus of L. lactis it is expected to make all D residues in lactococcal tRNA; the positions (U17 and possibly U20/U20a) are not determined.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the single L. lactis Dus, like M. capricolum DusB, make D20 and D20a as well as D17, or do lactococcal tRNAs lack D at some positions?

Q: Is the positive correlation between yudI expression and heat-stress survival causal, for example through tRNA D-loop flexibility at elevated temperature?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Map dihydrouridines in tRNAs of L. lactis wild type and a yudI deletion (AlkAnilineSeq, Rho-seq or LC-MS of tRNA digests) to define the positions the enzyme modifies.

Type: tRNA modification mapping

Experiment: Express YudI in an E. coli dusA dusB dusC triple mutant and assay D17 and D20/D20a restoration, as done for M. capricolum DusB.

Type: heterologous complementation

Experiment: Compare heat-stress survival of a yudI deletion with wild type and the complemented strain in L. lactis MG1363 or KF147.

Type: mutant phenotyping

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: The dihydrouridine positions in L. lactis tRNAs, and which of them YudI makes, are unknown. E. coli DusB is mono-site (U17) while the single Mollicute DusB is multisite (U17, U20, U20a); L. lactis, with one Dus, could resemble either.

BIOLOGY

Gap: The link between yudI and heat-stress survival rests on an expression correlation in L. lactis and a transposon screen in L. paracasei; no deletion phenotype or mechanism has been reported for any lactococcal Dus.

Q9CE84 is closest to E. coli DusB of the three E. coli enzymes, consistent with

M. capricolum DusB, the only Dus of a Mollicute and the one experimentally shown to
make D17, D20 and D20a (PMID:33685366), is also much closer to B. subtilis Dus1 (46.4%)

lost DusB2. Taken together, the lactococcal protein most likely belongs to the
Firmicute DusB1 subgroup, the same subgroup as the multisite M. capricolum enzyme.
This assignment is inferred from pairwise identity only; we could not access the
Faivre et al. full text to check which B. subtilis paralog they place in each
subgroup, and no phylogeny was built here.

3. Number of Dus enzymes in Lactococcus

UniProt reference proteomes searched for Pfam PF01207 or InterPro IPR001269/IPR035587:

  • IL1403 (UP000002196): Q9CE84 only.
  • MG1363 (UP000000364): A2RN88 (llmg_2209) only.

L. lactis therefore encodes a single Dus enzyme, like Mollicutes and unlike
B. subtilis (two) or E. coli (three). If lactococcal tRNAs carry D at more than one
position (D17, D20, D20a are the bacterial sites), this one enzyme would have to make
all of them. No tRNA modification map exists for L. lactis, so this is an expectation,
not an observation.
