Gene Description

SoxB is the 405-residue beta subunit and catalytic flavoprotein of the cytoplasmic heterotetrameric sarcosine oxidase (SoxABGD) of Corynebacterium sp. U-96, a member of the D-amino-acid-oxidase-like SoxB family. It binds one non-covalent FAD, at which sarcosine is oxidised to an iminium intermediate. Dimethylglycine and the inhibitors methylthioacetate and pyrrole-2-carboxylate are bound ligands in structures; their binding does not establish turnover. It also binds one FMN covalently attached to a histidine through its 8-alpha methyl group, which is reoxidised by molecular oxygen to give hydrogen peroxide. The two flavins lie about 10 A apart. The iminium intermediate is channelled to the folate site of the alpha subunit, where, with tetrahydrofolate present, glycine and 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate are formed (EC 1.5.3.24); without folate, glycine and formaldehyde are released. The soxB gene lies in a cluster with genes for serine hydroxymethyltransferase, serine dehydratase and 10-formyltetrahydrofolate hydrolase for catabolism of sarcosine to pyruvate.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

sarcosine oxidase (5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate-forming) activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: sarcosine + (6S)-5,6,7,8-tetrahydrofolate + O2 = (6R)-5,10-methylene-5,6,7,8-tetrahydrofolate + glycine + H2O2.

Justification: This proposal describes the complete folate-coupled reaction of heterotetrameric SoxABDG (EC 1.5.3.24; RHEA:70455), distinct from the glycine/formaldehyde-producing GO:0008115 reaction. It is an ontology proposal for the assembled complex, not the activity of each isolated subunit. The schema permits proposed_molecular_function together with contributes_to_molecular_function, but that syntactic permission does not make a complex reaction subunit-specific. The core therefore retains contribution to GO:0016647; beta cores additionally identify the separately defined imine-forming catalytic subreaction. Other subunits retain only their supported own functions. Structural and biochemical evidence comes from the characterized U-96 heterotetramer; isolated-subunit catalysis is not inferred.

sarcosine oxidase (imine-forming) activity

Definition: Catalysis of oxygen-dependent oxidation of sarcosine to the corresponding sarcosine imine, with reduction of molecular oxygen to hydrogen peroxide. In heterotetrameric sarcosine oxidase, this catalytic subreaction is localized to beta-bound FAD and FMN and is established in the assembled enzyme. It excludes subsequent imine hydrolysis and transfer of the one-carbon fragment to tetrahydrofolate.

Justification: Sarcosine reduces beta-bound FAD to FADH2; the two electrons pass through beta-bound FMN to reduce one O2 to H2O2. This defines the oxidation step before alpha-associated processing of the imine. GO:0008115 additionally specifies glycine/formaldehyde production, while the separate folate-coupled proposal specifies the complete heterotetramer reaction. Neither is a precise name for this beta-localized catalytic subreaction. The activity proposal does not assert that an isolated beta preparation is catalytically competent. The U-96 structural/kinetic evidence maps the oxidation sites to this beta subunit.

Core Functions

Supplies the beta-localized catalytic oxidation of sarcosine to its imine at FAD, followed by electron transfer through FMN to oxygen, forming hydrogen peroxide within the assembled sarcosine oxidase. The proposed imine-forming activity records this catalytic subreaction, not autonomous activity of purified beta. The separate contributes_to function records participation in collective enzyme turnover. Subsequent alpha-associated folate capture yields glycine and 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate; it is excluded from the beta-specific proposal. The U-96 structural/kinetic evidence maps the oxidation sites to this beta subunit. Isolated beta and beta/delta constructs lacked flavin under the reported U-96 expression conditions, so active-site localization does not prove stand-alone turnover.
