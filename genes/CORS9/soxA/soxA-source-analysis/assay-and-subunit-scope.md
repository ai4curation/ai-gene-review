# U-96 sarcosine oxidase assay and subunit scope

Audit 2026-11-06. PDBe/SIFTS 1VRQ and3AD7 map the deposited proteins to the frozen Q50LF0/Q50LF2/Q50LE9/Q50LF1 sequences at identity1.0 over their mapped regions. Sources are Corynebacterium sp.U-96; expression hosts are E. coli (BL21(DE3) recorded for1VRQ). Alpha and beta chains omit the native initiating methionine, explaining paper beta Lys171/Lys358 versus UniProt Lys172/Lys359. Gamma has incomplete mapped N-terminal coverage; no full-length sequence equality is invented. See structure-identity.yaml for exact mapping spans and source provenance.

The inherited ligand inventory remains valid: NAD on alpha, FAD and covalent FMN on beta, zinc on delta, and no Fe-S cluster. The independent cofactor study PMID11902668 supports the Fe-S rejection; a ferredoxin-like fold alone is not a cluster assignment.

PMID17395614 measures effects of beta-site substitutions on substrate reduction and oxygen reoxidation of the heterotetramer. These are catalytic-subunit evidence beyond simply locating a flavin. They do not establish that isolated beta protein completes the net sarcosine oxidation reaction. PMID15914915 reports low soluble expression of beta and beta-delta, not an unambiguous isolated-subunit activity result in its accessible abstract. The full PMID15914915 publisher PDF was subsequently obtained by the PSEPK reviewer. Its scoped assembly-evidence.md excerpt documents that the isolated beta/beta-delta preparations lacked bound flavin and became insoluble on further purification. This is construct-dependent evidence of assembly/cofactor-loading problems, not an impossibility theorem. Other target full texts remain unavailable where documented.

PMID20675294 describes methylthioacetate and pyrrole-2-carboxylate as bound inhibitors, and PMID15946648 describes bound dimethylglycine. These ligand structures do not establish turnover of these molecules by SoxB. The substrate claim is sarcosine oxidation.

The folate-coupled reaction belongs to the assembled heterotetramer. PMID9185627 reports faster oxidation and enzymatic methylenetetrahydrofolate formation by heterotetramers; monomeric enzymes yield free formaldehyde that may react nonenzymatically with folate. Thus lack of a dedicated folate-coupled activity does not mean that no methylenetetrahydrofolate can ever be detected in a monomeric-enzyme mixture. Target/strain identity of each enzyme in that abstract must not be inferred from the family comparison.
