Gene Description

SoxG is the 205-residue gamma subunit of the cytoplasmic heterotetrameric sarcosine oxidase (SoxABGD) of Corynebacterium sp. U-96. It has a GcvT/TrmE-like folate-binding-domain fold and contacts both domains of the alpha subunit, but no cofactor or ligand has been located on it. The assembled complex oxidises sarcosine at the FAD of the beta subunit, reoxidises its flavins with oxygen to give hydrogen peroxide and, with tetrahydrofolate present, transfers the methylene group to tetrahydrofolate at a site in the alpha subunit, yielding glycine and 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate (EC 1.5.3.24); without folate it releases formaldehyde. The specific contribution of SoxG to folate binding, complex assembly or catalysis has not been determined. The soxG gene follows soxA in a cluster of sarcosine-to-pyruvate catabolism genes.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

sarcosine oxidase (5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate-forming) activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: sarcosine + (6S)-5,6,7,8-tetrahydrofolate + O2 = (6R)-5,10-methylene-5,6,7,8-tetrahydrofolate + glycine + H2O2.

Justification: This proposal describes the complete folate-coupled reaction of heterotetrameric SoxABDG (EC 1.5.3.24; RHEA:70455), distinct from the glycine/formaldehyde-producing GO:0008115 reaction. It is an ontology proposal for the assembled complex, not the activity of each isolated subunit. Structural and biochemical evidence comes from the characterized U-96 heterotetramer; isolated-subunit catalysis is not inferred.

Parent term: oxidoreductase activity, acting on the CH-NH group of donors, oxygen as acceptor

Core Functions

Contribute as the gamma accessory subunit to oxygen-dependent sarcosine oxidation in the assembled heterotetramer. The collective folate-coupled reaction yields glycine and methylenetetrahydrofolate; it is not assigned as an autonomous subunit activity. The folate-free formaldehyde-forming branch remains a conditional secondary complex activity.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does SoxG bind tetrahydrofolate or its polyglutamates, given its predicted folate-binding fold, or is the alpha-subunit site the only folate site of the complex?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express the heterotetramer without SoxG (or with SoxG interface mutants) and measure assembly, the Km for tetrahydropteroyltriglutamate and the ratio of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to formaldehyde formed; test isolated SoxG for folate binding by isothermal titration calorimetry.

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: The function of the gamma subunit within the heterotetramer is undetermined: whether its GcvT/TrmE-like fold binds folate (or polyglutamylated folate), stabilises the alpha-subunit folate channel, or is needed only for assembly.

BIOLOGY

What is known: SoxG is an established subunit of the crystallised heterotetramer and contacts both domains of the alpha subunit; no ligand is bound to it in six crystal structures.
