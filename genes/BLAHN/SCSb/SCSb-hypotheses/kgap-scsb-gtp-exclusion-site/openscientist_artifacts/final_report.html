Where GTP is excluded from Blastocystis succinyl-CoA synthetase: entrance, site, or both

Target: SCSb, UniProt B3FHP0, Blastocystis sp. ATCC 50177/Nand II (NCBITaxon:478820) Focus: free text, hypothesis slug kgap-scsb-gtp-exclusion-site, source genes/BLAHN/SCSb/SCSb-ai-review.yaml Analysis type: structural and electrostatic computation on public coordinates, plus primary literature retrieval. No new experimental data.

Summary

Unresolved, leaning to exclusion inside the site. The question the seed poses can be answered in part by calculation and cannot be closed by any published measurement. Three things are now established by direct computation on the deposited coordinates. The nucleotide site of this enzyme is a closed pocket in every crystal form, with the adenine 87.1 % buried and no opening wider than a 2.3 Å probe, so the crystallographic channel is a solvent opening and the ATP-grasp fold must open for a nucleotide to enter at all. Lys46β and Lys114β do not line that opening: their side-chain tips sit 11.6 and 13.6 Å off the channel axis and 7.44 and 5.78 Å from the base, while the charges nearest the axis are Lys9 at 4.45 Å and Lys50 at 5.04 Å. And the electrostatic effect their replacement produces is base-blind. ATP and GTP carry identical net charge, so the 0.66 kT/e shift in mouth potential that the K46βE/K114βD swap creates applies equally to both. The only base-specific electrostatic term available at the mouth is the difference between the guanine and adenine dipoles, and over 40 combinations of dielectric, ionic strength and evaluation distance it never reaches 1 kT. Its ceiling is 0.358 kT, equivalent to 1.43-fold discrimination, against the complete exclusion the wild-type enzyme shows.

An entrance gate of this geometry therefore buys affinity, not selectivity, and the published kinetics show exactly that. In the ED mutant the ATP Michaelis constant rises from 145 to 230 µM with kcat unchanged, which is the signature of a change in approach or association. The one measurement that would decide between the two mechanisms, wild-type turnover with GTP, is reported as "not detected" with no Km and no kcat (PMID: 27478903, Table 1), so a raised Km and an abolished kcat cannot be told apart. No structure of the enzyme with a guanine nucleotide exists, in wild type or in the double mutant, which crystallised only with Mg-ADP (PMID: 31282474).

Comparing the pocket with a real GTP-specific enzyme supplies a competing account of the mutagenesis. After superposing the purine rings of ADP in 6NO0 and GTP in pig SCS 2FP4 (9 atoms, RMSD 0.040 Å), five of the ten residues lining the base differ between the two enzymes, the pig site carries two more units of negative charge at every radius from 8 to 15 Å around the base, and three of the four substitutions that reverse specificity install the exact residue the pig enzyme carries at the structurally equivalent position: K46βE gives pig Glu42, V113βL gives pig Leu109, L227βF gives pig Phe219. The quadruple change is close to a partial conversion of the Blastocystis site into a GTP-using architecture, in the second shell as well as at two base contacts. Nothing in this analysis supports a GO annotation change, and none is proposed. The curation action it supports is narrow: soften any free text that states the entrance mechanism as settled.

Key findings

The two lysines do not line the path, and the pocket is closed

Ray casting from the adenine centroid of 6NO0, with atomic radii and a Shrake-Rupley surface, gives an open solid angle of 4.0 % of 4π at a 2.0 Å probe (cone half-angle 72°) and zero at 2.4 Å and above. The adenine is 87.1 % buried and the whole ADP 78.2 % buried. The channel leaves the protein envelope, defined as more than 5 Å clearance from any protein atom, at 4.0 Å from the adenine centroid. That point is the mouth used for all the electrostatic comparisons below.

Lys46 NZ and Lys114 NZ sit 12.9 and 14.0 Å from the adenine centroid and 11.6 and 13.6 Å off the channel axis. The charged groups nearest the axis are Lys9 at 4.45 Å and Lys50 at 5.04 Å, and Lys50 makes direct contact with the ADP phosphates at 2.71 Å. The two residues named by the gatekeeper hypothesis are peripheral to the opening by more than 11 Å. This is consistent with the companion run's measurement of 6.98 and 5.21 Å from the bound ADP for the two side chains against 1.77 Å for Gln20, and adds the geometric point that the perpendicular offset from the entry axis is larger still.

A charged mouth shifts affinity by the same amount for ATP and GTP

Debye-Hückel screened potential at the mouth point (ε = 78.5, 150 mM, 298 K, formal charges on side-chain tip atoms) is +0.517 kcal/mol/e in wild type and +0.127 in the double mutant, a change of −0.390 kcal/mol/e, which is −0.66 kT/e. Residues 46 and 114 together contribute 102 % of that change, so the substitutions are the whole effect and the rest of the protein cancels. For a nucleoside triphosphate (net charge −3) the interaction energy changes by 1.17 kcal/mol, and for a diphosphate (−2) by 0.78 kcal/mol. Both nucleotides have the same net charge, so this number is identical for ATP and GTP and cannot discriminate between them.

The one electrostatic term that can discriminate is the base dipole. Guanine carries 6.6 D against adenine's 2.5 D. With the computed axial field of 0.0250 kcal/(mol·e·Å), the difference is 0.021 kcal/mol (0.036 kT) for a perfectly aligned dipole and 0.0006 kcal/mol (0.001 kT) after rotational averaging, 55-fold below the base-blind term at best alignment.

The ceiling on mouth discrimination is 1.4-fold

Repeating the comparison over 40 settings removes the suspicion that the result depends on one choice of parameters: dielectric 78.5 and 40, ionic strength 0, 50, 150 and 300 mM, evaluation points at 2, 4, 6, 8 and 12 Å along the exit path, using the full field vector rather than its axial component, with charge sets from the superposed 6NO0, 6NO1 (K46βE), 6NO2 (K114βD) and 6NO5 (K46βE/K114βD). The base-blind charge term exceeds the best-case dipole term in every setting, by 5.8-fold at worst, 24-fold at the median and 103-fold at best. The absolute bound matters more than the ratio: the dipole term never reaches 1 kT, with a maximum over all settings of 0.2125 kcal/mol = 0.358 kT. That is 1.43-fold selectivity for a perfectly aligned base dipole, less after rotational averaging. The wild-type enzyme discriminates completely, ATP kcat/Km 96 M⁻¹s⁻¹ against GTP not detected.

The mouth potential falls monotonically with the substitutions, as expected when the two residues dominate the local field: wild type +0.87 kT/e, K46βE +0.52, K114βD +0.32, K46βE/K114βD +0.21.

A mechanism with a ceiling of 1.4-fold cannot be the source of complete exclusion. This refutes long-range electrostatic steering as a sufficient explanation of base selectivity in this enzyme, while leaving intact the observation that the two residues perturb selection somehow.

The alpha subunit is 20 Å from the path, which removes the main structural caveat

All seven Blastocystis structures (6NO0 to 6NO6) are of the isolated beta nucleotide-binding domain, which invites the objection that the computed channel is an artefact of a missing subunit. Measuring the pig holoenzyme 2FP4, which has alpha chain A (304 residues) and beta chain B (393 residues) with Mg-GTP bound to beta, settles it. The closest alpha atom to any GTP atom is 11.92 Å and zero alpha atoms lie within 15 Å of the guanine centroid. Ray casting gives identical results with and without the alpha subunit: 8.75 % open solid angle at a 1.4 Å probe, 0.92 % at 2.0 Å, zero at 2.4 Å and above, GTP 73.6 % buried and guanine 87.4 % buried in both cases. Along the exit path the nearest alpha atom stays between 20.1 and 22.0 Å at every point from 2 to 13 Å out.

The pocket, its rim and its solvent channel are built entirely by the beta subunit. The degree of enclosure is also conserved: the ATP-specific and the GTP-specific enzyme bury their base to 87.1 % and 87.4 % and admit nothing wider than a 2.3 Å probe, so lid opening is required in both. What the crystallographic channel describes is the closed state, not the trajectory of a nucleotide into an open ATP-grasp fold.

Gln20β is present in both enzymes and reads the two bases with opposite polarity

Superposing the purine rings of ADP in 6NO0 chain A and GTP in 2FP4 chain B (9 ring atoms, RMSD 0.040 Å) puts both pockets in one frame without needing a sequence alignment.

The same glutamine, at the same beta-subunit position 20, is the closest residue to the base in both enzymes, at 2.6 Å in each. The hydrogen bonds are inverted: an acceptor presented to the adenine N6 donor in one, a donor presented to the guanine O6 acceptor in the other. After ring superposition the Gln20 backbone N and CA are displaced 3.19 and 3.02 Å between the two enzymes while the amide carbon sits within 0.68 Å of the same point, with OE1 and NE2 exchanged by about 1.7 Å. This is a direct measurement of what PMID: 31282474 states in words: the discriminating variable is the conformation of the Gln20 backbone and side chain together with the linker, and not the identity of the residue.

Thermus aquaticus SCS, which prefers GDP and GTP, has no glutamine near the base at all. Its only polar base contacts are backbone atoms, Val94 O to N1 at 3.22 Å and Val94 N to O6 at 3.19 Å. A position-20 glutamine is therefore neither necessary nor sufficient for guanine use. Gln20β cannot discriminate adenine from guanine by its presence.

The GTP-specific enzyme carries its extra negative charge inside the pocket

Counting formal side-chain charges around the base centroid, the pig GTP-specific enzyme is two units more negative than the ATP-specific Blastocystis enzyme at every radius tested: −1 against +1 at 8 Å, −2 against −1 at 10 Å, −2 against 0 at 12 Å, 0 against +3 at 15 Å. Within 12 Å the Blastocystis set is Lys50+ at 5.5 Å, Lys9+ 7.6, Glu118− 7.6, Glu5− 9.1, Asp228− 9.6, Arg58+ 10.0. The pig set is Lys46+ 6.7, Glu42− 7.4, Glu114− 7.8, Asp110− 8.9, Lys9+ 9.0, Asp220− 9.1, Glu5− 10.1, Glu107− 11.1, Arg54+ 11.1, Lys71+ 11.2. The excess negative charge of a real GTP user sits inside the 12 Å shell around the base, including two positions that contact it.

What the published kinetics show on the Km against kcat axis

A pure entrance effect changes the association rate and so the Michaelis constant, while leaving kcat, a property of the already-bound complex, unchanged. An inside-the-site effect that misorients the base degrades kcat, or abolishes productive binding altogether. Table 1 of PMID: 27478903, retrieved in full from PMC5404824, reads as follows.

Wild type GTP not detected not detected not detected

The ED mutation does for ATP exactly what an entrance effect predicts: Km up 1.59-fold (ΔΔG +0.27 kcal/mol, the same sign and order as the computed upper bound of 1.17 kcal/mol) with kcat unchanged within error, 0.0139 to 0.0176 s⁻¹. That concerns ATP, not GTP discrimination. The combined mutant is the opposite case, with kcat for GTP falling 1.8-fold below the wild-type ATP value, an inside-the-site signature. For the wild type with GTP the entry is "not detected", carrying no Km and no kcat, so the one comparison that would decide the question was never made. A nucleotide that cannot reach the site and a nucleotide that reaches it but cannot be held productively both give no measurable rate.

Mechanistic model

The measurements fit a model in which the entrance field sets how strongly any anionic nucleotide is drawn towards the mouth, and the bound-state geometry decides whether the base that arrives can be held in a catalytically productive orientation. The two branches are separable in principle by their kinetic signature and are not separated by any published measurement.

The competing readings of the ED+LF experiment are worth stating side by side.

Entrance steering (the gatekeeper model) Partial site conversion (this analysis)
Where the substitutions act field at the mouth, 11-14 Å from the base second shell and base contacts, within 12 Å
Base discrimination available 1.43-fold at best alignment, computed here set by hydrogen-bond geometry, unbounded in principle

Fit to ED ATP data good: Km up 1.59-fold, kcat unchanged neutral
Fit to ED+LF GTP data poor: removing positive charge should weaken GTP binding too good: three of four substitutions give the pig residue
Fit to family panel poor: Thermus prefers GTP with a basic residue at position 46 not tested across the family

The distinction the seed requires holds in both readings. The ED substitutions are sufficient to perturb selection in this protein, which the kinetics show directly. Neither the kinetics nor this analysis shows that basic character at those positions is the family-wide determinant, and the companion run's seven-protein panel shows it is not.

Mechanistic scope

The direct gene-product activity under test is nucleotide selection by the beta subunit of succinyl-CoA synthetase: which nucleoside diphosphate is phosphorylated by the phosphohistidine intermediate during substrate-level phosphorylation in the TCA cycle. That is a molecular property of the beta-subunit ATP-grasp domain, measured in vitro on refolded recombinant protein by a coupled assay.

Everything downstream of that is outside the scope of the hypothesis. The localisation of the enzyme to the mitochondrion-like organelle of Blastocystis (PMID: 18452512) is a separate observation and is not affected by whether GTP is rejected at the mouth or in the site. The physiological consequence of ADP-forming rather than GDP-forming activity, which nucleotide pool the organelle charges, is an inference from the in vitro specificity and was not measured. No loss-of-function phenotype is involved anywhere in this evidence base.

Conflicts and alternatives

  • Direct conflict between primary papers. PMID: 18452512 and PMID: 27478903 conclude an electrostatic gate outside the site. PMID: 31282474, from a different group using the same protein and experimental structures rather than homology models, concludes the opposite. The 2008 conclusion rested on a homology model built before any Blastocystis structure existed.
  • Numbering. The 2008 paper names Lys42 and Lys110 (mature protein), the structures name Lys46β and Lys114β. Same residues. A curator comparing papers will see two numbering systems.
  • Charge identity. Any monopole argument about nucleotide entry applies equally to ATP and GTP. A model that explains GTP exclusion by electrostatics alone has to specify a base-specific term, and the dipole difference available at this geometry is 0.001-0.036 kT at a single evaluation point and at most 0.358 kT over the whole parameter sweep.
  • The isolated domain. All seven Blastocystis structures (6NO0-6NO6) are of the beta nucleotide-binding domain alone. Tested against the pig holoenzyme 2FP4, the alpha subunit turns out to be irrelevant to the pocket, so this limitation is weaker than it looks. What remains is that the ATP-grasp lid must open for a nucleotide to enter at all, in both enzymes, so the crystallographic channel is a closed-state snapshot and not the entry route.
  • The ED+LF set is close to a partial conversion into a GTP-using site. Three of the four substitutions install the residue the pig GTP-specific enzyme carries at the structurally equivalent position (K46βE gives pig Glu42, V113βL gives pig Leu109, L227βF gives pig Phe219); K114βD is the exception, since the pig equivalent is Ser112. The pig enzyme is two charge units more negative than the Blastocystis one at every radius from 8 to 15 Å around the base. This offers a competing account of why the quadruple change switches specificity, through second-shell charge and two base contacts of the kind a real GTP user has, with no long-range steering required.
  • Alternative reading of the ED result. Removing two rim charges 10-14 Å from the base could act through a modest repacking of the loop carrying Gln20 or of the linker rather than through the field at the mouth. The reported similarity of the mutant conformations argues against it in the ADP-bound state, but the relevant state is the guanine-bound one, which was never captured.
  • Enzyme preparation. The kinetics come from protein refolded from inclusion bodies with kcat around 0.014 s⁻¹. A fraction of misfolded or inactive enzyme would scale kcat without affecting Km, so kcat comparisons across constructs carry less weight than Km comparisons.
  • Family-level counterexample, carried forward. Basic character at either position fails to separate measured GTP users from measured ATP users across a seven-protein panel, with the Thermus enzyme preferring GDP and GTP while carrying a basic arginine at the first position and the ATP-specific mammalian subunits carrying aspartate there. The present analysis adds that Thermus also has no glutamine near the base.

Knowledge gaps

  1. Wild-type GTP kinetics are absent. Checked: Table 1 of 27478903 in full from PMC5404824. All three wild-type GTP entries read "not detected". This matters because a raised Km and an abolished kcat are the two mechanisms, and the ND entry is consistent with both. Resolved by measuring turnover at saturating GTP and reporting Km and kcat, or by reporting the detection limit and the highest GTP concentration tested.
  2. No structure with a guanine nucleotide. Checked: all seven Blastocystis entries 6NO0-6NO6 and the statement in 31282474 that only the Mg-ADP complex of the double mutant was obtained. This matters because the hydrogen-bonding argument is about the guanine-bound state, which has never been seen in this protein. Resolved by soaking or co-crystallising the ED or ED+LF mutant with Mg-GDP or a non-hydrolysable analogue.
  3. The entry route is unobserved. Checked: ray casting on 6NO0 and on 2FP4 with and without the alpha subunit. Both enzymes are closed, 87 % base burial, nothing wider than a 2.3 Å probe. The channel measured is the solvent opening of a closed state. Resolved by a simulation of lid opening or by a structure of an open ATP-grasp conformation of this family.
  4. Whether the mouth field does any work at all. Checked: computed, not measured. The predicted 1.17 kcal/mol for an NTP is consistent in sign and size with the observed ATP Km change, but no experiment has tested the field directly. Resolved by ionic-strength dependence of kcat/Km.
  5. One GTP-specific exemplar. Checked: pig SCS 2FP4 for the pocket and charge comparison, Thermus 3UFX for base contacts. The pocket composition and charge census rest on a single GTP-using structure with a bound guanine nucleotide. Resolved by repeating the comparison across 7JFP, 7JJ0, 7JKR and 7JMK, which carry GDP, GMPPCP and GMPPNP.
  6. pKa and protonation states. Checked: the charge census uses formal charges on Lys, Arg, Asp and Glu side-chain tips. It measures composition and not electrostatic potential. Resolved by a Poisson-Boltzmann calculation with computed pKa values.

Discriminating tests

The single experiment that would separate the two mechanisms: measure GTP turnover by the wild-type enzyme at saturating GTP, and report Km and kcat rather than ND. If exclusion is at the mouth, the barrier is a concentration-surmountable kon penalty and GTP should show a large Km with a kcat near the ATP value of 0.014 s⁻¹. If exclusion is inside the site, no concentration rescues it and kcat stays at zero. The computed mouth term of 1.17 kcal/mol corresponds to a steering penalty of about 7-fold in affinity, so an entrance-only mechanism predicts GTP activity should be clearly visible at low millimolar GTP, within reach of a standard coupled assay.

Supporting experiments, in order of value:

  1. Ionic-strength dependence. A steering effect is screened by salt, so kcat/Km for ATP should fall with increasing ionic strength in the wild type and respond differently in the ED mutant. A bound-state hydrogen-bonding effect is largely salt-insensitive. This is a direct, inexpensive test of whether the field at the mouth does any work at all.
  2. Co-crystallise or soak the K46βE/K114βD mutant with Mg-GDP or a non-hydrolysable GTP analogue (GMPPCP or GMPPNP, both already used for SCS in 7JJ0 and 7JKR) and compare the Gln20 backbone, the Gln20 amide rotamer and the linker against 6NO5.
  3. Charge-neutral controls at the same positions, K46βQ and K114βN. These remove the monopole term without introducing a negative charge. If GTP use appears in these too, selectivity is coming from packing and not from the field.
  4. Test a charge pair that genuinely lines the corridor, for instance Lys9, against the peripheral pair. If steering is real, the on-axis charge should have the larger effect per unit charge.
  5. Single-turnover or pre-steady-state measurement of nucleotide association, to separate kon from the equilibrium constant that Km conflates.
  6. Separate the pocket-conversion account from the steering account by testing K46βE alone against K46βE plus the pig second-shell identities at the positions this analysis pairs with Blastocystis Gly111 and Cys110 (pig Glu107 and Val44). Steering predicts the extra second-shell changes add little; pocket conversion predicts they matter more than the rim charge does.

Evidence base

Three primary papers carry the whole experimental record for this question.

ATP-specificity of succinyl-CoA synthetase from Blastocystis hominis, PMID: 31282474. Seven crystal structures of the beta nucleotide-binding domain at 1.91-2.44 Å, wild type and five mutants. The abstract states that "Nucleotide-specificity is provided by hydrogen-bonding interactions between the adenine base and Gln20β, Gly111β and Val113β" and that "It is the different conformations of the backbone at Gln20β, of the side chain of Gln20β and of the linker that make the enzyme ATP-specific." It also records the structural gap directly: "Although the K46βE/K114βD double mutant of Blastocystis hominis SCS binds GTP better than ATP according to kinetic assays, only the complex with Mg2+-ADP was obtained." This paper supports the inside-the-site mechanism and supplies the coordinates on which the present calculations were run.

Engineering Nucleotide Specificity of Succinyl-CoA Synthetase in Blastocystis: The Emerging Role of Gatekeeper Residues, PMID: 27478903. States the entrance mechanism in its own terms: "Charged, solvent-exposed residues at the entrance to the substrate binding site (gatekeeper residues) produce electrostatic dipole interactions with approaching substrates, and control their access by a novel mechanism called \"electrostatic gatekeeper effect\"", and reports that "we demonstrated that the gatekeeper mutant (ED) resulted in ATP-specific SCS to show high GTP specificity". The result that constrains the interpretation is the requirement for the pocket mutations as well: "via combination of the gatekeeper mutant with the nucleotide binding site mutant (ED+LF), a complete reversal of nucleotide specificity was obtained with GTP, but no detectable activity was obtained with ATP". Table 1 of this paper is the source of every kinetic number quoted above.

Localization and nucleotide specificity of Blastocystis succinyl-CoA synthetase, PMID: 18452512. The origin of the entrance claim, from a homology model: "GTP is destabilized by electrostatic dipole interactions with Lys 42 and Lys 110, the side-chains of which lie outside the nucleotide-binding cavity." This paper also provides the organelle localisation. Its structural argument predates any Blastocystis structure.

The dipole language in both entrance papers is the term this analysis bounded. At the geometry of the deposited structures it reaches 0.358 kT at best over 40 parameter settings, equivalent to 1.43-fold discrimination.

Limitations of this analysis

The electrostatic calculation is a continuum Debye-Hückel estimate from formal charges on side-chain tip atoms. It omits desolvation, the protein low-dielectric interior, backbone and polar-group partial charges, and pKa shifts, and it is evaluated only at and beyond the protein surface where a bulk dielectric is defensible. It should be read as an order-of-magnitude comparison between a base-blind and a base-specific term, which is the comparison the argument needs, and not as a binding free energy. The 40-setting sweep over dielectric, ionic strength and evaluation distance shows the comparison does not turn on those choices, but it cannot cover errors in the model form itself. The base dipole values are gas-phase literature figures; in water the effective moments are larger, which would raise both terms, and the dipole term would have to grow by about two orders of magnitude relative to the charge term to change the conclusion.

The exit channel is computed on a static closed-state structure, so it describes the solvent opening and not the trajectory of a nucleotide into an open ATP-grasp fold. Residue equivalence between Blastocystis and pig was assigned by superposing structures, not by sequence alignment. Two superposition methods, purine-ring and global CA matching, agree on every assignment used here except Leu227, where they return Phe219 and Asp220, so the pairings are structural proximity assignments and should be checked against an alignment before being quoted. The pocket and charge comparisons rest on one GTP-specific exemplar, pig SCS in 2FP4, and the charge census counts formal charges on Lys, Arg, Asp and Glu without pKa calculation, so it measures composition rather than electrostatic potential.
