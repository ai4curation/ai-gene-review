D15 (P06229, Escherichia phage T5): is the late-transcription block a direct template requirement or an indirect consequence of failed replication?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-d15-late-transcription. Source: genes/BPT5/D15/D15-ai-review.yaml, free-text focus. Taxon: NCBITaxon:2695836. UniProt: P06229.

Summary

Verdict: the direct template-structure explanation is refuted. Separability is formally untested. No published experiment uncouples D15's replication requirement from its late-transcription requirement. The 1973 observation that started the claim (PMID: 4357874) scored late proteins by SDS-PAGE, lysozyme activity and serum-blocking power, never late RNA, in an infection that cannot replicate its genome. The nick/gap mechanism appears in that paper as a proposal, in the authors' own words: "It is proposed that one requirement for the expression of late T5 genes is the introduction of gaps or nicks in the T5 DNA so that late transcription can occur." Fifty-three years later, that sentence is still the sole evidential basis for the GO:0019086 annotation on P06229.

The decisive experiment has not been done. What the record does contain is five independent lines of evidence that bear on the proposed mechanism, and all five come out against it. The strongest is a timing contradiction found in this investigation: the enzyme that makes the single-strand interruptions is a dedicated nicking endonuclease, UniProt Q6QGD4 (ORF142 / T5.153 / T5p149, 145 residues), annotated as expressed in the late phase and mapped here to positions 105,428-105,865 of GenBank AY587007, 86.8% of the genome, immediately beside the portal protein and inside the late morphogenesis cluster. If the interruptions licensed late promoters, late transcription would be a prerequisite for its own prerequisite. The other four: nicked against ligase-sealed T5 DNA shows no transcription difference in vitro with the same host sigma70 polymerase that reads T5 late promoters (PMID: 1095368); more than 60 interruption-deficient T5 mutants, including near interruption-free ones, are all viable (PMID: 430606); blocking replication through host gyrase with phage D15 intact abolishes late gene expression anyway (PMID: 2419589); and the identical replication-plus-late-expression phenotype arises from gene D5, a DNA-binding protein with no nuclease activity (PMID: 219226, PMID: 224043).

Two computed analyses were run against public sequence data and both fail the direct model's own prediction. The five principal interruptions sit 2.3 to 19.4 kb from the nearest annotated virion or morphogenesis gene start on AY587007, with none inside the late structural cluster, and they are no closer to predicted sigma70 promoters than random genome positions (median 474 bp against a permutation null median of 315 bp, one-sided p = 0.77, 20,000 draws). A database usage audit adds a curation-specific point: of all 729 UniProt entries carrying GO:0019086 late viral transcription, 369 are transcription factors or coactivators, 278 are RNA polymerase sigma-like factors or subunits, 81 are MON1 homologs, and exactly one is a nuclease. That nuclease is P06229.

Full text was not machine-retrievable for any of the historical papers. PMC holds the 1973 PNAS paper (PMC427268) and the other pre-1990 records tested (PMC252817, PMC515921, PMC353203) as scanned page images; efetch db=pmc returned metadata-only XML with no body text in every case. The Ficht and Moyer 1980 JBC paper (PMID: 6248541) has neither a PMC record nor a DOI in PubMed and is abstract-only by any route available here. Every pre-1990 claim in this report is abstract-level, and the curator should obtain the print articles before acting on the 1973 and 1980 sources.

Executive judgment

Verdict on the direct template-structure explanation: refuted. Verdict on separability: unresolved, with no experiment in the record that separates the two requirements.

The seed hypothesis allowed "unresolved" as an acceptable outcome. The honest answer is split. Separability itself is unresolved: nobody has run a D15 shift-off or catalytically dead D15 with late transcripts scored and replication independently controlled. The specific mechanism proposed to make the requirement direct is a different matter, and it does not survive contact with the rest of the record.

  1. The timing is circular. The enzyme that makes the single-strand interruptions is not D15. It is a dedicated nicking endonuclease, UniProt Q6QGD4 / ORF142 / T5.153, 145 residues, UniProt FUNCTION "Endonuclease responsible for the single-chain interruptions (nicks) located at specific positions in the minus strand of the viral genome", INDUCTION "Expressed in the late phase of the viral replicative cycle", GO:0004519 as IDA from PMID: 24198424. Mapped here to AY587007 105,428-105,865 on the minus strand, 86.8% of the genome, adjacent to the portal protein (104,217-105,428), the putative major tail subunit and the prohead protease. In the related nicked-genome phage phiKF77, nicks "are introduced into the phage DNA at late stages of the lytic cycle" (PMID: 19592061). A mark made by a late gene product cannot license the late programme that produces it.
  2. The one direct in vitro test is negative. Knopf and Bujard transcribed T5+ DNA in the nicked form and after sealing the interruptions with DNA ligase, using E. coli RNA polymerase holoenzyme with and without rho: "The results indicate that the single-strand breaks in the DNA of bacteriophage T5, though in genetically defined positions, do not have any specific effect on transcription in vitro" (PMID: 1095368).
  3. The genetic test is negative. More than 60 independent interruption-deficient T5 mutants, some "essentially free from interruptions or had a reduced frequency of interruptions throughout the genome", and "All of the mutants were viable" (PMID: 430606, PMID: 430607). Viability requires late gene expression.
  4. A replication block with D15 intact reproduces the phenotype. "Inhibition of T5 growth due to inactivation of host DNA gyrase was caused mainly by inhibition of T5 DNA replication", and "late T5 genes were barely expressed" under that block (PMID: 2419589). Replication failure is sufficient to abolish late expression.
  5. The phenotype is not nuclease-specific. "Gene D5 is not only necessary for replication of bacteriophage T5 DNA and for shutoff of expression of some early genes, but has been found to be necessary also for the expression of late T5 genes" (PMID: 219226), and purified gpD5 "is required to initiate transcription of the late region of the T5 genome" (PMID: 224043). gpD5 is a DNA-binding protein with no nuclease activity.

Supporting these: D15 needs pre-existing interruptions to cut at all, since "the enzyme can also introduce endonucleolytic scissions into mature T5 DNA in a reaction that requires duplex T5 DNA and preexisting, single-stranded interruptions" (PMID: 904023), which places it downstream of the nickase. Parental interruptions are repaired in vivo before the late period: "Sealing begins at about 6 min after infection and is independent of DNA replication" (PMID: 4522782). And a protein-based alternative mechanism for the early-to-late switch exists: host sigma70 RNA polymerase plus phage factors gpC2 and gpT5.026 (PMID: 32722583, PMID: 4359432, PMID: 1092331).

The annotation that carries the claim rests on one line. QuickGO returns a single annotation of P06229 to GO:0019086: evidence IMP, reference <a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4357874/" rel="noopener noreferrer" title="Visit PubMed page for PMID 4357874" class="pubmed-badge" style="display:inline-flex;align-items:center;text-decoration:none;white-space:nowrap;"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 16 16" width="14" height="14" class="pubmed-icon" style="display:inline !important;width:14px;height:14px;min-width:14px;min-height:14px;flex-shrink:0;vertical-align:middle;margin-right:3px;"><rect x="1" y="1" width="14" height="14" rx="2" fill="#326599"/><text x="8" y="12" text-anchor="middle" font-size="11px" font-weight="bold" font-family="Arial,sans-serif" fill="white">P</text></svg>4357874</a>, assigned by CACAO, With/From empty.

Most important caveats. The 1975 in vitro assay lacked gpC2 and gpT5.026, so it tested whether nicks alone suffice, not whether nicks are needed in the presence of the late factors. The interruption-deficient mutants were scored on mature virion DNA; the template for late transcription in vivo is the replicating concatemer, whose nicking status in those mutants was not reported. The "late phase" expression of the nickase is a UniProt annotation, corroborated here only by its genomic position, not by a direct transcript time course. And no experiment anywhere scores late transcripts in a D15-defective infection, so the possibility that late promoters fire normally in an amD15 infection and the proteins simply fail downstream has never been excluded either.

Key findings

F1. The one in vitro nicked-against-sealed transcription test is negative

This is exactly the experiment the seed hypothesis asks for: an in vitro transcription assay on nicked against intact template. Knopf and Bujard (PMID: 1095368) purified T5+ DNA, prepared it both in the native nicked state and after sealing the specific single-strand interruptions with DNA ligase, and transcribed both with E. coli RNA polymerase holoenzyme, with and without the rho factor. Products were compared by size distribution and by which sequences were transcribed. No nick-specific effect was detected.

The assay is relevant because the polymerase is the right one. All three T5 temporal classes "are transcribed by host RNA polymerase (RNAP) containing the σ70 promoter specificity subunit" (PMID: 32722583). T5 encodes no phage RNA polymerase, so the enzyme that fires T5 late promoters in vivo is the same enzyme that was used in the 1975 reaction.

The limitation is specific and worth stating precisely. The reaction contained polymerase, template and rho. It did not contain gpC2, "The product of this gene is required for the normal turn-on of synthesis of late RNA's and proteins" (PMID: 4359432), nor gpT5.026, the phage protein that binds the RNAP beta subunit and shifts the early/late transcript balance (PMID: 32722583). So the result rules out nicks being sufficient and rules out nicks having any detectable template effect on bare holoenzyme. It does not rule out a nick requirement that only appears when the late-specific factors are present. That is a narrow escape route, and nothing in the record occupies it.

F2. Interruption-free T5 mutants are viable

Rogers, Godwin, Shinosky and Rhoades screened roughly 800 hydroxylamine-mutagenised T5 particles and isolated over 60 independent interruption-deficient mutants (PMID: 430606). They fell into two classes: site-specific losses, with five sites identified, and a second class in which "Mutants in the other class were essentially free from interruptions or had a reduced frequency of interruptions throughout the genome." The result that matters for this hypothesis is one sentence: "All of the mutants were viable and could be grouped into two classes." Complementation indicated at least two genes are needed to generate the interruptions of mature T5 DNA.

The companion paper examined T5HA4, which lacks the interruptions at 7.9% and 99.6%, and found "The interruption at 7.9% is therefore not required for stopping DNA transfer after the initial 8% segment has been injected" (PMID: 430607). The same logic applies here: viability requires late gene expression, which requires assembled virions, which requires the capsid and tail proteins encoded in the late region. A T5 genome with few or no single-strand interruptions still turns on its late programme.

The caveat is real and should go to the curator. The interruptions scored in these mutants are those of mature virion DNA, assayed after packaging. The template for late transcription in vivo is the replicating branched concatemer, whose nicking status in interruption-deficient mutants was not reported. Concatemeric T5 DNA was studied for single-stranded regions and interruptions by S1 digestion and electron microscopy (PMID: 7264606), but not in the interruption-deficient background.

F3. The interruptions are reproducible in position, fractional in occupancy, too few, and not promoter-proximal

The seed hypothesis sets the test: an interruption pattern that is reproducible and promoter-proximal supports the direct proposal, a variable one does not. The pattern passes the first half and fails everything else.

Scheible and Rhoades located four principal interruptions at approximately 7.9, 18.5, 32.6 and 64.8% from one end (PMID: 330881), with a fifth at 99.6% in the terminal repeat. The positions are sequence-defined and reproduce between preparations, which is the part that favours the direct model. The occupancy does not: "The results indicate that single-chain interruptions occurr in a variable, but nonrandom, manner in T5 DNA", the principal sites are present in 80-90% of molecules, over 20 additional low-frequency sites exist, and "The average number of interruptions per genome, as determined by this method, is 8." Electrophoretic mapping confirmed over 20 potential sites (PMID: 894794), restriction mapping precisely localised five (PMID: 6287031), and the related phage BF23 has the same five principal positions (PMID: 158099).

Eight interruptions per 121.8 kb genome, mostly fractional, have to license 5 mapped late promoters. Of 40 promoters characterised by electron microscopy of in vitro transcription complexes, "6 map in the two terminal "pre-early" regions, 29 in the "early" and 5 in the "late" region" (PMID: 745595).

Computed here from GenBank AY587007 (121,752 bp, 168 CDS parsed): the annotated virion and morphogenesis CDS have starts spanning 16.6-90.7%, with the contiguous cluster at 70.7-86.6% by the 10th-90th percentile, or 69.0-89.9% by a stricter keyword set. Distances from each principal interruption to the nearest annotated late or structural gene start are 10.6, 2.3, 19.4, 7.2 and 10.8 kb for the sites at 7.9, 18.5, 32.6, 64.8 and 99.6%. No principal interruption falls inside the late cluster. The closest, at 64.8%, is 7.2 kb to the left of it and 1.7 kb from D15 itself.

The gene-start proxy was then replaced with a direct promoter test. AY587007 was scanned on both strands for sigma70 architecture, a -35 TTGACA plus a 15-19 bp spacer plus a -10 TATAAT, allowing at most 2 total mismatches across the 12 informative bases. That yields 156 predicted promoters, one per 780 bp. Distances from the five principal interruptions to the nearest predicted promoter are 474, 331, 1194, 895 and 441 bp, median 474 bp. A permutation null of 20,000 draws of five random genome positions gives a median-of-five distance of 315 bp (mean 350 bp). One-sided permutation p = 0.77. The interruptions are if anything slightly farther from predicted promoters than chance.

Consensus-motif scanning is a crude promoter predictor, and at one hit per 780 bp the genome is saturated enough that no position in T5 is far from some predicted promoter. The test therefore has limited power and gives a clean absence of enrichment rather than proof of exclusion. It would be superseded by the exact base coordinates of the five late promoters mapped in PMID: 745595 and the four cloned and sequenced in PMID: 6316267, neither of which was machine-retrievable here.

F4. D15 does not make the interruptions, and the enzyme that does is a late gene product

This is the finding that changes the status of the 1973 proposal from unproven to self-contradictory.

Three separate facts place D15 downstream of the nickase. Zivanovic et al. assign the interruptions to a dedicated enzyme among the capsid-maturation functions: "the single head-to-tail joining protein, which closes the T5 capsid after DNA packaging, and the nicking endonuclease responsible for the single-strand interruptions in the T5 genome" (PMID: 24198424). Rogers and Rhoades had purified four T5-induced site-specific endonucleases that make ligase-repairable interruptions, and the fragments they produce "do not correspond to those derived from mature T5 DNA" (PMID: 5725). Complementation requires at least two genes for interruptions (PMID: 430606). And D15 itself requires pre-existing nicks to cut: "the enzyme can also introduce endonucleolytic scissions into mature T5 DNA in a reaction that requires duplex T5 DNA and preexisting, single-stranded interruptions" (PMID: 904023). The same paper places D15 at a later maturation step: "The formation of unit-length, single-stranded DNA chains does not require the early T5 gene D15 nuclease: however, the subsequent formation of the single-stranded fragments does require that the D15 nuclease be functional."

The nickase was then identified and mapped. UniProt query on taxon 2695836 with a nicking-endonuclease name filter returns Q6QGD4 (ORF142 / T5.153 / T5p149), 145 residues, FUNCTION "Endonuclease responsible for the single-chain interruptions (nicks) located at specific positions in the minus strand of the viral genome", INDUCTION "Expressed in the late phase of the viral replicative cycle", with GO:0004519 endonuclease activity as IDA from PMID: 24198424. Mapping its sequence back onto AY587007 places it at 105,428-105,865 on the minus strand, 86.8% of the genome, immediately adjacent to the portal protein at 104,217-105,428, the putative major tail subunit and the prohead protease. D15 by contrast maps to the exo5 CDS at 80,222-81,052, midpoint 66.2%, and is early.

Corroboration comes from a different phage family. In the phiKMV-like Pseudomonas phages, localized interruptions "are present with high frequencies within the phage population and are introduced into the phage DNA at late stages of the lytic cycle" (PMID: 19592061). The same structural feature with the same late timing appears in Pseudomonas putida phage tf, where 14 nicks map to a consensus 5'-TACT/RTGMC-3' and two were shown to be present in more than 90% of the phage population (PMID: 23236447). Localized nicking is a packaging-era feature of these genomes.

Timing closes the argument from the other end. Parental interruptions are not available as a persistent late mark, because "Sealing begins at about 6 min after infection and is independent of DNA replication" (PMID: 4522782). A nick-licensing model therefore has to invoke nicks re-created on progeny DNA, which makes the requirement replication-dependent by construction and dissolves the distinction the seed hypothesis is trying to draw.

F5. The phenotype is shared across the T5 replication machinery

Loss of any of several proteins that build a T5 replication fork produces the same late-expression failure. Gene D5 encodes a DNA-binding protein, and "Gene D5 is not only necessary for replication of bacteriophage T5 DNA and for shutoff of expression of some early genes, but has been found to be necessary also for the expression of late T5 genes" (PMID: 219226). The purified protein was then shown to act on late transcription: "We also show here that the protein is required to initiate transcription of the late region of the T5 genome" (PMID: 224043). gpD5 has no nuclease activity, so it cannot be making a template mark.

Ficht and Moyer isolated a roughly 5 x 10^6 Da complex from T5-infected cells containing host RNA polymerase with gpC2, the DNA polymerase gpD9, gpD5 and gpD15, protecting about 2-3% of the genome as duplex DNA, and describe "the proteins gpD5 (DNA-binding protein), and gpD15 (nuclease) which are both known to be essential for T5 DNA replication and for the initiation of late transcription" (PMID: 6248541). The complex gives a physical basis for replication-transcription coupling. It is an association. It does not show that D15 catalysis acts on the transcription template.

The host-side test is the closest published equivalent of the inhibitor experiment the seed hypothesis requests. T5 fails to grow in E. coli gyrB(Ts) at 42 C and is inhibited by novobiocin, coumermycin A1 and nalidixic acid. "Inhibition of T5 growth due to inactivation of host DNA gyrase was caused mainly by inhibition of T5 DNA replication. In addition, however, late T5 genes were barely expressed when" the gyrase was inactivated (PMID: 2419589; the abstract sentence is truncated in PubMed and should be verified against the PMC scan PMC252817). D15 is an early gene product and is made normally in these cells. Replication failure alone is therefore sufficient to produce the amD15 late-expression phenotype, which means that phenotype carries no independent information about a template requirement.

F6. The 1973 experiment scored proteins, and nothing since has scored late transcripts in a D15 mutant

Chinnadurai and McCorquodale assayed D15 amber infections three ways: "Electrophoretic separation in sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gels of the proteins induced by this mutant in nonpermissive Escherichia coli show a virtual absence of late proteins", plus lysozyme activity and serum-blocking power (PMID: 4357874). All three are protein-level or particle-level readouts. The same paper confirms the early programme is intact, since "the extent of synthesis of an early enzyme, deoxyribonucleoside monophosphokinase, is similar to that in wild-type infections". The mechanism then enters as a proposal: "It is proposed that one requirement for the expression of late T5 genes is the introduction of gaps or nicks in the T5 DNA so that late transcription can occur."

The absence of an RNA measurement is a real gap, not a limitation of the era. The same group published on gene C2 the following year and did score RNA: "The product of this gene is required for the normal turn-on of synthesis of late RNA's and proteins" (PMID: 4359432). They measured transcripts when they chose to.

A systematic PubMed search found no temperature-sensitive or shiftable D15 allele, no host or phage background in which T5 replicates without D15, and no replication inhibitor applied with D15 active and late transcripts scored. Serum-blocking power and lysozyme report assembled or functional products, which also fail if packaging fails, so they are poor proxies for promoter firing.

F7. D15 is the only nuclease in the entire GO:0019086 annotation set

Mechanistic model

The record supports a simple causal chain in which the late-expression defect of amD15 is two steps removed from the enzyme's chemistry. The direct model requires an arrow that the evidence does not supply, shown dashed.

The dashed arrow is the 1973 proposal. It closes a loop: interruptions are made by a late gene product, so they cannot be upstream of the late programme. The solid arrows carry the explanation that the evidence supports. D15 is required for replication; replication is required for late expression by a route that also depends on gpD5 and on host gyrase; and the early-to-late switch itself is driven by phage proteins acting on host RNA polymerase.

GO curation implications

All items below are leads requiring curator verification. The decision table is saved as provenance/go_decision_table_D15.csv.

The decision that matters: GO:0019086 late viral transcription, currently on P06229 as IMP from CACAO. The lead is to not treat this as a core function. Either mark it non-core as a downstream phenotype of the replication defect, or remove it in favour of the replication terms. The reasoning:

  • Its only support is the amD15 late-protein phenotype (PMID: 4357874), measured in an infection that cannot replicate.
  • An IMP annotation from a loss-of-function phenotype is appropriate when the phenotype is reasonably specific to the gene product's own action. Here it is not: gene D5 gives the same phenotype (PMID: 219226), and so does a host gyrase block with D15 intact (PMID: 2419589).
  • The mechanistic bridge the 1973 authors proposed, template nicks, fails its own direct test (PMID: 1095368) and its own genetic test (PMID: 430606).
  • QuickGO returns exactly one annotation of P06229 to this term: IMP, 4357874, CACAO, no With/From. That is the whole evidential basis, and the cited paper scored late proteins, never late RNA.
  • Of the 729 UniProt entries carrying GO:0019086, P06229 is the only nuclease. Every other protein bearing the term is part of the transcription apparatus.

If a curator wants to record the observed phenotype without endorsing the mechanism, the defensible statement is that D15 is required for T5 genome replication and that late gene expression fails as a consequence, with the direct-template proposal noted as a 1973 hypothesis that later work did not support.

Mechanistic scope

Immediate molecular activity. Magnesium-dependent hydrolysis of DNA at a 5' single-strand/double-strand junction, in one active site, with the 5' arm threaded through a helical arch. Both the processive 5'-3' exonucleolytic and the structure-specific endonucleolytic modes come from that one site, and substrate choice is structural.

Immediate cellular consequence. The enzyme is needed for T5 genome replication, and for the step that converts unit-length single strands derived from concatemers into the shorter single-stranded pieces characteristic of virion DNA (PMID: 904023).

Downstream and inferred. The absence of late proteins in amD15 infections is a loss-of-function phenotype two steps removed from the enzyme's chemistry: no replication, therefore no late expression. The "introduction of nicks or gaps so that late transcription can occur" is a proposal from 1973 that was never tested on transcripts. The co-purification of gpD15 with RNAP and gpC2 in a single complex (PMID: 6248541) is an association that makes the coupling physically attractive but does not show that D15 catalysis acts on the transcription template.

Conflicts and alternatives

Protein-factor switch rather than template structure. gpC2 is required for the turn-on of late RNA and binds host RNAP (PMID: 4359432, PMID: 1092331). gpT5.026 binds the RNAP beta subunit and shifts the early/late transcript balance (PMID: 32722583). These supply a mechanism for the early-to-late switch that needs no template modification, and they match how GO:0019086 is used on the 647 transcription factors and sigma-like factors that carry it.

Replication-coupled transcription generally. gpD5 (PMID: 219226, PMID: 224043) and host gyrase (PMID: 2419589) both produce the same late-expression failure. The simplest reading is that T5 late promoters are read efficiently only on replicating or newly replicated DNA, by whatever mechanism, so any replication-defective mutant looks like a late-transcription mutant. The seed hypothesis asks to distinguish a requirement for a nick from a requirement for replication-generated ends or for displacement synthesis. Nothing in the record tests the latter two directly, so they remain live and untested, and the indirect model does not specify which of them operates.

Attribution and timing of the nicks. The direct model needs D15 to write the nicks. It does not. A dedicated nicking endonuclease does, identifiable as Q6QGD4 / ORF142 / T5.153 (PMID: 24198424), at least two genes are needed for interruptions (PMID: 430606), and purified D15 requires pre-existing interruptions to cut (PMID: 904023). The nickase is a late gene product at 86.8% of the genome among the capsid and tail genes, so the nicks arrive after the late programme has started. The 1973 proposal is circular as stated.

Interruption transience. Parental interruptions are sealed from about 6 min, independent of replication (PMID: 4522782). A nick-licensing model must invoke nicks re-created on progeny DNA, which makes the requirement replication-dependent by construction.

Readout mismatch. For D15, only proteins were scored (PMID: 4357874). For C2, from the same group the following year, RNA and proteins (PMID: 4359432). The difference is a real gap in the record.

Possible artifact to keep in view. Serum-blocking power and lysozyme report assembled or functional products, which also fail if DNA packaging fails. They are poor proxies for promoter activity.

A caveat against the refutation. One transfection result is sometimes read as showing the interruptions are dispensable for infectivity, since ligase treatment of T5 DNA did not change transfection efficiency and "the single-strand interruptions of T5 phage DNA are probably not essential to the survival of the parental T5 phage DNA" (PMID: 4596299). Spheroplast transfection is a different route of entry and should not be weighted heavily either way.

Discriminating tests

Ranked by how efficiently each separates the direct template model from the indirect replication model.

  1. Late-transcript measurement in a catalytically dead D15 infection. Build a D15 active-site mutant (the conserved acidic metal-binding residues, or K83 which is required both for binding and for the chemistry, PMID: 9889266, PMID: 10364212) complemented in trans, infect, and quantify the 5 late promoters by strand-specific RT-qPCR or RNA-seq at a time course, with replication independently scored by qPCR of genome copy number. This is the single experiment the record lacks. If late transcripts are present at near wild-type level while late proteins are absent, the 1973 interpretation is wrong at the level of the readout, not only the mechanism.
  2. In vitro late-promoter transcription with the full late factor set. Repeat the nicked-against-ligase-sealed comparison of PMID: 1095368 on a defined late-promoter template, with purified host sigma70 RNAP plus gpC2, gpD5 and gpT5.026. This closes the only escape route left for the direct model.
  3. Transcript time course for ORF142 / Q6QGD4. RT-qPCR against known pre-early, early and late markers, to turn the database INDUCTION annotation into an experimental result and put the circularity argument on direct evidence.
  4. Nicking status of the replicating concatemer in interruption-free mutants. S1 nuclease plus alkaline agarose on replicating DNA from the PMID: 430606 class-two mutants. If the concatemer is also interruption-free and late expression proceeds, the genetic refutation becomes airtight.
  5. Exact late-promoter coordinates and a recomputed proximity test. Recover the mapped positions from PMID: 745595 and the four cloned promoter sequences from PMID: 6316267, map them to AY587007, and recompute interruption-to-promoter distances against the same permutation null. This replaces a motif-scan proxy with real coordinates.
  6. Replication block with D15 active, late transcripts scored. The gyrase experiment of PMID: 2419589 with RNA readouts on the mapped late promoters, which would confirm directly that a replication block alone shuts down late promoter firing with D15 fully functional.

Limitations

Full text was not machine-retrievable for any of the historical papers; PMC holds them as page scans and efetch db=pmc returned metadata-only XML in every case tested. Every claim drawn from work published before about 1990 in this report is abstract-level, including the 1973 and 1980 primary papers the seed hypothesis names. The gene-start distance calculation substitutes late gene starts for late promoter positions, and the motif-scan test substitutes predicted sigma70 promoters for mapped ones; at one predicted hit per 780 bp the test has limited power. The interruption positions are taken from 1977 and 1982 mapping on a T5 strain that may differ slightly from the sequenced ATCC 11303-B5 used for coordinates. The "late phase" status of the nickase Q6QGD4 rests on a UniProt INDUCTION annotation plus its genomic position, not on a measured transcript time course. And the central negative, that no experiment scores late transcripts in a D15-defective infection, is an argument from a systematic PubMed search, which cannot exclude a result published in a venue PubMed does not index.
