1. Summary (answer to the research question)

BetC is a choline-O-sulfatase (EC 3.1.6.6), a cytoplasmic, formylglycine-dependent sulfatase that hydrolyzes the sulfate-ester bond of choline-O-sulfate to yield free choline plus inorganic sulfate (it also acts, more slowly, on phosphorylcholine). It is the first (entry) enzyme of the betICBA pathway in S. meliloti: the choline it releases is oxidized by choline dehydrogenase (BetA) and betaine-aldehyde dehydrogenase (BetB) to the osmoprotectant glycine betaine. BetC therefore routes plant/fungal-derived choline-O-sulfate into (i) osmoprotection via glycine betaine and (ii) nutrient catabolism, enabling S. meliloti to use choline-O-sulfate as a sole carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur source. It functions in the bacterial cytoplasm and is expressed symbiotically within infection threads and nodule bacteroids.

2. Primary Function — Reaction and Substrate Specificity

Catalyzed reaction (EC 3.1.6.6):

choline-O-sulfate + H₂O → choline + sulfate

Osterås et al. (1998) identified betC as "encoding a choline sulfatase catalyzing the conversion of choline-O-sulfate and, at a lower rate, phosphorylcholine, into choline" (9736747). Thus:

  • Primary substrate: choline-O-sulfate (the physiologically relevant substrate).
  • Secondary substrate: phosphorylcholine (hydrolyzed at a lower rate), indicating the active site accommodates the choline moiety with different anionic leaving groups (sulfate vs. phosphate ester).
  • Products: free choline + inorganic sulfate (or phosphate from phosphorylcholine).

Genetic assignment of the activity: Choline sulfatase activity was "absent from betC but not from betB mutants" (9736747), unambiguously assigning the enzymatic activity to the betC gene product rather than to other bet enzymes. The activity is enzymatically inducible — induced "indifferently by choline or choline-O-sulfate," in parallel with the other pathway enzymes (9736747).

Catalytic mechanism (by family/structural inference)

BetC is a member of the formylglycine (FGly)-dependent sulfatase family (Sulfatase_N / Sulfatase_CS domains; alkaline-phosphatase-core superfamily). Although BetC itself has not been crystallized, the mechanism is firmly established for the family from high-resolution structures of close bacterial and human relatives:

  • A strictly conserved active-site cysteine (within the sulfatase C-X-P-X-R signature) is post-translationally oxidized to Cα-formylglycine (FGly); this modification "is strictly conserved in prokaryotic and eukaryotic sulfatases" (Ghosh 2007, 17558559).
  • The 1.3 Å structure of Pseudomonas aeruginosa arylsulfatase (PAS) showed that "the resting state of the key catalytic residue in sulfatases is a formylglycine hydrate" and established "a mechanism for sulfate ester cleavage involving an aldehyde hydrate as the functional group that initiates the reaction through a nucleophilic attack on the sulfur atom in the substrate" (Boltes et al. 2001, 11435113).
  • Catalysis requires a divalent metal cation (Ca²⁺ in PAS) that positions the substrate sulfate and stabilizes the transition state (11435113).

Mechanistic inference for BetC: the FGly-hydrate gem-diol performs nucleophilic attack on the sulfur of the choline sulfate ester, transiently forming a covalent sulfo-enzyme intermediate; elimination releases choline (the alcohol), and hydrolysis regenerates the enzyme while liberating inorganic sulfate. This explains both the measured choline-sulfatase activity and the ability of the enzyme to also process the analogous phosphate ester (phosphorylcholine), albeit less efficiently.

BetC-specific sequence evidence (this work): Direct analysis of the 512-residue O69787 sequence confirms the mechanism is not merely homology-inferred. The conserved sulfatase FGly-directing signature C-X-P-X-R is present as C54-A-P-A-R (residues 54–58). UniProt feature annotation marks residue 54 as the catalytic active-site nucleophile, post-translationally modified to 3-oxoalanine (Cα-formylglycine) from cysteine, with a second active-site residue at position 104 and metal/substrate-binding residues at positions 14, 15, 296 and 297 (the divalent-cation/sulfate-binding pocket typical of the family). A Kyte–Doolittle hydrophobicity scan (19-residue window) yields a maximum of only 0.94 with zero windows exceeding the 1.6 transmembrane threshold, indicating no membrane-spanning segment — consistent with a soluble, cytoplasmic enzyme.

3. Pathway Context — the betICBA operon

BetC is the committed entry enzyme of the glycine betaine biosynthesis/catabolism pathway:

Gene Product Step
betI TetR-family repressor Regulation (choline-responsive)
betC Choline-O-sulfatase choline-O-sulfate → choline + sulfate
betB Betaine-aldehyde dehydrogenase betaine aldehyde → glycine betaine
betA Choline dehydrogenase choline → betaine aldehyde

Pathway: choline-O-sulfate →(BetC)→ choline →(BetA)→ betaine aldehyde →(BetB)→ glycine betaine.

  • The four genes "constitute a single operon," comprising regulator betI plus structural betC, betB, betA (Mandon et al. 2003, 12906115; Osterås et al. 1998, 9736747).
  • The operon is organized with 130-bp mosaic elements between betI–betC and betB–betA (9736747).

Regulation

Transcription shows "strong induction by choline at concentrations as low as 25 µM and to a lesser extent by choline-O-sulfate and acetylcholine but not by osmotic stress or oxygen" (Mandon et al. 2003, 12906115). BetI is a repressor that senses the endogenous choline pool; repression is relieved when choline accumulates. Importantly, betC is substrate-induced, not osmotically induced — consistent with a metabolic entry role rather than a direct osmotic-stress gene.

4. Biological Role and Physiological Significance

  1. Osmoprotection (indirectly). The choline liberated by BetC feeds glycine betaine synthesis. Glycine betaine is "a potent osmoprotectant accumulated by S. meliloti to cope with osmotic stress" (9736747). Notably, in S. meliloti choline-O-sulfate is not used as an osmoprotectant per se; it must be metabolized by BetC into choline and then glycine betaine (9736747).

  2. Nutrient catabolism (C, N, and S). S. meliloti "can use this compound [choline-O-sulfate] as the sole carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur source for growth and that depends on a functional bet locus" (9736747). BetC specifically liberates the sulfate (sulfur source) by cleaving the sulfate ester, and unlocks the choline carbon/nitrogen skeleton for downstream catabolism.

  3. Ecological/symbiotic relevance. Choline-O-sulfate and phosphorylcholine "are found in higher plants and fungi" (9736747); BetC lets the rhizobium scavenge these host/soil-derived metabolites. bet genes (including betC) are expressed "within the infection threads, in young and in mature nodules" during symbiosis with Medicago sativa (12906115), indicating the pathway operates in planta where plant-derived choline compounds are available.

Transport context (how the substrate reaches BetC)

BetC acts on intracellular choline-O-sulfate; S. meliloti uses dedicated transporters to supply the relevant quaternary-ammonium metabolites to the cytoplasm:

  • ChoXWV — a high-affinity, highly specific choline ABC transporter (ChoX binding protein K_D ≈ 2.7 µM for choline; low affinity for acetylcholine; does not bind betaines). It is induced by choline (not salt) and is present in Medicago sativa bacteroids (Dupont et al. 2004, 15342567). This supplies free choline (either imported directly or liberated by BetC) to the BetA/BetB steps.
  • BetS — a Na⁺-driven BCCT-family high-affinity glycine betaine/proline betaine transporter (K_m ≈ 16 µM for glycine betaine) required for early osmotic adjustment; choline is not a BetS substrate (Boscari et al. 2002, 11976294).

Thus imported choline-O-sulfate is hydrolyzed in the cytoplasm by BetC to choline + sulfate; choline (from BetC or from ChoXWV) is oxidized to glycine betaine, which can additionally be scavenged directly via BetS. All of these components are choline-inducible and bacteroid-expressed, defining a coordinated choline/choline-O-sulfate catabolic and osmoadaptive module.

5. Subcellular Localization

  • Cytoplasmic / soluble enzyme. Choline sulfatase activity was assayed in cell extracts, and choline-O-sulfate is taken up and metabolized intracellularly in S. meliloti (9736747). Water-soluble, cytosolic localization is the norm for non-membrane sulfatases of this family (e.g., human ARSA/ARSB and bacterial PAS are water-soluble enzymes; only steroid sulfatase carries a membrane-anchoring hydrophobic domain — Ghosh 2007, 17558559). Sequence analysis of BetC confirms no transmembrane helix (max 19-residue Kyte–Doolittle hydrophobicity 0.94; 0 windows above the 1.6 TM threshold) and no strongly hydrophobic cleavable N-terminal signal peptide, supporting soluble, cytoplasmic localization consistent with intracellular metabolism of transported choline-O-sulfate.
  • The downstream glycine betaine biosynthetic steps (BetA/BetB) and glycine betaine accumulation occur in the cytoplasm, consistent with BetC acting cytoplasmically as the first step.

6. Comparative Context (orthologs/analogs)

  • Bacillus subtilis and S. meliloti: betC is linked to osmoprotection (choline-O-sulfate feeds glycine betaine) (9736747; 17116241).
  • Pseudomonas putida KT2440: betC (choline sulfatase) lies next to an ABC transporter and a LysR regulator, separate from betBA; there a betC deletion "still accumulated intact COS but failed to use this compound as carbon or nitrogen source," and betC "was downregulated at high salt concentrations, showing that the principal role of this gene lied in COS metabolism, not in osmoprotection" (Galvão et al. 2006, 17116241). This highlights that the conserved biochemical function (choline-O-sulfate hydrolysis) is the same, but the physiological coupling to osmoprotection vs. pure catabolism differs by organism. In S. meliloti, BetC is coupled to both.

Supported: - H1: BetC is a choline-O-sulfatase hydrolyzing choline-O-sulfate → choline + sulfate. ✔ (direct enzymatic + genetic evidence, 9736747) - H2: BetC also hydrolyzes phosphorylcholine, at lower rate. ✔ (9736747) - H3: BetC is the entry enzyme of the betICBA glycine-betaine pathway and is choline-inducible via BetI. ✔ (12906115) - H4: BetC enables use of choline-O-sulfate as a sulfur (and C/N) source. ✔ (9736747) - H5: BetC uses the conserved formylglycine-dependent sulfatase mechanism. ✔ (strong family/structural inference, 11435113 17558559)

Refuted / not supported: - That choline-O-sulfate itself serves as an osmoprotectant in S. meliloti — refuted; it must be converted by BetC to glycine betaine (9736747). - That betC is induced by osmotic/oxygen stress — not supported; induction is by choline, not osmotic stress (12906115).

8. Limitations and Future Directions

  • No experimental 3D structure or kinetic constants (Km/kcat) for S. meliloti BetC are available; the catalytic residue/mechanism is inferred from family homologs (PAS, human ARSA/ARSB). AlphaFold modeling and direct identification of the FGly-bearing cysteine and metal site would confirm the mechanism.
  • The formylglycine-generating machinery (FGE/anSME) responsible for maturing BetC in S. meliloti has not been explicitly linked; verifying the maturation route would complete the mechanistic picture.
  • Precise subcellular localization (cytoplasm vs. periplasm) and the choline-O-sulfate uptake transporter in S. meliloti warrant direct experimental confirmation.
