Gene Description

bcoE-bcoF (cdhAB, SMc01638) encodes a bifunctional cytoplasmic enzyme of Sinorhizobium meliloti L-carnitine catabolism. The N-terminal region (residues 1-335, 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase family) oxidizes L-carnitine to 3-dehydrocarnitine with NAD(+), and the C-terminal hotdog-fold region (residues 336-496) hydrolyzes betainyl-CoA (N,N,N-trimethylglycyl-CoA) to glycine betaine and CoA, so one polypeptide carries the first and last steps of the route whose middle step is the BcoG cleavage enzyme. In Pseudomonas the two activities are separate proteins (CdhA and CdhB). The purified homodimer was characterized kinetically, and gene disruption abolishes growth on gamma-butyrobetaine, which S. meliloti converts to carnitine before degrading it.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

betainyl-CoA thioesterase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: N,N,N-trimethylglycyl-CoA + H2O = glycine betaine + CoA + H+.

Justification: EC 3.1.2.33 (RHEA:45716) is measured for this enzyme and for P. aeruginosa CdhB; GO has only the broader acyl-CoA hydrolase term.

Parent term: acyl-CoA hydrolase activity

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is the C-terminal thioesterase region homologous to the stand-alone CdhB hotdog thioesterases of Pseudomonas, and did the fusion arise once in Rhizobiales and Roseobacter lineages?

Q: Is L-carnitinyl-CoA ever formed in S. meliloti, or is the 158-fold slower hydrolysis of it by this enzyme purely an in vitro property of a pocket evolved for betainyl-CoA?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express the two regions separately and test whether each retains its activity, and whether the fusion channels betainyl-CoA formed by BcoG.

Type: protein dissection and coupled enzyme assay
