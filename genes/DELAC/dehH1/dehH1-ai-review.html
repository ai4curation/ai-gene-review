Gene Description

DehH1 is haloacetate dehalogenase H-1 (EC 3.8.1.3), a plasmid-encoded bacterial enzyme of the alpha/beta-hydrolase fold that hydrolytically cleaves the carbon-halogen bond of haloacetates, releasing the halide anion and glycolate. It is the better-known of two unrelated haloacetate dehalogenases carried on the 65-kb plasmid pUO1 of Delftia acidovorans strain B (originally described as Moraxella sp. strain B), and it is distinguished from its plasmid partner DehH2 by acting preferentially on fluoroacetate, whose carbon-fluorine bond is among the strongest in natural products. Catalysis follows the canonical two-step alpha/beta-hydrolase route with an aspartate rather than a serine nucleophile: Asp105, on a classical nucleophile elbow, displaces the halide to form a covalent ester intermediate, which His272 then activates water to hydrolyse. The enzyme is a soluble cytoplasmic protein with no signal peptide or membrane anchor, and it also turns over non-natural 2-fluorocarboxylic acids such as alpha-fluorophenylacetic acid and 2-fluoropropionic acid, stereoselectively, making it a workhorse for studies of microbial organofluorine degradation and of intracellular fluoride stress.

Core Functions

DehH1 hydrolytically cleaves the carbon-halogen bond of haloacetates in the bacterial cytoplasm, converting haloacetate plus water into glycolate and the free halide anion. Catalysis proceeds by the two-step alpha/beta-hydrolase route with an aspartate nucleophile: Asp105, positioned on a classical nucleophile elbow, attacks the substrate alpha-carbon and displaces the halide to form a covalent ester intermediate, which is then hydrolysed by a water molecule activated by the base catalyst His272. The enzyme's defining property is that it performs this chemistry on fluoroacetate, unlike its plasmid partner DehH2, and it also accepts non-natural 2-fluorocarboxylic acids such as alpha-fluorophenylacetic acid and 2-fluoropropionic acid, acting stereoselectively on them. The reaction both detoxifies the haloacetate and yields glycolate (or the corresponding hydroxy acid) as an assimilable carbon source, while liberating halide into the cytoplasm.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should GO have a biological-process term for haloacetate or organofluorine catabolism, and if so should dehalogenases be annotated to it? At present no such class exists: GO:0042206 halogenated hydrocarbon catabolic process is defined over compounds derived from hydrocarbons, which a halogenated carboxylic acid is not. Consistent with that, none of the best-characterised dehalogenases carries a process term, including the experimentally annotated Rhodopseudomonas palustris FAcD (UniProtKB:Q6NAM1) and the (S)-2-haloacid dehalogenase UniProtKB:Q53464. No process term is proposed here on the strength of that convention, but it is worth asking whether the convention is deliberate.

Q: Can UniProt merge or cross-reference Q7DI61 and Q01398? They are the same sequence of the same gene of the same strain, separated only by the reclassification of Moraxella sp. strain B as Delftia acidovorans. The split means the Delftia-named record lacks the EC number, the active-site features and the protein-level evidence that the biology actually supports, and any pipeline keyed on the modern organism name sees an uncharacterised predicted protein.

Q: How broad is DehH1's substrate range across the 2-halocarboxylic acids, and which structural features set the fluorine preference that distinguishes it from DehH2? Specific activities have been reported for a handful of fluorinated substrates, but there is no systematic comparison across halide and chain-length series for this enzyme.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine an experimental structure of DehH1, which does not currently exist; work on this enzyme has had to rely on an AlphaFold model aligned to the Burkholderia sp. FA1 FAcD crystal structure. A structure with fluoroacetate or a 2-fluorocarboxylic acid bound would show how the halide-binding pocket accommodates fluoride and would ground the comparison with the structurally characterised Rhodopseudomonas palustris and Burkholderia enzymes.

Experiment: Localise the enzyme directly in Delftia acidovorans strain B, by cell fractionation of plasmid-bearing cells or by tagging the native locus. The cytoplasmic assignment proposed here rests on the purified protein's solubility, the absence of targeting signals and the behaviour of the enzyme in a recombinant Pseudomonas host, none of which is a localisation assay in the native organism.

Experiment: Measure steady-state kinetics across a matched series of 2-halocarboxylic acids (fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodoacetate, then the 2-halopropionates and alpha-halophenylacetates) for DehH1 and DehH2 side by side, to quantify the fluorine preference that currently rests on a qualitative statement, and to give the pair a proper substrate-specificity profile.

dehH1 (UniProtKB:Q7DI61, Delftia acidovorans) — curation notes

No deep-research provider was available for this review; the literature below was
assembled by hand from PubMed, PMC full text, UniProt and the PANTHER/PAINT data
already in this repository. This file is the journal for that work.

1. What the entry is

Q7DI61_DELAC is an unreviewed (TrEMBL) entry, 294 aa, derived from EMBL
BAB85585.1 / BAC81979.1, gene dehH1, on plasmid pUO1 of Delftia
acidovorans strain B (NCBITaxon:80866). The record carries no EC number, no
curated comment blocks, and PE 4: Predicted;. Its only domain feature is
AB hydrolase-1 (Pfam PF00561) spanning residues 27–276, and it is classified in
PANTHER PTHR43798:SF33.

Despite the bare record, this protein is one of the best-characterised C–F bond
cleaving enzymes in the literature. The key is that the identical sequence also
exists as a curated Swiss-Prot entry, Q01398 / DEH1_MORSB.

2. Q7DI61 and Q01398 are the same protein (duplicate UniProt entries)

Both entries are 294 aa with identical CRC64 checksums
(7BA7F07359182703), identical sequence, the same gene name dehH1 and the same
plasmid (pUO1 / POU1); verified live against the UniProt REST API in

Strain B was originally described as Moraxella sp. and later reclassified; the
2002 plasmid paper describes the same two dehalogenase genes as being on
Delftia acidovorans plasmid pUO1 PMID:11976102. So the
two accessions are the same gene of the same strain deposited under the old and
the new organism name. This is recorded as a source_defects entry
(defect_type: DUPLICATE_ENTRY, resource UNIPROT) on the review.

The practical consequence for annotation is stark: Q01398 carries
GO:0018785 haloacetate dehalogenase activity (via its curated EC 3.8.1.3),
while the byte-identical Q7DI61 carries only GO:0003824 catalytic activity.
Nothing biological distinguishes them.

3. The enzyme: haloacetate dehalogenase H-1 / fluoroacetate dehalogenase

dehH1 encodes haloacetate dehalogenase H-1, one of two unrelated
dehalogenases carried on the strain B plasmid. Kawasaki and colleagues sequenced
both genes and showed they share no homology, and that the two enzymes differ in
substrate range PMID:1512562 —
whereas H-2 (dehH2, a HAD-superfamily 2-haloacid dehalogenase, UniProtKB:Q7DI63)
does not act on fluoroacetate. The same paper notes that DehH1's sequence
resembles the Xanthobacter autotrophicus haloalkane dehalogenase, placing it in
the alpha/beta-hydrolase fold, not the HAD superfamily.

The reaction is hydrolytic C–X bond cleavage: haloacetate + H2O → glycolate +
halide (EC 3.8.1.3, GO:0018785). Cleaving the C–F bond of fluoroacetate is the
distinguishing feature; this bond is among the strongest in organic chemistry
PMID:15063312.

Mechanism

The enzyme uses the canonical alpha/beta-hydrolase two-step mechanism with an
aspartate nucleophile. Working on the Moraxella sp. B enzyme — i.e. this protein
— Liu et al. showed by H2-18O turnover and tandem MS that Asp105 is the
nucleophile that displaces the halide and forms a covalent ester intermediate
PMID:9812982, and that His272 is the
base catalyst that activates water to hydrolyse that intermediate: an H272N
mutant was inactive yet still became alkylated by substrate PMID:9812982. Asp105 is essential;
replacing it with Asn abolishes dehalogenation, and the apparent residual activity
of the D105N mutant is due to deamidation regenerating wild-type enzyme
PMID:15063312.

Both residues are present in the Q7DI61 sequence, Asp105 on a textbook nucleophile
elbow (G-H-D-R-G) — see dehH1-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md. There is no reason
to treat this entry as a degenerate or pseudoenzymatic copy.

Substrate range beyond fluoroacetate

Dodge et al. re-purified the enzyme (expressed from a codon-optimised synthetic
gene) and compared it head-to-head with the well-studied Rhodopseudomonas
palustris FAcD, finding it the better catalyst with a wider substrate range
PMID:38063407. It
defluorinates non-natural 2-fluorocarboxylic acids — alpha-fluorophenylacetic acid
and 2-fluoropropionic acid — stereoselectively PMID:38063407. Chlorofluoroacetic
acid is also a substrate but the slowest one, and did not support growth.

4. Localization: the enzyme is soluble, not a membrane protein

GOA gives Q7DI61 GO:0016020 membrane (IEA, GO_REF:0000118, TreeGrafter, source
node PANTHER:PTN008685326). This is a propagation artefact:

  • PTHR43798 is a very broad alpha/beta-hydrolase superfamily family (164,495
    members, 13 subfamilies per interpro/panther/PTHR43798/PTHR43798-metadata.yaml).
    The only GO:0016020 IBD nodes in PTHR43798-paint.tsv are PTN000177089
    (seeded by UniProtKB:P07000, SGD:S000001577, MGI:MGI:1346042,
    AGI_LocusCode:AT1G52760, UniProtKB:Q57U43) and PTN001661081 (seeded by
    MGI:MGI:1913332) — all lipid-acting enzymes (lysophospholipases,
    monoacylglycerol lipases) whose membrane association follows from acting on
    membrane lipids.
  • DehH1 acts on a small, freely water-soluble carboxylic acid. The membrane
    association of the lipase branch has no reason to transfer with the fold.
  • Dodge et al. describe it explicitly as a soluble enzyme and as acting in the
    cytoplasm PMID:38063407 — the entire premise of that paper
    is that fluoride is released inside the cell.
  • Neither UniProt entry carries a signal peptide, transmembrane feature or lipid
    anchor, and a hydropathy screen finds no candidate TM segment
    (dehH1-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md).

So GO:0016020 is removed, and GO:0005737 cytoplasm proposed in its place as a
NEW/IC row. The same wrong membrane row sits on Q01398, so the defect is
reported once against the TreeGrafter propagation rather than against this entry
alone.

5. What was deliberately not proposed

No biological-process term. It is tempting to add a haloacetate or
organofluorine catabolic process term, since DehH1 performs the first step that
lets strain B grow on haloacetate. Two things argue against it:

  • GO has no fluoroacetate- or haloacetate-catabolism process term.
    GO:0042206 halogenated hydrocarbon catabolic process is defined over
    compounds "derived from hydrocarbons by replacing one or more hydrogen atoms
    with halogen atoms"; haloacetate is a halogenated carboxylic acid, and forcing
    it under that term would overstate the fit.
  • Comparator check, per the NEW bar in CLAUDE.md. Of the 690 proteins carrying
    GO:0018785, the ones with the strongest evidence carry no process term:
    R. palustris FAcD UniProtKB:Q6NAM1 has GO:0018785 by EXP
    (PMID:21510690) and only MF rows; the (S)-2-haloacid dehalogenase
    UniProtKB:Q53464 likewise has MF rows only; UniProtKB:Q01398, this same
    protein, has none. A systematic absence across well-studied dehalogenases is a
    curation convention, not an oversight to fill in.

This is recorded as a suggested_questions item instead.

No fluoroacetate-specific MF term. GO has no fluoroacetate dehalogenase activity class; GO:0018785 haloacetate dehalogenase activity is the EC 3.8.1.3
term and covers the activity, including on fluoroacetate. No new term is proposed:
the existing one is adequate, and the fluorine preference is a specificity fact
better captured in core_functions prose than as a new class.

    bacterial cytoplasm, converting haloacetate plus water into glycolate and the
    free halide anion. Catalysis proceeds by the two-step alpha/beta-hydrolase
    route with an aspartate nucleophile: Asp105, positioned on a classical
    nucleophile elbow, attacks the substrate alpha-carbon and displaces the halide
    to form a covalent ester intermediate, which is then hydrolysed by a water
    molecule activated by the base catalyst His272. The enzyme's defining property
    is that it performs this chemistry on fluoroacetate, unlike its plasmid
    partner DehH2, and it also accepts non-natural 2-fluorocarboxylic acids such
    as alpha-fluorophenylacetic acid and 2-fluoropropionic acid, acting
    stereoselectively on them. The reaction both detoxifies the haloacetate and
    yields glycolate (or the corresponding hydroxy acid) as an assimilable carbon
    source, while liberating halide into the cytoplasm.

suggested_questions:
- question: >-
    Should GO have a biological-process term for haloacetate or organofluorine
    catabolism, and if so should dehalogenases be annotated to it? At present no
    such class exists: GO:0042206 halogenated hydrocarbon catabolic process is
    defined over compounds derived from hydrocarbons, which a halogenated
    carboxylic acid is not. Consistent with that, none of the best-characterised
    dehalogenases carries a process term, including the experimentally annotated
    Rhodopseudomonas palustris FAcD (UniProtKB:Q6NAM1) and the (S)-2-haloacid
    dehalogenase UniProtKB:Q53464. No process term is proposed here on the
    strength of that convention, but it is worth asking whether the convention is
    deliberate.
- question: >-
    Can UniProt merge or cross-reference Q7DI61 and Q01398? They are the same
    sequence of the same gene of the same strain, separated only by the
    reclassification of Moraxella sp. strain B as Delftia acidovorans. The split
    means the Delftia-named record lacks the EC number, the active-site features
    and the protein-level evidence that the biology actually supports, and any
    pipeline keyed on the modern organism name sees an uncharacterised predicted
    protein.
- question: >-
    How broad is DehH1's substrate range across the 2-halocarboxylic acids, and
    which structural features set the fluorine preference that distinguishes it
    from DehH2? Specific activities have been reported for a handful of
    fluorinated substrates, but there is no systematic comparison across halide
    and chain-length series for this enzyme.
suggested_experiments:
- description: >-
    Determine an experimental structure of DehH1, which does not currently exist;
    work on this enzyme has had to rely on an AlphaFold model aligned to the
    Burkholderia sp. FA1 FAcD crystal structure. A structure with fluoroacetate
    or a 2-fluorocarboxylic acid bound would show how the halide-binding pocket
    accommodates fluoride and would ground the comparison with the structurally
    characterised Rhodopseudomonas palustris and Burkholderia enzymes.
- description: >-
    Localise the enzyme directly in Delftia acidovorans strain B, by cell
    fractionation of plasmid-bearing cells or by tagging the native locus. The
    cytoplasmic assignment proposed here rests on the purified protein's
    solubility, the absence of targeting signals and the behaviour of the enzyme
    in a recombinant Pseudomonas host, none of which is a localisation assay in
    the native organism.
- description: >-
    Measure steady-state kinetics across a matched series of 2-halocarboxylic
    acids (fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodoacetate, then the 2-halopropionates and
    alpha-halophenylacetates) for DehH1 and DehH2 side by side, to quantify the
    fluorine preference that currently rests on a qualitative statement, and to
    give the pair a proper substrate-specificity profile.
