Gene Description

Protein 114 (gp29/ORF114a) of Sulfolobus islandicus rod-shaped virus 1 is a small homodimeric DUF1874-family cyclic tetra-adenylate ring nuclease. Its dimer-interface pocket recognizes cA4, and a conserved catalytic site cleaves the signal to linear di-adenylate with a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate. The enzyme prefers cA4 over cA6. Ectopic plasmid expression can suppress cA4-dependent type III CRISPR immunity and permit infection by another virus. Its native anti-CRISPR role is disputed: a later SIRV2 infection study found no protection from native gp37 or SIRV1 gp29 transplanted into SIRV2 under the tested expression conditions, whereas earlier induced gp29 expression restored protection. This did not test infection by SIRV1 itself. The established enzymatic activity therefore does not demonstrate that all native infections or DUF1874 homologs use it as an effective counterdefence.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

cyclic oligoadenylate ring nuclease activity

Definition: Catalysis of the endonucleolytic cleavage of an internal 3'-5' phosphodiester linkage within a cyclic oligoadenylate second messenger, opening the ring to yield linear oligoadenylate products bearing a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate.

Justification: GO has no term for cleavage of a cyclic oligoadenylate messenger, and each available alternative is either too general or simply wrong. GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity, asserted on this entry as the nearest existing term, is defensible because cyclic tetra-adenylate is a short ribonucleotide and ring opening is an internal phosphodiester cleavage, but it loses the substrate entirely, and the substrate is what the enzyme is for. GO:0001730 2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetase activity is the metazoan OAS enzyme, which synthesises a different, linear 2'-5'-linked molecule, so it is the wrong term in both direction and chemistry, and it must not be used for this family or for the Cas10 cyclase that makes the substrate. The cyclic-nucleotide phosphodiesterase term GO:0004112 is about cAMP and cGMP second messengers and about generating a linear nucleoside monophosphate, which is neither the substrate nor the product here. The term is not a single-gene request. It is needed for experimentally established ring nucleases in viral and cellular lineages, and it would equally serve the host-encoded Crn1 and Crn2 ring nucleases, for example UniProtKB:Q97YD2, which perform the same chemistry as self-regulation of the host response rather than as counter-defence. It is also the mirror image of a second gap, the absence of a term for the cyclic oligoadenylate synthase activity of the Cas10 Palm domain, recorded in MODULE:crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity, and the two should be requested together so that the ontology can represent both ends of the signalling step.

Parent term: RNA endonuclease activity

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: If a SIRV1 isolate becomes available, compare native-promoter wild type, catalytic-site mutant and complemented virus across defined host CRISPR and target-transcript backgrounds, measuring expression timing and flanking-gene expression. In the available SIRV2 system, distinguish native gp37 from transplanted gp29 and include early-expression controls. Interpret a CRISPR-dependent phenotype together with direct cA4 cleavage and signal measurements; knockout necessity alone does not establish process participation or automatically justify a new annotation.

Hypothesis: If SIRV1 gp29 contributes to counterdefence in an appropriate native infection context, catalytic disruption should selectively impair protection under conditions in which the protein is expressed before the relevant CRISPR response.

Type: matched viral-expression and catalytic-mutant infection assays

Experiment: Measure full Michaelis-Menten parameters for cyclic tetra-adenylate turnover side by side for this enzyme and for the host Crn1 under identical conditions, separating the contributions of substrate affinity and maximum rate. A difference concentrated in the maximum rate rather than in affinity would support the selection argument, and would also give the kinetic parameters that the GO term request and any future quantitative model of the signalling step need.

Hypothesis: Catalytic speed, not substrate affinity, is what the virus is selected for, which would explain the sixty-fold rate difference from the host's own ring nuclease.

Type: comparative steady-state enzyme kinetics

Experiment: Identify interface residues from the 1.55 Angstrom structure, build substitutions designed to shift the monomer-dimer equilibrium without perturbing the monomer fold, verify the oligomeric state by size-exclusion chromatography with inline light scattering and the fold by circular dichroism, then assay cyclic tetra-adenylate cleavage. A quantitative correlation between dimer fraction and activity would establish that the functional unit is the dimer, which would in turn justify annotating protein homodimerization activity on this entry, something the current evidence supports structurally but has not tested functionally.

Hypothesis: Dimerisation is obligatory for activity, because the substrate pocket is formed at the dimer interface.

Type: interface mutagenesis with paired oligomeric-state and activity readouts

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: Plasmid-expression sufficiency and nuclease chemistry are established. The Comment publisher captions identify SIRV2 infection and a SIRV2 Para-gp29 construct; they do not establish native SIRV1 infection behavior. The normal PMID caches lack full text. Publisher excerpts are reproducibly available; the prior main-text inspection and more detailed Reply objections remain documented in notes with their access limits.

Significance: An experimentally effective ectopic inhibitor need not protect its native virus at the time CRISPR immunity acts. Conversely, failure in one infection context does not prove absence of every conditional viral counterdefence role.

What would resolve it: Resolve expression timing, host/target and multiplicity-of-infection dependence with matched constructs and controls for flanking-gene effects. Distinguish native SIRV2 gp37, transplanted SIRV1 gp29 and native SIRV1 infection. A negative result in one context does not settle all contexts.
