Gene Description

Protein 114 of Sulfolobus islandicus rod-shaped virus 1, also called gp29 in the literature, is a 114-residue homodimeric enzyme of the DUF1874 family and the founding characterised member of the AcrIII-1 class of anti-CRISPR ring nucleases. It defeats type III CRISPR immunity by destroying the second messenger that drives it rather than by touching any Cas protein. When a type III effector detects viral RNA, the cyclase domain of Cas10 synthesises cyclic oligoadenylates from ATP, and cyclic tetra-adenylate in turn activates downstream CRISPR-associated Rossmann fold nucleases that degrade RNA indiscriminately and can drive an infecting virus to extinction. The enzyme binds cyclic tetra-adenylate in a pocket at its own dimer interface, on a fold unrelated to the Rossmann fold modules that are the only other known receptors for this signal, and opens the ring at a conserved active site to give linear di-adenylate carrying a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate, a product that no longer activates the downstream nucleases. Turnover exceeds five per minute, more than sixty-fold faster than the host's own ring nuclease, and the enzyme strongly prefers the tetrameric over the hexameric signal. Because the target is a small-molecule messenger rather than a protein surface, a single enzyme works against any type III subtype that signals through cyclic tetra-adenylate, and the family is correspondingly widespread across archaeal and bacterial viruses and proviruses.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

cyclic oligoadenylate ring nuclease activity

Definition: Catalysis of the hydrolytic cleavage of a phosphodiester bond within a cyclic oligoadenylate second messenger, such as cyclic tetra-adenylate or cyclic hexa-adenylate, opening the ring to yield linear oligoadenylate products that bear a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate and that no longer activate cyclic-oligoadenylate-dependent effector enzymes.

Justification: GO has no term for cleavage of a cyclic oligoadenylate messenger, and each available alternative is either too general or simply wrong. GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity, asserted on this entry as the nearest existing term, is defensible because cyclic tetra-adenylate is a short ribonucleotide and ring opening is an internal phosphodiester cleavage, but it loses the substrate entirely, and the substrate is what the enzyme is for. GO:0001730 2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetase activity is the metazoan OAS enzyme, which synthesises a different, linear 2'-5'-linked molecule, so it is the wrong term in both direction and chemistry, and it must not be used for this family or for the Cas10 cyclase that makes the substrate. The cyclic-nucleotide phosphodiesterase term GO:0004112 is about cAMP and cGMP second messengers and about generating a linear nucleoside monophosphate, which is neither the substrate nor the product here. The term is not a single-gene request. It is needed for the whole AcrIII-1 family, which is widespread in archaeal and bacterial viruses and in proviruses, and it would equally serve the host-encoded Crn1 and Crn2 ring nucleases, for example UniProtKB:Q97YD2, which perform the same chemistry as self-regulation of the host response rather than as counter-defence. It is also the mirror image of a second gap, the absence of a term for the cyclic oligoadenylate synthase activity of the Cas10 Palm domain, recorded in MODULE:crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity, and the two should be requested together so that the ontology can represent both ends of the signalling step.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: The viral enzyme is more than sixty-fold faster than the host's own Crn1 ring nuclease on the same substrate. Is that because the host must keep its messenger around long enough for immunity to work, so Crn1 is under selection to be slow, while the virus is under selection only to be fast?

Suggested experts: Januka S. Athukoralage, Malcolm F. White

Q: Does GO want a cyclic tetra-adenylate binding term as a sibling of GO:0180001 cyclic-di-AMP binding, given that specificity for the tetramer over the hexamer is a measured property of this enzyme and of the host CARF-domain receptors alike?

Q: PubMed lists a 2025 Nature comment on PMID:31942067 (640(8059) pages E8 to E14) whose content was not retrievable here. Does it revise any of the gp29 results, and if so which annotations on this entry need revisiting?

Q: SIRV1 carries this gene while the lytic virus SSeV used as the reporter does not. Is the presence or absence of a duf1874 gene a good predictor of which archaeal viruses can persist in type III-competent Sulfolobus populations?

Suggested experts: Rachel J. Whitaker, Mart Krupovic

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Construct a SIRV1 deletion mutant lacking this gene and compare plaque size and titre on a Saccharolobus islandicus host with an intact type III system, on an isogenic effector-nuclease deletion, and with the gene supplied in trans from a plasmid. A defect only in the type-III-competent host, rescued in trans, would convert the current in-trans sufficiency result into a necessity result on the virus of origin and would justify an additional IMP annotation.

Hypothesis: SIRV1 requires this gene to overcome type III CRISPR immunity in its natural host, so deleting it should phenocopy the small-plaque defect reported for the homologous STIV gene.

Type: viral gene deletion and plaquing assay

Experiment: Measure full Michaelis-Menten parameters for cyclic tetra-adenylate turnover side by side for this enzyme and for the host Crn1 under identical conditions, separating the contributions of substrate affinity and maximum rate. A difference concentrated in the maximum rate rather than in affinity would support the selection argument, and would also give the kinetic parameters that the GO term request and any future quantitative model of the signalling step need.

Hypothesis: Catalytic speed, not substrate affinity, is what the virus is selected for, which would explain the sixty-fold rate difference from the host's own ring nuclease.

Type: comparative steady-state enzyme kinetics

Experiment: Identify interface residues from the 1.55 Angstrom structure, build substitutions designed to shift the monomer-dimer equilibrium without perturbing the monomer fold, verify the oligomeric state by size-exclusion chromatography with inline light scattering and the fold by circular dichroism, then assay cyclic tetra-adenylate cleavage. A quantitative correlation between dimer fraction and activity would establish that the functional unit is the dimer, which would in turn justify annotating protein homodimerization activity on this entry, something the current evidence supports structurally but has not tested functionally.

Hypothesis: Dimerisation is obligatory for activity, because the substrate pocket is formed at the dimer interface.

Type: interface mutagenesis with paired oligomeric-state and activity readouts

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: GO has no molecular-function term for cleavage of a cyclic oligoadenylate second messenger, so the activity of this enzyme can only be asserted with GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity, which omits the substrate that defines it.

OPEN ONTOLOGY

What is known: The chemistry, the product end-groups, the rate, the substrate preference and the active site are all established, and the process term GO:0098672 fits exactly. The gap is purely in the ontology's molecular-function coverage.

Significance: Without the term, this enzyme is indistinguishable in GO from any other RNA endonuclease, and the signalling layer of type III CRISPR immunity is invisible from both ends, since the Cas10 cyclase that makes the messenger has no term either and GO:0001730 is the wrong term for it. The gap is also not confined to viral counter-defence, because the host's own Crn1 and Crn2 regulators perform the same reaction.

What would resolve it: Create the proposed term cyclic oligoadenylate ring nuclease activity under GO:0004521, paired with the cyclic oligoadenylate synthase request recorded in MODULE:crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity, then move its identifier into the molecular_function slot of the core function here.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "Both proteins possess a potent ring nuclease activity, rapidly degrading cA4 to generate linear di-adenylate (ApA>P) with a cyclic 2’,3’ phosphate" — PMID:31942067

Gap: The UniProt entry is reviewed Swiss-Prot yet still titled "Uncharacterized protein 114", with no FUNCTION line and zero GO annotations, six years after the protein was characterised enzymatically, structurally and in a living host.

RESOLVED CURATION

What is known: There is no scientific uncertainty to resolve. Catalysis, substrate, product, rate, structure, active-site residue and in vivo sufficiency are all published in one paper.

Significance: This is the accession-level gap recorded in modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml, which stated that the assayed AcrIII-1 protein could not be matched to a UniProt entry from local data and used this accession only as an orienting family exemplar. The module now records the gap as resolved and the exemplar as de-caveated. That gap is now closed in the opposite direction from what the module anticipated, because the orienting exemplar turns out to be the assayed protein. The reason it was hard to see is nomenclature, since the literature says gp29, UniProt says protein 114 and PDB says ORF 114a for one molecule, and RefSeq NP_666617 is the identifier that joins them. A reviewer following the module to this entry would otherwise conclude the protein is uncharacterised.

What would resolve it: Update the module by closing its third knowledge gap, citing NP_666617 and PDB 6SCF, and by removing the caveat on the representative_members description for UniProtKB:Q8QL27. Separately, nominate the Swiss-Prot entry for a FUNCTION line and a RecName citing PMID:31942067, and add gp29 as an alternative name.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "AcrIII-1 homologs were collected by using gp29 (NP_666617) of SIRV1 as a query" — PMID:31942067
  • "The structural coordinates and data have been deposited in the Protein Data Bank with deposition code 6SCF." — PMID:31942067

Gap: Whether this gene is required for SIRV1 itself to overcome type III CRISPR immunity in its natural host is undetermined. Sufficiency was shown by expressing it in trans to rescue a different, duf1874-less virus; necessity in the virus of origin has not been tested.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: Sufficiency in a living host is established, and so is the enzymology. The untested claim is the loss-of-function counterpart, which for other anti-CRISPR families was the experiment that grounded the original assignments.

Significance: Sufficiency in trans shows the enzyme can defeat type III immunity. It does not show that SIRV1 depends on it, and SIRV1 may carry other counter-defence genes. A deletion that leaves SIRV1 unimpaired would mean the enzyme is one redundant option among several, which changes how central the activity is to the virus even though it would not change the molecular function.

What would resolve it: Delete the gene from SIRV1 and compare plaquing on a Saccharolobus islandicus host with an intact type III system against an isogenic effector deletion, with complementation in trans as the control. The precedent is the small-plaque phenotype reported for deletion of the homologous STIV gene B116.

description: Protein 114 of Sulfolobus islandicus rod-shaped virus 1, also called gp29
  in the literature, is a 114-residue homodimeric enzyme of the DUF1874 family and
  the founding characterised member of the AcrIII-1 class of anti-CRISPR ring nucleases.
  It defeats type III CRISPR immunity by destroying the second messenger that drives
  it rather than by touching any Cas protein. When a type III effector detects viral
  RNA, the cyclase domain of Cas10 synthesises cyclic oligoadenylates from ATP, and
  cyclic tetra-adenylate in turn activates downstream CRISPR-associated Rossmann fold
  nucleases that degrade RNA indiscriminately and can drive an infecting virus to extinction.
  The enzyme binds cyclic tetra-adenylate in a pocket at its own dimer interface, on
  a fold unrelated to the Rossmann fold modules that are the only other known receptors
  for this signal, and opens the ring at a conserved active site to give linear di-adenylate
  carrying a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate, a product that no longer activates the downstream
  nucleases. Turnover exceeds five per minute, more than sixty-fold faster than the
  host's own ring nuclease, and the enzyme strongly prefers the tetrameric over the
  hexameric signal. Because the target is a small-molecule messenger rather than a protein
  surface, a single enzyme works against any type III subtype that signals through cyclic
  tetra-adenylate, and the family is correspondingly widespread across archaeal and
  bacterial viruses and proviruses.

- proposed_name: cyclic oligoadenylate ring nuclease activity
  proposed_definition: Catalysis of the hydrolytic cleavage of a phosphodiester bond
    within a cyclic oligoadenylate second messenger, such as cyclic tetra-adenylate
    or cyclic hexa-adenylate, opening the ring to yield linear oligoadenylate products
    that bear a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate and that no longer activate cyclic-oligoadenylate-dependent
    effector enzymes.
  justification: GO has no term for cleavage of a cyclic oligoadenylate messenger,
    and each available alternative is either too general or simply wrong. GO:0004521
    RNA endonuclease activity, asserted on this entry as the nearest existing term,
    is defensible because cyclic tetra-adenylate is a short ribonucleotide and ring
    opening is an internal phosphodiester cleavage, but it loses the substrate entirely,
    and the substrate is what the enzyme is for. GO:0001730 2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetase
    activity is the metazoan OAS enzyme, which synthesises a different, linear 2'-5'-linked
    molecule, so it is the wrong term in both direction and chemistry, and it must
    not be used for this family or for the Cas10 cyclase that makes the substrate.
    The cyclic-nucleotide phosphodiesterase term GO:0004112 is about cAMP and cGMP
    second messengers and about generating a linear nucleoside monophosphate, which
    is neither the substrate nor the product here. The term is not a single-gene request.
    It is needed for the whole AcrIII-1 family, which is widespread in archaeal and
    bacterial viruses and in proviruses, and it would equally serve the host-encoded
    Crn1 and Crn2 ring nucleases, for example UniProtKB:Q97YD2, which perform the
    same chemistry as self-regulation of the host response rather than as counter-defence.
    It is also the mirror image of a second gap, the absence of a term for the cyclic
    oligoadenylate synthase activity of the Cas10 Palm domain, recorded in MODULE:crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity,
    and the two should be requested together so that the ontology can represent both
    ends of the signalling step.
