Gene Description

BSK1 (BRASSINOSTEROID-SIGNALING KINASE 1, At4g35230) is a receptor-like cytoplasmic kinase of the RLCK-XII family with an N-terminal kinase domain and a C-terminal tetratricopeptide-repeat (TPR) domain. It is anchored to the cytoplasmic face of the plasma membrane by N-myristoylation and S-acylation (by PAT19/20/22). In brassinosteroid signalling the activated receptor BRI1 phosphorylates BSK1 at Ser230, which promotes BSK1 binding to the BSU1 phosphatase; BSU1 then dephosphorylates and inactivates the GSK3-like kinase BIN2, allowing BZR1/BES1 accumulation. BSK1 belongs to a family of twelve partially redundant BSKs, and structural work on BSK8 suggests that BSKs may function largely as allosteric pseudokinase scaffolds, although BSK1 shows in vitro kinase activity. BSK1 also associates with the immune receptor FLS2 and contributes to pattern-triggered immunity and resistance to bacterial, fungal and oomycete pathogens.

Core Functions

Plasma-membrane-anchored receptor-like cytoplasmic kinase (RLCK-XII) that is phosphorylated by the activated BR receptor BRI1 (Ser230) and, once phosphorylated, binds the BSU1 phosphatase, relaying the BR signal from the receptor complex toward inactivation of BIN2. Its catalytic contribution is debated (paralog structures suggest pseudokinase behaviour), but in vitro kinase activity has been reported and is required for its immune function.
