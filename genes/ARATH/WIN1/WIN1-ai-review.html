Gene Description

WIN1 (At1g80600), also known as TUMOR PRONE5 (TUP5), is a pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent acetylornithine aminotransferase of Arabidopsis arginine biosynthesis. It converts N-acetylglutamate semialdehyde and glutamate to N-acetylornithine and 2-oxoglutarate. Functional GFP experiments demonstrate chloroplast localization. Arginine deficiency in tup5 mutants causes a blue-light-dependent loss of root meristem maintenance, while null alleles impair gametophytes and embryos. WIN1 also interacts with the bacterial effector HopW1-1, and its overexpression modifies pathogen responses and salicylic-acid accumulation.

Core Functions

WIN1 (At1g80600), also known as TUMOR PRONE5 (TUP5), is a pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent acetylornithine aminotransferase of Arabidopsis arginine biosynthesis. It converts N-acetylglutamate semialdehyde and glutamate to N-acetylornithine and 2-oxoglutarate. Functional GFP experiments demonstrate chloroplast localization. Arginine deficiency in tup5 mutants causes a blue-light-dependent loss of root meristem maintenance, while null alleles impair gametophytes and embryos. WIN1 also interacts with the bacterial effector HopW1-1, and its overexpression modifies pathogen responses and salicylic-acid accumulation.

Biological and evidence distinctions

Current UniProt name WIN1 is retained; TUP5 is the same At1g80600/Q9M8M7 product, explicitly identified in full PMID:23321422. Functional GFP complementation establishes chloroplast targeting, and two stromal proteomic assignments support the finer core location. Mitochondrial assignment remains uncertain: UniProt is by-similarity and the PMID:28887381 original supplement classifies this row as plastid with no dual-localization flag. That result does not exclude a second location. Copper capture cannot be adjudicated from the abstract of PMID:20018591; normal force-fetch plus Europe PMC and web full-text attempts did not retrieve the target-specific table (500/captcha); use UNDECIDED, not REMOVE. PMID:18266921 explicitly separates WIN1 from WIN2/3: WIN1 overexpression suppresses rather than promotes resistance, so do not copy WIN2/3 effects. Root-development refinement preserves the stronger primary-root phenotype without assigning a direct blue-light sensor function.

    acetylornithine aminotransferase of Arabidopsis arginine biosynthesis. It converts N-acetylglutamate
    semialdehyde and glutamate to N-acetylornithine and 2-oxoglutarate. Functional GFP experiments demonstrate
    chloroplast localization. Arginine deficiency in tup5 mutants causes a blue-light-dependent loss of
    root meristem maintenance, while null alleles impair gametophytes and embryos. WIN1 also interacts
    with the bacterial effector HopW1-1, and its overexpression modifies pathogen responses and salicylic-acid
    accumulation.
