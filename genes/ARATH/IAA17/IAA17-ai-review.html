Gene Description

IAA17 (AUXIN RESISTANT 3, AXR3; At1g04250) is an Aux/IAA transcriptional repressor of auxin-responsive genes. Like other canonical Aux/IAAs it is a short-lived nuclear protein with an EAR-motif domain I that recruits TOPLESS-family co-repressors, a domain II degron recognized by the SCF(TIR1/AFB) auxin receptors in an auxin-dependent manner, and a C-terminal PB1 domain that mediates homo-oligomerization and heterodimerization with AUXIN RESPONSE FACTORs, including the activators ARF5, ARF7 and ARF19. At low auxin IAA17 represses ARF-dependent transcription; auxin promotes SCF(TIR1)-dependent ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of IAA17 (half-life of minutes), releasing ARFs. The semidominant axr3-1 mutation in domain II stabilizes the protein and causes pleiotropic auxin-related phenotypes including altered root growth, root hair and gravitropic defects, and increased apical dominance. IAA17 is itself rapidly induced by auxin, forming part of the negative feedback in the pathway.

  EAR-motif domain I that recruits TOPLESS-family co-repressors, a domain II degron recognized by
  the SCF(TIR1/AFB) auxin receptors in an auxin-dependent manner, and a C-terminal PB1 domain that
  mediates homo-oligomerization and heterodimerization with AUXIN RESPONSE FACTORs, including the
  activators ARF5, ARF7 and ARF19. At low auxin IAA17 represses ARF-dependent transcription; auxin
  promotes SCF(TIR1)-dependent ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of IAA17 (half-life of
  minutes), releasing ARFs. The semidominant axr3-1 mutation in domain II stabilizes the protein and
  causes pleiotropic auxin-related phenotypes including altered root growth, root hair and
  gravitropic defects, and increased apical dominance. IAA17 is itself rapidly induced by auxin,
  forming part of the negative feedback in the pathway.
