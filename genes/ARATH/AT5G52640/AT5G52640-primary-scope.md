# HSP90.1 primary and phylogenetic scope check (2026-09-20)

P27323/AT5G52640 is HSP90.1. The actual PTHR11528 PAINT tree places leaf PTN000163824 below root PTN000163527 and cytosolic-HSP90 node PTN000163629. These supply the current positive protein-folding, ATPase, ATP-binding, protein stabilization, complex, cytosol, plasma-membrane, perinuclear and cellular-heat assertions. The exact path, source GAF rows, IBDs and raw-response hash are in AT5G52640-P27323-paint-lineage.json. No ancestral loss on that path was established. The obsolete GO:0051082 assertion is absent from current PAINT; term retirement does not demonstrate loss of unfolded-protein recognition.

Direct target biochemistry (PMID:23827697, central cache abstract-only) reports both holdase and ATP-dependent foldase activity for HSP90.1 and HSP90.3; quantitative differences do not mean absence in HSP90.1. PMID:19366428 explicitly describes heat-induced nuclear ROF1–HSP90.1 complexes. PMID:17644812 purified plasma-membrane proteomics included peripheral proteins; absence of a transmembrane helix is no localization refutation. For the cell-wall HDA from PMID:16287169, the abstract does not resolve target-specific peptide attribution or distinguish an extracellular pool from co-purification. Its experimental annotation is unresolved, not disproven.

The 2025 published TWD1/ABCB study (PMID:41027915; DOI:10.1038/s41467-025-63780-w, full central cache) supersedes the 2024 preprint used in Falcon. Native-promoter HSP90.1–mNeonGreen colocalizes with TWD1 in roots, and Figure 2e reports ABCB1 co-immunoprecipitation with both HSP90.1 and HSP90.3. The detailed FRET interface assays mainly use HSP90.3. hsp90.1 and hsp90.3 mutants reduce basipetal auxin transport, while the combined pharmacological experiments establish membrane-client stabilization. ABCB proteins move auxin; HSP90 contributes chaperoning and transporter abundance regulation. PIN1 redistribution does not establish PIN1 as a direct HSP90.1 client.

[TIR1 primary, PMID:26728313](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4728404/) uses HSP90.2 for recombinant pull-down and BiFC, with broader HSP90 inhibitor/immunoblot experiments. It does not establish the old exact-HSP90.1 TIR1 claim. [ZTL primary, PMID:21949396](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3189077/) reduces the four cytosolic paralogs together, explicitly acknowledges antibody inability to distinguish them, and supports a cytosolic-family clock mechanism. Gene-specific participation remains plausible but the old proposal is not an isolated HSP90.1 result.

[COP1–ELF3 primary, PMID:36707919](https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nph.18776), full publisher Results and Methods read although the central cache is abstract-only: “HSP90.1 enhanced the interaction between COP1 and ELF3”. This uses an explicit HSP90.1-Flag construct in the luciferase complementation experiment (Figure 6a). Growth phenotypes use RNAi against HSP90.1–4 or inhibitors; the antibody recognizes HSP90.1–2. PIF4 transcription is regulated through ELF3, and overexpressed PIF4 protein abundance is not reduced by inhibitor treatment. Thus PIF4 is not established as a directly matured client. The combination supports HSP90.1 participation in the growth-repressor degradation module, with combined-paralog physiological scope. Darkness and warm short-day experiments do not separately establish far-red, shade-avoidance or photoperiodic-flowering functions. The final composition of a COP1–ELF3 complex after chaperoning was not established, which matters to GO:0051131's exclusion of the chaperone from the finished complex.

QuickGO term/ancestry and comparator snapshots accompany this note. GO:0010100 is a regulation process; CUL4, CSN4 and SPA1 carry it, so non-DNA-binding components of the repression machinery can do regulatory work. HSP90.1 contributes a specific protein-interaction step rather than merely being a required substrate. No P27323 entry was found in gocams/index.tsv. Human HSP90AA1/HSP90AB1 and PTGES3 carry GO:0051131, so its withdrawal here is about the precise target experiment and final-complex requirement, not a claim that HSP90s never assemble complexes.

The Falcon body and limitations were read; immune and ABCB conclusions are useful with the assay-specific caveats above. The Perplexity body was read but conflates family-level, nonplant and paralog-specific results with HSP90.1, including TIR1, direct PIF4 maturation and essential embryogenesis. Viable hsp90.1 mutants refute an unqualified claim that this single gene is invariably essential; they do not exclude a redundant embryonic role.
