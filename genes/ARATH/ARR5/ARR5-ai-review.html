Gene Description

ARR5 (ARABIDOPSIS RESPONSE REGULATOR 5; IBC6, ATRR2) is a type-A response regulator of Arabidopsis thaliana. It is a small (184 aa) protein consisting of a two-component receiver domain with a conserved phospho-accepting aspartate (Asp87) and a short C-terminal extension, without a DNA-binding output domain. ARR5 is a primary cytokinin-response gene: its transcription is induced within minutes of cytokinin treatment by type-B ARRs, with highest expression in root and shoot apical meristems. ARR5 protein is phosphorylated through the AHK-AHP phosphorelay, which is required for its activity and stabilizes the protein in response to cytokinin; it is otherwise turned over by the proteasome in an AXR1/RUB-dependent manner. Acting redundantly with other type-A ARRs, ARR5 provides negative feedback on cytokinin signaling, probably by competing with type-B ARRs for phosphoryl groups from AHPs and through phosphorylation-dependent interactions; higher-order type-A arr mutants are hypersensitive to cytokinin and show altered red light responses. ARR5 is found in the nucleus and cytoplasm.

Core Functions

ARR5 is a cytokinin-induced type-A response regulator whose receiver domain accepts phosphoryl groups from the AHP phosphorelay; phosphorylated ARR5 is stabilized and acts as a negative feedback regulator that attenuates cytokinin signaling.
