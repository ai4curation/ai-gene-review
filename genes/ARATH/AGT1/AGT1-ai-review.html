Gene Description

AGT1 (At2g13360), also called serine:glyoxylate aminotransferase (SGAT) or alanine:glyoxylate aminotransferase 1, is a peroxisomal, pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent class-V aminotransferase related to animal AGT1/AGXT. Encoded by a single gene, it catalyses the photorespiratory serine:glyoxylate transamination. Serine made in the mitochondria is converted to hydroxypyruvate, and its amino group aminates a second glyoxylate to glycine, which closes the nitrogen balance of the C2 cycle. The enzyme is a homodimer with a C-terminal type 1 peroxisomal targeting signal, and serine-binding residues from the partner subunit explain its preference for serine over alanine. It also transaminates alanine:glyoxylate, serine:pyruvate and asparagine with glyoxylate, pyruvate or hydroxypyruvate, and asparagine is a high-capacity donor. The sat mutants, which carry a P251L substitution at the dimer interface, completely lack serine:glyoxylate aminotransferase activity, accumulate serine and glycine, and are inviable in air but normal at elevated CO2. AGT1 is most highly expressed in green tissues, but it is also expressed in roots, where it is induced by asparagine.

Identity and family

  • AGT1 (alanine:glyoxylate aminotransferase 1; serine:glyoxylate aminotransferase, SGAT) is a class
    V PLP-dependent aminotransferase, homolog of animal AGT1/AGXT, encoded by a single gene with a
    C-terminal PTS1 PMID:11309139. PANTHER PTHR21152:SF24; Pfam PF00266 (Aminotran_5).
  • Crystal structure: homodimer, PLP bound non-covalently; residues Tyr35'/Arg36' from the partner
    subunit bind L-serine, unlike human/Anabaena AGT1 that prefer alanine PMID:31681359.

Activities (substrate breadth)

  • Recombinant AGT1 catalyses Ala:glyoxylate, Ser:glyoxylate and Ser:pyruvate transamination, with
    Ser:glyoxylate preferred PMID:11309139.
  • Native leaf SGAT: Ala:glyoxylate about 55% of Ser:glyoxylate rate; kinetic constants suggest Ala and
    Ser could be used similarly as amino donors PMID:18235971.
  • Asparagine is an efficient donor (Vmax 5-20x higher than Ser/Ala); Asn induces AGT1 transcripts in
    roots PMID:23098902.

Judgement: L-serine:glyoxylate transaminase (GO:0050281) is the core photorespiratory activity, the
one whose loss in the sat mutants is lethal in air. Ala:glyoxylate (GO:0008453), Ser:pyruvate
(GO:0004760) and Asn:oxo-acid (GO:0047297) activities are real but non-core.

Localization

  • Peroxisome by in vitro import and fractionation PMID:11309139; BiFC with RabGAP22 in peroxisomes
    PMID:24505423; abundant in leaf peroxisome proteome (PMID:17951448).
  • Proteomic hits in chloroplast/stroma, mitochondrion, plasma membrane, apoplast and crude cytosol
    are treated as contamination.

Physiological role

  • sat mutants lack SGAT activity, are inviable in air but normal at high CO2, and accumulate Ser and Gly
    PMID:16592821.
  • The sat lesion is a P251L substitution in AGT1 that abolishes activity PMID:11309139.
  • Role in the cycle: the SGAT step supplies the second amino group for glycine formation
    PMID:22303256.
  • Non-photorespiratory roles: Asn catabolism (PMID:23098902); root development and salt tolerance in
    overexpressors (PMID:31161264, not cached; mentioned in UniProt only).

Annotation-specific notes

  • Peroxisome EXP from PMID:24505423 (RabGAP22 paper): BiFC signal of the RabGAP22-AGT1 pair in
    peroxisomes of N. benthamiana. Supports peroxisomal localization; accepted.
  • L-alanine:oxo-acid transaminase (GO:0047635, Rhea IEA) is the parent of GO:0008453; the Rhea reaction
    mapped (Asn + pyruvate = 2-oxosuccinamate + Ala) is a reversible transamination involving alanine.

description: >-
  AGT1 (At2g13360), also called serine:glyoxylate aminotransferase (SGAT) or alanine:glyoxylate
  aminotransferase 1, is a peroxisomal, pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent class-V aminotransferase
  related to animal AGT1/AGXT. Encoded by a single gene, it catalyses the photorespiratory
  serine:glyoxylate transamination. Serine made in the mitochondria is converted to hydroxypyruvate,
  and its amino group aminates a second glyoxylate to glycine, which closes the nitrogen balance
  of the C2 cycle. The enzyme is a homodimer with a C-terminal type 1 peroxisomal targeting
  signal, and serine-binding residues from the partner subunit explain its preference for serine
  over alanine. It also transaminates alanine:glyoxylate, serine:pyruvate and asparagine with
  glyoxylate, pyruvate or hydroxypyruvate, and asparagine is a high-capacity donor. The sat
  mutants, which carry a P251L substitution at the dimer interface, completely lack
  serine:glyoxylate aminotransferase activity, accumulate serine and glycine, and are inviable in
  air but normal at elevated CO2. AGT1 is most highly expressed in green tissues, but it is also
  expressed in roots, where it is induced by asparagine.
