Gene Description

EARLY FLOWERING 3 (ELF3; also PYK20) is a large (695 aa) nuclear protein with no recognisable enzymatic or DNA-binding domain. It is the central scaffold of the evening complex (EC), a dusk-phased transcriptional repressor complex of the Arabidopsis circadian clock made up of ELF3, ELF4 and the MYB-family DNA-binding protein LUX ARRHYTHMO. ELF3 is necessary and sufficient to bridge ELF4 and LUX. LUX targets the complex to promoters, and the EC then represses evening and midday clock genes (PRR9, PRR7, GI, LUX) and growth regulators such as PIF4 and PIF5, gating hypocotyl elongation to the end of the night. ELF3 is required for sustained rhythms in continuous light and under temperature cycles, and it acts as a zeitnehmer that antagonises light input to the clock during the night. A C-terminal polyglutamine-containing prion-like domain lets ELF3 sense ambient temperature: warmth drives reversible phase separation into nuclear speckles and reduces EC binding to its targets, which links the clock to thermomorphogenesis. ELF3 also binds phytochrome B and acts as a substrate adaptor that lets COP1 ubiquitinate GIGANTEA. Loss of ELF3 causes photoperiod-insensitive early flowering, long hypocotyls and arrhythmia in the light.

Core Functions

Scaffold of the evening complex. ELF3 bridges the DNA-binding protein LUX and ELF4 into a dusk-phased nuclear repressor complex, which LUX targets to promoters of clock genes (PRR9, PRR7, GI, LUX) and of growth regulators (PIF4/PIF5).

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: How does phase separation of the ELF3 prion-like domain mechanistically reduce EC occupancy at target promoters: by sequestering ELF3 away from LUX, or by altering the conformation of the bound complex?

Q: Does ELF3's COP1 substrate-adaptor role in GI degradation operate within the evening complex or in a separate, EC-independent pool of ELF3?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Temperature-shift ChIP-seq of LUX and ELF3 in lines expressing PrD-deleted or PrD-swapped ELF3 variants, combined with in vitro EC DNA-binding at varying temperatures.

Hypothesis: PrD-driven ELF3 condensation is sufficient to release the EC from target promoters at warm temperature.
