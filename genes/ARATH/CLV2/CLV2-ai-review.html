Gene Description

CLAVATA2 (CLV2) is a single-pass type I transmembrane receptor-like protein (AtRLP10) with an extracellular leucine-rich repeat domain and a short cytoplasmic tail that lacks a kinase domain. CLV2 forms a heteromeric receptor complex with the membrane pseudokinase CORYNE (CRN); the two proteins associate through their transmembrane/juxtamembrane regions and require each other for export from the endoplasmic reticulum to the plasma membrane. The CLV2/CRN complex perceives CLV3 and other CLE peptides, in parallel with and partly in cooperation with the CLV1/BAM receptor kinases and CIK co-receptor kinases, to restrict WUSCHEL expression and stem cell number in shoot and floral meristems. Whether CLV2 binds CLE peptides directly is contested (radioligand binding versus photoaffinity labelling), so it is best viewed as a CLE co-receptor. CLV2/CRN is also needed for responses to many root-active CLE peptides in the protophloem and proximal root meristem (including CLE14-triggered root meristem differentiation under phosphate starvation), for gynoecium and floral organ development, and for perception of CLE-like effectors secreted by cyst nematodes.

Functional annotation of Arabidopsis thaliana CLV2

Identity and primary function

Identity verified. The requested protein is A. thaliana CLAVATA2 (CLV2), locus At1g65380, also called AtRLP10; UniProt O80809 is the accession supplied in the query. Independent Arabidopsis work explicitly equates CLV2 with AtRLP10, and a primary study identifies its rlp10-1 allele as a clv2 null mutant. This report does not substitute CLV2 homologs from other plants for the Arabidopsis protein. (wang2009theroleof pages 93-97, jones2021clavatasignalingensures pages 1-3)

CLV2 is a plasma-membrane receptor-like protein, not an enzyme or transporter. Its extracellular leucine-rich-repeat (LRR) region and single membrane-spanning region fit the supplied RLP-family/domain annotation; its short intracellular region lacks a protein-kinase domain. Its principal established role is to help assemble and operate cell-surface receptor complexes that regulate responses to secreted CLAVATA3/EMBRYO SURROUNDING REGION-related (CLE) peptide signals. The best-characterized outputs are restriction of shoot-meristem stem-cell proliferation and context-dependent regulation of root protophloem and flower development. It therefore has no assigned catalytic reaction or transported substrate; importantly, involvement in peptide responses is not equivalent to proof that every implicated peptide binds CLV2 directly. (hazak2017perceptionofroot‐active pages 1-2, demesaarevalo2024intercellularcommunicationin pages 4-6, hazak2017perceptionofroot‐active pages 7-8, ren2019cle25peptideregulates pages 11-13)

Molecular role and site of action

CLV2 constitutively associates with CORYNE (CRN/SOL2) through membrane-proximal regions. CRN contributes an intracellular pseudokinase domain but has little extracellular receptor domain. Interaction assays and imaging support a CLV2–CRN complex at the plasma membrane, including assemblies with CLV1 in some experimental settings. These observations establish a membrane-associated signaling platform; they do not establish that CLV2 itself phosphorylates a substrate or that CRN has demonstrated kinase catalytic activity. Other active receptor kinases can participate according to tissue and signal. (zhu2010analysisofinteractions pages 1-2, demesaarevalo2024intercellularcommunicationin pages 4-6, yamaguchi2016clepeptidesand pages 4-5)

Localization is functionally interdependent: when CLV2 and CRN were expressed separately, much of each protein remained intracellular, whereas co-expression increased their plasma-membrane delivery. In Arabidopsis roots, loss of CRN made fluorescent CLV2 largely diffuse, and loss of CLV2 reduced membrane accumulation of fluorescent CRN. Native-promoter experiments place the relevant root complex in developing protophloem sieve-element cells. Thus, the extracellular LRR region faces the cell-wall/apoplastic environment where secreted CLE peptides act, while receptor-associated signaling is organized at the plasma membrane. (hazak2017perceptionofroot‐active pages 7-8, hazak2017perceptionofroot‐active pages 6-7)

Signaling pathways and experimental evidence

Shoot stem-cell homeostasis

Stem cells in the shoot central zone produce the processed, secreted CLV3 peptide. WUSCHEL (WUS), produced in the underlying organizing center, promotes stem-cell identity; CLV signaling limits WUS expression, forming a negative-feedback circuit that constrains the stem-cell pool. CLV2–CRN is required for normal pathway output, alongside receptor systems involving CLV1, RPK2 and CLV3-insensitive kinases (CIKs). clv2 and crn mutants overproliferate stem cells and develop enlarged meristems and excess floral organs. The relatively stronger clv1 clv2 or clv1 crn phenotypes, versus the nonadditive clv2 crn phenotype, support a shared CLV2–CRN module with functions at least partly parallel to CLV1. (muller2008thereceptorkinase pages 1-2, demesaarevalo2024intercellularcommunicationin pages 4-6, yamaguchi2016clepeptidesand pages 4-5)

Ligand-binding qualification: A study summarized in a 2016 review reported binding of a CLV3-derived CLE peptide by CLV2, but the authoritative 2024 Annual Review of Plant Biology states that direct binding of CLV3 or another CLE peptide by the physiological CLV2–CRN heteromer has not been established; the precise contribution of that heteromer to ligand recognition versus receptor organization remains unresolved. By contrast, direct CLV3 recognition by CLV1 is better established. CLV2–CRN may additionally stabilize or organize larger membrane receptor complexes, but this remains a mechanistic possibility rather than a demonstrated universal mechanism. These findings should not be collapsed into the unqualified statement that CLV2 is the direct CLV3 receptor. (demesaarevalo2024intercellularcommunicationin pages 4-6, yamaguchi2016clepeptidesand pages 4-5)

Root CLE responses and vascular development

In developing root protophloem, CLV2–CRN supports responses to CLE45, whose directly demonstrated ligand-binding receptor is BAM3. Purified BAM3 ectodomain binds CLE45 with a dissociation constant of approximately 120 nM and 1:1 stoichiometry; this is not a CLV2–CLE45 affinity. CRN helps maintain BAM3 protein abundance and receptor-complex function, and expressing CRN specifically in protophloem restores the crn mutant’s sensitivity to root-active CLE peptides. CLV2–CRN therefore has a well-supported role in cellular competence for CLE45 signaling without a resolved direct CLV2–CLE45 binding reaction. (hazak2017perceptionofroot‐active pages 1-2, hazak2017perceptionofroot‐active pages 7-8, hazak2017perceptionofroot‐active pages 6-7)

CLV2 also interacts with the LRR receptor kinase CLERK/CIK2 in a proposed CLE25-responsive complex that promotes early phloem initiation. Bimolecular fluorescence complementation and co-immunoprecipitation support CLERK–CLV2 association at the plasma membrane; CLE25 loss delays protophloem differentiation, whereas an antagonistic CLE25 variant markedly disrupts phloem initiation and continuity. In one assay of that antagonist, stem sieve-plate counts averaged 1.2 ± 1.1, versus 5.3 ± 1.6 in wild type (n = 6 per group). The interaction and genetic evidence support a CLERK–CLV2 model, but do not provide a purified-CLV2/CLE25 binding constant. The proposed positive CLE25–CLERK–CLV2 pathway must also be distinguished from the largely inhibitory CLE45–BAM3-associated pathway later in protophloem differentiation. (ren2019cle25peptideregulates pages 7-9, ren2019cle25peptideregulates pages 1-2, ren2019cle25peptideregulates pages 11-13, ren2019cle25peptideregulates pages 13-15)

Peptide-treatment results require spatial and physiological caution. clv2 mutants can be resistant to externally supplied CLE40, yet clv2 loss alone need not increase root-meristem growth under ordinary conditions; mutant analyses localized the CLE40-peptide response chiefly to the proximal, not distal, root meristem. A 2023 study identified CLE33 as another root-active peptide acting redundantly with CLE45 in protophloem regulation through BAM3, expanding the known ligand context without establishing CLE33 as a directly bound CLV2 ligand. (pallakies2014thecle40and pages 7-8, carbonnel2023thecle33peptide pages 4-5)

Flower outgrowth, temperature and auxin

CLV2–CRN has a separable reproductive function: it promotes auxin-dependent flower-primordium outgrowth in colder and ambient conditions. In clv2/rlp10-1 and crn mutants, primordia can initiate but fail to develop into flowers; CIK1/2/4 mutants show similar defects, and mutation of the phosphatase POLTERGEIST suppresses the crn outgrowth defect. This phenotype is not reproduced by clv3 or a tested 12-CLE-gene mutant, so its activating peptide remains unidentified and should not automatically be assigned to CLV3. (jones2021clavatasignalingensures pages 1-3, jones2021clavatasignalingensures pages 4-5)

The defect is prominent around 16–18°C and ambient 22–24°C, but is substantially suppressed at approximately 31°C, when heat-responsive auxin production can compensate. Experimentally increasing YUC1-dependent auxin production suppressed the crn defect in 9 of 13 transgenic lines at ambient temperature but only partially in 2 of 18 lines in colder conditions; clv2 yuc1 yuc4 plants showed particularly severe combined defects in warm conditions. These are laboratory genetic findings, not evidence of a deployed CLV2-based crop intervention. (jones2021clavatasignalingensures pages 5-6, jones2021clavatasignalingensures pages 6-7, jones2021clavatasignalingensures pages 7-8)

Biological interactions and applications

Arabidopsis clv2 and crn mutants also have pathogen-dependent changes in susceptibility, and some plant-parasitic nematodes deploy CLE-like peptides that exploit host CLAVATA signaling. The 2024 Plant Physiology review notes involvement of Arabidopsis CLV2–CRN in responses to certain nematode CLE mimics, but emphasizes that the endogenous ligand and detailed receptor pathway for several plant–pathogen responses remain unknown. These findings make CLV2 relevant to research on developmental resilience and host–pathogen interactions; they do not establish a general CLV2 resistance mechanism or a field-validated application. Findings for tomato CLV2 or other homologs should not be counted as direct evidence about Arabidopsis O80809. (bashyal2024clavatasignalingin pages 11-12, bashyal2024clavatasignalingin pages 12-13)

Current interpretation and research limits

The most defensible functional annotation is “kinase-lacking, plasma-membrane LRR receptor-like component of tissue-specific CLE signaling complexes.” CLV2’s partnership with CRN is experimentally established, while partnerships with active kinases, including CLERK/CIKs or BAM3-containing assemblies, depend on cellular context. Its best-supported Arabidopsis processes are CLV-associated shoot stem-cell control, CLE-responsive root protophloem signaling, and temperature-sensitive flower-primordium outgrowth. The central unresolved questions are which ligands bind native CLV2-containing complexes directly, how receptor assemblies activate intracellular signaling given CRN’s pseudokinase status, and which cell-specific complexes operate under natural conditions. The 2024 expert reviews explicitly warn that synthetic-peptide application and overexpression can produce responses outside a peptide’s normal expression domain and that the in-vivo composition of many CLE receptor complexes remains uncertain. (bashyal2024clavatasignalingin pages 15-16, demesaarevalo2024intercellularcommunicationin pages 4-6, hazak2017perceptionofroot‐active pages 7-8, jones2021clavatasignalingensures pages 4-5)

CLV2 (CLAVATA2, AtRLP10, At1g65380; UniProt O80809) curation notes

Identity

  • UniProt O80809, receptor-like protein with extracellular LRRs, a single TM helix, and a short cytoplasmic tail with no kinase domain PMID:10521522.

Key findings

  • Genetics: clv2 enlarges shoot and floral meristems, acting in the CLV1/CLV3 pathway for meristems but separately for organ development PMID:9729492.
  • CLV2/CRN acts in parallel with CLV1 PMID:18381924.
  • CLV2 and CRN need each other for ER export PMID:19933383.
  • Ligand binding is disputed:
  • Radioligand binding gives a Kd of about 32 nM for CLV2 PMID:20626648.
  • Photoaffinity labelling found no binding [PMID:25754504 "We showed that CLV2 and RPK2 exhibited no direct binding to the CLV3 peptide."; "the CLV2/CRN complex and RPK2 are not involved in direct ligand interactions but may act as co-receptors."].
  • The deep research (falcon) also notes that direct binding by the physiological heteromer is not established.
  • Decision: core MF is coreceptor activity (GO:0015026). The IMP GO:0001653 is MODIFIED to it, and the CLE IPI rows are MODIFIED to peptide hormone binding (GO:0017046), matching the CLV1 review's use of that term. The MODIFY rests on the radioligand data the curator used; the photoaffinity refutation is stated in each row's summary rather than cited as support, and GO:0017046 is not used as a core function.
  • Root: CLV2 is required for the response to CLE peptides that consume the root meristem PMID:16055633. CLV2/CRN is needed to sense root-active CLEs, with the main action in the protophloem PMID:28607033. CLE14 acts through CLV2 under low phosphate PMID:28586647.
  • CIK co-receptors PMID:29581511.
  • Nematode CLE mimics PMID:21265896.

Decisions summary

  • Accepted: plasma membrane, regulation of meristem growth, meristem development, and IBA signaling receptor activity.
  • Kept as non-core: ER membrane (transit compartment), root, phloem and organ phenotypes.
  • Modified:
  • Protein binding with CRN to signaling receptor binding.
  • Protein binding with CLE peptides to peptide hormone binding.
  • Signal transduction to cell surface receptor signaling pathway.

PR #4384 review follow-up (2026-10-06)

  • Removed PMID:25754504 ("no direct binding") from supported_by of the CLE-peptide MODIFY rows: it refutes rather than supports the action; the dispute stays in each row's summary. Reworded reason so it justifies the MODIFY instead of claiming non-core.
  • Trimmed the GO:0015026 reason: coreceptor activity entails combining with the messenger, so it is not fully neutral on the binding dispute.

  associate through their transmembrane/juxtamembrane regions and require each other for export from the
  endoplasmic reticulum to the plasma membrane. The CLV2/CRN complex perceives CLV3 and other CLE peptides,
  in parallel with and partly in cooperation with the CLV1/BAM receptor kinases and CIK co-receptor kinases,
  to restrict WUSCHEL expression and stem cell number in shoot and floral meristems. Whether CLV2 binds
  CLE peptides directly is contested (radioligand binding versus photoaffinity labelling), so it is best
  viewed as a CLE co-receptor. CLV2/CRN is also needed for responses to many root-active CLE peptides
  in the protophloem and proximal root meristem (including CLE14-triggered root meristem differentiation
  under phosphate starvation), for gynoecium and floral organ development, and for perception of CLE-like
  effectors secreted by cyst nematodes.
