Gene Description

TOO MANY MOUTHS (TMM; RLP17) is a plasma-membrane leucine-rich-repeat receptor-like protein (RLP) with an extracellular LRR domain and a single transmembrane segment but no cytoplasmic kinase domain. It is expressed in proliferating stomatal-lineage (postprotodermal) cells and functions as a co-receptor for the ERECTA-family receptor kinases (ERECTA, ERL1, ERL2). TMM forms constitutive heteromeric complexes with ERECTA and ERL1 (and associates with SERK co-receptor kinases), and the TMM-ERf complex creates the binding pocket that recognizes the inhibitory EPF1 and EPF2 peptides and the competing positive peptide stomagen/EPFL9, while excluding the stem-growth EPFL4/EPFL6 peptides. TMM therefore acts as a specificity switch that directs ERECTA-family signalling, via the YODA-MKK4/5-MPK3/6 cascade, toward inhibition of stomatal development and the orientation of spacing divisions. Loss of TMM randomizes the orientation of asymmetric spacing divisions and permits entry divisions next to existing stomata, producing stomatal clusters in leaves, whereas stomata are absent from some organs such as inflorescence stems, reflecting region-specific positive and negative effects mediated through different EPF/EPFL ligands.

Core Functions

Non-catalytic LRR receptor-like co-receptor that forms constitutive complexes with ERECTA-family receptor kinases at the plasma membrane and, together with them, creates the binding site for the inhibitory EPF1/EPF2 peptides (and the competing stomagen), conferring ligand specificity on ERECTA-family signalling that orients spacing divisions and prevents stomatal clustering.
