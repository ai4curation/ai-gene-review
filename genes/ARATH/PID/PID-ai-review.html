Gene Description

PINOID (PID) is a plant-specific AGCVIII (AGC3 subclass) serine/threonine protein kinase that controls the direction of polar auxin transport. It associates with the inner face of the plasma membrane, enriched at the apical (shootward) side of cells, through phospholipid binding by a kinase insertion domain, and is activated by PDK1-mediated trans-phosphorylation and autophosphorylation. PID phosphorylates serines in conserved motifs of the central hydrophilic loop of PIN auxin efflux carriers. This phosphorylation recruits PINs into GNOM-independent apical targeting, acting as a binary switch from basal to apical PIN polarity that is opposed by PP2A/PP6 phosphatases, and it also activates PIN-mediated auxin efflux. PID is expressed in embryos, organ primordia, vasculature and the inflorescence meristem, and its expression is auxin-inducible. Loss of PID shifts PIN1 basally in the shoot apex and gives pin-like inflorescences and defects in cotyledon and floral organ formation; overexpression shifts PINs apically and causes root meristem collapse. PID acts with the related WAG1/WAG2/PID2 kinases and the NPH3-family MAB4/NPY proteins.

description: PINOID (PID) is a plant-specific AGCVIII (AGC3 subclass) serine/threonine protein
  kinase that controls the direction of polar auxin transport. It associates with the inner
  face of the plasma membrane, enriched at the apical (shootward) side of cells, through phospholipid
  binding by a kinase insertion domain, and is activated by PDK1-mediated trans-phosphorylation
  and autophosphorylation. PID phosphorylates serines in conserved motifs of the central hydrophilic
  loop of PIN auxin efflux carriers. This phosphorylation recruits PINs into GNOM-independent
  apical targeting, acting as a binary switch from basal to apical PIN polarity that is opposed
  by PP2A/PP6 phosphatases, and it also activates PIN-mediated auxin efflux. PID is expressed
  in embryos, organ primordia, vasculature and the inflorescence meristem, and its expression
  is auxin-inducible. Loss of PID shifts PIN1 basally in the shoot apex and gives pin-like
  inflorescences and defects in cotyledon and floral organ formation; overexpression shifts
  PINs apically and causes root meristem collapse. PID acts with the related WAG1/WAG2/PID2
  kinases and the NPH3-family MAB4/NPY proteins.
