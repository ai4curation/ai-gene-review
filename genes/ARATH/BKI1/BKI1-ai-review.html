Gene Description

BKI1 (BRI1 KINASE INHIBITOR 1, At5g42750) is a small, largely intrinsically disordered protein of the plant-specific BKI1/MAKR family. In resting cells it is tethered to the inner face of the plasma membrane by a reiterated polybasic [KR][KR] motif, where it binds the cytoplasmic kinase domain of the brassinosteroid receptor BRI1 and prevents BRI1 from associating with its coreceptor BAK1, keeping the receptor inactive. Brassinosteroid binding activates BRI1, which phosphorylates BKI1 on a tyrosine within the membrane-targeting motif and on serines in its C-terminal region; phosphorylated BKI1 is released into the cytosol, allowing formation of the active BRI1-BAK1 complex. Cytosolic phospho-BKI1 binds 14-3-3 proteins, which may relieve 14-3-3-mediated cytoplasmic retention of the BZR1/BES1 transcription factors. BKI1 therefore acts as a receptor-level negative regulator (and, after release, a modest positive modulator) of brassinosteroid signalling.

Core Functions

Plasma-membrane-anchored intrinsically disordered inhibitor of the BRI1 receptor kinase: binds the BRI1 kinase domain and blocks BRI1 association with its coreceptor BAK1, keeping the receptor quiescent in the absence of brassinosteroid. BR-activated BRI1 phosphorylates BKI1 (including a tyrosine in its polybasic membrane-targeting motif), releasing it into the cytosol.
