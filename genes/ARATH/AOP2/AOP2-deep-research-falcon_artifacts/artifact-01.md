| Aspect | AOP2 role or finding | Evidence and interpretation | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis | AOP2 acts at the side-chain modification stage, converting methylsulfinylalkyl glucosinolates into alkenyl glucosinolates. Established Arabidopsis substrate–product pairs are 3-methylsulfinylpropyl glucosinolate (glucoiberin) → 2-propenyl/allyl glucosinolate (sinigrin), and 4-methylsulfinylbutyl glucosinolate (glucoraphanin) → 3-butenyl glucosinolate (gluconapin). | Biochemical assays and Arabidopsis complementation support a direct metabolite-forming function rather than a merely correlative pathway association. | (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015) |
| Control of pathway flux | Expression of functional AOP2 can increase both short- and long-chain aliphatic glucosinolate accumulation; one study reported an approximately twofold increase in total aliphatic glucosinolates. | AOP2 therefore influences not only product identity but also pathway-wide glucosinolate abundance through feedback regulation. | (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000041) |
| MYB28 and MYB29 interaction | AOP2 increases MYB28 and MYB29 transcript abundance. Its stimulation of aliphatic glucosinolate accumulation depends principally on MYB28, with a smaller contribution from MYB29; MYB28 and MYB29 can bind the AOP2 promoter. | These reciprocal relationships support a positive-feedback circuit between AOP2 and the principal transcriptional regulators of aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis. | (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000029) |
| MYC2-associated regulation | AOP2 expression increases MYC2 transcript levels and feeds into a MYC2–MYB28–MYB29 regulatory network. | MYC2 provides a mechanistic connection between AOP2-associated glucosinolate metabolism and jasmonate-responsive defense transcription, although the immediate molecular signal from cytoplasmic AOP2 remains unresolved. | (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000022) |
| Jasmonic-acid feedback | AOP2 changes the expression of 12 of 25 examined jasmonate-biosynthesis or metabolism genes, including strong induction of LOX3, LOX4, AOC1, AOC3, OPR3, CYP94B3, and CYP94C1, and increases transcripts for eight JAZ repressors. | The coordinated effects on jasmonate synthesis, turnover, and signaling components indicate feedback from a jasmonate-inducible specialized-metabolism pathway onto jasmonate signaling itself. | (pqac-00000017) |
| Jasmonate-response phenotype | AOP2-expressing seedlings have shorter roots under control conditions but reduced sensitivity to exogenous jasmonate; the phenotype persists independently of MYB28 and MYB29 and is not accompanied by a significant change in measured endogenous jasmonate concentration. | This separates at least part of AOP2’s signaling effect from MYB28/MYB29-dependent glucosinolate accumulation and suggests an additional, incompletely defined regulatory activity. | (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000022) |
| Additional transcriptional effects | In a myb28 myb29 background lacking detectable aliphatic glucosinolate substrates, AOP2 still upregulates GGP1, CYP83B1, and SOT16 and downregulates CYP81F4, GS-OX2, MBP1, NSP1, NSP3, and NSP4. | Persistence without detectable aliphatic substrates suggests that some effects may not require the canonical AOP2 reaction; proposed alternatives include low-level metabolites or an unidentified function of AOP2 RNA or protein. Direct nuclear regulation is considered unlikely because AOP2 is cytoplasmic. | (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000023) |
| Flowering time and circadian behavior | Introducing functional AOP2 into AOP-null backgrounds can alter circadian period and flowering time. AOP2 significantly delayed flowering in Col-0 but showed a non-significant tendency to accelerate it in Cph-0. | The developmental outcome is genetic-background dependent, supporting context-specific cross-talk between defense metabolism and reproductive timing rather than a universal flowering effect. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000024) |
| Relationship to AOP3 | AOP2 and AOP3 are closely related 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases derived from gene duplication. AOP2 forms alkenyl products from 3- and 4-carbon methylsulfinylalkyl glucosinolates, whereas AOP3 preferentially converts the 3-carbon substrate into 3-hydroxypropyl glucosinolate. Natural accessions commonly express AOP2, AOP3, or neither rather than both. | Divergence in catalytic specificity and accession-dependent expression makes the GS-AOP locus a major determinant of Arabidopsis glucosinolate chemotype. AOP2 generally has broader substrate activity and a stronger effect on total pathway flux than AOP3. | (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000034) |
| Chemical defense | AOP2 changes the aliphatic glucosinolate repertoire available to the glucosinolate–myrosinase defense system. Following tissue damage, glucosinolate hydrolysis generates bioactive products, including isothiocyanates, that contribute to defense against herbivores and microbial attackers. | AOP2 is best viewed as a modifier of defense chemistry: ecological outcomes depend on the structures produced, downstream hydrolysis, attacking organism, and plant genotype. Direct ecological effects of individual AOP2 products are less firmly established than the biochemical reaction. | (pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042, pqac-00000044, pqac-00000045) |
| Adaptation and fitness trade-offs | Natural structural and expression variation at the GS-AOP locus generates contrasting chemotypes. Increased glucosinolate production may improve defense but incurs metabolic costs and can alter growth or reproductive timing. | Experts interpret AOP2-linked diversity as a potential substrate for local adaptation, while cautioning that defense, growth, and flowering effects are conditional on genetic and environmental context. | (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000042) |
| Crop and nutritional significance | Functional AOP2 redirects glucoraphanin, whose hydrolysis can yield health-associated sulforaphane, toward alkenyl glucosinolates such as gluconapin; downstream pathway activity may also contribute to progoitrin accumulation. Loss of AOP2 function can therefore preserve glucoraphanin. | AOP2 orthologs are practical metabolic-engineering and breeding targets for modifying flavor, defense chemistry, and the balance between health-promoting and antinutritional glucosinolates in Brassica crops. | (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000044) |
