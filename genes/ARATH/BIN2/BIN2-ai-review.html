Gene Description

BIN2 (BRASSINOSTEROID-INSENSITIVE 2; also ASK7/ASK-eta, AtSK21, DWF12, UCU1; At4g18710) is a group-II GSK3/SHAGGY-like serine/threonine kinase and the central negative regulator of brassinosteroid (BR) signalling. In the absence of BR, active BIN2 (requiring autophosphorylation of Tyr200 in its activation loop) phosphorylates the BZR1 and BES1/BZR2 transcription factors, which inhibits their DNA binding, promotes 14-3-3 binding and cytoplasmic retention/nuclear export, and targets them for degradation. BR perception by BRI1-BAK1 leads, via BSK1 and CDG1, to activation of the BSU1 phosphatase, which dephosphorylates BIN2 pTyr200 and inactivates it; BR also promotes KIB1-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of BIN2. Gain-of-function bin2 alleles (e.g. bin2-1, in the conserved TREE motif) are semidominant BR-insensitive dwarfs. BIN2 is found in the cytoplasm and nucleus and can be recruited to the plasma membrane by scaffolds and receptors (OPS, POLAR/BASL, TDR). It also phosphorylates many other substrates, including YDA and SPCH (stomatal development), ARF2/ARF7 (auxin signalling), EGL3/TTG1 (root epidermal cell fate) and RAPTOR1B (TOR/autophagy), making it a hub that integrates BR status with other developmental and stress pathways. BIN2 acts redundantly with its paralogs BIL1/ASK-iota and BIL2/ASK-theta.

description: >-
  BIN2 (BRASSINOSTEROID-INSENSITIVE 2; also ASK7/ASK-eta, AtSK21, DWF12, UCU1; At4g18710) is a group-II
  GSK3/SHAGGY-like serine/threonine kinase and the central negative regulator of brassinosteroid (BR)
  signalling. In the absence of BR, active BIN2 (requiring autophosphorylation of Tyr200 in its activation
  loop) phosphorylates the BZR1 and BES1/BZR2 transcription factors, which inhibits their DNA binding,
  promotes 14-3-3 binding and cytoplasmic retention/nuclear export, and targets them for degradation.
  BR perception by BRI1-BAK1 leads, via BSK1 and CDG1, to activation of the BSU1 phosphatase, which dephosphorylates
  BIN2 pTyr200 and inactivates it; BR also promotes KIB1-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation
  of BIN2. Gain-of-function bin2 alleles (e.g. bin2-1, in the conserved TREE motif) are semidominant BR-insensitive
  dwarfs. BIN2 is found in the cytoplasm and nucleus and can be recruited to the plasma membrane by scaffolds
  and receptors (OPS, POLAR/BASL, TDR). It also phosphorylates many other substrates, including YDA and
  SPCH (stomatal development), ARF2/ARF7 (auxin signalling), EGL3/TTG1 (root epidermal cell fate) and
  RAPTOR1B (TOR/autophagy), making it a hub that integrates BR status with other developmental and stress
  pathways. BIN2 acts redundantly with its paralogs BIL1/ASK-iota and BIL2/ASK-theta.
