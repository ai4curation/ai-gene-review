Gene Description

BCAT3 (BRANCHED-CHAIN AMINOTRANSFERASE 3, At3g49680) is a plastid-targeted, pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent class-IV aminotransferase with a dual role in primary and specialized metabolism. As a canonical branched-chain aminotransferase it catalyzes the final, reversible transamination steps of branched-chain amino acid biosynthesis in the chloroplast, converting 4-methyl-2-oxopentanoate, (S)-3-methyl-2-oxopentanoate and 3-methyl-2-oxobutanoate to leucine, isoleucine and valine using glutamate/2-oxoglutarate; it complements the BCAA auxotrophy of a yeast bat1/bat2 mutant, and bcat3 knockouts have reduced free valine. BCAT3 also transaminates the omega-methylthio-2-oxo acids of the methionine chain-elongation cycle and is thought to catalyze the terminal transamination that converts chain-elongated 2-oxo acids to homomethionine and dihomomethionine for aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis; it can partly substitute for the cytosolic BCAT4 in the initial deamination of methionine, as shown by the strong additive loss of methionine-derived glucosinolates in bcat3 bcat4 double mutants. BCAT3 is expressed in the phloem, is wound-inducible and is co-expressed with BCAT4 and MAM1.

Comprehensive Research Report: BCAT3 (Branched-Chain-Amino-Acid Aminotransferase 3) in Arabidopsis thaliana

Gene Identity Confirmation

BCAT3 (UniProt: Q9M401, Locus: At3g49680) encodes a plastid-localized branched-chain amino acid aminotransferase in Arabidopsis thaliana (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2, diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 5-8). This enzyme belongs to the class-IV pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent aminotransferase family and is part of a small gene family of six to seven BCAT genes in Arabidopsis (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 3-5, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2).

Primary Enzymatic Function and Substrate Specificity

Catalyzed Reactions

BCAT3 is a pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP)-dependent enzyme that catalyzes reversible transamination reactions, converting 2-oxo acids to their corresponding amino acids (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2). The enzyme operates through the characteristic ping-pong bi-bi mechanism of PLP-dependent aminotransferases, in which the amino acid substrate reacts with enzyme-bound PLP to generate pyridoxamine phosphate (PMP) and release a keto acid product; subsequently, a keto acid substrate reacts with PMP to regenerate PLP and produce the corresponding amino acid (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 7-8).

Substrate Specificity - Branched-Chain Amino Acids

BCAT3 exhibits highest catalytic activity toward the branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) precursors. Detailed kinetic characterization of recombinant BCAT3 revealed the following substrate preferences (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3):

  • 4-Methyl-2-oxopentanoate (4MOP) - the leucine precursor - is the most preferred substrate with Km = 0.14 ± 0.04 mM and Vmax = 27.42 mmol·min⁻¹·mg⁻¹
  • 3-Methyl-2-oxopentanoate (3MOP) - the isoleucine precursor - shows comparable affinity with Km = 0.14 ± 0.3 mM and Vmax = 13.33 mmol·min⁻¹·mg⁻¹
  • 3-Methyl-2-oxobutanoate (3MOB) - the valine precursor - exhibits substantially weaker affinity with Km = 1.38 mM

These kinetic parameters demonstrate that BCAT3 catalyzes the terminal transamination step in BCAA biosynthesis, producing leucine, isoleucine, and valine from their respective 2-oxo acid precursors (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 3-4, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2).

Substrate Specificity - Methionine Chain Elongation

Importantly, BCAT3 also demonstrates substantial activity toward intermediates of the methionine chain elongation pathway (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 3-4, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10). The enzyme converts:

  • 4-Methylthio-2-oxobutanoate (MTOB) with Km = 1.92 mM
  • 5-Methylthio-2-oxopentanoate (MTOP) to homomethionine
  • 6-Methylthio-2-oxohexanoate (MTOH) to dihomomethionine

This dual substrate specificity enables BCAT3 to function at the intersection of primary amino acid metabolism and secondary glucosinolate biosynthesis (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2).

Subcellular Localization

BCAT3 is targeted to and functions within plastids (chloroplasts) (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 5-8, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2, diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 1-2). This localization was established through multiple experimental approaches:

  1. GFP fusion studies: Transient expression of BCAT3-GFP fusion proteins in tobacco protoplasts demonstrated colocalization with chloroplast autofluorescence (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 5-8)

  2. Targeting sequence prediction: The N-terminal sequence contains a plastid transit peptide consistent with chloroplast import (schuster2006branchedchainaminotransferase4is pages 1-2, diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 3-5)

  3. Functional colocalization: BCAT3 colocalizes with other plastid enzymes including RidA (reactive intermediate deaminase) and threonine dehydratase, supporting its role in plastid-localized BCAA biosynthesis (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 5-6, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 1-2, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 11-12)

The plastid localization is functionally significant because this is the cellular compartment where both BCAA biosynthesis and the methionine chain elongation pathway predominantly occur (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10).

Biochemical Pathways and Metabolic Roles

Dual Function in Primary and Secondary Metabolism

BCAT3 has the unique characteristic of participating in both primary amino acid metabolism and secondary metabolite biosynthesis, demonstrating the interconnection between these traditionally separate metabolic categories (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10).

Role 1: Branched-Chain Amino Acid Biosynthesis

In primary metabolism, BCAT3 catalyzes the terminal transamination reactions producing leucine, isoleucine, and valine (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2). Evidence from bcat3-1 knockout mutants revealed:

  • Significantly reduced free valine levels in rosette leaves (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8)
  • Reduced serine and threonine levels (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3)
  • Normal seed amino acid levels, suggesting tissue-specific or developmental regulation (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3)

The enzyme's lower affinity for the valine precursor 3MOB (Km = 1.38 mM) compared to the leucine biosynthesis enzyme isopropylmalate synthase (Km ≈ 0.3 mM) may represent substrate competition that helps preserve 3MOB for leucine biosynthesis (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8).

Role 2: Methionine-Derived Aliphatic Glucosinolate Biosynthesis

BCAT3 plays a critical role in determining glucosinolate chain length by catalyzing terminal transamination steps in the methionine chain elongation cycle (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8). The enzyme preferentially transaminates shorter-chain keto acids, particularly MTOP and MTOH, converting them to homomethionine and dihomomethionine, respectively (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10).

Metabolite profiling of bcat3-1 mutants demonstrated:

  • Approximately 30% increase in total aliphatic glucosinolates in rosette leaves (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8)
  • Shift toward longer-chain glucosinolates (C5 and greater) (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10)
  • Reduced shorter-chain glucosinolate species (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8)
  • Specific increases in 5-methylthiopentyl (5MTP) and 5-methylsulfinylpentyl (5MSOP) glucosinolates (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10)

These changes indicate that in wild-type plants, BCAT3 preferentially terminates the chain elongation cycle at shorter lengths by transaminating the corresponding keto acids. In the absence of BCAT3, keto acids persist and undergo additional elongation cycles, producing longer-chain glucosinolates (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8).

Functional Relationship with BCAT4

BCAT3 demonstrates partial functional overlap with BCAT4, the cytosolic enzyme that catalyzes the initial transamination of methionine to MTOB (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2). Analysis of bcat3-1/bcat4-2 double mutants revealed:

  • More severe reduction in methionine-derived glucosinolates than either single mutant (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10)
  • Residual glucosinolate production indicating additional aminotransferases contribute to the pathway (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10)
  • Evidence that BCAT3 can partially compensate for BCAT4 loss by initiating methionine chain elongation in plastids (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10)

BCAT3 works in concert with methylthioalkylmalate synthases (MAM1 and MAM3), which catalyze the condensation reactions that extend the methionine carbon chain before BCAT3 performs the terminal transamination (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8).

Gene Family Context and Evolutionary Relationships

The Arabidopsis BCAT Family

Arabidopsis contains six to seven BCAT genes (BCAT1-BCAT7), which share 47.5-84.1% amino acid identity with one another (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 3-5, diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 2-3, diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 1-2). BCAT3 is particularly similar to BCAT4 (80.9% identity), suggesting recent gene duplication and subsequent functional specialization (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 3-5).

The family has diversified through subcellular compartmentation rather than substrate specialization alone (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 5-8, schuster2006branchedchainaminotransferase4is pages 1-2):

  • BCAT1: Mitochondrial, primarily involved in BCAA catabolism
  • BCAT2, BCAT3, BCAT5: Plastidial, primarily involved in BCAA biosynthesis
  • BCAT4, BCAT6: Cytosolic, involved in methionine metabolism and glucosinolate biosynthesis
  • BCAT7: Uncertain transcription and function

Evolutionary Origin

BCATs belong to aminotransferase class III and PLP fold type IV, evolutionarily distinct from most other aminotransferases (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 7-8, koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 8-11, koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 6-7). The structural architecture includes a smaller N-terminal domain with a six-stranded antiparallel β-sheet and a larger C-terminal domain containing a pseudo-β-barrel and the active-site lysine for PLP binding (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 7-8, koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 8-11). BCATs typically function as homodimers and can further oligomerize into hexamers (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 7-8).

The evolutionary connection between leucine biosynthesis and methionine chain elongation is reflected in the recruitment of related enzyme families (BCATs and MAMs) for both pathways, suggesting a common evolutionary origin (schuster2006branchedchainaminotransferase4is pages 1-2, schuster2006branchedchainaminotransferase4is pages 2-3).

Protein Structure and Catalytic Mechanism

Cofactor Requirement and Mechanism

BCAT3 is absolutely dependent on pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP) as a cofactor (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2, binder2010branchedchainaminoacid pages 7-8). The enzyme belongs to PLP fold type IV, where PLP binds in the opposite orientation compared to type I aminotransferases, resulting in distinctive substrate stereospecificity (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 7-8). The active-site lysine forms a Schiff-base linkage (internal aldimine) with PLP, and a conserved glutamic acid interacts with the PLP ring nitrogen (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 7-8).

The catalytic mechanism proceeds through:
1. Formation of external aldimine with the amino acid substrate
2. Direct conversion to ketimine through 1,3-prototropic shift (characteristic of class III enzymes)
3. Hydrolysis to release the keto acid product and generate PMP
4. Reverse reaction with a keto acid substrate to regenerate PLP and the amino acid product (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 7-8, koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 8-11)

Protection from Reactive Intermediates

Interaction with RidA

BCAT3 is vulnerable to inactivation by reactive enamine/imine intermediates generated when threonine dehydratase acts on serine, producing 2-iminopropanoate (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 5-6, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 3-5, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 1-2). This reactive intermediate can attack the PLP cofactor of BCAT3, forming an adduct that inactivates the enzyme (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 2-3).

The plastid-localized RidA (Reactive Intermediate Deaminase A) protein protects BCAT3 by hydrolyzing these reactive enamine/imine intermediates before they can damage the aminotransferase (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 5-6, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 3-5, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 6-8). In vitro experiments demonstrated:

  • Preincubation of BCAT3 with threonine dehydratase and serine reduced BCAT3 activity by 64% (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 3-5)
  • Addition of RidA proteins (from Arabidopsis or maize) protected BCAT3 from inactivation (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 5-6, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 3-5)
  • The serine-derived imine was much more damaging than the threonine-derived imine (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 5-6, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 6-8)

RidA knockout plants show reduced root growth, accumulation of 2-oxobutanoate, and isoleucine deficiency, consistent with impaired BCAT3 function in BCAA biosynthesis (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 6-8, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 8-9). The strong correlation between RidA and BCAT3 gene expression supports their functional coupling in plastids (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 2-3, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 11-12).

Expression Patterns and Regulation

Limited information is available about BCAT3 expression patterns, but key observations include:

  • Expression in vascular bundles and phloem (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2)
  • Diurnal expression pattern coordinated with BCAT4 and MAM1 (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3)
  • Wound induction (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3)

These expression patterns are consistent with roles in both vascular transport of amino acids and glucosinolate defense metabolism.

Experimental Evidence Summary

The functional characterization of BCAT3 rests on multiple complementary experimental approaches:

Genetic Evidence

  • T-DNA knockout mutants (bcat3-1): Confirmed gene function through loss-of-function phenotypes (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3)
  • Double mutants (bcat3-1/bcat4-2): Revealed functional overlap and pathway interdependence (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10)
  • Yeast complementation: Demonstrated BCAA aminotransferase activity by rescuing BCAT-deficient yeast (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 2-3)

Biochemical Evidence

  • Recombinant enzyme assays: Established kinetic parameters (Km, Vmax) for multiple substrates (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3, binder2010branchedchainaminoacid pages 7-8)
  • In vitro activity measurements: Quantified relative activities toward BCAA and methionine chain elongation substrates (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 3-4, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 1-2)
  • Protection assays: Demonstrated vulnerability to reactive intermediates and RidA-mediated protection (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 5-6, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 3-5)

Metabolomic Evidence

  • Amino acid profiling: Revealed reduced valine, serine, and threonine in mutants (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8)
  • Glucosinolate profiling: Demonstrated altered glucosinolate chain length distribution in mutants (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 2-3, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10)
  • Quantitative metabolite changes: Documented specific increases in C5 glucosinolates and shifts in pathway intermediates (knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 7-8, knill2008arabidopsisbranchedchainaminotransferase pages 8-10)

Structural/Bioinformatic Evidence

  • Sequence analysis: Identified conserved catalytic residues and PLP-binding motifs (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 3-5, diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 2-3)
  • Targeting prediction and GFP localization: Confirmed plastid localization (diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 5-8, diebold2002thebranchedchainamino pages 1-2)
  • Phylogenetic relationships: Placed BCAT3 within the class III/fold type IV aminotransferase family (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 7-8, koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 8-11, koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 6-7)

Recent Developments and Current Understanding

While the foundational work on BCAT3 was conducted primarily between 2002-2008, recent reviews have provided important evolutionary and mechanistic context:

2022 Evolutionary Perspective

Koper et al. (2022) placed plant BCATs within the broader evolutionary framework of aminotransferases, emphasizing their broad substrate specificity and the retention of catalytic promiscuity that enables enzymes like BCAT3 to function across multiple metabolic pathways (koper2022evolutionaryoriginand pages 1-2). This review highlighted that some isoforms of plant BCATs, including BCAT3 and BCAT6, have substantial activity toward non-canonical substrates, supporting the concept of metabolic pathway interconnection.

Protective Mechanisms (2014)

Niehaus et al. (2014) established the importance of RidA-mediated protection for plastidial BCAA biosynthesis, revealing that BCAT3 requires protective mechanisms to function effectively in the presence of threonine dehydratase (niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 5-6, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 3-5, niehaus2014arabidopsisandmaize pages 1-2). This work demonstrated that metabolic enzyme function depends not only on intrinsic catalytic properties but also on cellular systems that prevent collateral damage from reactive metabolic intermediates.

Conclusions and Functional Summary

BCAT3 (At3g49680) in Arabidopsis thaliana is a plastid-localized, PLP-dependent branched-chain aminotransferase with the following key functional characteristics:

Primary Function: BCAT3 catalyzes reversible transamination reactions converting branched-chain 2-oxo acids to their corresponding amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, valine) and methionine chain elongation intermediates to chain-extended methionine derivatives (homomethionine, dihomomethionine).

Substrate Specificity: The enzyme shows highest affinity for 4-methyl-2-oxopentanoate (leucine precursor; Km = 0.14 mM) and 3-methyl-2-oxopentanoate (isoleucine precursor; Km = 0.14 mM), with lower but significant activity toward valine precursor (Km = 1.38 mM) and methionine chain elongation intermediates.

Subcellular Localization: BCAT3 functions exclusively in plastids (chloroplasts), where it has access to both BCAA biosynthetic substrates and methionine chain elongation intermediates.

Biochemical Pathways: The enzyme operates at the intersection of primary and secondary metabolism, participating in both BCAA biosynthesis (terminal transamination step) and methionine-derived aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis (terminal transamination in the chain elongation cycle). BCAT3 preferentially terminates shorter chain elongation cycles, thereby influencing glucosinolate chain length distribution.

Evolutionary Context: BCAT3 is part of a six-member gene family (BCAT1-6) in Arabidopsis that has diversified primarily through subcellular compartmentation. It is most closely related to BCAT4 (80.9% identity) but differs in localization (plastid vs. cytosol) and preferred pathway role.

Regulatory Protection: BCAT3 requires protection from reactive enamine/imine intermediates by the RidA enzyme to maintain full catalytic activity in plastids.

This dual functionality demonstrates that single enzymes can serve as critical links between primary amino acid metabolism and specialized secondary metabolism, challenging traditional boundaries between these metabolic categories. The preferential substrate specificity of BCAT3 for shorter-chain intermediates provides a mechanism for regulating glucosinolate structural diversity, with important implications for plant defense chemistry.

BCAT3 (At3g49680, Q9M401) review notes

Identity and localization

  • Plastid-targeted BCAT: PMID:12068099; many HDA/IEA/ISM chloroplast rows all ACCEPT; the NOT-cytosol RCA row (PMID:21166475) is consistent and accepted.
  • Complements yeast BCAA auxotrophy: PMID:12068099.

Dual function (Knill et al. 2008)

  • BCAA: PMID:18162591; bcat3-1 has reduced Val PMID:18162591
  • Glucosinolates: PMID:18162591; double mutant with bcat4 is additive PMID:18162591.

Curation decisions

  • All 22 GOA rows ACCEPT (activities are experimentally supported; leucine/valine biosynthesis IEA sound).
  • Two core functions: (1) GO:0004084 in Leu/Val biosynthesis in chloroplast; (2) GO:0010326 in glucosinolate and homomethionine biosynthesis.
  • NEW GO:0019761 glucosinolate biosynthetic process (IMP, PMID:18162591): BCAT3 lacks it in GOA although it catalyzes elongation-cycle transaminations; comparator BCAT4 and MAM1/MAM3 carry it.
  • NEW GO:0033322 L-homomethionine biosynthetic process: term currently unannotated anywhere (QuickGO), so not a deliberate convention.
  • No deep-research file was available at the time of review.

2026-10-01: GO:0033322 withdrawn

The NEW annotation to GO:0033322 (L-homomethionine biosynthetic process) has been removed, and the
term dropped from core_functions. GO obsoleted the sibling terms GO:0033506 ("glucosinolate
biosynthetic process from homomethionine": "a specific pathway variant, which is out of scope for
GO", replaced by GO:0019761) and GO:0033321 ("homomethionine metabolic process": "an unnecessary
grouping term"). No gene product in any species carries GO:0033322. Read together, this is a GO
convention to annotate the chain-elongation enzymes to glucosinolate biosynthetic process
(GO:0019761), which this gene already carries, not a gap to fill. The question is kept as a
suggested_question for GO.

  BCAT3 (BRANCHED-CHAIN AMINOTRANSFERASE 3, At3g49680) is a plastid-targeted, pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent
  class-IV aminotransferase with a dual role in primary and specialized metabolism. As a canonical
  branched-chain aminotransferase it catalyzes the final, reversible transamination steps of
  branched-chain amino acid biosynthesis in the chloroplast, converting 4-methyl-2-oxopentanoate,
  (S)-3-methyl-2-oxopentanoate and 3-methyl-2-oxobutanoate to leucine, isoleucine and valine
  using glutamate/2-oxoglutarate; it complements the BCAA auxotrophy of a yeast bat1/bat2 mutant,
  and bcat3 knockouts have reduced free valine. BCAT3 also transaminates the omega-methylthio-2-oxo
  acids of the methionine chain-elongation cycle and is thought to catalyze the terminal transamination
  that converts chain-elongated 2-oxo acids to homomethionine and dihomomethionine for aliphatic
  glucosinolate biosynthesis; it can partly substitute for the cytosolic BCAT4 in the initial
  deamination of methionine, as shown by the strong additive loss of methionine-derived glucosinolates
  in bcat3 bcat4 double mutants. BCAT3 is expressed in the phloem, is wound-inducible and is
  co-expressed with BCAT4 and MAM1.
