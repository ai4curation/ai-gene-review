MPK18 catalytic competence and substrate class: a hypothesis-level evaluation

Target: Arabidopsis thaliana MPK18, UniProtKB Q9C5C0, NCBITaxon:3702, organism code ARATH Hypothesis slug: kgap-mpk18-catalytic-competence Focus type: free_text Source file: genes/ARATH/MPK18/MPK18-ai-review.yaml Verdict: Partially supported

Summary

MPK18 is predicted to be a catalytically competent protein kinase, and the prediction rests on two independent computational lines that agree. A regex motif scan of the 615-residue Q9C5C0 sequence, benchmarked against all 20 Arabidopsis MPKs plus human ERK2, p38α and JNK1, finds every one of the six catalytic elements the seed hypothesis asked about present and canonical: the glycine-rich loop GKGSYG at 32-37, the β3 VAIK lysine K54 at 51-54, the catalytic loop HRDLKPKN at 149-156 with catalytic aspartate D151, the magnesium-binding DFG at 169-171 with D169, the activation-loop motif at 187-189, and APE at 197-199. Zero substitutions, zero deletions. The AlphaFold model AF-Q9C5C0-F1 agrees: the kinase domain 25-310 is modelled at mean pLDDT 89.5, and the active site is assembled, with a 3.52 Å K54 NZ to E72 CD salt bridge marking the αC-in active conformation, D151 to K153 at 3.44 Å, D151 to N156 at 3.30 Å, and a 12.89 Å P-loop to DFG span matching an open ATP cleft. MPK18 is a kinase with an intact catalytic machine, not a pseudokinase.

The activation-loop motif is TDY (T187, D188, Y189), placing MPK18 in plant MAPK group D. The TDY/TEY split separates the Arabidopsis MPK family cleanly: TDY appears in exactly the eight group D members (MPK8, 9, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20) and TEY in all twelve group A-C members. One trap for automated curation is worth flagging: MPK18 carries a TEY at residues 17-19 (FFTEYGD) that lies N-terminal to the kinase domain and is not an activation-loop motif. A naive full-sequence TEY regex calls MPK18 a TEY MAPK incorrectly.

The substrate half of the hypothesis resolves to the seed's own stated expectation, with a sharper reason than the seed anticipated. MPK18's common docking site is degenerate: the 9-residue αL16 window is LSKIEREPS, net charge 0, with a lysine at the column where ERK2 has the first of its two CD aspartates, a charge reversal at the determining position. TEY-clade MAPKs average +2.38 net acidity at this window against +0.38 for TDY (Mann-Whitney U=58.0, one-sided p=0.0029, rank-biserial r=+0.81). A cross-species scan replicates the same split in rice (TEY n=7 mean +2.43 vs TDY n=11 mean +0.36, U=72.0, p=0.00082, r=+0.87), so the degenerate CD site is a conserved clade architecture, not a lesion in Arabidopsis MPK18. The alternative docking feature, the DEF/FXFP pocket, cannot rescue a substrate prediction either, because MPK3 and MPK6, whose substrates are experimentally established, are as diverged from ERK2 at those four columns as MPK18 is. Sequence cannot nominate substrates for MPK18. Docking is the determinant for a MAPK, and MPK18 has no usable docking determinant to read.

Two elements of the seed's framing are corrected by primary literature. No MAP2K is a credible activator of MPK18: PMID: 28848569 reports that MKK3 does not interact with MPK18 and that the MAP3K MKKK20 phosphorylates MPK18 directly in vitro, bypassing the MAP2K tier. The clade prior from PMID: 21419340, where MKK3 activates the TDY group D member MPK8, exists but does not transfer. The cortical microtubule phenotype has never been attributed to MPK18 kinase activity: PMID: 19392697 offers the PHS1-MPK18 switch explicitly as a proposed model, assays MPK18 only as a phosphoacceptor, and reports no kinase-dead rescue, no MPK18 kinase assay and no substrate. The GO record carries the same gap: of 26 QuickGO annotation rows for Q9C5C0, zero are experimental evidence for any catalytic activity, and the GO:0004707 ISS traces to PMID: 11544109, a 2001 Trends in Plant Science review.

Key findings

F1. All six catalytic elements are intact and canonical

The motif scan was run on the UniProt Q9C5C0 sequence (615 aa) and benchmarked against 19 other MAPKs so that the method had both a reference standard and a chance to fail. Each element was located by a consensus regex anchored to the preceding element, which prevents spurious hits in the long C-terminal tail.

This supports retaining MF terms for protein kinase activity on sequence grounds, as a prediction.

F2. MPK18 is a TDY group D MAPK, and its CD docking site is degenerate

The activation-loop motif partitions the Arabidopsis MPK family exactly along the published clade boundary. TDY appears in the eight group D members (MPK8, 9, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20); TEY appears in all twelve group A-C members. MPK18 is TDY at 187-189.

The common docking site was scored as net acidic charge (D+E minus K+R) over the 9-residue αL16 window anchored 85 or more residues after the APE motif. This window is the one that carries the ERK2 D318/D321 pair which recruits D-motif/KIM peptides from both substrates and upstream MAP2Ks.

The clade also differs in C-terminal architecture. After the APE motif, Arabidopsis TDY MAPKs carry a mean 353-residue extension against 162 residues for TEY MAPKs, and MPK18 has the longest at 416 residues, which is 28.2 % Ser+Thr+Pro, low complexity, and predicted disordered.

F4. No MAP2K activates MPK18; a MAP3K phosphorylates it directly

The seed asks which MAP2K could activate MPK18 and expects the TDY/TEY split to constrain the answer. The primary literature gives a different architecture.

PMID: 28848569 (Benhamman et al. 2017) reports: "In vitro phosphorylation assays showed that MKKK20 phosphorylates both MKK3 and MPK18." The same paper reports the negative interaction result: "suggesting a direct access to MPK18, consistent with the absence of interaction between MKK3 and MPK18 in protein-protein interaction assays". Combining all three kinases produced no synergy, and the genetic analysis placed mkkk20 and mkk3 in two non-complementary pathways affecting root microtubule function.

The clade prior does exist. PMID: 21419340 (Takahashi et al. 2011) states that "MPK8 is also phosphorylated and activated by a MAPKK MKK3 in the prototypic kinase cascade", establishing MKK3 as the MAP2K for a different TDY group D member, with calmodulin required for full activation. It does not transfer to MPK18, where the direct interaction test is negative.

PMID: 30081740 (Bai and Matton 2018) locates the docking determinant on the upstream side: "showed that the MKKK20 C-terminal region interacted with MKK3 that comprised a typical DEF domain akin to MAPKs docking domains". A clade whose members lack an acidic CD groove engaging partners through a DEF-type site on the upstream kinase is internally consistent with the sequence result in F2.

F5. The microtubule phenotype is never attributed to MPK18 kinase activity

PMID: 19392697 (Walia et al. 2009) is the sole primary source for the MPK18 microtubule phenotype. Its evidence consists of a yeast two-hybrid interaction with PHS1, BiFC colocalisation in the cytoplasm, in vitro dephosphorylation of phospho-MPK18 by recombinant PHS1, a T-DNA loss-of-function phenotype, and partial suppression of phs1-1 root phenotypes by mpk18. The phenotype statement is: "Mutant mpk18 seedlings show defects in microtubule-related functions, and have moderately stabilized microtubules." The mechanistic claim is framed explicitly as a proposal: "We propose a model whereby the PHS1-MPK18 signalling module is involved in a phosphorylation/dephosphorylation switch that regulates cortical microtubule functions." The only biochemistry on MPK18 in the paper treats it as a substrate: "in vitro dephosphorylation assays indicated that phospho-MPK18 can be dephosphorylated by recombinant PHS1".

There is no kinase-dead MPK18 transgene rescue (no K54R, no TDY-to-ADF allele), no in vitro MPK18 kinase assay against any substrate, and no MPK18 substrate reported. PMID: 28848569 likewise assays MPK18 only as a phosphoacceptor for MKKK20.

A competing mechanism is biochemically complete and does not need MPK18. PMID: 24120637 (Fujita et al. 2013) shows that PHS1 carries its own atypical kinase domain, and that "This atypical kinase domain phosphorylates Thr349 of" α-tubulin, with the genetic consequence that "In the phs1 null mutant, stress-induced microtubule depolymerization does not occur." The causal microtubule activity sits in PHS1. The mpk18 phenotype remains real, and it is equally consistent with MPK18 acting as a PHS1 substrate or regulatory sink rather than as the effector kinase on a cytoskeletal target.

The eight IPI partners are PHS1 (Q75QN6), MAPKKK20 (Q9SND6), the DELLA proteins GAI (Q9LQT8), RGA (Q9SLH3) and RGL2 (Q8GXW1), and BREVIS RADIX (Q17TI5). The latter four come from large-scale binary yeast two-hybrid maps: PMID: 21798944 and PMID: 32612234, the latter stating "Here, we experimentally generated a systems-level map of the Arabidopsis phytohormone signalling network, consisting of more than 2,000 binary protein-protein interactions." They are untargeted screens, so they nominate a substrate class (transcriptional regulators and signalling scaffolds) only weakly.

The absence of a substrate set has a specific explanation. PMID: 19095804 (Popescu et al. 2009), the main plant MAPK substrate screen, states: "We determined which Arabidopsis thaliana MKKs preferentially activate 10 different MPKs in vivo and used the activated MPKs to probe high-density protein microarrays to determine their phosphorylation targets." MPK18 was not among the 10 profiled kinases.

F7. DEF-pocket divergence is a failed control and must not be used as an argument

Before concluding that MPK18 cannot engage substrates, the second MAPK docking feature was checked as an independent test. The four ERK2 DEF/FXFP-pocket columns (human Y233, L234, L237, Y263; equal to rat Y231/L232/L235/Y261) were mapped onto plant MAPKs by the invariant FPG[KR] anchor of the αF-αG loop.

MPK6, whose substrates are experimentally established, is the most diverged of every protein tested at these columns, with zero aromatic residues. MPK3 is comparably diverged. DEF-pocket divergence therefore has no discriminating power for MPK18 and must not be offered as evidence that MPK18 lacks substrates. The MPK18 C-terminal extension contains a single FEF at position 336 and no FxFP motif, which is consistent but equally uninformative.

The conclusion about substrates stands on the narrower ground that survives this control: no docking determinant in MPK18 is informative enough to nominate substrates.

F8. The degenerate CD site is a conserved clade architecture, replicated in rice

The final test asks whether the CD degeneracy says something about MPK18 specifically or about its whole clade, because the two readings imply different curation actions.

A UniProt query for reviewed MAPKs across 8 plant taxon IDs returned 26 entries, of which 18 scored successfully: 11 TDY and 7 TEY.

Mann-Whitney U=72.0, one-sided p=0.00082, r=+0.87 U=77.0, one-sided p=0.00028

Every rice TDY CD window is neutral: OsMPK9 LSKIDREPS (0), OsMPK7/10/11/8 LAKVEREPS and LAKAEREPS (0), OsMPK12 LANVEREPS (+1), OsMPK13 ISKLSREPS (-1), OsMPK15/14 IANVDREPS (+1), OsMPK17/16 LANSEREPI (+1). AtMPK18 is LSKIEREPS, differing from rice OsMPK9 at one position. The Arabidopsis-internal comparison gave the same result (TEY +2.38 vs TDY +0.38, U=58.0, p=0.0029, r=+0.81).

Stated plainly: the UniProt query recovered reviewed MAPKs from only 2 species (Oryza sativa subsp. japonica and Physcomitrium patens), not the 8 taxa requested, and all 11 TDY hits are rice. The cross-species replication rests on a single monocot genome.

Monocots and dicots separated roughly 150 million years ago, so even a single-monocot replication shows the architecture is maintained rather than drifting. Two consequences follow for curation. An argument of the form "MPK18 lacks the CD site, therefore it is not a genuine MAPK" is not available, because the statement applies to the whole conserved clade. And the clade has no canonical CD site through which docking-based substrate predictions could be transferred, so substrate assignment from sequence is blocked for MPK18 specifically and for group D generally.

Mechanistic model

The findings assemble into a single coherent reading. MPK18 has a complete and confidently modelled catalytic machine, so the question of catalytic competence is answered affirmatively as a prediction. The question of what it acts on is blocked at a different level: the docking determinant that would carry a substrate prediction is absent across the entire TDY group D clade, and the alternative determinant (DEF) is uninformative in all plant MAPKs tested. Above MPK18, the MAP2K tier is skipped, which is consistent with a kinase that has no CD groove for MAP2K D-motif recruitment. Below MPK18, nothing is known, because the one screen that would have found substrates did not include it.

The immediate molecular function being tested is phosphotransfer by MPK18 onto a protein substrate. That activity has never been measured. What has been measured is MPK18 as a phosphoacceptor (by MKKK20, PMID: 28848569) and as a dephosphorylation target (by PHS1, PMID: 19392697). Everything downstream of MPK18 in the cortical microtubule story is a loss-of-function inference. The competing explanation from PMID: 24120637, where PHS1's own kinase domain phosphorylates α-tubulin Thr349 and the phs1 null loses stress-induced depolymerisation entirely, supplies a complete mechanism in which MPK18 catalysis is not required.

  • Any term attributing the cortical microtubule phenotype to MPK18 catalysis.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Method limitations of this analysis. Catalytic competence here is a prediction from sequence motifs and from a single predicted structure. AlphaFold models of protein kinases are biased toward active-like conformations, so the αC-in geometry is weaker evidence than a crystal structure would be. The CD-site score is a net-charge proxy over a 9-residue window, not a measured docking affinity; a non-canonical docking mode operating through different chemistry would not be detected by it. The cross-species replication (F8) draws on one monocot genome, because the UniProt query for reviewed MAPKs returned only 2 species rather than the 8 taxa requested. Two NCBI eutils esummary calls failed with a KeyError on PMIDs 32612234 and 19095804, and those records were retrieved through PubMed search instead; this is stated rather than worked around silently. Local *-bioinformatics analyses in the repository were withheld by design and were not consulted.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Ordered by how efficiently each distinguishes the hypothesis from its alternatives.

  1. In vitro kinase assay with a generic substrate. Recombinant MPK18, with and without MKKK20 pre-activation, on myelin basic protein or a MAPK consensus peptide. This is the single missing experiment behind the entire catalytic-competence question. A positive result converts the prediction into IDA evidence for GO:0004707; a negative result after MKKK20 activation would be the strongest argument yet that the intact motifs are not used.

  2. K54R / D151N rescue of mpk18. Complement the mpk18 T-DNA line with wild-type and kinase-dead MPK18 and score cortical microtubule stability and drug sensitivity. If wild-type rescues and kinase-dead does not, catalysis is required and the GO:0043622 IMP gains mechanistic grounding. If both rescue, the function is kinase-independent and the microtubule phenotype must never be used to support a catalytic term.

  3. Phosphosite mapping of T187 and Y189. Phospho-specific detection or targeted MS from plant tissue, in wild type and mkkk20. Determines whether the TDY motif is phosphorylated in planta and whether MKKK20 is the physiological activating kinase, which would settle the unusual MAP3K-to-MAPK architecture reported in PMID: 28848569.

  4. Protein-microarray or proximity-labelling substrate screen with activated MPK18. The direct fix for the substrate gap left by PMID: 19095804. Run MPK3 or MPK6 alongside as a positive control so that a null result for MPK18 is interpretable rather than ambiguous.

  5. Direct DELLA phosphorylation test. Take GAI (Q9LQT8), RGA (Q9SLH3) and RGL2 (Q8GXW1) from the Y2H leads into an in vitro assay with activated MPK18. This is the cheapest way to promote or kill the only substrate-class lead on the record, and either outcome is informative.

  6. CD-site swap. Replace the MPK18 window LSKIEREPS with the MPK6 window LHDISDEPE and test D-motif peptide binding. Distinguishes a genuine loss of docking from a different docking mode that the charge proxy cannot see.

  7. Tail deletion. Compare full-length and APE-truncated MPK18 for MKKK20 binding and for mpk18 rescue. Asks directly whether the 416-residue group D tail is the clade's substitute targeting module.

  8. Apply the same checks to the other seven Arabidopsis group D members (MPK8, 9, 15, 16, 17, 19, 20). The CD degeneracy, the long tail and the shortage of experimental catalytic evidence are clade-wide, so the curation question is clade-wide. MPK17 needs separate attention: it carries DLG at 160-162 in place of DFG, a genuine substitution at the magnesium-binding position that should be checked against whatever catalytic annotations it holds.
