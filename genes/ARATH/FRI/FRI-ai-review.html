Gene Description

FRI (FRIGIDA) is a plant-specific nuclear coiled-coil protein of Arabidopsis, the founding member of the Frigida family, and the major determinant of natural variation between the winter-annual and rapid-cycling habits. Functional FRI alleles, as in winter-annual accessions such as H51 or Sf-2, up-regulate the floral repressor FLC to levels that block flowering before winter, creating a vernalization requirement. Most rapid-cycling accessions, including Col-0 and Ler, carry independent loss-of-function deletions. FRI has no DNA-binding domain. It acts as a scaffold of the FRI-C transcription activator complex with FRL1, FES1, FLX and the FLC-promoter-binding zinc-finger protein SUF4. It recruits chromatin modifiers (SWR1/H2A.Z exchange, SDG8 H3K36 methylation and a WDR5a-containing COMPASS-like H3K4 methyltransferase) and interacts with the nuclear cap-binding complex (CBP20) to promote co-transcriptional FLC expression. In the cold, FRI quickly partitions into nuclear condensates away from the active FLC locus, lowering FRI occupancy at FLC as a first step in its shutdown.

Scaffold/coactivator of the FRI-C transcription activator complex (with SUF4, FLX, FES1, FRL1). It recruits chromatin modifiers and the cap-binding complex to up-regulate FLC, establishing the winter-annual vernalization requirement.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: What is the structural basis of FRI scaffolding (coiled-coil versus disordered C-terminus), and is FRI-C a stable stoichiometric complex or a dynamic condensate?

Q: How does cold-induced partitioning of FRI into condensates relate mechanistically to COOLAIR induction and to the later VIN3-dependent Polycomb nucleation?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Tether FRI (or FRI deletion variants) to the FLC promoter in fri suf4 backgrounds using a dCas9 fusion. Measure FLC transcription, H3K4me3/H3K36me3 and H2A.Z, and test which FRI-C partners are required.

Hypothesis: FRI scaffolding of SUF4 and FLX is necessary and sufficient to activate FLC.
