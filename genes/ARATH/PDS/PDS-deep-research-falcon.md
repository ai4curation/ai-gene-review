# Functional annotation report: Arabidopsis **PDS/PDS3** (At4g14210; UniProt Q07356)

The requested identity is verified. **PDS**, also called **PDS3**, is the single-copy *Arabidopsis thaliana* locus **At4g14210**, encoding the plastid-targeted **15-cis-phytoene desaturase** (EC 1.3.5.5). The literature reports a 566-amino-acid precursor, a 75-residue plastid-targeting peptide, and a 491-residue mature protein. **15-cis-phytoene → 9,15-di-cis-phytofluene → 9,15,9′-tri-cis-ζ-carotene.**

## 1. Identity verification and nomenclature

The authoritative Arabidopsis pathway synthesis identifies **PDS3/At4g14210** as the gene encoding phytoene desaturase. It is reported as a single-copy Arabidopsis gene, distinct from **ZDS/At3g04870**, the downstream ζ-carotene desaturase. Thus, literature using PDS for human pyruvate dehydrogenase phosphatases, microbial enzymes, or unrelated same-symbol genes is not relevant to Q07356 and was excluded. (ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 7-8, ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 3-4)

The domain assignments are coherent with the biochemistry. PDS is a flavoprotein oxidoreductase; sequence analyses describe plant PDS/ZDS proteins as containing NAD-binding and amine-oxidase-like regions, while plant-PDS structures reveal an FAD-containing oxidoreductase fold. These observations support, rather than conflict with, the supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations. The “NAD-binding” fold designation should not be mistaken for evidence that NAD(P) is the physiological electron acceptor: biochemical evidence instead supports enzyme-bound FAD and diffusible plastoquinone. (brausemann2017structureofphytoene pages 1-3, mcquinn2018atpdsoverexpressionin pages 4-7, gemmecker2015phytoenedesaturasefrom pages 1-2)

## 2. Primary biochemical function

### Reaction and substrate specificity

PDS acts on the highly hydrophobic C40 carotene **15-cis-phytoene**. Two sequential, symmetric dehydrogenations extend the phytoene chromophore:

1. 15-cis-phytoene is desaturated at one C11–C12 position to form **9,15-di-cis-phytofluene**.
2. The opposite C11′–C12′ bond is desaturated to produce **9,15,9′-tri-cis-ζ-carotene**.

The chemistry both removes hydrogen and establishes the characteristic poly-cis product geometry. Direct plant-PDS assays identified phytofluene as the intermediate and the tri-cis ζ-carotene as the final PDS product. Experiments with shortened phytoene analogues indicate that the central 15-cis configuration is an important positional determinant of regioselectivity. (koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 1-3, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 7-8, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 20-22, chen2010isolationandcharacterization pages 1-2)

The best current kinetic model, developed with purified rice PDS, is an **ordered ping-pong bi-bi mechanism**: carotene reduction of enzyme-bound FAD and subsequent FAD reoxidation by quinone occur sequentially, apparently using overlapping regions of the catalytic cavity. Intermediate channeling between subunits may increase the efficiency of the two-step conversion. Because these detailed assays used rice PDS, the catalytic chemistry is strong plant-homolog evidence for AtPDS rather than a direct kinetic determination of recombinant Q07356. (brausemann2017structureofphytoene pages 1-3, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 1-3, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 7-8)

### Cofactor and electron acceptor

**FAD is the protein-bound redox cofactor.** During carotene dehydrogenation it is reduced to FADH₂/FADred; oxidized plastoquinone then accepts the reducing equivalents and becomes plastoquinol. Purified plant PDS did not use molecular oxygen as an effective substitute for quinone, supporting classification as a **15-cis-phytoene:plastoquinone oxidoreductase**, rather than a conventional oxygen-dependent oxidase. Arabidopsis mutants deficient in plastoquinone synthesis fail in phytoene desaturation, providing genetic evidence that this electron carrier is essential in vivo. (gemmecker2015phytoenedesaturasefrom pages 17-18, gemmecker2015phytoenedesaturasefrom pages 1-2, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 24-25)

Plastoquinol is reoxidized by photosynthetic electron transport in green tissue and by the plastid terminal oxidase **PTOX** or related routes, particularly in nonphotosynthetic plastids. PTOX is therefore functionally coupled to PDS through the shared quinone pool, although it is not a stable PDS subunit and is not absolutely required for all desaturation in illuminated green tissue. (koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 1-3, ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 7-8, chen2010isolationandcharacterization pages 1-2, ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 5-7)

## 3. Position in carotenoid metabolism

PDS acts immediately after phytoene synthase, which condenses two geranylgeranyl diphosphate molecules to produce 15-cis-phytoene. The subsequent plant poly-cis sequence is:

**PSY → PDS → Z-ISO → ZDS → CRTISO → all-trans-lycopene.**

PDS produces 9,15,9′-tri-cis-ζ-carotene; **Z-ISO** isomerizes its central 15-cis bond to create the substrate accepted by **ZDS**. ZDS introduces two additional double bonds, and **CRTISO**, assisted by photoisomerization in illuminated tissue, completes conversion of poly-cis intermediates toward all-trans-lycopene. Lycopene is then cyclized and modified to form α- and β-carotene-derived xanthophylls. (ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 7-8, chen2010isolationandcharacterization pages 1-2, ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 5-7)

This pathway explains why the primary consequences of PDS deficiency extend beyond pigmentation. Carotenoids stabilize photosynthetic pigment–protein complexes and quench chlorophyll triplets and singlet oxygen; they also supply precursors for abscisic acid, strigolactones, retinal-related compounds, and other apocarotenoid signals. Nevertheless, these downstream effects should be distinguished from PDS’s precise molecular function, which is phytoene desaturation rather than direct hormone synthesis or signaling. (gupta2022thegeneticcomponents pages 4-5, gemmecker2015phytoenedesaturasefrom pages 1-2)

## 4. Cellular and intraplastid localization

AtPDS is nucleus encoded and synthesized as a precursor carrying an N-terminal plastid-targeting sequence. Reported Arabidopsis annotation gives a **566-aa precursor**, **75-aa targeting peptide**, and **491-aa mature protein**. Chloroplast proteomic assignments place approximately **84% at envelope membranes and 16% in thylakoid membranes**. Independent biochemical observations indicate that catalytically active plant PDS is membrane associated, fitting the extreme hydrophobicity of phytoene, ζ-carotene, and plastoquinone. (ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 7-8, ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 3-4)

Plant PDS is best viewed as a **monotopically membrane-associated enzyme**, not a multipass transporter. Its active site faces the lipid–water interface and contains hydrophobic channels that permit carotene and quinone access from the membrane. Rice-PDS chromatography, cross-linking, electron microscopy, kinetics, and crystallography support an active **homotetramer arranged as a dimer of dimers**. A roughly 43-Å cavity accommodates extended phytoene, and the tetrameric arrangement may channel phytofluene between catalytic sites. This quaternary model is persuasive for plant PDS but remains homolog-based inference for AtPDS because an AtPDS structure has not been reported in the retrieved evidence. (brausemann2017structureofphytoene pages 1-3, gemmecker2015phytoenedesaturasefrom pages 17-18, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 20-22)

## 5. Genetic and physiological evidence in Arabidopsis

Arabidopsis **pds3** T-DNA loss-of-function mutants are albino and dwarfed. CRISPR knockout likewise produces an easily recognized albino/null phenotype, and RNAi or virus-induced silencing causes bleached sectors. These findings are consistent with depletion of colored and photoprotective carotenoids, followed by photooxidative damage and impaired chloroplast differentiation. Because the phenotype is visually conspicuous, PDS has become a standard positive control for plant RNA-silencing and genome-editing systems. (gupta2022thegeneticcomponents pages 4-5)

The phenotype is not evidence that PDS directly synthesizes chlorophyll or gibberellin. Rather, blocking carotenoid production destabilizes developing photosynthetic systems and perturbs plastid metabolism and plastid-to-nucleus signaling; downstream hormone changes and dwarfism are consequently pleiotropic manifestations of the primary carotenoid lesion.

## 6. Recent research, 2023–2024

### Carotenoid engineering context

A 2023 review emphasized modern carotenoid-engineering strategies—including CRISPR base editing, targeted transgene insertion, synthetic chloroplast-to-chromoplast conversion, viral expression, and microbial production. Within this framework, PDS remains both a metabolic-engineering target and a sensitive visible reporter, but PSY and carotenoid-storage capacity frequently exert stronger control over total pathway output. Thus, PDS is not universally rate limiting; its control coefficient depends on tissue, developmental state, substrate supply, and sequestration capacity.

### 2024 retinal–auxin findings

Xu and colleagues reported in August 2024 that Arabidopsis **pds3-1** has short primary roots and reduced lateral-root formation. Acute pharmacological inhibition with fluridone suppressed wild-type primary-root growth, increased lateral-root density while redistributing lateral roots between pre-existing and newly grown root regions, increased root IAA after 8 and 24 hours, and transiently activated the DR5:LUC auxin reporter. These responses were absent or attenuated in material carrying a fluridone-insensitive PDS transgene, strengthening target attribution. (xu2024exogenousapplicationof pages 2-4, xu2024exogenousapplicationof pages 1-2, xu2024exogenousapplicationof pages 9-10)

Exogenous retinal at **20 µM** partially rescued pds3-1 short roots and initially rescued fluridone-treated wild-type roots; rescue weakened after more than 60 hours. Retinal did not simply normalize every phenotype—it did not consistently restore lateral-root density or directly impose a reproducible change in free IAA—so the authors’ model that a retinal-derived signal promotes auxin homeostasis is biologically plausible but not yet a complete molecular mechanism. The most secure conclusion is that PDS-dependent carotenoid metabolism influences root auxin dynamics and architecture through one or more downstream apocarotenoids, with retinal able to compensate for part of the defect. [Xu et al., *Plant Physiology* 196:1659–1673, published August 2024; https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiae405]. (xu2024exogenousapplicationof pages 2-4, xu2024exogenousapplicationof pages 8-9, xu2024exogenousapplicationof pages 1-2, xu2024exogenousapplicationof pages 11-12)

## 7. Inhibitors, herbicide mechanism, and resistance

PDS is the molecular target of bleaching herbicides including **norflurazon** and **fluridone**. Inhibition blocks FAD reoxidation by plastoquinone, causing phytoene accumulation and depletion of downstream carotenoids. Without carotenoid photoprotection, chlorophyll and membranes undergo photooxidative injury, producing the characteristic white phenotype. Structural evidence indicates that norflurazon binds in or near the plastoquinone region and stabilizes reduced FAD; excess quinone can relieve inhibition in vitro, consistent with competition. (brausemann2017structureofphytoene pages 1-3, gemmecker2015phytoenedesaturasefrom pages 17-18, gemmecker2015phytoenedesaturasefrom pages 1-2)

Resistance substitutions can reduce herbicide affinity, but there is a substantial catalytic trade-off. In rice-PDS structure–function experiments, several norflurazon-resistant variants retained less than **5%** of wild-type activity; substitutions at the distal end of the carotene cavity similarly left only about 5% activity while preserving product identity. Accordingly, target-site resistance is chemically feasible but may carry a strong fitness or flux penalty. (koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 1-3, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 20-22, gupta2022thegeneticcomponents pages 4-5)

## 8. Applications and real-world implementations

### Functional-genomics reporter

PDS is among the most widely used visible marker genes in plant CRISPR, RNAi, and virus-induced gene-silencing experiments. Successful disruption produces albino or mosaic white tissue without requiring specialized analytical equipment. In Arabidopsis, efficient CRISPR systems have generated visible PDS3-null phenotypes as early as the T1 generation. This use is technically valuable, although the severe phenotype means PDS is generally a method-validation target rather than a desirable agronomic knockout. (gupta2022thegeneticcomponents pages 4-5)

### Herbicide discovery and selectable markers

The phytoene/plastoquinone/FAD cavity is an established site for bleaching-herbicide development. Conversely, engineered inhibitor-resistant PDS variants can serve as selectable markers or herbicide-tolerance traits. Structure-guided design must balance inhibitor resistance against the frequently observed loss of catalytic activity. (brausemann2017structureofphytoene pages 1-3, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 1-3, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 20-22)

### Carotenoid biofortification

Heterologous AtPDS overexpression in four stable tomato lines demonstrated that PDS can be a flux-control point in ripening fruit. Phytoene fell to **28.5–35.3%** and phytofluene to **24.9–30.7%** of control, whereas ζ-carotene rose to **213.2–262.6%**, lycopene to as much as **131.1%**, β-carotene to **142.8%**, and total carotenoids to as much as **128.5%**. Expressed differently, the largest increases were more than **160% for ζ-carotene**, **31.1% for lycopene**, **42.8% for β-carotene**, and **28.5% for total carotenoids**. [McQuinn et al., *Plant Biotechnology Journal* 16:482–494, published 2018; https://doi.org/10.1111/pbi.12789]. (mcquinn2018atpdsoverexpressionin pages 7-11, mcquinn2018atpdsoverexpressionin pages 26-29)

These results demonstrate practical pathway engineering but do not imply that PDS is limiting in every Arabidopsis tissue or crop. Effects were tissue dependent: ripe tomato fruit responded strongly, whereas leaves showed smaller carotenoid changes and broader transcriptional feedback. (mcquinn2018atpdsoverexpressionin pages 1-4, mcquinn2018atpdsoverexpressionin pages 14-17)

## 9. Evidence assessment and remaining uncertainties

The **identity, plastid targeting, membrane localization, pathway position, essential genetic role, and bleaching phenotype** are supported directly in Arabidopsis. The **precise reaction, FAD/plastoquinone chemistry, inhibitor-binding mechanism, tetrameric assembly, substrate cavity, and channeling model** are supported most deeply by biochemical and structural work on rice PDS. These properties are highly likely to apply to AtPDS because the proteins are orthologous plant-type PDS enzymes with conserved fold, catalytic chemistry, and inhibitor sensitivity, but they should not be mislabeled as direct structural measurements of Q07356. (brausemann2017structureofphytoene pages 1-3, koschmieder2017planttypephytoenedesaturase pages 1-3, ruizsola2012carotenoidbiosynthesisin pages 7-8, gemmecker2015phytoenedesaturasefrom pages 1-2)

The principal open questions are how AtPDS membrane association and oligomerization are regulated in vivo, whether its envelope-versus-thylakoid distribution changes among chloroplasts and chromoplasts, and which endogenous retinal-related metabolite mediates the newly described root–auxin effects. The 2024 work advances the signaling connection but does not yet establish a complete biosynthetic route, receptor, or direct molecular target for retinal.
