Gene Description

TGG1 (THIOGLUCOSIDE GLUCOHYDROLASE 1, BGLU38; At5g26000) is one of the two classical myrosinases of Arabidopsis shoots, a glycoside hydrolase family 1 beta-thioglucosidase (EC 3.2.1.147) that hydrolyzes the thioglucosidic bond of glucosinolates, releasing glucose and an unstable aglucone that rearranges to isothiocyanates or, with specifier proteins, to nitriles, epithionitriles or thiocyanates. Like other myrosinases it lacks the catalytic acid/base glutamate of O-glucosidases, and it retains only weak O-beta-glucosidase activity. It is a signal-peptide-bearing glycoprotein stored in vacuole-derived compartments of phloem-associated myrosin idioblasts and in guard cells, spatially separated from its glucosinolate substrates, so that hydrolysis occurs on tissue damage (the "mustard oil bomb"). TGG1 acts redundantly with TGG2: the double mutant lacks detectable leaf myrosinase activity and damage-induced breakdown of aliphatic glucosinolates, and is more susceptible to generalist lepidopteran herbivores. TGG1 is also one of the most abundant proteins of guard cells, where TGG1 and TGG2 are required, by an as yet undefined mechanism, for ABA-, methyl jasmonate- and isothiocyanate-induced stomatal closure.

Core Functions

Beta-thioglucosidase (myrosinase) that hydrolyzes glucosinolates on tissue damage, initiating formation of isothiocyanates and related toxic products that defend against generalist insect herbivores; stored in vacuole-derived compartments of myrosin cells and guard cells.
