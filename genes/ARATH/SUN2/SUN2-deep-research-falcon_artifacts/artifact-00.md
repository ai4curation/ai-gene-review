| **Identity and domain architecture** | Genetic and sequence analyses identify **AtSUN2 as At3g10730**, a canonical C-terminal SUN-domain protein with an N-terminal region, nuclear-localization signal, transmembrane segment, coiled-coil region, and C-terminal SUN domain; the latter has been mapped to approximately residues 285–447. | SUN domain: approximately aa 285–447. | Matches **Arabidopsis thaliana Q9SG79** and the supplied Sad1/UNC-84 annotations. | **Inner nuclear-envelope localization** | AtSUN2–GFP imaging in seedlings and living anthers, together with peptide-antibody immunolocalization, detected AtSUN2 around the nuclear periphery and at the nuclear envelope of pollen mother cells. | In meiotic interphase, AtSUN2–GFP was distributed around the nuclear envelope; during prophase I it formed usually **one prominent focus per nuclear envelope**, sometimes connected by an AtSUN2-positive membrane thread. | Strong direct localization evidence supports AtSUN2 as an integral inner nuclear-envelope component. | **Mitotic nuclear-envelope dynamics** | Live spinning-disk confocal imaging of fluorescent SUN proteins, chromatin, centromeres, microtubules, and ER showed SUN proteins dispersing into mitotic/ER membranes after nuclear-envelope breakdown and reassociating directionally with segregated chromatin during anaphase–telophase. | Complete enclosure of daughter chromatin by SUN-marked membrane required about **4–6 min** in the reported imaging series. | Supports a structural role in nuclear-envelope reassembly and nuclear shaping. Much of the detailed time-course was recorded with AtSUN1 as the representative SUN reporter; extrapolation of identical kinetics to AtSUN2 should therefore be cautious, although AtSUN2 showed corresponding mitotic membrane behavior. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013) | Oda & Fukuda, online 21 March 2011, [doi:10.1111/j.1365-313X.2011.04523.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-313X.2011.04523.x) |
| **SUN1 redundancy and nuclear-shape maintenance** | RNA interference/knockdown and sun1-knockout–sun2-knockdown analyses produced rounder nuclei, especially in elongated root-hair cells; fluorescent reporters showed both proteins at the nuclear envelope. | Nuclear-shape effects were strongest when both SUN proteins were depleted; single-mutant phenotypes were weak or absent in later meiotic analyses. | AtSUN2 is best annotated as a **partly redundant structural scaffold with SUN1**, helping maintain elongated or polarized nuclear morphology. Combined-depletion evidence is **not AtSUN2-specific**, and the sun2 allele used in some experiments was a knockdown rather than a null. | **WIP–RanGAP anchoring pathway** | Co-immunoprecipitation placed AtSUN2, AtWIP1, and AtRanGAP1 in one complex. Fluorescence localization and nuclear-localization-index measurements showed reduced nuclear-envelope enrichment of AtWIP1 and more diffuse AtRanGAP1–GFP in sun1-KO sun2-KD roots. | AtRanGAP1 localization: **n = 55**, *P* < 0.01; AtWIP1 localization: **n = 50**, *P* < 0.01. | Supports a SUN2-containing **SUN–WIP–RanGAP scaffold** in which inner-envelope SUN proteins retain outer-envelope WIPs that recruit RanGAP. | **OPENER recruitment to the nuclear envelope** | OPNR–YFP colocalized with SUN2–CFP during interphase and prophase and lost nuclear-envelope enrichment in the sun1-1 sun2-2 double mutant; conversely, OPNR loss did not alter SUN1/2 localization. | No binding constant or direct-interaction measurement was reported. | **Rapid meiotic chromosome movements and nuclear reorganization** | Live meiotic imaging in the 2024 study showed rapid, uncoordinated centromere movements during zygotene and pachytene. SUN1/2 redistributed into telomere-associated nuclear-envelope domains; the sun1 sun2 double mutant lacked rapid movements and showed defective telomere attachment, bouquet formation, and nucleolar displacement. | Centromere velocities reached **up to approximately 500 nm/s**; rapid movements were abolished in the double mutant. | Current evidence establishes canonical SUN1/2 proteins as essential inner-envelope components of the machinery that transfers cytoskeletal force to meiotic chromosomes. The knockout evidence is **not AtSUN2-specific**, so an exclusive AtSUN2 motor-coupling function cannot be assigned.
