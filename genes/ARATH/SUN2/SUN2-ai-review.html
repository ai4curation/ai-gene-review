Gene Description

SUN2 (At3g10730) is one of the two Arabidopsis thaliana C-terminal SUN (Cter-SUN) domain proteins (455 aa), the plant counterparts of animal SUN1/SUN2 and of fission yeast Sad1. It is a type II single-pass inner nuclear membrane protein with a nucleoplasmic N-terminus carrying a nuclear localization signal, a transmembrane segment, a luminal coiled-coil that mediates homo-oligomerization and heteromerization with SUN1, and a C-terminal SUN domain in the perinuclear space. The SUN domain binds the C-terminal KASH tails of plant-specific outer-nuclear-membrane KASH proteins (WIP1-3, SINE1-4, TIK) through a conserved KASH-binding pocket, forming SUN-KASH bridges (LINC complexes) that retain these proteins, and via WIPs the RanGTPase-activating protein RanGAP1, at the nuclear envelope. Through the WIP-bound tail-anchored WIT2 protein and the actin-based motor myosin XI-i, the bridge couples the actomyosin cytoskeleton to the nucleus and transmits cytoplasmic forces that give epidermal, root hair and trichome nuclei their elongated shape; on the nucleoplasmic side SUN proteins can also recruit the lamin-like CROWDED NUCLEI 1 (CRWN1/LINC1). SUN2 is expressed at lower levels than SUN1 but is up-regulated in proliferating tissues; it acts largely redundantly with SUN1, which predominates in nuclear shape determination, so sun2 phenotypes are seen mainly in sun1 sun2 double mutants. Together with its paralog it lines the nuclear envelope in meiotic prophase I and is needed for timely meiotic progression, full synapsis and normal crossover formation, the two paralogs attach telomeres to the nuclear envelope and drive rapid prophase chromosome movements, a conserved SUN-KASH role in meiosis. During open mitosis it follows nuclear envelope breakdown into the endoplasmic reticulum and marks the reforming nuclear envelope in coordination with the phragmoplast.

Functional annotation report: Arabidopsis thaliana SUN2

Executive conclusion

Identity is verified. The requested protein is Arabidopsis thaliana SUN2/AtSUN2, encoded by At3g10730 and corresponding to UniProt Q9SG79. It is a canonical C-terminal SUN-domain protein, not mammalian SUN2 and not the Arabidopsis mid-SUN proteins SUN3–SUN5. The literature’s domain organization—N-terminal nucleoplasmic region, nuclear-localization determinants, transmembrane segment, coiled-coil region, and C-terminal SUN domain—agrees with the supplied InterPro/Pfam annotations; the SUN domain has been mapped approximately to residues 285–447. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8, andov2021characterisationofthe pages 40-44)

AtSUN2 is not an enzyme or transporter. Its primary function is structural: it is an integral inner nuclear membrane (INM) scaffold and core component of plant LINC complexes. Its nucleoplasmic N terminus associates with the plant lamina-like/nucleoskeletal protein LINC1/CRWN1, while its C-terminal SUN domain faces the perinuclear space and binds KASH-related outer-nuclear-membrane proteins, including WIPs and SINEs. Through these interactions, AtSUN2 helps mechanically and organizationally couple the nucleoplasm and chromosomes to the cytoskeleton. Its best-supported functions are maintenance of nuclear shape, recruitment or stabilization of nuclear-envelope proteins, RanGAP anchoring, nuclear-envelope dynamics during division, and—with the partly redundant SUN1 protein—telomere-led chromosome movement, pairing, synapsis, and recombination during meiosis. (zhou2013howplantslinc pages 3-5, zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 4-5, graumann2014evidenceforlinc1sun pages 1-2, cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 14-15)

1. Identity, nomenclature, and ambiguity control

The gene symbol SUN2 is highly ambiguous across organisms. Searches retrieve extensive mammalian SUN2 literature, including human/mouse nuclear-mechanics and viral-infection studies; those proteins were excluded. Searches also retrieve Arabidopsis SUN3, SUN4, and SUN5, which belong to a structurally distinct mid-SUN class and were likewise excluded from target-specific conclusions.

The relevant protein is explicitly identified in Arabidopsis literature as AtSUN2/At3g10730, one of two canonical C-terminal SUN proteins, the other being AtSUN1. This identity matches the supplied organism, accession, and Sad1/UNC-84-domain annotations. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8, andov2021characterisationofthe pages 40-44)

2. Molecular architecture and membrane topology

AtSUN2 has the characteristic architecture of a canonical SUN protein: an N-terminal region containing nuclear-targeting determinants, a coiled-coil region, a membrane-spanning segment, and a conserved C-terminal SUN domain. The established LINC model places the large N-terminal region in the nucleoplasm and the C-terminal SUN domain in the nuclear-envelope lumen/perinuclear space, where it binds the short luminal tails of outer-nuclear-membrane KASH-related proteins. (zhou2013howplantslinc pages 3-5, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8)

Deletion experiments support contributions from the N-terminal region, nuclear-localization signal, coiled-coil region, and SUN-containing C terminus to efficient nuclear-envelope targeting or retention. AtSUN2 also participates in homomeric complexes and heteromeric complexes with AtSUN1, as supported by in-planta fluorescence interaction assays. The available literature does not establish native oligomeric stoichiometry or provide a high-resolution experimental structure of AtSUN2; therefore, exact trimerization or mechanochemical models derived from non-plant SUN proteins should not be assigned without qualification. (zhou2013howplantslinc pages 3-5, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8)

3. Subcellular localization and cell-cycle behavior

Fluorescent-protein imaging in Arabidopsis seedlings showed AtSUN2 at the nuclear periphery in cotyledon, hypocotyl, root-meristem, root-cortex, stele, and root-hair cells. Peptide-antibody immunolocalization and native-promoter AtSUN2–GFP imaging also placed it at the nuclear envelope of pollen mother cells. These data support INM localization across somatic and meiotic tissues rather than a tissue-restricted role. (oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 1-2, oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 3-5, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12)

AtSUN proteins remain associated with the continuous ER–nuclear-envelope membrane system during open mitosis. They disperse into mitotic/ER membranes after nuclear-envelope breakdown and reassociate directionally around daughter chromatin during anaphase and telophase. In the detailed live-imaging series, SUN-marked membrane completed enclosure of daughter chromatin in approximately 4–6 minutes, coordinated with phragmoplast organization. Much of this kinetic analysis used AtSUN1 as the reporter, so identical timing for AtSUN2 is plausible but not independently established at the same resolution. (oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 1-2, oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 3-5)

During meiosis, AtSUN2 localization is dynamic. It is broadly distributed around the envelope in meiotic interphase, then becomes polarized and forms prominent foci during prophase I—usually one major focus per nuclear envelope in the 2015 observations. Some AtSUN2 foci appeared separated from the envelope but connected to it by an AtSUN2-positive membrane thread. AtSUN2 was also prominent at reforming nuclear envelopes around the four haploid products in tetrads. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 12-15)

4. Primary biochemical and structural role: a plant LINC-complex scaffold

4.1 Outer-membrane WIP proteins and RanGAP anchoring

AtSUN2 interacts with plant WIP proteins through its SUN domain. WIPs are outer-nuclear-membrane KASH-related proteins whose short perinuclear tails engage SUN proteins. Co-immunoprecipitation placed AtSUN2, AtWIP1, and AtRanGAP1 in the same complex. In a sun1-knockout/sun2-knockdown background, AtWIP1 nuclear-envelope localization was reduced or lost and AtRanGAP1–GFP became significantly more cytoplasmic. The localization measurements used n = 50 for AtWIP1 and n = 55 for AtRanGAP1, with P < 0.01. (zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 4-5)

The resulting pathway is best represented as nucleoplasm–AtSUN1/2–WIP–RanGAP/cytoplasmic machinery. AtSUN2 is therefore not a RanGAP enzyme and has no known catalytic substrate; it is part of the membrane scaffold that positions RanGAP at the nuclear envelope. Co-immunoprecipitation demonstrates complex association but does not prove direct AtSUN2–RanGAP binding. Moreover, the decisive genetic background combined SUN1 loss with SUN2 knockdown, so this pathway cannot be assigned exclusively to SUN2. (zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 4-5)

4.2 SINE-family KASH proteins

Co-immunoprecipitation demonstrated SUN-domain-dependent interactions between AtSUN2 and SINE3, SINE4, and SINE5. FRAP experiments further showed that coexpression with AtSUN2 reduced SINE1 and SINE2 mobility at the nuclear envelope; mutating/deleting the SUN interaction region generally removed this effect. These FRAP comparisons used n = 35 and showed P < 0.01 for the wild-type SUN-dependent mobility reductions. Removing the terminal four residues of SINE KASH tails significantly reduced nuclear-envelope enrichment (n = 30, P < 0.01). (zhou2014identificationofunique pages 6-7)

These findings provide direct evidence that AtSUN2 is the INM component of multiple plant-specific LINC bridges. SINE1 links the envelope to actin in guard cells, while other SINE proteins have distinct expression patterns and biological functions. However, many interaction assays used transient expression in Nicotiana benthamiana, establishing interaction competence more firmly than native Arabidopsis stoichiometry or tissue-specific complex composition. (zhou2014identificationofunique pages 6-7)

4.3 Nucleoskeletal connection through LINC1/CRWN1

The nucleoplasmic side of AtSUN2 associates with LINC1, now generally grouped with the CRWN plant lamina-like proteins. AtSUN2 promoted LINC1 accumulation and immobilization at the nuclear periphery. Acceptor-photobleaching FRET detected interaction between full-length AtSUN2 and LINC1, with a FRET efficiency of 1.90 ± 1.0%, versus −1.47 ± 0.28% for the control; the AtSUN2 N-terminal 1–106-residue fragment also gave a positive signal (1.29 ± 0.76%; n = 35, P < 0.05). N-terminal deletion eliminated detectable interaction. Approximately 75 nuclei were evaluated per localization sample. (graumann2014evidenceforlinc1sun pages 2-4)

This supports a trans-envelope mechanical arrangement in which the AtSUN2 N terminus couples to a nucleoskeletal network while the luminal SUN domain couples to outer-membrane KASH-related proteins. Because the work used transient heterologous expression and fluorescence proximity assays, it does not by itself demonstrate purified-protein binding or define atomic contact residues. (graumann2014evidenceforlinc1sun pages 1-2, graumann2014evidenceforlinc1sun pages 2-4)

4.4 Other associated nuclear-envelope proteins

AtSUN1/2 recruit or associate with OPENER/OPNR, a nuclear-envelope- and mitochondria-associated protein required for cell-cycle progression. OPNR–YFP colocalized with SUN2–CFP during interphase and prophase and lost nuclear-envelope enrichment in the sun1 sun2 double mutant. Conversely, OPNR loss did not prevent SUN1/2 targeting to the INM. Direct SUN2–OPNR binding remains unresolved and could require an intermediary or cell-cycle-dependent modification. (wang2019openerisa pages 14-17)

Plant NEAP proteins have low mobility and biochemical properties consistent with a stable nuclear-envelope network, and the broader literature places them in SUN-associated nuclear-periphery assemblies. However, the retrieved primary evidence was insufficient to assign a precise direct AtSUN2–NEAP binding mechanism; this should remain a candidate association rather than a core annotation. (pawar2016anovelfamily pages 5-6)

5. Cellular functions

5.1 Nuclear shape and positioning

Combined depletion of SUN1 and SUN2 produces rounder, less elongated nuclei, especially in root hairs. The most defensible interpretation is that AtSUN2, redundantly with AtSUN1, helps distribute cytoskeletal and nucleoskeletal forces across the envelope to maintain polarized nuclear shape. WIP/WIT/myosin-XI-i and SINE–actin assemblies provide candidate cytoplasmic force-transmission routes. AtSUN2’s contribution to nuclear movement is therefore structural and adaptor-like, not motor activity. (zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 4-5, oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 1-2)

Single sun2 mutants are often phenotypically mild or normal, whereas combined SUN1/SUN2 disruption reveals strong defects. Functional redundancy is consequently a central feature of AtSUN2 annotation and a major limitation on assigning SUN2-exclusive phenotypes. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12)

5.2 Mitosis and nuclear-envelope reassembly

AtSUN2 tracks nuclear-envelope membrane reorganization during cell division and is present in membranes that re-enclose daughter chromatin. Its stable membrane association and proximity to chromosomes suggest roles in spatially coordinating envelope reassembly and nuclear shaping. Nevertheless, current evidence supports a structural contribution rather than an essential enzymatic checkpoint function, and detailed mechanistic separation from SUN1 remains incomplete. (oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 1-2, andov2021characterisationofthe pages 44-47)

5.3 Meiosis, telomere attachment, and recombination

The strongest organismal phenotype comes from the sun1-1 sun2-2 double mutant. Single mutants appeared normal, while double mutants had severely reduced fertility: mean silique length was 6.8 ± 1.3 mm versus 15.1 ± 0.5 mm in wild type, and seed number was 2.1 ± 0.9 versus 60.4 ± 2.5 per silique, a 96.5% reduction (n = 20 for each comparison). (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12)

Meiotic S phase to tetrad formation took approximately 48 hours in the double mutant versus 36 hours in wild type, reflecting an approximately 12-hour delay in prophase I. The mutant failed to complete normal synapsis, retained interlock-like chromosome configurations, formed univalents, and showed chromosome mis-segregation. Mean chiasma number fell to 3.32 ± 0.18 per cell (n = 128) compared with 10.2 ± 0.14 in Col-0 (n = 69) and 9.85 ± 0.40 in Ws (n = 56). The normal transient telomere bouquet was not observed. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 12-15)

These findings indicate that SUN1/SUN2-dependent nuclear-envelope attachment allows cytoskeletal forces to move chromosome ends, promote homolog encounters, remove chromosome interlocks, complete synapsis, and support normal crossover formation. Because the phenotype requires loss of both paralogues, it establishes an overlapping SUN1/SUN2 function rather than an AtSUN2-exclusive pathway. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 22-26, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 1-4)

6. Latest research: rapid meiotic chromosome movement

The major recent advance is Cromer et al., published in Nature Communications in July 2024 (DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-50169-4). Live imaging demonstrated rapid, uncoordinated Arabidopsis centromere movements during zygotene and pachytene, reaching approximately 500 nm/s. SUN proteins reorganized into telomere-associated nuclear-envelope domains during these stages. Rapid movements were abolished in the sun1 sun2 double mutant, which also showed defective telomere–envelope association, bouquet formation, and nucleolar displacement. (cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 14-15)

This work upgrades the earlier static and genetic model to a dynamic mechanistic one: canonical plant SUN proteins are required to transfer cytoskeletal force across the nuclear envelope during meiotic prophase. It also establishes Arabidopsis as a tractable model for plant rapid prophase movements. Residual telomere association in the double mutant suggests that attachment is not wholly SUN1/2-dependent or that other SUN-domain proteins partially compensate. Again, the genetic evidence does not resolve a unique AtSUN2 contribution. (cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 14-15)

8. Current applications and real-world relevance

AtSUN2 currently has research rather than deployed agricultural applications. Fluorescent AtSUN2 constructs serve as INM/nuclear-envelope markers and as tools for studying envelope breakdown and reassembly, LINC-complex assembly, nuclear mechanics, and meiotic chromosome movement. Dominant-negative SUN constructs and SUN-domain interaction mutants can experimentally disrupt nucleocytoskeletal coupling. (oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 1-2, zhou2014identificationofunique pages 6-7, graumann2014evidenceforlinc1sun pages 2-4)

Potential crop applications are plausible but not yet demonstrated for Arabidopsis AtSUN2 itself. Because canonical SUN proteins affect nuclear architecture, meiotic pairing, crossover formation, fertility, and force transmission, their orthologues or interaction interfaces could eventually be manipulated to alter recombination or reproductive performance. However, the severe fertility phenotype of combined SUN1/SUN2 loss warns that broad disruption is more likely to be deleterious than beneficial. Targeted, tissue-specific, or partial modulation would require substantial validation before breeding use.

9. Expert assessment and annotation confidence

High-confidence annotation: AtSUN2 is a canonical C-terminal SUN-domain INM protein and a core structural component of plant LINC complexes. It binds or stabilizes outer-envelope WIP/SINE proteins through its SUN domain, associates on its nucleoplasmic side with LINC1/CRWN1, contributes to RanGAP nuclear-envelope anchoring, and participates in nuclear shape and envelope dynamics. (zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 4-5, zhou2014identificationofunique pages 6-7, graumann2014evidenceforlinc1sun pages 2-4)

High-confidence but paralogue-shared function: SUN1 and SUN2 jointly support meiotic telomere attachment, rapid chromosome movement, bouquet organization, synapsis, crossover formation, chromosome segregation, and fertility. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 12-15, cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 14-15)

Moderate-confidence assignments: AtSUN2 participates in nuclear positioning/movement through WIP/WIT/myosin or SINE/actin assemblies and recruits OPNR to the nuclear envelope. These models are well supported at the complex level, but direct native interactions and SUN2-specific necessity remain incompletely resolved. (andov2021characterisationofthe pages 36-40, wang2019openerisa pages 14-17)

Unresolved questions: Native oligomeric stoichiometry, high-resolution AtSUN2 structure, exact topology/contact residues, tissue-specific LINC composition, direct binding to OPNR or NEAP proteins, and functions that distinguish SUN2 from SUN1 remain open. The scarcity of AtSUN2-specific 2023–2024 studies is itself informative: the 2024 advance concerns the redundant SUN1/SUN2 module, while much 2023 plant-SUN research focused on the distinct mid-SUN proteins and should not be transferred to Q9SG79.

Final functional statement

Arabidopsis SUN2 (At3g10730; Q9SG79) is a non-catalytic inner nuclear membrane scaffold whose C-terminal SUN domain engages outer-nuclear-membrane KASH-related proteins and whose nucleoplasmic N terminus associates with the plant lamina-like network. In partially redundant partnership with SUN1, it forms force-transmitting LINC complexes that maintain nuclear architecture, position RanGAP and other envelope proteins, coordinate nuclear-envelope dynamics, and couple meiotic chromosome ends to cytoskeletal movement required for efficient pairing, synapsis, recombination, segregation, and fertility.

description: SUN2 (At3g10730) is one of the two Arabidopsis thaliana C-terminal SUN (Cter-SUN) domain
  proteins (455 aa), the plant counterparts of animal SUN1/SUN2 and of fission yeast Sad1. It is a type
  II single-pass inner nuclear membrane protein with a nucleoplasmic N-terminus carrying a nuclear localization
  signal, a transmembrane segment, a luminal coiled-coil that mediates homo-oligomerization and heteromerization
  with SUN1, and a C-terminal SUN domain in the perinuclear space. The SUN domain binds the C-terminal
  KASH tails of plant-specific outer-nuclear-membrane KASH proteins (WIP1-3, SINE1-4, TIK) through a conserved
  KASH-binding pocket, forming SUN-KASH bridges (LINC complexes) that retain these proteins, and via WIPs
  the RanGTPase-activating protein RanGAP1, at the nuclear envelope. Through the WIP-bound tail-anchored
  WIT2 protein and the actin-based motor myosin XI-i, the bridge couples the actomyosin cytoskeleton to
  the nucleus and transmits cytoplasmic forces that give epidermal, root hair and trichome nuclei their
  elongated shape; on the nucleoplasmic side SUN proteins can also recruit the lamin-like CROWDED NUCLEI
  1 (CRWN1/LINC1). SUN2 is expressed at lower levels than SUN1 but is up-regulated in proliferating tissues;
  it acts largely redundantly with SUN1, which predominates in nuclear shape determination, so sun2 phenotypes
  are seen mainly in sun1 sun2 double mutants. Together with its paralog it lines the nuclear envelope
  in meiotic prophase I and is needed for timely meiotic progression, full synapsis and normal crossover
  formation, the two paralogs attach telomeres to the nuclear envelope and drive rapid prophase chromosome
  movements, a conserved SUN-KASH role in meiosis. During open mitosis it follows nuclear envelope breakdown
  into the endoplasmic reticulum and marks the reforming nuclear envelope in coordination with the phragmoplast.
