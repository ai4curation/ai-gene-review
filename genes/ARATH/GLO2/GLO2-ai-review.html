Gene Description

GLO2 (GOX2, At3g14415) encodes one of the two major leaf glycolate oxidases of Arabidopsis, a peroxisomal FMN-dependent (S)-2-hydroxy-acid oxidase that oxidizes glycolate to glyoxylate with O2, producing H2O2. Recombinant GOX2 prefers glycolate and oxidizes L-lactate, hydroxypyruvate and glycerate only weakly in vitro; it uses O2 and not NAD(P)+ as electron acceptor, and it restores growth of a cyanobacterial glycolate dehydrogenase mutant in low CO2. In the photorespiratory C2 cycle it catalyzes the peroxisomal glycolate-to-glyoxylate step together with its tandem paralog GLO1 (GOX1, At3g14420); the two genes are co-regulated with other photorespiratory genes and largely redundant, so single mutants grow normally in air whereas combined knock-down causes a severe photorespiratory phenotype. Loss of GOX2, unlike loss of GOX1, does not suppress the catalase-deficient cat2 phenotype. H2O2 from glycolate oxidase also contributes to defence signalling during nonhost resistance. The GOX1/GOX2 names for At3g14420/At3g14415 are crossed in part of the literature.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Hackenberg et al. 2011 describe GOX2 (At3g14415) as the main photorespiratory isoform, whereas Kerchev et al. 2016 find that GOX1 (At3g14420), not GOX2, matters most under severe photorespiration and in cat2. Given the crossed GOX1/GOX2 naming in parts of the literature (e.g. PMID:17951448), which locus is quantitatively dominant in leaf peroxisomes?

  are co-regulated with other photorespiratory genes and largely redundant, so single mutants grow normally
  in air whereas combined knock-down causes a severe photorespiratory phenotype. Loss of GOX2, unlike
  loss of GOX1, does not suppress the catalase-deficient cat2 phenotype. H2O2 from glycolate oxidase also
