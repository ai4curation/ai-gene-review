| Identity | *Arabidopsis thaliana* **At1g10830** encodes **Z-ISO**, corresponding to UniProt **Q9SAC0**, a chloroplast-targeted 15-cis-ζ-carotene isomerase. | Mutant mapping, functional expression, proteomics, and cross-species complementation | **High.** The symbol, locus, organism, accession, and biochemical activity are concordant; no similarly named protein was substituted. |
| Catalytic reaction | Z-ISO isomerizes the central 15–15′ double bond of **9,15,9′-tri-cis-ζ-carotene** to form **9,9′-di-cis-ζ-carotene**. | Carotenoid profiling in mutants and engineered *E. coli*; biochemical reconstitution | **High.** The stereospecific substrate and product are directly supported. |
| Pathway position | Z-ISO acts **after phytoene desaturase (PDS)** and **before ζ-carotene desaturase (ZDS)** during the plastidial conversion of 15-cis-phytoene toward all-trans-lycopene. Its product supplies the 15-trans configuration required by ZDS. | Pathway genetics, metabolite accumulation, and heterologous pathway reconstruction | **High.** This is its primary physiological function. |
| Localization and topology | The precursor is imported into chloroplasts and functions as a multipass **integral membrane protein** in plastids. A transit-peptide cleavage near residue 58 and five to seven transmembrane helices have been predicted; the precise plastid membrane and experimentally resolved topology remain uncertain. | Chloroplast proteomics and sequence/topology prediction | **High** for plastid integral-membrane localization; **moderate** for cleavage position, helix count, and exact membrane compartment. |
| Cofactor and redox chemistry | Plant Z-ISO is a **heme-b-dependent** isomerase whose active state requires reduced, ferrous heme; redox-regulated axial-ligand switching has been proposed. | Purified maize Z-ISO in membrane/liposome systems, spectroscopy, mutagenesis, and comparative modeling | **High** for the plant-enzyme heme-b and ferrous dependence; **moderate** when transferred specifically to Arabidopsis because the decisive biochemical work used maize protein. |
| Structural mechanism | AlphaFold-guided models place the carotenoid and heme within a transmembrane cavity. H266 and H354 are leading candidate heme ligands, while H150 may participate near the substrate’s 15-cis bond rather than bind heme directly. | Computational docking plus comparative mutagenesis | **Provisional.** No experimentally determined Z-ISO structure is available, and residue-level assignments remain hypotheses. |
| Domain and evolution | Q9SAC0 contains the **NnrU domain** represented by InterPro IPR009915/Pfam PF07298. Plant Z-ISO likely evolved from an NnrU-like bacterial membrane protein, but ordinary denitrification-associated NnrU proteins do not necessarily possess carotenoid-isomerase activity. | Domain annotation, phylogenetics, gene-neighborhood analysis, and functional comparison | **High** for homology/domain membership; **high** that domain identity alone is insufficient to infer Z-ISO activity. |
| Loss-of-function evidence | Arabidopsis and maize loss-of-function mutants accumulate the tri-cis substrate, have impaired carotenoid synthesis, appear pale in darkness, and green slowly after illumination. Light can photochemically bypass part of the block but does not fully replace the enzyme. | Forward genetics, mutant metabolite profiling, and de-etiolation phenotyping | **High.** Phenotypes directly connect the gene to light-independent and efficient light-exposed carotenoid synthesis. |
| *E. coli* functional evidence | Full-length functional Arabidopsis Z-ISO converts most accumulated 9,15,9′-tri-cis-ζ-carotene to the di-cis product in engineered *E. coli*; a shortened alternative product and C-terminal mutant lack detectable activity. | Heterologous functional assay | **High.** Demonstrates catalytic sufficiency and the importance of the C-terminal region. |
| 2022 cross-species rescue | Arabidopsis At1g10830 lacking its predicted 58-residue transit peptide restored β-carotene synthesis in a *Synechocystis* Δslr1599 Z-ISO mutant under light-activated heterotrophic conditions. | In-vivo trans-complementation | **High** for conserved light-independent Z-ISO activity; the study did not report a quantitative Arabidopsis rescue efficiency. |
| Plastid-development signaling | In a carotenoid-isomerase-defective **ccr2** background, **ziso-155** prevents downstream cis-carotene formation and restores prolamellar bodies in more than 94% of etioplasts, supporting a cis-carotene-derived apocarotenoid signal that regulates etioplast/chloroplast development. | Arabidopsis epistasis, carotenoid profiling, microscopy, and de-etiolation measurements | **High** for the genetic interaction and altered metabolite requirement; the precise signaling molecule remains unidentified. This is a downstream consequence, not Z-ISO’s primary enzymatic reaction. |
| 2023–2024 research status | No major 2023–2024 Arabidopsis-specific study located in the reviewed literature materially revised At1g10830’s catalytic annotation. Recent crop and algal studies mainly treat Z-ISO ortholog expression as a carotenoid-pathway marker or engineering candidate. | Targeted recent-literature search | **Moderate.** This is a search-based absence statement, not proof that no relevant publication exists; ortholog findings should not be presented as direct At1g10830 evidence. |
