Gene Description

AFB2 (AUXIN SIGNALING F-BOX 2; At3g26810) is a leucine-rich-repeat F-box protein of the TIR1/AFB auxin receptor family. As the substrate-recognition subunit of an SCF(ASK-cullin-F-box) E3 ubiquitin ligase, it binds auxin in a pocket of its LRR domain and, with auxin acting as molecular glue, captures the degron (domain II) of Aux/IAA transcriptional repressors, forming a TIR1/AFB-Aux/IAA co-receptor. This targets Aux/IAAs for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, releasing AUXIN RESPONSE FACTOR transcription factors to activate auxin-responsive genes. AFB2 belongs to the AFB2/AFB3 clade and acts redundantly with TIR1 and AFB3; together with TIR1 it is one of the dominant auxin receptors in the seedling root, and quadruple tir1 afb1 afb2 afb3 loss causes auxin insensitivity and embryonic patterning defects resembling monopteros/bodenlos. AFB2 is found in the nucleus and the cytoplasm, and its transcript is targeted by miR393. The InsP6 cofactor-binding assignment is inferred from the conserved TIR1 receptor fold; direct adenylate cyclase activity has not been established for AFB2 by the available receptor assays.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the substantial cytoplasmic pool of AFB2 (unlike nuclear TIR1) have a distinct function, e.g. in rapid non-transcriptional auxin responses, or is it a reservoir?

Q: Which Aux/IAA partners are preferentially degraded by SCF(AFB2) versus SCF(TIR1) in vivo, given their different auxin-dependent affinities in yeast and in vitro assays?

Q: Does purified, properly expressed AFB2 catalyze cAMP production, and can catalytic mutations preserve Aux/IAA recognition as in TIR1?

Q: Which AFB2 peptides and vacuolar fraction underlie the original PMID17151019 identification, and is the pool reproducible with targeted imaging or fractionation?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Compare Aux/IAA degradation kinetics (e.g. ratiometric degron reporters for IAA12/BDL, IAA17, IAA7) in tir1 versus afb2 backgrounds to resolve receptor-specific substrate preferences in planta.

Hypothesis: AFB2 and TIR1 differ in substrate preference and auxin sensitivity, contributing to tissue-specific auxin responses.

Experiment: Express nucleus-restricted versus cytoplasm-restricted AFB2 in tir1 afb2 mutants and score complementation of root growth and rapid root growth inhibition.

Hypothesis: Cytoplasmic AFB2 contributes to fast, non-transcriptional auxin responses.
