# Functional annotation report: Arabidopsis SYP121

## Identity and primary function

**The target is Arabidopsis thaliana syntaxin SYP121**, also called **PEN1** and **SYR1**. The supplied UniProt record identifies it as **Q9ZSD4**, encoded by **At3g11820**; Arabidopsis primary studies independently identify SYP121 with PEN1/SYR1 and classify it as a plasma-membrane syntaxin. The literature examined concerns this Arabidopsis protein, not similarly named proteins in other species. Its syntaxin and SNARE domains are consistent with its experimentally established classification as a **Qa-SNARE**. (grefen2010anovelmotif pages 1-2, assaad2004thepen1syntaxin pages 1-2, hachez2014arabidopsissnaressyp61 pages 1-2)

**SYP121’s primary molecular role is to help fuse secretory vesicles with the plasma membrane.** Its membrane-anchored syntaxin supplies the Qa component of a SNARE assembly; **SNAP33** supplies Qb and Qc components, and vesicle-associated **VAMP721 or VAMP722** supplies the R component. Assembly draws the vesicle and target membranes together. Biochemical complex formation, interactions in plants, and mutations that impair both SNARE assembly and immune function support this assignment. SYP121 is **not an enzyme, potassium transporter, or aquaporin**: it regulates where membrane proteins and secreted material are delivered and also directly regulates certain potassium channels. [Pajonk et al., *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, 3 October 2008](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M805236200); [Waghmare et al., *Plant Physiology*, November 2019](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.19.00919). (pajonk2008activitydeterminantsand pages 1-2, waghmare2019k+channelsec11binding pages 1-2)

The evidence across its principal functions is summarized below.

## Where SYP121 acts

SYP121 is principally **resident at the plasma membrane**, with its SNARE machinery operating on the cellular face where secretory vesicles arrive. Functional fluorescent fusions and membrane-fraction studies support that localization. It is also detected in intracellular trafficking compartments: during powdery-mildew attack it concentrates at plasma-membrane domains beneath fungal penetration sites and in nearby compartments, while immune-function experiments indicate cycling between the plasma membrane and endosomes. Its presence in isolated **apoplastic extracellular-vesicle populations** should not be mistaken for evidence that its principal fusion activity occurs outside the cell. [Assaad et al., *Molecular Biology of the Cell*, November 2004](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e04-02-0140); [Kim et al., *Plant & Cell Physiology*, June 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/pcp/pcw061); [Zimmerman et al., *Plant Direct*, September 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/pld3.70006). (assaad2004thepen1syntaxin pages 5-6, assaad2004thepen1syntaxin pages 6-7, kim2016synaptotagmin1negatively pages 7-9, zimmerman2024arabidopsisapoplasttet8 pages 1-2)

## Mechanistic pathways and experimentally defined cargo

**Polarized secretion in fungal penetration resistance.** In epidermal cells challenged by powdery mildew, PEN1/SYP121 accumulates beneath the fungal appressorium. This places its exocytic activity at the site where the plant rapidly constructs a **papilla**, a protective cell-wall apposition between its plasma membrane and the attempted fungal entry point. In the original Arabidopsis experiments, GFP-PEN1 signal at attack sites was approximately **6.3-fold** that at the surrounding plasma membrane; *pen1-1* papilla formation was delayed by approximately **two hours**. The study reported **26.6 ± 2.5%** penetration by the normally nonhost barley powdery mildew in *pen1-1*, and PEN1 recruitment was substantially stronger than that of its close paralog SYP122. Mature papillae nevertheless formed: the experimentally supported function is **timely, focal delivery**, not synthesis of a known papilla constituent or an exclusive requirement for papilla formation. [Assaad et al., November 2004](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e04-02-0140). (assaad2004thepen1syntaxin pages 3-5, assaad2004thepen1syntaxin pages 5-6, assaad2004thepen1syntaxin pages 6-7)

SNAP33 and VAMP721/722 provide a molecular link between that localization and secretion: they assemble with PEN1, and mutations in the PEN1 SNARE region impair immune activity. PEN1 also recycles through endosomal compartments; disrupting recycling with brefeldin A reduces its focal accumulation and compromises penetration resistance. These results implicate a **plasma-membrane–endosome recycling–exocytosis pathway** in rapid defense, but do not establish a complete inventory of the material secreted by PEN1-positive vesicles. [Pajonk et al., October 2008](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M805236200); [Kim et al., June 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/pcp/pcw061). (pajonk2008activitydeterminantsand pages 1-2, kim2016synaptotagmin1negatively pages 7-9, kim2016synaptotagmin1negatively pages 6-7)

**Aquaporin delivery and membrane water permeability.** A more precisely identified membrane cargo is **PIP2;7**, an Arabidopsis aquaporin. SYP121 associates with PIP2;7 at the plasma membrane and with **SYP61**, a syntaxin concentrated at the trans-Golgi network/early endosome. Co-immunoprecipitation, interaction assays, localization, and functional interference support a role for both syntaxins in PIP2;7’s post-Golgi delivery. Induction of a dominant-negative SYP121 fragment reduced peripheral PIP2;7 fluorescence by **57%** and reduced the measured osmotic water permeability of PIP2;7-expressing mesophyll protoplasts by **64%**. Other aquaporins and the BRI1 receptor were also affected, whereas PIN2 was not; this is a **cargo-selective pathway**, not proof that every plasma-membrane protein requires SYP121. Association between SYP61 and SYP121 does not, on its own, resolve the precise fusion intermediate or prove that one specific SYP61–SYP121 complex accounts for all the effects. [Hachez et al., *The Plant Cell*, July 2014](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.114.127159). (hachez2014arabidopsissnaressyp61 pages 1-2, hachez2014arabidopsissnaressyp61 pages 8-10, hachez2014arabidopsissnaressyp61 pages 4-6)

**Potassium-channel regulation beyond vesicle fusion.** SYP121 binds the regulatory **KC1** channel subunit and associates with the **KC1–AKT1** inward-rectifying potassium-channel complex. Its N-terminal **F₉xRF** motif is required for KC1 interaction and normal channel-gating effects. Root-epidermal voltage-clamp experiments found that substitutions **F9A** and **F12A** failed to restore the characteristic inward potassium current to *syp121* plants, whereas **S10A** restored wild-type-like current. This establishes a direct regulatory role in potassium uptake when channel-mediated acquisition is limiting; the potassium ion passes through the **channel**, not SYP121. [Grefen et al., *The Plant Cell*, September 2010](https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.110.077768). (grefen2010anovelmotif pages 1-2, grefen2010anovelmotif pages 9-10)

Channel binding may also coordinate uptake with the membrane expansion needed for growth. In a later interaction and electrophysiology study, potassium-channel binding was found to compete with binding of the SNARE regulator **SEC11**; the authors proposed that this exchange helps prime SYP121 for assembly with SNAP33 and VAMP721. This is a useful experimentally informed **mechanistic model**, rather than a fully visualized sequence of fusion events in intact Arabidopsis cells. The related plasma-membrane syntaxin **SYP122** overlaps with SYP121 in basal growth-related secretion: their single mutants can lack obvious growth defects, whereas double mutants are severely dwarfed and necrotic. Such redundancy explains why an isolated *syp121* phenotype need not represent the full contribution of this secretory machinery. [Waghmare et al., November 2019](https://doi.org/10.1104/pp.19.00919); [Assaad et al., November 2004](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.e04-02-0140). (waghmare2019k+channelsec11binding pages 1-2, assaad2004thepen1syntaxin pages 3-5)

## Recent research and present interpretation

A **2024 expert review** identifies SYP121–PIP2;7 interaction and its aquaporin-targeting effect as established, while noting that *syp121*, unlike *syp122*, has been reported to show delayed light-induced stomatal opening and Ca²⁺-evoked closure. It describes joint control by SYP121, aquaporins, and potassium channels as an appealing **hypothesis**, not a demonstrated tripartite complex. Distinct work on the related syntaxin **SYP132** must not be assigned to SYP121: in particular, the review attributes stress-associated PIP2;1/AHA1 endocytosis to SYP132. [Ding, Fox and Chaumont, *Plant, Cell & Environment*, May 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.14942). (ding2024multifacetedroleand pages 15-18)

A separately indexed **2024 primary study** is titled *“LKS4-mediated SYP121 phosphorylation participates in light-induced stomatal opening in Arabidopsis”* ([Ding et al., *Current Biology*, July 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.06.001)). Its full text was unavailable in the retrieved material. Consequently, its title supports noting a **reported light–LKS4–SYP121 regulatory connection**, but phosphorylation sites, effect sizes, and the precise causal mechanism are not assessed here; they should not be inferred from the title.

Also in 2024, an Arabidopsis study linked PEN1 to **extracellular-vesicle and leaf-senescence biology**. Apoplastic TET8 protein was approximately **twofold higher** in eight-week-old *pen1* rosettes than in wild type, but not higher at four weeks, and the increase was not accompanied by increased *TET8* transcript. Mutations in **TET3** or **TET8** reversed the early-senescence phenotype observed in *pen1*. PEN1-associated and TET8-associated extracellular vesicles are distinguished experimentally; increased TET8 in the *pen1* background is therefore **not** evidence that PEN1 directly produces TET8 vesicles or that vesicle production causes senescence. [Zimmerman et al., September 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/pld3.70006). (zimmerman2024arabidopsisapoplasttet8 pages 1-2, zimmerman2024arabidopsisapoplasttet8 pages 4-6)

**Overall assessment.** The most defensible functional annotation is **plasma-membrane Qa-SNARE mediating selective secretory-vesicle fusion**, with experimentally supported roles in rapid fungal-defense secretion and aquaporin delivery, plus a separable direct role in potassium-channel gating. Recent literature extends its possible connections to light-responsive stomatal regulation and extracellular-vesicle-associated senescence, but these should not displace the established fusion function or be treated as proof of specific secreted cargo or a single universal SYP121 pathway. A 2024 review of plant immune trafficking similarly emphasizes that defense depends on timely, localized cargo delivery amid overlapping trafficking routes. [Bhandari and Brandizzi, *Journal of Cell Biology*, March 2024](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.202307066). (bhandari2024logisticsofdefense pages 1-2, bhandari2024logisticsofdefense pages 10-11)
