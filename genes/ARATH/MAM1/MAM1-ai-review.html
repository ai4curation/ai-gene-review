Gene Description

MAM1 (METHYLTHIOALKYLMALATE SYNTHASE 1, At5g23010; the GSL-ELONG / GSM1 locus) is a plastid-targeted 2-oxo-acid/acetyl-CoA condensing enzyme of the DRE-TIM metallolyase (LeuA-like) family that catalyzes the committed step of each turn of the methionine side-chain elongation cycle in aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis: condensation of an omega-methylthio-2-oxoalkanoate with acetyl-CoA to form a 2-(omega-methylthio)alkylmalate. MAM1 evolved from isopropylmalate synthase of leucine biosynthesis by gene duplication and neofunctionalization but has lost IPMS activity; it acts on 4-methylthio-2-oxobutanoate and 5-methylthio-2-oxopentanoate (first and second elongation cycles; trace activity on the C6 acid), requires a divalent metal ion and is activated by ATP. MAM1 therefore determines the predominance of 4-carbon side-chain (C4) aliphatic glucosinolates in Col-0; missense or insertion mutants shift the profile from C4 to C3 glucosinolates. Natural presence/absence and allelic variation at the MAM1/MAM2 locus underlies much of the side-chain-length diversity of glucosinolates among Arabidopsis accessions and Brassica crops. MAM1 expression is co-regulated with BCAT4, is wound-, light- and drought-responsive, and is controlled by the MYB28/MYB29 transcription factors.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Which N-terminal active-site residues of MAM1 restrict it to the C4/C5 omega-methylthio-2-oxo acids while excluding 2-oxoisovalerate, and can these be engineered to tune glucosinolate chain length in Brassica oilseeds?

Q: How does ATP activation of MAM1 (absent in MAM3) regulate flux into the first elongation cycles in vivo?

Q: GO has an unused term, GO:0033322 L-homomethionine biosynthetic process, whose definition (formation of L-homomethionine from L-methionine by chain elongation) matches the methionine chain-elongation cycle this enzyme catalyzes. GO obsoleted the related terms GO:0033506 (glucosinolate biosynthetic process from homomethionine; a pathway variant out of scope, replaced by GO:0019761) and GO:0033321 (homomethionine metabolic process; unnecessary grouping), and no gene product in any species carries GO:0033322. Should the chain-elongation enzymes (BCAT4, MAM1/MAM3, IPMI, IPMDH1, BCAT3) be annotated to it, or should GO obsolete it in favour of glucosinolate biosynthetic process?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Solve the structure of Arabidopsis MAM1 with bound substrate and compare with IPMS1/2 and Brassica juncea MAM to map residues responsible for loss of IPMS activity; test by reciprocal mutagenesis and E. coli leuA complementation.

Experiment: Generate mam1 mam3 double mutants by CRISPR (previously unobtainable by crossing) to test whether any aliphatic glucosinolate synthesis persists without MAM activity.

2026-10-01: GO:0033322 withdrawn

The NEW annotation to GO:0033322 (L-homomethionine biosynthetic process) has been removed, and the
term dropped from core_functions. GO obsoleted the sibling terms GO:0033506 ("glucosinolate
biosynthetic process from homomethionine": "a specific pathway variant, which is out of scope for
GO", replaced by GO:0019761) and GO:0033321 ("homomethionine metabolic process": "an unnecessary
grouping term"). No gene product in any species carries GO:0033322. Read together, this is a GO
convention to annotate the chain-elongation enzymes to glucosinolate biosynthetic process
(GO:0019761), which this gene already carries, not a gap to fill. The question is kept as a
suggested_question for GO.
