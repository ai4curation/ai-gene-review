Gene Description

DWARF14 (AtD14) is the strigolactone (SL) receptor of Arabidopsis, a 267-residue alpha/beta-hydrolase-fold protein with a Ser97-His247-Asp218 catalytic triad in a hydrophobic ligand pocket. Binding of SL (and of the synthetic analog GR24) triggers a conformational change of the lid domain that allows D14 to associate with the F-box protein MAX2 (SCF(MAX2)/ASK1) and with the SMXL6/SMXL7/SMXL8 transcriptional co-repressors, leading to their polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. D14 also slowly hydrolyses the butenolide D-ring of SLs; whether hydrolysis (and a covalently linked D-ring intermediate) is required to activate signalling or mainly deactivates the hormone after signalling remains debated. D14 is found in the nucleus and cytoplasm, acts locally in the shoot rather than as a mobile signal, and is itself degraded in an SL- and MAX2-dependent manner as negative feedback. Loss of D14 causes increased shoot branching, altered leaf shape and insensitivity to SL; its paralog KAI2 mediates karrikin responses through the same F-box protein.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

strigolactone receptor activity

Definition: Combining with a strigolactone and transmitting the signal, typically by an SL-induced conformational change that promotes association with an F-box protein (MAX2/D3) and SMXL/D53-family co-repressors, leading to their ubiquitin-dependent degradation.

Justification: D14 orthologs in Arabidopsis, rice, petunia and pea are bona fide SL receptors, but GO has no SL-specific receptor MF; analogous terms exist for auxin (GO:0038198). The term would be a child of GO:0038023 signaling receptor activity.

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:27479325 : define AtD14 as a non-canonical hormone receptor with dual functions to generate and sense the active form of strigolactone

strigolactone hydrolase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the hydrolysis of the butenolide D-ring (a cyclic carboxylic ester) of a strigolactone, releasing the ABC-ring moiety and a hydroxy-butenolide D-ring product, typically via a Ser-His-Asp catalytic triad.

Justification: D14 hydrolyses GR24 and natural SLs; GO has only substrate-specific lactonase children of GO:0052689 carboxylic ester hydrolase activity (e.g. limonin-D-ring-lactonase activity) and none for strigolactone. The term would be a child of GO:0052689.

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:23381136 : only the D14 proteins were able to hydrolyze GR24, demonstrating hormone substrate specificity for this class of signaling hydrolases

Summary: UniProt IMP annotation of strigolactone biosynthetic process citing the carlactone paper (Alder et al. 2012).
Reason: D14 performs no step of strigolactone biosynthesis. The pathway that forms SL from beta-carotene is catalysed by D27, CCD7 and CCD8 (to carlactone) and then MAX1 and downstream oxidases/methyltransferases, as the cited paper's own abstract states; D14 acts downstream as the receptor, d14 mutants are SL-insensitive rather than SL-deficient, and D14's hydrolase converts SL into a covalently bound intermediate and inactive products (perception/deactivation, not formation of strigolactone). The full text of PMID:22422982 is not in the cache and is not open access (no PMC copy; publisher page paywalled), so the exact assay the UniProt curator used could not be inspected. The same paper also supports the IMP secondary shoot formation row above, which indicates the D14 assay was a branching phenotype (consistent with SL insensitivity of d14), not a biosynthetic measurement. In any case no d14 phenotype could show that D14 does any of the work of SL formation - a branching or SL-level phenotype in d14 reflects loss of perception or feedback on SL levels - so the assertion is contradicted on the same biological grounds as the IBA row above, independent of evidence code.

Core Functions

Strigolactone receptor: binds SL in its alpha/beta-hydrolase pocket, undergoes an SL-induced conformational change, and recruits the F-box protein MAX2 and SMXL6/7/8 co-repressors into an SCF(MAX2) complex that ubiquitinates the SMXLs for proteasomal degradation.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is D14 catalysis required for signalling in planta (CLIM model, Yao et al. 2016) or is intact SL binding sufficient, with hydrolysis serving mainly to deactivate the hormone (Seto et al. 2019)?

Q: What did the Alder et al. 2012 carlactone paper (PMID:22422982) assay on Arabidopsis D14 that underlies the UniProt IMP annotation to strigolactone biosynthetic process (GO:1901601), and should that result instead be captured as a response/signalling term (e.g. response to strigolactone), given that d14 mutants are SL-insensitive rather than SL-deficient?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Knock-in catalytic-triad mutants (S97A, H247A, D218A) at the endogenous AtD14 locus, combined with quantitative SMXL7 degradation reporters and BRC1 expression, to separate binding-driven from hydrolysis-driven receptor activation.

Type: genetic complementation with biochemical readout
