Gene Description

GDCST (At1g11860; synonym GDT1) encodes the T protein (aminomethyltransferase, EC 2.1.2.10) of the mitochondrial glycine cleavage system, also called the glycine decarboxylase complex, and is the only T-protein gene in Arabidopsis. The nuclear-encoded precursor carries an N-terminal mitochondrial transit peptide, and the mature GcvT-family enzyme acts in the mitochondrial matrix with the P, H and L proteins. After the P protein decarboxylates glycine and loads the aminomethyl group onto the lipoyl arm of the H protein, the T protein releases ammonia and transfers the one-carbon unit to tetrahydrofolate, forming 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate and leaving the H-protein lipoyl arm reduced. Mitochondrial serine hydroxymethyltransferase (SHM1) then uses this 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to convert a second glycine to serine. Together the two enzymes make up the mitochondrial part of the photorespiratory C2 cycle, and they also supply one-carbon units. T protein is far more abundant in leaves than the other glycine cleavage proteins. Plants with about 5% of normal T protein grow in air with only moderate glycine accumulation, but knockout plants die even in CO2-enriched air. This shows that the enzyme is essential beyond photorespiration, most likely for one-carbon metabolism.

Core Functions

Glycine cleavage system T protein (aminomethyltransferase). It takes the aminomethyl group from the lipoyl arm of the H protein, releases NH3 and transfers the methylene unit to tetrahydrofolate to form 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate. This step of mitochondrial glycine decarboxylation feeds the SHM1 reaction in photorespiration and supplies one-carbon units.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Why is GDCST knockout lethal even in CO2-enriched air when a knockdown with about 5% residual T protein grows almost normally in air? Which one-carbon sinks (for example in vascular tissue) depend on glycine cleavage-derived 5,10-methylene-THF?

Q: GDCST, GLDP1/2, GDCH and the mitochondrial LPDs lack photorespiration (GO:0009853) annotations in GOA, but SHM1 and the other C2-cycle enzymes have them. Should the GDC catalytic subunits be annotated to photorespiration consistently?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Tissue-specific complementation of the lethal gdcst knockout, using a mesophyll-specific promoter versus a vascular promoter, to separate the photorespiratory and non-photorespiratory requirements for T protein.

Biochemical function

  • T-protein is the aminomethyltransferase of the glycine cleavage system (GCS;
    glycine decarboxylase complex, GDC): it accepts the aminomethyl group carried on
    the lipoyl arm of H-protein, releases NH3, and transfers the methylene unit to
    tetrahydrofolate (THF) forming 5,10-methylene-THF
    PMID:22303256
    PMID:22303256.
  • UniProt catalytic activity: RHEA:16945 (aminomethyl-dihydrolipoyl-[protein] + THF =
    dihydrolipoyl-[protein] + 5,10-methylene-THF + NH4+); this is annotated by
    similarity (no Arabidopsis-specific kinetic data in the cache).
  • The GDC is composed of four proteins, P, T, L and H (UniProt SUBUNIT).
    In pea the complex stoichiometry was 4P:27H:9T:2L
    PMID:22303256.

Physiological role

  • GDC + mitochondrial SHMT form the mitochondrial part of the photorespiratory C2
    cycle PMID:22303256.
  • Timm et al. 2018 (abstract only in cache): T-protein is essential for
    photorespiration and one-carbon metabolism; knockout is lethal even in 1% CO2,
    whereas a knockdown with ~5% residual T-protein survives in air with moderate
    glycine accumulation; overexpression (up to 3x) has no effect on photosynthesis
    or growth; T-protein is in large excess over the other GCS proteins
    PMID:28866761
    PMID:28866761
    PMID:28866761.
  • The lethality at high CO2 indicates the non-photorespiratory (one-carbon supply)
    role of GCS is essential; this matches GLDP1/GLDP2 double mutants (see the C2
    cycle module).
  • T-protein knockdown (gldt1) has been used as a genetic background for
    photorespiration studies PMID:38518065.

Localization

  • Mitochondrion (matrix enzyme; predicted transit peptide). Repeated detection in
    mitochondrial proteomes (PMID:18385124, PMID:25293756) and IDA (PMID:28866761,
    curator-assigned; full text not cached).
  • Detections in chloroplast (PMID:18431481, PMID:20061580, PMID:12938931),
    cytosolic ribosome (PMID:15821981), apoplast (PMID:18538804) and extracellular
    region (PMID:28887381) proteomes are most consistent with carry-over of an
    abundant leaf mitochondrial protein. The chloroplast proteome authors themselves
    estimate many non-plastid contaminants
    PMID:18431481.
    The 80S ribosome study reports co-migrating non-ribosomal proteins
    PMID:15821981.

Other annotations
