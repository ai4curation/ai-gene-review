Gene Description

EIL1 (ETHYLENE INSENSITIVE3-LIKE 1, At2g27050) is a nuclear, plant-specific EIN3-family transcription factor that, together with its closest paralog EIN3, is one of the two master transcription factors of the ethylene response. EIN3/EIL proteins bind as sequence-specific DNA-binding proteins to primary ethylene response elements (EIN3-binding sites) in target promoters such as ERF1 and activate their transcription; EIL1 can complement ein3 and, when overexpressed, confers constitutive ethylene responses. In the absence of ethylene EIL1 protein is continuously degraded by the 26S proteasome via the F-box proteins EBF1 and EBF2; ethylene signalling through EIN2 inhibits EBF1/EBF2 and stabilizes EIL1 in the nucleus. EIL1 acts redundantly but differentially with EIN3 (EIL1 mainly in leaf expansion and stem elongation of adult plants) and the ein3 eil1 double mutant is almost completely ethylene-insensitive. EIL1 also integrates ethylene with jasmonate (repression by JAZ and MYC2/MYC-type proteins), iron uptake (interaction with FIT), photomorphogenic greening (PORA/PORB activation), salicylic-acid-mediated immunity (repression of SID2) and the sulfur-deficiency response.

Core Functions

Nuclear sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription activator of the EIN3 family that binds EIN3-binding sites (primary ethylene response elements) in target promoters such as ERF1 and activates ethylene-responsive transcription; stabilized by ethylene when EBF1/EBF2-mediated degradation is relieved, and acting redundantly with EIN3.
