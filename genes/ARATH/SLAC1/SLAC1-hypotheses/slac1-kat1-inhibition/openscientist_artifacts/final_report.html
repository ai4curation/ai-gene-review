SLAC1 (Q9LD83) as a Potassium Channel Inhibitor (KAT1/AKT2) — Hypothesis Evaluation

Gene: SLAC1 (Arabidopsis thaliana, UniProt Q9LD83, AT1G12480) Term under test: GO:0019870 potassium channel inhibitor activity (MF) Seed reference: Zhang et al. 2016, Plant Cell 28(4):949-955 (27002025) Hypothesis slug: slac1-kat1-inhibition

Executive Judgment

Verdict: Partially supported (weak–moderate), not independently replicated.

The seed hypothesis faithfully represents what Zhang et al. 2016 report, and that single paper provides genuinely direct evidence for the molecular activity: SLAC1 physically interacts with KAT1, KAT2 and AKT2 (Y2H, co-IP) and, in Xenopus oocyte voltage-clamp, functionally inhibits the inward-K⁺ (K⁺in) channels KAT1 and AKT2 but not KAT2, with guard-cell gain-of-function (overexpression) reducing K⁺in currents and light-induced stomatal opening. This is sufficient to qualify SLAC1 as a candidate potassium-channel inhibitor (GO:0019870).

However, the stronger clauses of the hypothesis — "independently replicated or corroborated and not contradicted by later work" — are not met:

  1. No independent functional replication. Searching the 45 PubMed "cited-by" records (2017–2026) found no independent lab reproducing SLAC1→KAT1/AKT2 patch-clamp inhibition. Citations are predominantly reviews or tangential.
  2. Database silence on the K⁺-channel interaction. IntAct curates 19 SLAC1 interactions, but all are signaling kinases/phosphatases (CPK6/21/23, PP2CA, SnRK2s/OST1) — none with KAT1, AKT2 or KAT2. The Zhang 2016 channel interaction has not been captured by interaction databases.
  3. The term is not currently annotated — this is an ADDITION. UniProt/TAIR/ QuickGO carry no potassium-channel inhibitor (GO:0019870) or regulator (GO:0015459) MF term for SLAC1; current MF terms are anion-channel activity and kinase/phosphatase binding. So the curation question is whether to add a new MF annotation on single-study evidence, not whether to retain one.
  4. Competing/contradicting in-planta directionality. Laanemets et al. 2013 (23126621) show that slac1 loss-of-function reduces guard-cell K⁺in and slows opening — the opposite of the "relief of inhibition" predicted by a direct-inhibition model — and attribute it to elevated cytosolic [Ca²⁺] and increased Ca²⁺ sensitivity of K⁺in channels. The physiological role therefore rests mainly on gain-of-function overexpression.
  5. Conceptually corroborated. The broader principle of S-type anion channels regulating K⁺ channels by protein–protein interaction is independently supported by analogous pairs (SLAH3–GORK/SKOR; ALMT12–SLAC1), lending indirect plausibility but not specific replication.

Curation bottom line: GO:0019870 is defensible as a non-core MF annotation with explicit evidence/qualifier caveats (single study, heterologous oocyte assay + overexpression), but it must not be presented as SLAC1's core function, which remains S-type anion (Cl⁻/malate/NO₃⁻) channel activity driving stomatal closure.

GO Curation Implications

  • Current state (verified this run): GO:0019870 is not currently annotated to SLAC1 in UniProt/TAIR/QuickGO. The decision is therefore whether to ADD a new MF term on single-study evidence — a higher bar than retaining an existing one.
  • Term GO:0019870 (potassium channel inhibitor activity, MF): Supportable only as a cautious, explicitly-caveated non-core lead. There is direct functional evidence (oocyte voltage-clamp inhibition of KAT1 and AKT2) plus physical interaction. Appropriate evidence code would be IDA (functional, from heterologous assay) optionally paired with IPI (physical interaction). Mandatory caveats: single study, single lab, heterologous oocyte system, gain-of-function overexpression, no independent replication, no database curation of the interaction, and a directionally opposite in-planta loss-of-function phenotype. Note it does not inhibit KAT2 despite binding it. A conservative curator could reasonably decline the addition pending independent replication.
  • Do not treat as core function. SLAC1's primary MF is S-type anion channel activity (voltage-gated anion/chloride channel; the gene drives stomatal closure). The K⁺-channel-inhibitor activity is a secondary/regulatory "moonlighting" activity. If the review is deciding whether GO:0019870 should be a prominent/core term, the answer is no — keep it, but mark non-core.
  • Alternative/parent term consideration: GO:0015459 (potassium channel regulator activity) is the more conservative parent if a curator judges "inhibitor" too strong given the single-study, heterologous basis. Given the data do show inhibition (not activation), GO:0019870 is biologically correct; the conservatism concern is about evidential strength, not direction.
  • Associated BP: negative regulation of stomatal opening / regulation of stomatal movement is reasonable but is largely a downstream/physiological outcome and is confounded by the Laanemets loss-of-function result.

Mechanistic Scope

  • Immediate molecular activity being tested: SLAC1 protein binding to Shaker inward-K⁺ channel subunits (KAT1, AKT2) and reduction of their K⁺in conductance — i.e., a channel–channel inhibitory protein–protein interaction.
  • Directly supported (in the one study): physical interaction (Y2H/co-IP) and current inhibition in oocytes.
  • Downstream / inferred, not the molecular activity itself: suppression of light-induced stomatal opening; drought/ABA physiology. These are pathway-level consequences shown mainly by overexpression and are confounded in loss-of-function by Ca²⁺-sensitivity feedback.
  • Selectivity note: binding ≠ inhibition. SLAC1 binds KAT2 in Y2H yet does not inhibit it; this dissociation argues for a specific functional, not merely steric/co-localization, effect on KAT1/AKT2 — a point in the hypothesis's favor.

Conflicts and Alternatives

  1. Directional contradiction (Laanemets 2013, 23126621). Loss of SLAC1 lowers, not raises, K⁺in. Explained by elevated cytosolic [Ca²⁺] increasing Ca²⁺-sensitivity-mediated K⁺in inhibition — an alternative mechanism that does not require direct SLAC1–KAT1 contact.
  2. Kinase-route alternative (Acharya 2013, 24033256). OST1 interacts with both SLAC1 and KAT1 and inhibits K⁺in; K⁺in suppression during closure may be dominated by kinase signaling rather than direct anion-channel binding.
  3. Autoinhibition reinterpretation (Qin 2024, 38985761). The SLAC1 N/C termini that Zhang 2016 implicate in KAT1 binding are the same cytosolic regulatory domains that mediate SLAC1 autoinhibition; constructs/overexpression effects attributed to KAT1 binding deserve scrutiny.
  4. Heterologous-system artifact. Oocyte overexpression of a membrane protein can non-specifically depress co-expressed channel currents (membrane crowding, trafficking competition); KAT2 serving as a negative control mitigates but does not eliminate this.
  5. Paralog/term carry-over risk: SLAH3 (SLAC1 homolog) inhibits different K⁺ channels (GORK/SKOR). Curators should avoid propagating GO:0019870 across the SLAC/SLAH family by homology — the specific K⁺ targets differ per paralog.

Knowledge Gaps

  • Independent functional replication (highest priority). Checked: 45 citing papers; none reproduce SLAC1→KAT1/AKT2 patch-clamp inhibition independently. Resolution: an independent guard-cell patch-clamp or oocyte study.
  • Clean loss-of-function test. Does slac1 enhance K⁺in once the Ca²⁺ feedback is clamped out (Laanemets' <10 nM [Ca²⁺] condition)? This is the decisive in-planta test of direct inhibition and has not been reported.
  • Native interaction evidence. IntAct has no SLAC1 records; no reported endogenous co-IP from guard-cell tissue (as opposed to oocyte overexpression).
  • AKT2 specificity. Full-text confirms AKT2 K⁺in inhibition (Suppl. Fig. 2), but this rests on one supplemental figure; independent confirmation absent.
  • Structural basis. No structure of a SLAC1–KAT1 complex; unknown whether binding occludes the pore/voltage sensor or alters gating/trafficking.

Discriminating Tests

  1. Ca²⁺-clamped slac1 patch clamp: measure guard-cell K⁺in in slac1 vs WT with cytosolic [Ca²⁺] buffered <10 nM. Direct-inhibition model predicts higher K⁺in in slac1; Ca²⁺-feedback model predicts rescue to WT levels.
  2. Non-conducting SLAC1 point mutants: test whether a pore-dead SLAC1 (anion transport abolished) still inhibits KAT1 — separates the inhibitor activity from anion-channel function.
  3. Interface-mutant SLAC1: map the KAT1-binding surface and test a binding-dead, transport-competent SLAC1 for loss of K⁺in inhibition while retaining closure.
  4. Endogenous proximity assay: split-luciferase/BiFC or co-IP from native guard cells (not overexpression) ± drought to show the interaction occurs at physiological levels.
  5. Quantitative stoichiometry: compare SLAC1:KAT1 abundance in guard cells to judge whether direct binding can plausibly titrate KAT1 in planta.
