Gene Description

FVE (MSI4/ACG1) is one of five Arabidopsis MSI1-like WD40-repeat proteins, a homolog of the mammalian retinoblastoma-associated histone-binding proteins RbAp46/RbAp48 (RBBP7/RBBP4) and Drosophila p55/NURF55. These seven-bladed beta-propeller proteins bind histones H3/H4 and act as shared scaffolding subunits that bring chromatin-modifying enzymes to their nucleosomal substrates. FVE is a nuclear protein that is a subunit of RPD3-type histone deacetylase complexes (with HDA6, HDA5 and the LSD1 homolog FLD) and of a CLF-containing Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2), and also associates with the CUL4-DDB1 ubiquitin ligase as a DDB1-binding WD40 protein. Through these complexes FVE promotes histone deacetylation and H3K27 trimethylation at the floral repressor FLOWERING LOCUS C (FLC), repressing FLC and promoting flowering as part of the autonomous pathway; it also mediates thermosensory (ambient temperature) regulation of flowering. FVE has additional roles in repressing cold-responsive (CBF/DREB) genes, in RNA-directed DNA methylation at a subset of loci (via SUVH9 and Pol V association), in DNA repair after UV-B, and in systemic acquired resistance.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: How is FVE partitioned between its PRC2, HDAC and CUL4-DDB1 complexes, and which of these is limiting for FLC repression versus thermosensory flowering and cold-gene repression?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Use separation-of-function FVE variants (histone-binding pocket mutants; DDB1-binding WDxR mutants) in fve backgrounds and profile FLC H3K27me3, H3 acetylation and flowering time.

Hypothesis: Histone binding by FVE is required for both PRC2 and HDAC function at FLC, whereas DDB1 binding is specific to the CUL4-dependent arm.
