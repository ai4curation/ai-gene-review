Gene Description

GLASSY HAIR 3 (GLH3; At5g55720, also called PLL14) is a secreted member of the polysaccharide lyase 1 (PL1) pectate lyase family. It is needed to form papillae, the small raised cell-wall protrusions on the surface of Arabidopsis leaf trichomes that make the trichomes reflect light. The 392-residue precursor has an N-terminal signal peptide and a right-handed beta-helix pectate lyase domain. It keeps the catalytic arginine and the Ca2+-binding aspartates of characterized PL1 enzymes, so it is predicted to cleave de-esterified homogalacturonan (pectate) by beta-elimination. In leaves GLH3 is expressed only in trichomes, before papillae appear. Its expression is switched on by the Mediator complex through the tail subunits MED25 (GLH1) and MED16 (GLH2). The protein is secreted through the canonical pathway into the apoplast, where a GFP fusion accumulates in the papillae. glh3 mutants lack or have fewer papillae and so have glassy (transparent) trichomes. Trichome cellulose is unaffected, so the defect is limited to papillae. These data support a model in which focal, GLH3-mediated pectin breakdown in the trichome wall reshapes the cell surface into papillae. Pectate lyase activity of GLH3 itself has not been shown in the cached literature.

Core Functions

Secreted PL1 family pectate lyase, expressed in trichomes downstream of the Mediator subunits MED25 and MED16, that accumulates in papillae of the leaf trichome cell wall. There it is proposed to cleave de-esterified homogalacturonan locally, remodelling the wall to form the papillae that make trichomes reflect light.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does recombinant GLH3 have pectate lyase activity in vitro, and what are its substrate preferences (degree of homogalacturonan methylesterification), pH optimum and Ca2+ dependence?

Q: How does local pectin breakdown produce raised wall protrusions rather than wall thinning? For example, does it release turgor-driven local expansion, or change how other wall polymers are deposited?

Q: Is the extra trichome branching reported for glh3 in the original screen caused by the GLH3 lesion itself, or by a linked mutation?

Q: Is the antagonism with the pectin methylesterase QRT1 explained by QRT1 de-esterifying more homogalacturonan, which should create more pectate lyase substrate, or by Ca2+ crosslinking of de-esterified pectin making it resistant to GLH3?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express GLH3 and a catalytic-arginine mutant (R269A) in a heterologous host. Measure eliminative cleavage of polygalacturonate (A235 for unsaturated products) at a range of pH and Ca2+ levels. Test whether the R269A variant, expressed from the GLH3 promoter, rescues papillae in glh3.

Hypothesis: GLH3 is a catalytically active pectate lyase, and its activity is needed for papilla formation.

Type: enzyme assay and genetic complementation

Experiment: Immunolabel trichome walls of wild type and glh3 with homogalacturonan epitope antibodies (LM19 for low-esterified, LM20 for high-esterified, 2F4 for Ca2+-crosslinked HG) over the time course of papilla formation.

Hypothesis: Pectin in the papillae is selectively depolymerized during papilla development.

Type: immunohistochemistry / cell wall imaging

GLH3 (Q9FM66) active-site conservation

Question

Does GLH3 keep the catalytic arginine and the Ca2+-binding aspartates of PL1
pectate lyases whose activity has been measured? The answer bears on the
pectate lyase activity annotations (IBA, IEA), because no enzyme assay on GLH3
is reported in the cached literature.

Method

active_site_conservation.py downloads the UniProt entries for GLH3 (Q9FM66)
and the two Aspergillus nidulans pectate lyases that seed the PAINT node behind
GLH3's IBA (PTN001271105): plyB (Q00645) and plyA (Q5AVN4). Both references have
kinetics and pH optima in UniProt, with ECO:0000269 evidence from PubMed:16844780.
Each reference is globally aligned to GLH3 (Biopython PairwiseAligner, BLOSUM62,
gap open -10, extend -0.5). For every ACT_SITE and BINDING feature in the
reference entry, the script reports the aligned GLH3 residue. Feature positions
are read from UniProt at run time, not hardcoded.

Run: uv run python active_site_conservation.py (output in output.txt).

Results

Reference Identity Active-site Arg Ca2+ ligands
Q00645 plyB 35.0% over 306 aligned residues R219 -> GLH3 R269 D133/D162/D166 -> GLH3 D191/D213/D217
Q5AVN4 plyA 35.5% over 296 aligned residues R226 -> GLH3 R269 D140/D169/D173 -> GLH3 D191/D213/D217

All four functional residues are conserved, and they align to the same GLH3
positions against both references.

UniProt lists the first Ca2+ ligand of GLH3 as D189, not D191. GLH3 has the motif
S-D188/189-G-D191-G (residues 188-192 read SDGDG), so 189 and 191 are both Asp.
The two-residue difference is just where the aligner places the gap within this
motif; it does not affect the conclusion.

Interpretation and limits

  • GLH3 keeps the catalytic arginine and all three Ca2+-binding aspartates of
    characterized PL1 enzymes. This is consistent with a catalytically competent
    pectate lyase and gives no sign of a pseudoenzyme.
  • Residue conservation is necessary but not sufficient for activity. Substrate
    preference, pH optimum and real in-vivo activity of GLH3 are untested here.
  • Only two references are used: the PAINT seeds. A plant PL1 enzyme with measured
    activity would be a closer comparator.
