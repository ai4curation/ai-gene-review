Gene Description

IMDH1 (IPMDH1, AtIMD1, MAM-D1; At5g14200) is a chloroplast-stromal, NAD+- and Mg2+/Mn2+-dependent 3-isopropylmalate dehydrogenase of the isocitrate/isopropylmalate dehydrogenase family. It catalyzes the oxidative decarboxylation of 3-alkylmalates to 2-oxo acids, the third step of each chain-elongation cycle. Among the three Arabidopsis IPMDHs, IMDH1 is specialized for the methionine chain-elongation pathway of aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis: it acts on 3-(2'-methylthio)ethylmalate and longer homologues with far higher efficiency than its paralogs, a specificity conferred by an active-site phenylalanine (Phe-137) in place of the leucine found in leucine-pathway IPMDHs. Loss of IMDH1 reduces aliphatic glucosinolates with four or more side-chain carbons and also lowers free leucine, reflecting a secondary contribution to leucine biosynthesis, which is dominated by IPMDH2 and IPMDH3. IMDH1 is co-expressed with and regulated like other aliphatic glucosinolate genes (MYB28 target; induced by wounding and jasmonate), its activity is modulated by thiol-based redox regulation, and it has been reported to associate with the IPMI large subunit IIL1.

  It catalyzes the oxidative decarboxylation of 3-alkylmalates to 2-oxo acids, the third step
  of each chain-elongation cycle. Among the three Arabidopsis IPMDHs, IMDH1 is specialized
  for the methionine chain-elongation pathway of aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis: it
  acts on 3-(2'-methylthio)ethylmalate and longer homologues with far higher efficiency than
  its paralogs, a specificity conferred by an active-site phenylalanine (Phe-137) in place
  of the leucine found in leucine-pathway IPMDHs. Loss of IMDH1 reduces aliphatic glucosinolates
  with four or more side-chain carbons and also lowers free leucine, reflecting a secondary
  contribution to leucine biosynthesis, which is dominated by IPMDH2 and IPMDH3. IMDH1 is
  co-expressed with and regulated like other aliphatic glucosinolate genes (MYB28 target;
  induced by wounding and jasmonate), its activity is modulated by thiol-based redox regulation,
  and it has been reported to associate with the IPMI large subunit IIL1.
