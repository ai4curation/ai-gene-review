Gene Description

TOPLESS (TPL; WSIP1; At1g15750) is a Groucho/Tup1-type transcriptional co-repressor and the founding member of the TPL/TPR family. Its N-terminal TOPLESS domain (LisH, CTLH and CRA regions with a zinc finger) forms tetramers and binds the EAR (LxLxL/DLNxxP) repression motifs of a large set of transcriptional repressors, while two C-terminal WD40 beta-propellers mediate further interactions. TPL represses transcription by recruiting histone deacetylases such as HDA19 and by binding the Mediator middle-module subunits MED21 and MED10. In nuclear auxin signalling, Aux/IAA proteins (e.g. IAA12/BODENLOS) bind TPL through domain I and bridge it to AUXIN RESPONSE FACTORs such as ARF5/MONOPTEROS, keeping auxin-responsive genes repressed until auxin triggers Aux/IAA degradation. TPL is also recruited by NINJA to JAZ repressors in jasmonate signalling and by many EAR-motif transcription factors acting in meristem maintenance (WUS), flowering time, stress and immunity. The dominant-negative tpl-1 allele converts the embryonic shoot pole into a root pole and suppresses bdl patterning defects. TPL acts redundantly with four TPR paralogs.

  TOPLESS (TPL; WSIP1; At1g15750) is a Groucho/Tup1-type transcriptional co-repressor and

  mediate further interactions. TPL represses transcription by recruiting histone deacetylases
  such as HDA19 and by binding the Mediator middle-module subunits MED21 and MED10. In nuclear
  auxin signalling, Aux/IAA proteins (e.g. IAA12/BODENLOS) bind TPL through domain I and bridge
  it to AUXIN RESPONSE FACTORs such as ARF5/MONOPTEROS, keeping auxin-responsive genes repressed
  until auxin triggers Aux/IAA degradation. TPL is also recruited by NINJA to JAZ repressors
  in jasmonate signalling and by many EAR-motif transcription factors acting in meristem maintenance
  (WUS), flowering time, stress and immunity. The dominant-negative tpl-1 allele converts
  the embryonic shoot pole into a root pole and suppresses bdl patterning defects. TPL acts
  redundantly with four TPR paralogs.
