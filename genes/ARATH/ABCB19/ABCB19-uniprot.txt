CC   -!- FUNCTION: Brassinosteroid exporter that, in conjunction with ABCB1,
CC       supports the accumulation of exogenous brassinosteroids (BR) in the
CC       apoplast, thus promoting BR signaling initiation involving the specific
CC       receptor BRI1 and required for plant growth and stress responses
CC       (PubMed:38513023, PubMed:38944596, PubMed:39497419). Mediates the
CC       transport of castasterone (CSA) and brassinolide (BL) across the plasma
CC       membrane (PubMed:38513023). Auxin efflux transporter that acts as a
CC       negative regulator of light signaling to promote hypocotyl elongation
CC       by mediating leaf tip to petiole auxin flux (PubMed:32855213,
CC       PubMed:38513023). Required for the regulation of leaf position and
CC       morphology during PHOT1-mediated blue light responses involving auxin
CC       distribution, especially in low light fluence (PubMed:32855213,
CC       PubMed:35528937). Together with ABCB1 and in a FKBP42/TWD1-dependent
CC       manner, supports seed development by promoting stamen elongation and,
CC       to a lesser extent, anther dehiscence and pollen maturation, probably
CC       as auxin transporters (PubMed:35512423, PubMed:35528937). Contributes
CC       to the connective auxin transport (CAT) that ensures communication
CC       across the shoot system, including auxin loading at axillary bud apices
CC       to influence strigolactone-mediated bud outgrowth responses and shoot
CC       branching control (PubMed:30865619). Mediates the accumulation of
CC       chlorophyll and anthocyanin, as well as the expression of genes in
CC       response to light. Participates in auxin efflux and thus regulates the
CC       polar auxin basipetal transport (from auxin-producing leaves to auxin-
CC       sensitive tissues, and from root tips to root elongating zone).
CC       Involved in diverse auxin-mediated responses including gravitropism,
CC       phototropism and lateral root formation. Required for the regulation of
CC       organ bending, such as gravitropic root bending (PubMed:39777035).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11701880, ECO:0000269|PubMed:14517332,

CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Ubiquitous, mostly in shoot meristems
CC       (PubMed:11701880). Present in the majority of stem cells, predominantly
CC       in a non-polar manner (PubMed:30865619). Accumulates in seedlings roots
CC       and hypocotyls, and in roots apices and inflorescences
CC       (PubMed:35528937). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11701880,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:30865619, ECO:0000269|PubMed:35528937}.
CC   -!- DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE: In seedlings, confined to hypocotyls in darkness,
CC       but expressed in all tissues except in hypocotyls in light
CC       (PubMed:11701880). In flowers, present in all organs except petals
CC       (PubMed:11701880). During stamen development, accumulates strongly in
CC       the stamen filament in both epidermal and provascular cells, especially
CC       at the tip of the stamen filament (at protein level) (PubMed:35512423).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11701880, ECO:0000269|PubMed:35512423}.
CC   -!- INDUCTION: By auxin (IAA). Induced by red light, but repressed by far-
CC       red light (PubMed:11701880, PubMed:15908594). Induced by
CC       brassinosteroids (at protein level) (PubMed:38513023).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11701880, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15908594,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:38513023}.
CC   -!- PTM: Phosphorylated by PHOT1 in phototropic seedlings, to modulates
CC       auxin export and distribution and regulates leaf and petiole curling.
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:32855213}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Reduced rosette size associated with decreased
CC       brassinosteroid signaling (PubMed:38513023). Reduced sensitivity to red
CC       and blue light-mediated leaf positioning and morphology associated with
CC       reduced auxin efflux, thus leading to constitutive upright petiole
CC       angles, and irregularly wavy and small rosette leaves
CC       (PubMed:32855213). Aberrantly enhanced organ bending, including
CC       increased gravitropic root bending (PubMed:39777035). Reduction in bulk
CC       stem auxin transport and lower levels of shoot branching as well as
CC       small increase in branch angle when combined with the disruption of
CC       MAX2 and MAX4, genes involved in strigolactone-mediated signaling
CC       (PubMed:30865619). The abcb19 pin3 pin4 pin7 quadruple mutant exhibits
CC       an additive phenotype on strigolactone-mediated bud outgrowth responses
CC       and shoot branching control (PubMed:30865619). Partial restoration of
CC       the increased shoot branching observed in the brc1 brc2 double mutant
CC       (PubMed:30865619). In addition, the triple mutant brc1 brc2 abcb19 is
CC       insensitive to strigolactone (e.g. GR24) treatment (PubMed:30865619).
CC       The abcb1 abcb19 double mutant is severely dwarfed, with excessive leaf
CC       and petiole curling, reduced internode lengths, increased stem and
CC       silique waving and skewing, and exhibits poor flower fertility due to
CC       short stamen filaments not sufficiently elongated to position the
CC       anthers above the stigma during anthesis; this phenotype is associated
CC       with lower free auxin levels in blue light and reduced length of
CC       epidermal cells in the middle of stamen (PubMed:35512423,
CC       PubMed:35528937). The double mutant abcb1 abcb19 is also deficient in
CC       brassinosteroid export and is insensitive to brassinolide and its
CC       biosynthetic precursor 22-hydroxycampesterol, resulting in decreased
CC       brassinosteroid signaling; this phenotype is restored by bikinin (BIK)
CC       treatment, which can activate brassinosteroid signaling downstream of
CC       the BRI1 receptor (PubMed:38513023, PubMed:39497419). The xif xik
CC       abcb19 triple mutant has a stronger bending phenotype than the single
CC       mutant (PubMed:39777035). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:30865619,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:32855213, ECO:0000269|PubMed:35512423,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:35528937, ECO:0000269|PubMed:38513023,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:39497419, ECO:0000269|PubMed:39777035}.
