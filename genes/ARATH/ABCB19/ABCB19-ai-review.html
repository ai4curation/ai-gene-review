Gene Description

ABCB19 (ATP-BINDING CASSETTE B19; also MDR1/MDR11, PGP19) is a full-size ABCB/MDR/P-glycoprotein-type ABC transporter, with two transmembrane domains and two nucleotide-binding domains, that resides at the plasma membrane and uses ATP hydrolysis to export small hydrophobic hormones from the cell. Its best-characterized substrate is the auxin indole-3-acetic acid. ABCB19 is largely non-polar in the plasma membrane of vascular and bundle-sheath cells, where it drives long-distance basipetal auxin transport in inflorescence stems and hypocotyls (limiting reuptake and lateral leakage from the transport stream) and rootward transport in the root central cylinder. It binds the auxin-transport inhibitor NPA, requires the FKBP42 immunophilin TWISTED DWARF1 for folding and activity, interacts with and stabilizes PIN1 in sterol-enriched membrane microdomains, and is inhibited by phosphorylation by the blue-light receptor phototropin 1. ABCB19 also exports bioactive brassinosteroids: its ATPase is strongly stimulated by brassinolide, cryo-EM structures show bound BR, and with ABCB1 it positively regulates BR signaling. Loss of ABCB19 reduces auxin transport and gives epinastic cotyledons, reduced apical dominance, altered photomorphogenesis and leaf positioning, organ-boundary fusions and reduced late stamen elongation.

  ABC transporter, with two transmembrane domains and two nucleotide-binding domains, that
  resides at the plasma membrane and uses ATP hydrolysis to export small hydrophobic hormones
  from the cell. Its best-characterized substrate is the auxin indole-3-acetic acid. ABCB19
  is largely non-polar in the plasma membrane of vascular and bundle-sheath cells, where it
  drives long-distance basipetal auxin transport in inflorescence stems and hypocotyls (limiting
  reuptake and lateral leakage from the transport stream) and rootward transport in the root
  central cylinder. It binds the auxin-transport inhibitor NPA, requires the FKBP42 immunophilin
  TWISTED DWARF1 for folding and activity, interacts with and stabilizes PIN1 in sterol-enriched
  membrane microdomains, and is inhibited by phosphorylation by the blue-light receptor phototropin
  1. ABCB19 also exports bioactive brassinosteroids: its ATPase is strongly stimulated by
  brassinolide, cryo-EM structures show bound BR, and with ABCB1 it positively regulates BR
  signaling. Loss of ABCB19 reduces auxin transport and gives epinastic cotyledons, reduced
  apical dominance, altered photomorphogenesis and leaf positioning, organ-boundary fusions
  and reduced late stamen elongation.'
