| Recombinant biochemical assay | FMOGS-OX1 was expressed in *Escherichia coli*. Bacterial spheroplasts were incubated with Arabidopsis-derived desulfo-glucosinolate substrates, products were analyzed by HPLC, and empty-vector cells served as controls. | Recombinant FMOGS-OX1 converted 4-methylthiobutyl glucosinolate (4-MTB) to 4-methylsulfinylbutyl glucosinolate (4-MSB) and 8-methylthiooctyl glucosinolate (8-MTO) to 8-methylsulfinyloctyl glucosinolate (8-MSO), supporting activity on short- and long-chain substrates. | (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000018) |
| Substrate-recognition analysis | Recombinant activity was compared across intact glucosinolates, desulfo-glucosinolates, and earlier methionine-derived pathway intermediates. | FMOGS-OX1 accepts intact and desulfo methylthioalkyl glucosinolates but not methionine or aldoxime precursors lacking the glucosinolate S-glucose moiety, indicating glucosinolate-scaffold selectivity. | (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000003) |
| Genetic mapping and natural variation | GS-OX quantitative-trait loci and transcript abundance were analyzed in Arabidopsis mapping populations, including Bay-0 × Shahdara. | FMOGS-OX1 is associated with variation in methylthio-to-methylsulfinyl glucosinolate ratios and has a significant cis-expression QTL, supporting a contribution from allelic expression differences. | (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000024) |
| Loss-of-function genetics | Glucosinolate profiles and residual S-oxygenating activity were examined in an FMOGS-OX1 T-DNA knockout background. | Methylsulfinyl glucosinolates persisted after FMOGS-OX1 disruption. This shows that FMOGS-OX1 contributes to the reaction but is functionally redundant with related GS-OX paralogs. | (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000021) |
| In-planta physiological assignment | Mutant glucosinolate profiles were compared with the recombinant catalytic capacities of related FMOGS-OX enzymes. | FMOGS-OX1 contributes to 8-MSO production in planta. Differences between in-vitro activity and mutant phenotypes indicate that physiological function depends on expression context as well as intrinsic substrate acceptance. | (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000023) |
| Phylogenetic and family analysis | Arabidopsis FMOs were compared phylogenetically with homologs from other plant genomes. | FMOGS-OX1 clusters with GS-OX paralogs in a distinct plant FMO radiation associated with glucosinolate S-oxygenation. The paralogs exhibit overlapping activity together with substrate and expression subfunctionalization, particularly within crucifer or Brassicales lineages. | (pqac-00000034, pqac-00000037) |
| Domain and motif architecture | Family-level sequence analyses identified conserved Class-B FMO motifs and cofactor-binding architecture. | The expected architecture contains FAD- and NADPH-binding Rossmann-like regions, glycine-rich dinucleotide-binding motifs, and the characteristic plant-FMO signature. These features support an FAD-dependent, NADPH-driven monooxygenase mechanism, although no experimental FMOGS-OX1 structure was identified. | (pqac-00000040, pqac-00000041) |
| Expression analysis | Public microarray data were compared across Arabidopsis organs and developmental stages for FMOGS-OX family members. | GS-OX paralogs have distinct organ- and stage-dependent expression patterns consistent with expression-based subfunctionalization. A fully resolved and experimentally validated tissue-expression atlas specifically for FMOGS-OX1 was not provided. | (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000024) |
