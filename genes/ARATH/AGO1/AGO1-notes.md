The updated annotation-reviewer rule was applied: generic protein binding is uninformative rather than over-annotation. Six such annotations were removed without rejecting the underlying associations or assigning a partner's molecular activity to AGO1.

AGO1 guide binding and catalytic activity are directly established [PMID:16081530, "AGO1 is physically associated with miRNAs, transacting siRNAs, and transgene-derived siRNAs"] [PMID:16081530, "We also show that AGO1 has Slicer activity."]. Target-mRNA binding is a direct step in this mechanism, and was changed from non-core to core. The leaf RNA interactome study explicitly identifies AGO1 [PMID:32344669]. AGO1's participation in secondary-siRNA generation was also recognized as core pathway activity, without assigning it Dicer or RDR6 activity [PMID:15469823; PMID:34192539].

The protein-binding studies establish associations with AMP1, PDS5C/E, RICE proteins, ATRM2, SDE3 and viral P0 [PMID:23622241; PMID:38446745; PMID:28463111; PMID:29941559; PMID:22940249; PMID:17869109]. These observations do not establish distinct partner-specific molecular activities for AGO1. In particular, the AMP1 study explicitly notes that co-immunoprecipitation could reflect shared membrane proximity rather than a common complex. ER association and translational repression are retained in the functional synthesis, separately from this binding claim.

The broad electronic virus-defense annotation was refined to RNAi-mediated antiviral immunity using direct AGO1 viral-siRNA loading and virus-hypersensitivity evidence [PMID:17158744; PMID:11910010]. The PVX annotation remains non-core and context-qualified: suppressor-deficient PVX susceptibility does not imply AGO1 restricts wild-type PVX like AGO2 and AGO5 [PMID:26023161]. PAMP-triggered bacterial immunity is a separate endogenous-miRNA output [PMID:20164210].

## Correction of the SGS3-granule annotation

The prior rejection of the cytoplasmic ribonucleoprotein-granule annotation relied on an SGS3-focused abstract. The published full article was retrieved from a [PDF copy](https://alpalifebio.com/uploadfiles/2024/04/20240429114501601.pdf), title and DOI checked against PMID:36640363 (10.1016/j.celrep.2022.111985). Results on printed page 10 and Figure 6B/C on printed page 11 explicitly show AGO1 recruitment into SGS3 condensates in tobacco cells and AGO1-SGS3 BiFC foci in Arabidopsis protoplasts. The paper states: "AGO1 protein was incorporated into SGS3 condensates" [PMID:36640363, Results]. The annotation was changed to ACCEPT. The cached publication remains abstract-only; no full-text excerpt was inserted manually into that generated cache.

Independent corroboration was fetched and cached as PMID:38384836. This study reports AGO1 condensates under heat stress and colocalization with siRNA-body and stress-granule components. That primary paper now supports the row with a cache-verifiable snippet. Heat-associated localization is not evidence that every condensate has enhanced silencing activity: the study reports only moderate changes in guide loading and cleavage signatures.
