Gene Description

HPR2 (At1g79870; also called HPPR2) is a cytosolic NAD(P)H-dependent hydroxypyruvate reductase of the D-isomer specific 2-hydroxyacid dehydrogenase family (GyaR subfamily). It reduces 3-hydroxypyruvate to D-glycerate, preferring NADPH over NADH, and also reduces glyoxylate to glycolate and 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate to 4-hydroxyphenyllactate with lower efficiency; its activity is strongly inhibited by oxalate. In leaves HPR2 provides a cytosolic bypass to the peroxisomal NADH-dependent hydroxypyruvate reductase HPR1 in the photorespiratory C2 cycle, converting hydroxypyruvate that leaves the peroxisome into glycerate for return to the chloroplast. hpr2 single mutants are nearly wild type, but hpr1 hpr2 double mutants are air-sensitive and recover at elevated CO2, showing that at least one of the two enzymes is needed for normal growth in air. HPR2 also contributes to formation of the tyrosine-derived metabolite 4-hydroxyphenyllactate, and binds salicylic acid in vitro.

Core Functions

Cytosolic NAD(P)H-dependent (NADPH-preferring) reduction of 3-hydroxypyruvate to D-glycerate, providing an extraperoxisomal bypass to peroxisomal HPR1 in the photorespiratory C2 carbon pathway.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Under which natural conditions (light intensity, temperature, peroxisomal NADH supply) does the cytosolic HPR2 route carry a substantial fraction of photorespiratory hydroxypyruvate flux in wild-type plants?

Q: Does salicylic acid binding alter HPR2 catalytic activity at physiological SA concentrations, and is this relevant to SA-regulated photorespiratory or redox processes?

Q: How much of the in vivo 4-hydroxyphenyllactate formation depends on HPR2 relative to HPPR3, and does this have any physiological function in Arabidopsis, which does not accumulate rosmarinic acid?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: 13C or 14C flux analysis of hydroxypyruvate reduction in wild type, hpr1 and hpr2 under high light and high temperature to quantify the share of photorespiratory flux passing through cytosolic HPR2.

Experiment: Steady-state kinetics of recombinant HPR2 in the presence of salicylic acid to test whether SA binding inhibits or activates hydroxypyruvate reduction.

Identity and family

  • Glyoxylate/hydroxypyruvate reductase A HPR2; 313 aa; D-isomer specific 2-hydroxyacid
    dehydrogenase family, GyaR subfamily (UniProt SIMILARITY, ECO:0000305; Pfam PF00389/PF02826;
    PANTHER PTHR10996:SF178).
  • Also named HPPR2 (hydroxyphenylpyruvate reductase 2) in the tyrosine-pathway literature
    PMID:30233632.

Enzymatic activity

  • Purified from hpr1 knockout leaves as the NADPH-dependent HPR activity; identified as
    At1g79870 by MS and cross-reaction with anti-spinach HPR2 serum PMID:18952776.
  • Recombinant enzyme prefers NADPH and hydroxypyruvate; NADH rates ~4x lower; glyoxylate
    also reduced PMID:18952776
    PMID:18952776.
  • Strongly inhibited by oxalate (UniProt ACTIVITY REGULATION, ECO:0000269 PubMed:18952776).
  • Also reduces 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate (pHPP) to 4-hydroxyphenyllactate (pHPL), but its highest
    catalytic efficiency is with hydroxypyruvate PMID:30233632.

Localization

  • Cytosolic. GFP-HPR2 showed no organelle targeting PMID:18952776;
    independent GFP study in protoplasts PMID:30233632.
  • A mitochondrion HDA call (membrane-fraction SEC profiling, PMID:28887381) is not supported
    by any targeting data; the same study notes extensive cytosol/membrane partitioning.

Physiological role

  • Cytosolic bypass of peroxisomal HPR1 in photorespiration PMID:18952776.
  • hpr2 single mutants nearly wild-type; hpr1 hpr2 double mutants air-sensitive and rescued
    by 1% CO2 PMID:18952776.
  • HPR1 dominant; HPR2 an overflow route PMID:22303256.
  • A third enzyme HPR3 adds further redundancy PMID:21205613.
  • Secondary role in tyrosine-derived pHPL formation PMID:30233632.

Other

  • Salicylic acid binding: HPR2 bound SA in two in vitro assays (SPR with 3AESA; SA-competable
    4AzSA crosslinking) PMID:25628632.
    No effect of SA on HPR2 activity or physiological significance was reported.

Curation decisions (summary)

  • Core MF: hydroxypyruvate reductase [NAD(P)H] activity (GO:0016618), NADPH preferred
    (GO:0120509 also accepted). Glyoxylate reductase (NADPH) and HPPR activity kept as non-core.
  • Core BP: oxidative photosynthetic carbon pathway (GO:0009854, part_of photorespiration).
  • Core CC: cytosol. Cytoplasm rows modified to cytosol; mitochondrion HDA marked over-annotated.
  • SA binding kept as non-core (in vitro only).
