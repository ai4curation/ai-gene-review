# Comprehensive Research Report: GGP1 (Gamma-Glutamyl Peptidase 1) in Arabidopsis thaliana

## Gene Identity Verification

The gene GGP1 (At4g30530, UniProt accession Q9M0A7) encodes gamma-glutamyl peptidase 1 in *Arabidopsis thaliana*. The protein belongs to the peptidase C26 family and carries the EC number 3.4.19.16 (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12). This identity matches the UniProt annotation and is consistently described across multiple authoritative studies. GGP1 should not be confused with CGEP (chloroplast glutamyl peptidase), which is an unrelated chloroplast stromal S9D serine peptidase also present in Arabidopsis (bhuiyan2020autocatalyticprocessingand pages 1-2, bhuiyan2020autocatalyticprocessingand pages 2-3).

## Primary Enzymatic Function and Catalytic Activity

### Reaction Mechanism

GGP1 catalyzes the hydrolysis of the γ-glutamyl bond in reduced glutathione (GSH) and related glutathione conjugates. The primary reaction is: GSH + H₂O → L-glutamate + L-cysteinylglycine (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 1-2). This enzymatic activity directly releases glutamate without requiring ATP, making it more energy-efficient than the alternative γ-glutamyl cyclotransferase (GGCT) pathway, which produces 5-oxoproline that must subsequently be converted to glutamate in an ATP-dependent manner (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12).

As a member of the C26 cysteine peptidase family (MEROPS classification), GGP1 employs a conserved catalytic triad consisting of Cys100, His192, and Glu194 (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12). AlphaFold-based structural modeling has identified additional substrate-interacting residues including Gln104, Gln156, Asp157, Arg206, and Tyr212, which contribute to recognition and positioning of the γ-glutamyl, glycine, and hydrophobic portions of substrates (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12).

### Substrate Specificity and Kinetic Parameters

Detailed enzyme kinetic studies using purified recombinant GGP1 revealed the following parameters for GSH hydrolysis: apparent Km = 5.0 mM, kcat = 0.33 s⁻¹, and catalytic efficiency (kcat/Km) = 66 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 3-5). The Km value is physiologically relevant given reported cytosolic GSH concentrations in plants (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 9-11).

GGP1 exhibits notable substrate selectivity. It shows robust activity toward reduced GSH but little or no detectable activity toward oxidized glutathione (GSSG), γ-glutamylcysteine (γ-Glu-Cys), or γ-glutamylalanine (γ-Glu-Ala) (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 1-2). Beyond free GSH, GGP1 processes at least nine structurally diverse glucosinolate-related GSH conjugates bearing methylsulfinylalkyl, methylthioalkyl, and indol-3-ylmethyl side chains (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 5-7, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 8-10). The enzyme also accepts GS-IAN (S-[(indol-3-yl)acetonitrile]-L-glutathione), a GSH conjugate intermediate in camalexin biosynthesis (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 5-7, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 1-2).

Importantly, GGP1 shows substantially higher affinity for GSH conjugates than for free GSH. For example, the reported Km for the model glucosinolate conjugate GS-B is approximately 37–8 μM, compared to 5.0 mM for GSH (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12). This differential affinity suggests that conjugate processing may take precedence when conjugates accumulate, while GSH degradation becomes more prominent under conditions when conjugate levels are low (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 12-13).

## Subcellular Localization

GGP1 is a cytosolic enzyme. Multiple lines of experimental evidence support this localization:

1. **Fluorescent protein fusion studies**: C-terminal GGP1-YFP fusions transiently expressed in *Nicotiana benthamiana* epidermal cells and mesophyll protoplasts showed cytosolic fluorescence patterns when examined by confocal laser scanning microscopy (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 4-5, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 2-4).

2. **Biochemical fractionation**: Recombinant GGP1 expressed in *Escherichia coli* was recovered from the soluble fraction, consistent with cytosolic rather than membrane-associated localization (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3).

3. **Functional compatibility**: The cytosolic localization aligns with the known compartmentation of related pathway enzymes. The cytochrome P450 enzymes involved in glucosinolate biosynthesis have their catalytic domains facing the cytosol, and the glutathione S-transferase GSTF6, which generates GSH conjugates, is predicted to be cytosolic (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 8-10).

GGP1 functions inside the cell within the cytosolic compartment, where it has access to both cytosolic GSH and the GSH conjugates generated during specialized metabolite biosynthesis (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 1-2).

### Tissue and Cellular Expression Patterns

Quantitative RT-PCR expression profiling revealed that GGP1 transcripts are present in all examined organs but are particularly abundant in mature rosette leaves (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 3-5, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 9-11). Promoter-GUS reporter experiments using approximately 2 kb of the GGP1 promoter region driving GUS expression showed that GGP1 is expressed in or near vascular tissues in Arabidopsis leaves (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 4-5). This spatial expression pattern matches the sites where glucosinolate biosynthetic enzymes are predominantly active (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 9-11).

## Biological Processes and Biochemical Pathways

GGP1 plays dual roles in plant sulfur metabolism, participating in both primary and secondary metabolic pathways (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 1-2).

### Primary Sulfur Metabolism: Glutathione Degradation

GGP1 serves as a major enzyme responsible for degrading cytosolic GSH, particularly under sulfur-sufficient or normal growth conditions (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 9-11, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12). This function was definitively established through multiple experimental approaches:

- **Yeast complementation**: Arabidopsis GGP1 rescued *Saccharomyces cerevisiae dug2Δ* and *dug3Δ* mutants, which are defective in GSH utilization, enabling growth when GSH was the sole sulfur source (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 3-5, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3).

- **Mutant phenotypes**: Independent *ggp1* knockout lines consistently accumulated significantly more GSH than wild-type plants under normal growth conditions, demonstrating impaired GSH degradation (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 3-5, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 1-2). The elevated GSH was observed regardless of nutritional conditions, indicating that GGP1 constitutively contributes to GSH turnover (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 9-11).

GSH degradation by GGP1 releases glutamate, cysteine (via Cys-Gly), and glycine, supporting amino acid homeostasis and enabling sulfur reallocation within the plant (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 9-11, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 6-7). Under sulfur deficiency, the related enzyme GGCT2;1 is strongly induced and accelerates GSH degradation, supplementing or partially replacing GGP1 activity (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12). However, GGP1 appears to provide the principal, constitutive route for cytosolic GSH breakdown under ordinary conditions (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 9-11).

### Secondary Sulfur Metabolism: Glucosinolate Biosynthesis

GGP1 plays an essential role in glucosinolate biosynthesis by processing GSH conjugates formed during the pathway. Glucosinolates are sulfur-containing defense compounds characteristic of the Brassicales order that are important for plant defense against herbivores and pathogens (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 5-7, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 1-2).

The biochemical evidence for GGP1's role includes:

- **In vitro substrate processing**: Purified recombinant GGP1 hydrolyzed nine of ten glucosinolate-related GSH conjugates detected in mutant plants, converting them to corresponding Cys-Gly conjugates (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 5-7, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 8-10).

- **Mutant accumulation of intermediates**: The *ggp1-1 ggp3-1* double mutant accumulated up to ten glucosinolate-related GSH conjugates that are normally processed, demonstrating blockage at the γ-glutamyl cleavage step (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 5-7, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 7-8).

- **Altered glucosinolate profiles**: Loss of GGP1 and GGP3 function resulted in reduced levels of long-chain methionine-derived glucosinolates, particularly 6-methylsulfinylhexyl (6MSH), 7-methylsulfinylheptyl (7MSH), and 8-methylsulfinyloctyl (8MSO) glucosinolates (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 7-8, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 6-7). Corresponding isothiocyanate breakdown products, including sulforaphane-related compounds, were also reduced in *ggp1* mutants (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 7-8).

These findings established that GSH (rather than free cysteine) serves as the sulfur donor in glucosinolate biosynthesis, with GGP1 removing the γ-glutamyl group from conjugated intermediates to enable progression toward the mature glucosinolate structure (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 1-2, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 2-4).

### Camalexin Biosynthesis

GGP1 also participates in the biosynthesis of camalexin, an indolic phytoalexin produced in response to pathogen challenge. The enzyme processes GS-IAN (the GSH conjugate of indole-3-acetonitrile), yielding Cys-Gly-IAN and supporting camalexin accumulation (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 5-7, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 1-2). The *ggp1-1* single mutant showed reduced camalexin production, and the *ggp1-1 ggp3-1* double mutant exhibited an even stronger reduction, with high accumulation of GS-IAN upon camalexin induction (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 8-10, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 7-8).

### Connection to Sulfur-Starvation Signaling

*ggp1* mutants accumulated higher levels of O-acetyl-L-serine (OAS), a signaling molecule associated with sulfur starvation responses, particularly under sulfur-deficient conditions (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 7-8, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 1-2). This suggests that impaired GGP1 function affects sulfur-status perception or signaling, potentially because reduced GSH turnover limits sulfur reallocation and triggers compensatory responses (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 8-9).

## Experimental Evidence

The functional characterization of GGP1 is supported by multiple complementary experimental approaches:

### Genetic Evidence

- **T-DNA insertion mutants**: Independent knockout lines (*ggp1-1*, *ggp1-2*) were generated and characterized, with *ggp1-1* retaining only 0.7–1.0% of wild-type GGP1 transcript levels (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 3-5, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 2-4).

- **Artificial microRNA knockdown**: To overcome functional redundancy with GGP3 (which is genetically linked to GGP1), researchers created a *ggp1-1 ggp3-1* double mutant using amiRNA-mediated GGP3 knockdown, resulting in plants with approximately 1–4% of wild-type GGP1 and 6–30% of wild-type GGP3 transcripts (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 4-5).

- **Heterologous complementation**: Expression of Arabidopsis GGP1 in yeast GSH-utilization mutants fully rescued their growth defect, demonstrating that GGP1 alone is sufficient to support GSH degradation (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 3-5, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3).

### Biochemical Evidence

- **Enzyme kinetics**: Detailed Michaelis-Menten kinetics were determined using purified recombinant protein expressed in *E. coli* (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 3-5, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3).

- **Substrate specificity profiling**: A panel of γ-glutamyl compounds and GSH derivatives was tested to define the enzyme's substrate selectivity (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 2-3, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 5-7).

### Metabolomic Evidence

- **Targeted and untargeted metabolomics**: Widely targeted metabolomics and specific analyses of glucosinolates, isothiocyanates, GSH, OAS, and related compounds were performed on wild-type and mutant plants under multiple nutrient conditions (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 7-8, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 6-7, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 8-9).

- **GSH conjugate profiling**: Accumulated GSH conjugates in the double mutant were identified and quantified, with structures confirmed using synthetic standards where available (geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 5-7, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 7-8).

### Structural and Bioinformatic Evidence

- **Structural modeling**: AlphaFold prediction of GGP1 structure identified the C26 peptidase fold and catalytic triad (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 12-13).

- **Substrate docking**: Modeling of the glucosinolate conjugate GS-B into the predicted GGP1 active site identified key substrate-binding residues (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12).

- **Coexpression analysis**: Transcriptome-wide coexpression analyses linked GGP1 with sulfur-assimilation genes (APS1, APR1, APK1/2, APS3, SERAT2;2) and glucosinolate biosynthesis genes, supporting its role in coordinating sulfur allocation between primary and secondary metabolism (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 12-13).

## Current Understanding and Implications

The comprehensive characterization of GGP1 reveals it as a bifunctional enzyme operating at the interface of primary and secondary sulfur metabolism in Arabidopsis. Its dual substrate specificity—accepting both free GSH and specialized GSH conjugates—enables it to balance glutathione homeostasis with the production of defensive glucosinolates and phytoalexins (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 1-2).

The higher affinity of GGP1 for GSH conjugates compared to free GSH (Km ~37–8 μM vs. 5.0 mM) suggests a substrate-prioritization mechanism: when glucosinolate or camalexin biosynthesis is active and conjugates accumulate, GGP1 preferentially processes these pathway intermediates; when conjugate levels are low, it contributes to basal GSH turnover (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 12-13). This biochemical strategy may allow plants to coordinate sulfur allocation in response to developmental and environmental cues.

The energy efficiency of the GGP-mediated pathway compared to the GGCT pathway (direct glutamate production versus ATP-dependent conversion from 5-oxoproline) may explain why GGP1 provides the predominant route for GSH degradation in mature photosynthetic tissues where energy conservation is advantageous (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12). The functional redundancy with GGP3 and the nutrient-responsive induction of GGCT2;1 provide additional layers of regulation, ensuring robust GSH turnover across varying conditions (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 9-11, geuflores2011cytosolicγglutamylpeptidases pages 10-11).

Recent structural predictions and substrate-docking studies are beginning to reveal the molecular basis for GGP1's substrate recognition, although experimental crystal structures have not yet been reported. Understanding the structural determinants of substrate selectivity could inform efforts to engineer glucosinolate production in non-Brassicaceae species or to modulate plant sulfur metabolism for agricultural applications (ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 11-12, ito2022glutathionedegradationactivity pages 12-13).

## Conclusion

GGP1 (At4g30530, EC 3.4.19.16) is a cytosolic γ-glutamyl peptidase belonging to the C26 cysteine peptidase family that catalyzes hydrolysis of the γ-glutamyl bond in reduced glutathione and related glutathione conjugates. It functions in both primary sulfur metabolism (constitutive GSH degradation and amino acid recycling) and secondary sulfur metabolism (processing of GSH conjugates in glucosinolate and camalexin biosynthesis). The enzyme is localized to the cytosol, with particularly high expression in vascular tissues of leaves. Extensive experimental evidence from yeast complementation, enzyme kinetics, knockout mutant analyses, localization studies, and metabolomics supports these functional assignments. GGP1 exemplifies how a single enzyme with dual substrate specificity can integrate primary metabolic homeostasis with inducible defense responses, coordinating sulfur allocation according to physiological and environmental demands.
