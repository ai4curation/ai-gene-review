| Basic protein information | **Gene:** *GGP1*; **ordered locus:** *At4g30530*; **organism:** *Arabidopsis thaliana*; **UniProt:** Q9M0A7; **recommended name:** γ-glutamyl peptidase 1; **EC:** 3.4.19.16; **family:** cysteine peptidase C26; **annotated domains/folds:** ChyE-like and class-I glutamine-amidotransferase-like/GATase. These identifiers agree with the experimentally studied cytosolic GGP1. It must not be confused with CGEP, an unrelated chloroplast S9D serine peptidase. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000024) |
| Enzymatic function and catalytic mechanism | GGP1 hydrolyzes the **γ-glutamyl bond** of reduced glutathione: **GSH + H₂O → L-glutamate + L-cysteinylglycine**. It likewise removes the γ-glutamyl group from glutathione conjugates, producing corresponding Cys-Gly conjugates. As a C26 cysteine peptidase, it is predicted to use the conserved **Cys100–His192–Glu194** catalytic triad. Direct glutamate production avoids the ATP-dependent conversion of 5-oxoproline required by the alternative γ-glutamyl-cyclotransferase pathway. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000024) |
| Substrate specificity and kinetics | Purified recombinant GGP1 degraded reduced GSH with **apparent Kₘ = 5.0 mM**, **kcat = 0.33 s⁻¹**, and **catalytic efficiency = 66 M⁻¹ s⁻¹**. Little or no activity was detected toward oxidized glutathione, γ-glutamylcysteine, or γ-glutamylalanine. GGP1 also accepts at least nine structurally diverse glucosinolate-related GSH conjugates and the camalexin intermediate GS-IAN. Its reported affinity for the model conjugate GS-B is substantially higher than for free GSH. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000026, pqac-00000036) |
| Subcellular localization | **Cytosolic.** C-terminal GGP1–YFP expressed transiently in *Nicotiana benthamiana* epidermal cells and mesophyll protoplasts produced a cytosolic fluorescence pattern. This localization is consistent with access to cytosolic GSH and the cytosol-facing steps of glucosinolate and camalexin synthesis. There is no evidence that Q9M0A7 is secreted or functions in the chloroplast, vacuole, or apoplast. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000012, pqac-00000030) |
| Tissue and cellular expression | *GGP1* transcripts occur in all examined organs but are especially abundant in mature rosette leaves. Promoter–GUS experiments showed expression associated with leaf vascular tissue, spatially matching major sites of glucosinolate-pathway activity. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000030) |
| Primary sulfur metabolism | GGP1 is proposed to mediate a major component of **basal cytosolic GSH turnover**, releasing glutamate and Cys-Gly for subsequent amino-acid recovery. Its kinetic properties are compatible with cytosolic GSH concentrations. Under sulfur deficiency, induced GGCT2;1 supplements this pathway, whereas GGP1 appears particularly important under sulfur-sufficient or ordinary growth conditions. (pqac-00000015, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000023) |
| Specialized-metabolite pathways | In **glucosinolate biosynthesis**, GGP1 removes glutamate from pathway-specific GSH conjugates, supporting the conclusion that GSH supplies reduced sulfur and enabling progression toward thiohydroximate intermediates. In **camalexin biosynthesis**, it converts GS-IAN to Cys-Gly-IAN. GGP3 provides overlapping activity, explaining why strong defects are clearest when both enzymes are impaired. (pqac-00000036, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000042) |
| Key experimental evidence | Evidence includes rescue of GSH-utilization-defective yeast *dug2Δ* and *dug3Δ* mutants by Arabidopsis GGP1; direct kinetic and specificity assays with purified recombinant enzyme; GGP1–YFP localization; promoter–GUS expression mapping; metabolomics of single and combined mutants; and structural modeling that places GSH conjugates beside the conserved C26 catalytic triad and implicates Gln104, Gln156, Asp157, Arg206, and Tyr212 in substrate recognition. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000024, pqac-00000028, pqac-00000030) |
| Mutant phenotypes | Independent *ggp1* knockout or strong-loss lines accumulate significantly more GSH than wild type under normal and altered nutrient conditions, supporting defective degradation rather than enhanced synthesis. They also show increased O-acetyl-L-serine under sulfur deficiency and reduced levels of several glucosinolates and isothiocyanate products. Combined impairment of *GGP1* and *GGP3* causes accumulation of up to ten pathway-related GSH conjugates, reduced long-chain methionine-derived glucosinolates, GS-IAN accumulation, and impaired induced camalexin production. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000022, pqac-00000025, pqac-00000038) |
| Functional interpretation | The best-supported annotation is a **cytosolic C26 γ-glutamyl peptidase with dual roles**: degradation of reduced GSH in primary sulfur metabolism and processing of specialized GSH conjugates in glucosinolate and camalexin biosynthesis. GGP3 and GGCT enzymes provide partial redundancy, so phenotypes depend on tissue, nutrient status, and substrate availability. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000043) |


*Table: Key biochemical, localization, pathway, structural, and genetic evidence for Arabidopsis thaliana GGP1 (At4g30530; UniProt Q9M0A7). The table distinguishes this cytosolic C26 cysteine peptidase from the unrelated chloroplast S9D glutamyl peptidase CGEP.*
