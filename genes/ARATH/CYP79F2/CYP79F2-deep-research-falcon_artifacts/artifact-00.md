|---|---|---|
| Gene / protein identity | **Gene:** *CYP79F2*; **locus:** *At1g16400*; **UniProt:** Q9FUY7; **protein:** cytochrome P450 79F2 / hexahomomethionine N-hydroxylase | Literature identifies Arabidopsis CYP79F2 as a CYP79-family enzyme for long-chain methionine-derived glucosinolate biosynthesis (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002) |
| Enzyme classification | **EC 1.14.14.42**; microsomal cytochrome P450 monooxygenase/N-hydroxylase in the CYP79 family | CYP79 enzymes catalyze amino-acid-to-aldoxime conversion at the entry to glucosinolate core biosynthesis (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003) |
| Organism | *Arabidopsis thaliana* (mouse-ear cress) | The experimentally characterized locus is *A. thaliana* **AT1G16400** (pqac-00000002) |
| Protein family and domains | Cytochrome P450 family; expected P450 heme-binding catalytic fold and N-terminal membrane anchor, consistent with Cyt_P450/p450 domain annotations | CYP79F2 is a close CYP79-family paralog of CYP79F1 and retains conserved cytochrome P450 features (pqac-00000039, pqac-00000041) |
| Subcellular localization | **Endoplasmic-reticulum membrane.** The first 62 amino acids of CYP79F2 directed GFP to the ER in tobacco BY-2 cells. The catalytic domain is expected to face the cytosol, as is typical for microsomal plant P450s; the topology beyond ER targeting is therefore partly family-based inference | Direct N-terminal GFP targeting experiment supports ER localization; general P450 topology places catalysis on the cytosolic ER face (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000012) |
| Primary biochemical function | Catalyzes the first committed core-pathway step for long-chain methionine-derived glucosinolates: a chain-elongated amino acid is converted through sequential P450-mediated N-hydroxylation/dehydration/decarboxylation chemistry into the corresponding aldoxime | Heterologous expression demonstrated conversion of long-chain methionine homologs to aldoximes (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000041) |
| Overall reaction | **Pentahomomethionine or hexahomomethionine + O₂ + reducing equivalents → corresponding long-chain methylthioalkyl aldoxime + CO₂ + H₂O**, with electrons normally supplied through an NADPH–P450 reductase system | Product class and CYP79 reaction placement are experimentally established; reductase coupling follows microsomal P450 biochemistry (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012) |
| Specific substrates and kinetics | **5-Homomethionine (pentahomomethionine): Kₘ ≈ 374 µM**; **6-homomethionine (hexahomomethionine): Kₘ ≈ 26 µM**. The approximately 14-fold lower reported Kₘ for 6-homomethionine indicates greater apparent affinity under the assay conditions | Kinetic values summarized from the biochemical characterization literature (pqac-00000002) |
| Immediate products | Corresponding **(E)- and (Z)-aldoximes** of pentahomomethionine and hexahomomethionine; these are intermediates rather than mature glucosinolates | Heterologous assays and pathway reviews identify the corresponding aldoximes as products (pqac-00000017, pqac-00000041) |
| Substrate specificity relative to CYP79F1 | CYP79F2 is specialized for the longest tested methionine homologs—penta- and hexahomomethionine. CYP79F1 is broader, accepting homo- through hexahomomethionine. The enzymes overlap on the two longest substrates and are therefore partly redundant | Comparative biochemical evidence and reviews consistently distinguish narrow CYP79F2 specificity from broad CYP79F1 specificity (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000041) |
| Upstream pathway context | Methionine first enters an iterative side-chain elongation cycle: **BCAT4** forms a 2-oxo acid; **MAM1/MAM3**, **IPMI**, and **IPMDH** extend it by one methylene unit per cycle in plastids; **BCAT3** retransaminates the elongated 2-oxo acid to the amino acid used by CYP79F2 | Current pathway reconstruction places CYP79F2 after methionine side-chain elongation and retransamination (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000016) |
| Downstream pathway context | CYP79F2-derived aldoxime → **CYP83A1** oxidation → glutathione conjugation by GSTs such as **GSTF11/GSTU20** → processing by **GGP1** and **SUR1** → glucosylation by **UGT74C1** → sulfation by **SOT17/SOT18** → mature long-chain aliphatic glucosinolate; later enzymes may modify the side chain | Biochemical and pathway-engineering studies establish this core-pathway order (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000021) |
| Tissue and developmental expression | Transcripts occur in roots, rosette and stem leaves, stems, and siliques, but were not detected in flowers in the foundational RT-PCR study. Promoter-GUS activity is vascular-associated in hypocotyls, leaves, stems, silique stems, mature siliques, and almost the entire root except its tip; root expression begins by approximately three days after germination and is stronger/broader than CYP79F1 expression | RT-PCR and promoter-GUS experiments provide tissue-level evidence (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000024) |
| Transcriptional regulation | The aliphatic-glucosinolate R2R3-MYBs **MYB28/HAG1**, **MYB29/HAG3**, and **MYB76/HAG2** regulate the pathway. CYP79F2 was strongly transactivated by MYB28 and MYB29 and more weakly by MYB76; MYB28 is the dominant regulator of both short- and long-chain aliphatic glucosinolate production | Transactivation and genetic-regulatory evidence support the MYB hierarchy (pqac-00000025, pqac-00000028) |
| Biological role | Controls metabolic entry of long-chain methionine homologs into aliphatic glucosinolate biosynthesis. The resulting glucosinolates contribute to the glucosinolate–myrosinase chemical-defense system, whose hydrolysis products include isothiocyanates and nitriles active against herbivores and other attackers; CYP79F2 is a biosynthetic contributor, not the toxic effector itself | Defense role follows from CYP79F2 pathway position and established glucosinolate breakdown chemistry (pqac-00000035, pqac-00000036) |
| Perturbation evidence and caveat | Strong CYP79F1/F2 co-suppression nearly eliminates aliphatic glucosinolates and can produce curled leaves, bushy architecture, reduced fertility, and altered indole-glucosinolate/phenylpropanoid metabolism. These pleiotropic effects cannot be assigned uniquely to CYP79F2 because most experiments perturb both highly similar genes | Recent metabolic work and earlier omics studies emphasize combined CYP79F1/F2 effects rather than a clean CYP79F2-only phenotype (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032) |
| Closest and pathway-related genes | **CYP79F1:** closest paralog, approximately 88–89% amino-acid identity and broader substrate range; **CYP83A1:** downstream aliphatic-aldoxime oxidase; **CYP79A2/B2/B3/C1/C2:** other Arabidopsis CYP79 enzymes with different amino-acid preferences; **MYB28/MYB29/MYB76:** principal pathway regulators | Sequence, substrate-range, and pathway relationships are supported by comparative characterization (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000020, pqac-00000039) |
