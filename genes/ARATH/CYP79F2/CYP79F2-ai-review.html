Gene Description

CYP79F2 (hexahomomethionine N-hydroxylase) is an endoplasmic reticulum membrane-anchored cytochrome P450 monooxygenase that catalyzes the aldoxime-forming entry step of core structure formation for long-chain methionine-derived (aliphatic) glucosinolates. Using O2 and electrons from NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase it converts the long-chain methionine homologues penta- and hexahomomethionine to 8-methylthiooctanaldoxime and 9-methylthiononanaldoxime (EC 1.14.14.42). It arose by tandem duplication with CYP79F1 (about 89% amino-acid identity) but, unlike CYP79F1, does not accept short-chain homologues, so it cannot substitute for CYP79F1 in short-chain glucosinolate synthesis. CYP79F2 is expressed mainly in hypocotyl and root vasculature and is induced by methyl jasmonate. Loss of CYP79F2 substantially reduces long-chain aliphatic glucosinolates without affecting short-chain ones, and cyp79f1 cyp79f2 double mutants lack aliphatic glucosinolates entirely; the mild lateral-root phenotype of cyp79f2 mutants is regarded as a secondary consequence of the metabolic block.

Core Functions

CYP79F2 is an ER-membrane cytochrome P450 homomethionine N-monooxygenase specific for long-chain methionine homologues (penta- and hexahomomethionine), converting them to the corresponding omega-(methylthio)alkanaldoximes as the entry step of long-chain aliphatic glucosinolate core structure biosynthesis, mainly in hypocotyl and roots.
