Gene Description

HSK, also called DMR1, is a chloroplast homoserine kinase that uses Mg-ATP to generate O-phosphohomoserine. This metabolite supplies both threonine synthase and the cystathionine gamma-synthase branch toward methionine. HSK activity and homoserine availability influence amino-acid metabolism; reduced-function alleles accumulate homoserine and alter susceptibility to downy mildew and certain fungal pathogens in tissue- and allele-dependent ways.

HSK annotation review notes

PMID10562426 demonstrates recombinant L-homoserine-dependent ADP formation with Mg-ATP and distinguishes the plant branch to both Met and Thr. PMID15703056 native overexpression/substrate-feeding experiments show homoserine supply, rather than HSK abundance, limits flux under the tested conditions; this is not universal absence of flux control. PMID19622802 full text identifies HSK as At2g17265, verifies several mutant activities and separates chloroplastic native HSK from heterologous cytosolic yeast HSK rescue. No depletion of downstream amino acids in the mutant is not evidence of absent biosynthetic function. PMID15986928 publisher PDF retrieval returned403; publicly indexed Results/Fig5 text and later full19622802 show dmr1 did not acquire bacterial resistance, unlike dmr3/4. This does not prove absence of every bacterial response, but does not support the positive response-to-bacterium annotation. Later full PMID25432266 provides positive native fungal evidence (Fusarium), with allele/tissue dependence, so response-to-fungus is retained non-core. PMID15740643 is MISCITED: the full author reproduction at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7992558_Characterization_of_a_novel_developmentally_retarded_mutant_drm1_associated_with_the_autonomous_flowering_pathway_in_Arabidopsis was inspected, including Methods, mapping Results and Discussion. It maps developmentally retarded DRM1 to chromosome5 near MXK3.1; HSK/downy-mildew-resistant DMR1 is on chromosome2. Publisher access was preview only; the separate full reproduction was actually read. The flowering claim is removed on verified gene identity, not title-only inference.
