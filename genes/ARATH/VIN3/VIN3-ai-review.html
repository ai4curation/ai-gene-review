Gene Description

VIN3 (VERNALIZATION INSENSITIVE 3) is a nuclear PHD-superdomain/fibronectin III/VEL-domain protein of Arabidopsis whose transcription is induced only slowly during prolonged cold. This makes VIN3 accumulation a molecular readout of the length of winter. VIN3 forms a heterodimer with its constitutively expressed paralog VRN5/VIL1 and joins a PHD-PRC2 complex (VRN2, SWN/CLF, FIE, MSI1, VRN5, VEL1) at a small nucleation region near the first exon-intron boundary of the floral repressor FLC. There it is required for histone deacetylation and local H3K27me3 nucleation, which switches FLC alleles into a metastable silenced state that is later spread and maintained by other Polycomb factors. Its VEL domain undergoes head-to-tail polymerization that builds dynamic nuclear assemblies, and this is needed for multivalent chromatin association and efficient nucleation. Its PHD superdomain does not bind histone H3 tails. VIN3 also contributes to survival under hypoxia.

GO:0140006 histone H3 reader activity
IBA
GO_REF:0000033 		REMOVE
Summary: IBA histone H3 reader activity for the VEL clade, seeded from VEL1 (AT4G30200).
Reason: Direct structural and biophysical work contradicts this for VIN3. Crystal structures show that the VEL PHD finger is part of a tripartite PHD superdomain with no canonical three-pronged histone-binding pocket. ITC and NMR detected no binding of the Arabidopsis VIN3 PHD superdomain, or of its minimal PHD finger, to unmodified or modified H3 tails. An earlier pull-down result suggesting H3 binding is superseded by these quantitative assays. VIN3 is therefore outside the clade that inherited H3-reader activity, despite the PHD signature.

Core Functions

Cold-induced PRC2 accessory protein whose VEL domain head-to-tail polymerizes. This assembles dynamic nuclear VIN3 assemblies that give multivalent chromatin association and efficient PRC2-mediated H3K27me3 nucleation at FLC, establishing epigenetic silencing during vernalization.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: What controls the slow, quantitative induction of VIN3 transcription during prolonged cold (the cold-duration sensor), and how is it reset in warm conditions?

Q: Does the positively charged VEL PHD superdomain bind nucleic acids (DNA or COOLAIR RNA) in vivo, replacing the canonical histone-reader function?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Test binding of purified VIN3 PHD superdomain to DNA, nucleosomes and COOLAIR RNA in vitro. Complement vin3 with charge-reversal PHD mutants and assay H3K27me3 nucleation at FLC by ChIP-qPCR.

Hypothesis: The VIN3 PHD superdomain contacts FLC chromatin via nucleic-acid binding rather than histone tails.
