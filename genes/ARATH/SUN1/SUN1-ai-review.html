Gene Description

SUN1 (At5g04990) is one of the two Arabidopsis thaliana C-terminal SUN (Cter-SUN) domain proteins (471 aa), the plant counterparts of animal SUN1/SUN2 and of fission yeast Sad1. It is a type II single-pass inner nuclear membrane protein with a nucleoplasmic N-terminus carrying a nuclear localization signal, a transmembrane segment, a luminal coiled-coil that mediates homo-oligomerization and heteromerization with SUN2, and a C-terminal SUN domain in the perinuclear space. The SUN domain binds the C-terminal KASH tails of plant-specific outer-nuclear-membrane KASH proteins (WIP1-3, SINE1-4, TIK) through a conserved KASH-binding pocket, forming SUN-KASH bridges (LINC complexes) that retain these proteins, and via WIPs the RanGTPase-activating protein RanGAP1, at the nuclear envelope. Through the WIP-bound tail-anchored WIT2 protein and the actin-based motor myosin XI-i, the bridge couples the actomyosin cytoskeleton to the nucleus and transmits cytoplasmic forces that give epidermal, root hair and trichome nuclei their elongated shape; on the nucleoplasmic side SUN proteins can also recruit the lamin-like CROWDED NUCLEI 1 (CRWN1/LINC1). SUN1 is broadly expressed and is the predominant paralog for nuclear shape: sun1 single mutants already have rounder nuclei in root hairs and trichomes, and the phenotype is enhanced when SUN2 is also reduced. Together with its paralog it lines the nuclear envelope in meiotic prophase I and is needed for timely meiotic progression, full synapsis and normal crossover formation, the two paralogs attach telomeres to the nuclear envelope and drive rapid prophase chromosome movements, a conserved SUN-KASH role in meiosis. During open mitosis it follows nuclear envelope breakdown into the endoplasmic reticulum and marks the reforming nuclear envelope in coordination with the phragmoplast.

Research report: Arabidopsis thaliana SUN1 (At5g04990; UniProt Q9FF75)

Executive conclusion

The requested protein is correctly identified as AtSUN1, the product of Arabidopsis thaliana locus At5g04990 and UniProt accession Q9FF75. It is a classical C-terminal SUN-domain protein and an integral component of the inner nuclear membrane (INM). This identity is distinct from mammalian SUN1, SUN proteins in rice or moss, and the Arabidopsis mid-SUN proteins SUN3–SUN5. Arabidopsis AtSUN2 is the close paralog encoded by At3g10730. AtSUN1 contains the expected SUN/LINC architecture—an N-terminal nucleoplasmic region with a nuclear-localization signal, a transmembrane/coiled-coil region, and a conserved C-terminal SUN domain exposed to the perinuclear lumen—consistent with the supplied InterPro/Pfam assignments SUN1–5, SUN_dom, and Sad1_UNC. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8, zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 1-2)

AtSUN1 is not an enzyme or transporter. Its primary function is structural and mechanochemical: together with AtSUN2, it forms the inner-membrane half of plant LINC complexes that bridge nucleoplasmic/chromosomal structures to outer-nuclear-membrane KASH-like proteins and, through them, the cytoskeleton. Its best-supported functions are maintenance of nuclear architecture, anchoring of nuclear-envelope proteins, coordinated nuclear-envelope remodeling during mitosis, and—most decisively—attachment and movement of chromosomes at the nuclear envelope during meiosis. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 1-4, zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 3-4)

The central caveat is extensive functional redundancy with AtSUN2. AtSUN1 single mutants have no obvious fertility or meiotic phenotype, whereas combined SUN1/SUN2 disruption produces severe defects. Consequently, many physiological conclusions concern the SUN1/SUN2 module rather than an independently demonstrated SUN1-only activity. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12)

1. Molecular identity, family, and topology

The literature explicitly maps AtSUN1 to At5g04990 and distinguishes it from AtSUN2/At3g10730. AtSUN1 and AtSUN2 are the two Arabidopsis classical C-terminal SUN proteins, whereas SUN3, SUN4, and SUN5 are topologically different mid-SUN proteins with important ER-associated functions. The SUN1 designation is therefore genuinely ambiguous across organisms, but the Arabidopsis locus, accession, and domain architecture supplied in the question agree with the target-specific literature. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8, fernandezjimenez2020theroleof pages 13-14)

AtSUN1 is an integral INM protein. Its N-terminal region faces the nucleoplasm, while the conserved C-terminal SUN domain occupies the nuclear-envelope lumen/perinuclear space, where it can bind the short lumenal KASH-like tails of outer-nuclear-membrane proteins. Coiled-coil-mediated oligomerization and SUN1–SUN2 homo-/heteromeric association are thought to strengthen this bridge. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 1-4)

Fluorescent-protein reporters localized AtSUN1 to the chromatin/nuclear periphery in roots, hypocotyls, cotyledons, leaves, meristematic cells, epidermal cells, and root hairs. Deletion experiments showed that the N-terminal sequence, transmembrane segment, and coiled-coil region contribute to INM targeting or retention; deleting the SUN domain did not by itself abolish nuclear-envelope localization. Thus, the SUN domain primarily mediates partner binding rather than serving as the sole targeting signal. (oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 2-3)

2. Primary molecular function: the plant LINC bridge

The core LINC arrangement is:

nucleoplasm/chromatin or nucleoskeleton → AtSUN1/AtSUN2 in the INM → WIP/SINE KASH-like proteins in the outer nuclear membrane → cytoskeletal or motor-associated components.

Direct FRAP-based evidence showed that AtSUN1 significantly reduced the mobility of AtWIP1 at the nuclear envelope. Removing the SUN interaction region from AtSUN1 or the C-terminal VVPT KASH-like motif from WIP1 abolished this effect (P<0.01; n=30), demonstrating motif-dependent membrane interaction rather than simple colocalization. Some complementary co-immunoprecipitation assays used AtSUN2, so the strongest biochemical complex evidence cannot always be assigned uniquely to AtSUN1. (zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 3-4)

Arabidopsis LINC bridges can connect to actin through SINE1 or through a WIP–WIT–myosin XI-I assembly. AtSUN1 should therefore be viewed as an INM force-transmission adaptor: it does not itself hydrolyze ATP or generate motion, but couples nuclear or chromosomal structures to cytoplasmic force-generating systems. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 4-8)

3. Cellular localization and nuclear architecture

Interphase nuclear shape

Joint depletion of SUN1 and SUN2 makes normally elongated leaf epidermal, trichome, and root-hair nuclei more rounded. The relevant morphometric analyses examined 60 epidermal, 20 trichome, and 55 root-hair nuclei and found significant differences (P<0.01). Because the experiments combined SUN1 knockout with SUN2 knockdown, they establish a redundant SUN-module function rather than a unique SUN1 requirement. (zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 5-6)

SUN–WIP bridges also retain RanGAP at the nuclear envelope. In sun1-KO sun2-KD roots, AtWIP1 and AtRanGAP1 became significantly less concentrated at the nuclear periphery; RanGAP localization was quantified across n=55 cells (P<0.01). RanGAP binds WIP, which in turn binds SUN, so RanGAP anchoring is indirect with respect to AtSUN1. (zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 4-5, zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 5-6)

Mitosis and nuclear-envelope reassembly

Live spinning-disk confocal microscopy showed that AtSUN1 follows nuclear-envelope breakdown and reformation. During telophase it first accumulated on the distal surface of daughter nuclei and subsequently on the proximal surface. This asymmetric reassembly was spatially and temporally coordinated with spindle remnants and phragmoplast microtubules; taxol delayed proximal AtSUN1 accumulation. These observations support a role in rebuilding a polarized, mechanically organized envelope after open mitosis, although they do not establish AtSUN1 as the initiating factor. (oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 1-2, oda2011dynamicsofarabidopsis pages 5-7)

4. Meiotic pathway: telomere attachment, chromosome motion, and recombination

Current mechanistic model

During early meiotic prophase I, AtSUN1 and AtSUN2 redistribute from a relatively uniform nuclear-envelope pattern into polarized SUN-enriched domains. Chromosome ends associate with these domains, telomeres form the peripheral “bouquet,” and cytoskeletal forces drive rapid chromosome motion. The resulting movements facilitate productive homologue encounters, synapsis, interlock resolution, and crossover maturation. At diplotene, the SUN distribution becomes more broadly peripheral again. (cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 9-10, cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 7-9)

The major recent advance is Cromer et al., published July 2024 in Nature Communications (DOI/URL). Live imaging showed uncoordinated centromere movements during zygotene and pachytene, averaging approximately 100 nm/s and reaching 500 nm/s. These movements persisted in several mutants affecting chromosome organization or recombination but were essentially abolished as directed rapid movements in the sun1 sun2 double mutant. Mutant tracks averaged 54 ± 2 nm/s across 65 tracks and had a diffusion coefficient of only 0.002 µm²/s, resembling largely stationary or back-and-forth somatic movement. (cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 1-2, cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 10-12, cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 6-7)

SUN polarization was systematic at zygotene (13/13 cells), and SUN-rich clumps frequently corresponded to anchored chromosome ends. In wild type, approximately 93% of telomeres were peripheral and a clear peripheral bouquet was present in 95% of zygotene/pachytene cells. In the double mutant, only about 40% of telomeres remained peripheral; 20/22 cells instead contained nucleoplasmic clusters averaging 8.5 telomeres near the nucleolus. Normal movement of the nucleolus toward the nuclear periphery was also disrupted. (cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 9-10, cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 10-12)

These results establish that the SUN1/SUN2 module is upstream of telomere-led rapid chromosome movement and nuclear polarization. They do not demonstrate that SUN1 alone is indispensable, because SUN2 can compensate in single mutants. Residual telomere attachment in the double mutant may indicate partial compensation by another INM component. (cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 14-15)

Consequences for pairing, synapsis, and fertility

Varas et al., published January 2015 in The Plant Journal (DOI/URL), showed that complete combined loss of AtSUN1 and AtSUN2 causes a severe but mechanistically informative phenotype:

  • Silique length declined from 15.1 ± 0.5 mm to 6.8 ± 1.3 mm (n=20).
  • Seeds per silique fell from 60.4 ± 2.5 to 2.1 ± 0.9, a 96.5% reduction and approximately 3.5% residual seed set.
  • Early prophase I lasted approximately 28 h rather than 16 h, and total meiosis approximately 48 h rather than 36 h.
  • Synaptonemal-complex length fell from 165.26 ± 4.79 µm in wild type to 83.82 ± 4.89 µm; average synapsis was 50.71%, with a maximum of 72.98% among 19 cells.
  • Mean chiasmata declined from 10.2 ± 0.14 per Col-0 cell to 3.32 ± 0.18 in the double mutant (n=128).
  • RAD51 focus numbers were unchanged, indicating approximately normal double-strand-break formation, but DMC1, MSH4, and MLH1 foci fell to about 83%, 65%, and 30% of wild type, respectively. Thus, the principal defect lies in inter-homologue pairing/synapsis and crossover maturation, not DSB induction. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 41-45, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 15-19, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 12-15)

The double mutant produced univalents, rod bivalents, persistent interlock-like structures, lagging chromosomes, and unbalanced meiotic products. These observations support a causal sequence in which unstable telomere–envelope attachment impairs chromosome motion, which then reduces stable homologue pairing and synapsis, leaving fewer sites competent for MLH1-marked crossovers and balanced segregation. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 19-22, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 45-54)

5. AtSUN1 proteostasis and proximal molecular environment

Huang et al., published June 2020 in Nature Communications (DOI/URL), used AtSUN1–BioID2 as an INM bait. Relative to outer-membrane WIT1 and mock controls, 15 proteins met the SUN1-selective threshold of greater than twofold enrichment and adjusted P<0.05. These included CRWN1–CRWN4 and KAKU4, supporting spatial coupling between SUN1 and the plant nucleoskeleton, as well as UFD1 and PUX proteins associated with the CDC48 extraction machinery. BioID establishes proximity, not necessarily direct binding. (huang2020proximitylabelingproteomics pages 1-2, huang2020proximitylabelingproteomics pages 2-3)

Cycloheximide-chase and inhibitor experiments showed that AtSUN1 undergoes constitutive, ubiquitin/proteasome-dependent turnover: MG132 stabilized and increased polyubiquitinated SUN1, whereas autophagy inhibition did not. SUN1 associated biochemically with PUX4 and PUX5, although cellular interaction signals were weak and may represent transient or indirect contacts. Unexpectedly, loss of PUX3/4/5 accelerated SUN1 turnover—nearly 70% loss within four hours—suggesting that these factors restrain CDC48-dependent extraction or otherwise protect INM proteins rather than simply recruiting them for destruction. The responsible ubiquitin ligase and exact extraction sequence remain unknown. (huang2020proximitylabelingproteomics pages 3-4, huang2020proximitylabelingproteomics pages 5-6, huang2020proximitylabelingproteomics pages 6-8)

6. Recent developments, 2023–2024

The most important target-specific development in 2023–2024 is the 2024 direct visualization and quantification of SUN1/SUN2-dependent rapid meiotic chromosome movements. It converted the earlier inference—derived from bouquet, synapsis, and recombination defects—into a measured mechanochemical function and showed that the nuclear envelope itself undergoes stage-specific reorganization. (cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 9-10, cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 1-2)

A February 2023 authoritative review in Nucleus (DOI/URL) framed the plant nuclear envelope as an active hub for chromatin organization and gene regulation rather than a passive boundary. In that framework, SUN proteins are force-transmitting and genome-organizing components, but direct evidence that AtSUN1 controls specific transcriptional loci remains substantially weaker than the evidence for nuclear mechanics and meiosis. (tang2023plantnuclearenvelope pages 13-14)

A separate 2023 study of Arabidopsis mid-SUN proteins concerned SUN3/SUN4 and ER topology, not Q9FF75/AtSUN1. It should not be used to assign ER cargo-sorting or receptor-kinase chaperone functions to AtSUN1. This distinction is essential because the shared “SUN” nomenclature covers topologically and functionally different Arabidopsis subfamilies.

7. Applications and real-world relevance

At present, AtSUN1 has research applications rather than a deployed agricultural use:

  1. Nuclear-envelope and LINC reporter. Fluorescent AtSUN1 is used to visualize INM dynamics during interphase, mitosis, and meiosis.
  2. Proximity-proteomics anchor. AtSUN1–BioID2 provides an experimentally validated bait for defining the plant INM proteome and proteostasis machinery. (huang2020proximitylabelingproteomics pages 1-2)
  3. Mechanistic model for fertility. The severe seed-set, synapsis, and crossover defects after combined SUN1/SUN2 loss make the system useful for dissecting how nuclear mechanics controls reproductive chromosome behavior. (varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 15-19, varas2015absenceofsun1 pages 8-12)
  4. Comparative crop-science target. Conservation of SUN/LINC roles in crop species suggests potential relevance to fertility, meiotic recombination, nuclear positioning, and stress resilience. However, no validated SUN1-specific field intervention, breeding product, or crop-engineering implementation was identified. Manipulation would require caution because the same system affects nuclear structure, mitosis, and fertility.

8. Expert interpretation and unresolved questions

The most defensible functional annotation is:

AtSUN1 is an inner nuclear-membrane mechanochemical adaptor that, largely redundantly with AtSUN2, forms SUN–KASH-like LINC bridges coupling the plant nucleoskeleton and meiotic chromosome ends to outer-membrane/cytoskeletal machinery. It maintains nuclear architecture and enables telomere attachment, bouquet formation, rapid prophase chromosome movement, homologous synapsis, crossover maturation, and balanced meiotic segregation.

This interpretation is supported by convergent localization, deletion mapping, FRAP interaction assays, live-cell microscopy, genetics, cytology, and proximity proteomics. It is stronger than broad annotations such as “nuclear organization” because it specifies the molecular bridge, membrane compartment, and force-transmission role. (zhou2012novelplantsun–kash pages 3-4, cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 1-2)

Important limitations remain. Most organismal phenotypes require combined SUN1/SUN2 loss, so paralog-specific contributions are unresolved. The cytoplasmic motor responsible for Arabidopsis meiotic motion was not established by the 2024 study. Direct nucleoplasmic tethers connecting SUN1 to telomeres/chromatin remain incompletely defined, and BioID proximity to CRWN proteins does not prove stable direct binding. Finally, the relationship between SUN1-dependent mechanics and transcriptional regulation is plausible but less directly demonstrated than its meiotic and structural functions. (cromer2024rapidmeioticprophase pages 14-15, huang2020proximitylabelingproteomics pages 3-4)

description: 'SUN1 (At5g04990) is one of the two Arabidopsis thaliana C-terminal SUN (Cter-SUN) domain
  proteins (471 aa), the plant counterparts of animal SUN1/SUN2 and of fission yeast Sad1. It is a type
  II single-pass inner nuclear membrane protein with a nucleoplasmic N-terminus carrying a nuclear localization
  signal, a transmembrane segment, a luminal coiled-coil that mediates homo-oligomerization and heteromerization
  with SUN2, and a C-terminal SUN domain in the perinuclear space. The SUN domain binds the C-terminal
  KASH tails of plant-specific outer-nuclear-membrane KASH proteins (WIP1-3, SINE1-4, TIK) through a conserved
  KASH-binding pocket, forming SUN-KASH bridges (LINC complexes) that retain these proteins, and via WIPs
  the RanGTPase-activating protein RanGAP1, at the nuclear envelope. Through the WIP-bound tail-anchored
  WIT2 protein and the actin-based motor myosin XI-i, the bridge couples the actomyosin cytoskeleton to
  the nucleus and transmits cytoplasmic forces that give epidermal, root hair and trichome nuclei their
  elongated shape; on the nucleoplasmic side SUN proteins can also recruit the lamin-like CROWDED NUCLEI
  1 (CRWN1/LINC1). SUN1 is broadly expressed and is the predominant paralog for nuclear shape: sun1 single
  mutants already have rounder nuclei in root hairs and trichomes, and the phenotype is enhanced when
  SUN2 is also reduced. Together with its paralog it lines the nuclear envelope in meiotic prophase I
  and is needed for timely meiotic progression, full synapsis and normal crossover formation, the two
  paralogs attach telomeres to the nuclear envelope and drive rapid prophase chromosome movements, a conserved
  SUN-KASH role in meiosis. During open mitosis it follows nuclear envelope breakdown into the endoplasmic
  reticulum and marks the reforming nuclear envelope in coordination with the phragmoplast.'
