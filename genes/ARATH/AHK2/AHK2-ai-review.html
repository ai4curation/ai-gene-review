Gene Description

AHK2 (ARABIDOPSIS HISTIDINE KINASE 2) is one of three CHASE-domain hybrid sensor histidine kinases that serve as cytokinin receptors in Arabidopsis thaliana, together with AHK3 and CRE1/AHK4. It is an integral membrane protein, located mainly in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane with a smaller plasma-membrane pool, whose extracytosolic CHASE domain binds cytokinins. Ligand binding activates autophosphorylation of a conserved histidine in the cytoplasmic transmitter domain, and the phosphoryl group is relayed through the receiver domain to histidine phosphotransfer proteins (AHPs), which carry it to nuclear type-B response regulators (ARRs) that activate cytokinin-responsive transcription. In replacement experiments AHK2 favours the kinase over the phosphatase mode. Acting partially redundantly with AHK3, AHK2 promotes shoot and leaf growth, cambial activity and secondary growth, and inhibits root growth and branching; it also modulates seed germination and size, chlorophyll retention, and responses to drought, salt, cold and abscisic acid.

  root growth and branching; it also modulates seed germination and size, chlorophyll retention,
  and responses to drought, salt, cold and abscisic acid.
