Gene Description

ERS1 (ETHYLENE RESPONSE SENSOR 1, At2g40940) is one of the five Arabidopsis ethylene receptors and, with ETR1, makes up receptor subfamily 1. It is a multi-pass endoplasmic reticulum membrane protein with an N-terminal three-helix ethylene-binding sensor domain (copper-dependent by analogy with ETR1), a GAF domain and a histidine-kinase-like domain, but it lacks the C-terminal receiver domain found in ETR1. ERS1 forms disulfide-linked homodimers and heteromeric receptor complexes at the ER and binds ethylene with an affinity similar to ETR1. In the absence of ethylene the receptors keep the Raf-like kinase CTR1 active and thereby repress the pathway; ethylene binding inactivates this output, allowing EIN2-dependent signalling. ERS1 acts redundantly with ETR1 as a negative regulator of ethylene responses (etr1 ers1 double null mutants show a strong constitutive ethylene response), although genetic studies indicate it can also promote ethylene responses in some receptor-mutant backgrounds. ERS1 is transcriptionally induced by ethylene, providing negative feedback on signalling. In vitro it autophosphorylates on serine and, only with Mn2+, on histidine.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does ERS1 transmit its signal to CTR1 through direct stable association, or mainly through heteromeric complexes with receiver-domain receptors such as ETR1, given that ERS1 co-purifies weakly with CTR1?

Q: Under what conditions, and by what mechanism, does ERS1 promote rather than inhibit ethylene responses, as proposed from receptor-mutant genetics?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Quantitative in planta proximity labelling or single-molecule pull-down of ERS1-CTR1 versus ETR1-CTR1 complexes in etr1 and wild-type backgrounds, with and without ethylene.

Experiment: Test whether serine autophosphorylation of ERS1 changes with ethylene in vivo by phosphoproteomics of ERS1 from ethylene-treated and untreated seedlings.
