Gene Description

PLGG1 (plastidal glycolate/glycerate translocator 1; also AtLrgB; At1g32080) is a polytopic membrane protein of the chloroplast envelope, with twelve predicted transmembrane helices, belonging to the LrgB/CidB family that is shared with bacteria, archaea and fungi. It moves the photorespiratory intermediates glycolate and glycerate across the envelope, exporting glycolate made from 2-phosphoglycolate in the stroma and importing glycerate returning from the peroxisome for phosphorylation by glycerate kinase. It behaves as a glycolate/glycerate exchanger and shares glycolate export with the bile acid sodium symporter BASS6, whereas glycerate import depends on PLGG1 alone. Loss of PLGG1 causes accumulation of glycolate and glycerate, interveinal leaf chlorosis and cell death, and reduced photosynthesis in normal air, all suppressed by elevated CO2. The leaf phenotypes first led to proposals that it controls chloroplast development or cell death. Mutants are also reported to respond differently to abscisic acid in lateral root growth, stomatal movement and drought tolerance. PLGG1 is expressed in leaves, stems and flowers but not roots.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

glycolate:glycerate antiporter activity

Definition: Enables the transfer of a solute or solutes from one side of a membrane to the other according to the reaction: glycolate(in) + D-glycerate(out) = glycolate(out) + D-glycerate(in).

Justification: The chloroplast glycolate/glycerate carrier was characterized biochemically as an exchanger before its gene was known, and PLGG1 is described as a glycolate/glycerate antiporter, but GO only has separate glycolate and glycerate transporter activity terms (OLS search found no glycolate or glycerate antiporter term). A coupled exchange term would capture the stoichiometric link between the export and import legs of the C2 cycle. Besides the asserted parent GO:0043879, the term belongs under GO:0015297 antiporter activity, where GO places its other antiporter terms; proposed_parent holds only one term.

Parent term: glycolate transmembrane transporter activity

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:28351992 : BASS6 is a member of the family of sodium-dependent symporters, which are unidirectional in transport, whereas PLGG1 is a glycolate/glycerate antiporter.
  • PMID:22303256 : The transporter exchanged glycolate for glycerate ( Howitz and McCarty, 1991 ).

Core Functions

Chloroplast envelope glycolate/glycerate exchanger that exports photorespiratory glycolate from the stroma and imports glycerate for the final glycerate kinase step of the C2 cycle; glycolate export is shared with BASS6, and glycerate import depends on PLGG1 alone.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Is PLGG1 a strict 1:1 glycolate/glycerate antiporter, or can it also catalyse unidirectional glycolate efflux (e.g. proton-coupled), given that two glycolate molecules leave the chloroplast for every glycerate that returns?

Q: Are the ABA-related phenotypes of plgg1 mutants (lateral roots, stomata, germination) caused by photorespiratory metabolite accumulation, and are they suppressed by elevated CO2?

Q: Does the bacterial LrgB/CidB homolog family also transport glycolate or related small organic acids, and does that underlie its reported role in bacterial cell lysis?

  • Identified by coexpression with photorespiratory genes and reverse genetics

  • Transport evidence (Pick et al. 2013):
