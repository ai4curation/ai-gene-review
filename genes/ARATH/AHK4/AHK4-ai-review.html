Gene Description

AHK4 (CRE1/WOODEN LEG) is a CHASE-domain hybrid sensor histidine kinase and the first identified cytokinin receptor of Arabidopsis thaliana. It binds adenine-type and phenylurea cytokinins with nanomolar affinity in its extracytosolic CHASE sensor domain (crystal structures with bound ligands are known) and is located mainly in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane. Cytokinin binding triggers autophosphorylation of the conserved transmitter-domain histidine and phosphotransfer through its receiver domain to AHP histidine phosphotransfer proteins, which relay the signal to nuclear type-B ARR transcription factors. In the absence of cytokinin, AHK4 reverses the relay and removes phosphate from AHPs, lowering the phosphoload of the pathway. AHK4 is expressed predominantly in roots and vasculature; the wol allele blocks the asymmetric divisions that establish phloem and procambium in the embryonic root, and cre1 mutants are cytokinin-resistant in root growth, callus greening and shoot induction. It also contributes to cytokinin regulation of nutrient uptake (sulfate, phosphate, iron) and to osmotic stress responses.

description: AHK4 (CRE1/WOODEN LEG) is a CHASE-domain hybrid sensor histidine kinase and the

  and phosphotransfer through its receiver domain to AHP histidine phosphotransfer proteins,
  which relay the signal to nuclear type-B ARR transcription factors. In the absence of cytokinin,
  AHK4 reverses the relay and removes phosphate from AHPs, lowering the phosphoload of the
  pathway. AHK4 is expressed predominantly in roots and vasculature; the wol allele blocks
  the asymmetric divisions that establish phloem and procambium in the embryonic root, and
  cre1 mutants are cytokinin-resistant in root growth, callus greening and shoot induction.
  It also contributes to cytokinin regulation of nutrient uptake (sulfate, phosphate, iron)
  and to osmotic stress responses.
