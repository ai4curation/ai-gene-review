CC   -!- FUNCTION: Functions probably as a transcriptional factor that activates
CC       genes involved in secondary cell wall biosynthesis (PubMed:25228083,
CC       PubMed:25732536). Functions redundantly with C3H14 to regulate
CC       secondary cell wall formation (PubMed:25732536). C3H14 and C3H15 have
CC       overlapping roles in the regulation of secondary cell wall formation
CC       and anther development (PubMed:25732536). C3H14 may contribute more to
CC       secondary cell wall thickening while C3H15 could be more important in
CC       anther development (PubMed:25732536). May regulate at both the
CC       transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels the expression of many
CC       genes involved in various biological processes, particularly those
CC       associated with cell wall metabolism and pollen development
CC       (PubMed:25732536). Involved in the regulation of callose metabolism in
CC       male meiocytes, in integrity of newly formed microspores, and promotes
CC       male fertility (PubMed:24567187). May be involved in the regulation of
CC       the callose synthesis genes CALS5 and CALS12, the potential degradation
CC       of callose walls-related genes A6 and MYB80, as well as other putative
CC       beta-1,3-glucanase genes (PubMed:24567187). Negatively regulates cell
CC       elongation by inhibiting brassinosteroid (BR) signaling
CC       (PubMed:35139225). Functions downstream of the BRI1 receptor as a
CC       negative regulator in the BR pathway (PubMed:35139225).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:24567187, ECO:0000269|PubMed:25228083,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25732536, ECO:0000269|PubMed:35139225}.
CC   -!- SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Cytoplasm {ECO:0000269|PubMed:35139225}. Nucleus
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:35139225}. Note=Localizes in cytoplasm in the
CC       absence of brassinosteroids (BRs). Upon BR perception, C3H15
CC       accumulates in the nucleus. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:35139225}.

CC   -!- TISSUE SPECIFICITY: Highly expressed in secondary cell wall-forming
CC       tissues and the xylem cells of roots (PubMed:25228083). Expressed
CC       predominantly in inflorescence stems, flowers and siliques
CC       (PubMed:25732536). Highly expressed in the basal portion of stems,
CC       where cells are undergoing secondary cell wall thickening
CC       (PubMed:25732536). Highly expressed in meiocytes and tapetum from
CC       anthers (PubMed:24567187). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:24567187,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25228083, ECO:0000269|PubMed:25732536}.
CC   -!- PTM: Phosphorylated at Ser-111 by ASK7/BIN2 in the cytoplasm in the
CC       absence of brassinosteroids (BRs). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:35139225}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: No visible phenotype under normal growth
CC       conditions (PubMed:25228083). The double mutants c3h14 and c3h15 have
CC       reductions in stem secondary cell wall thickening and defects in anther
CC       development (PubMed:25732536). Callose deposition defects in
CC       microspores, and pollen exine formation severely affected, leading to
CC       male sterility (PubMed:24567187). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:24567187,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25228083, ECO:0000269|PubMed:25732536}.
CC   -!- MISCELLANEOUS: Plants overexpressing C3H15 exhibit small and slightly
CC       curled leaves, and retarded stem elongations due to cell elongation
CC       defect and ectopic secondary cell wall formation (PubMed:25228083).
CC       Overexpression of C3H15 results in secondary cell wall thickening in
CC       fibers and vessels (PubMed:25732536). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:25228083,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25732536}.
