Gene Description

ImcH (inner membrane cytochrome H, locus GSU3259) is a 507-residue multiheme c-type cytochrome of the NapC/NirT/NrfH superfamily anchored in the cytoplasmic (inner) membrane of Geobacter sulfurreducens by three transmembrane helices, with a large periplasm-facing domain carrying seven c-type heme motifs. It is one of at least three inner-membrane routes by which electrons leave the menaquinol pool on their way to acceptors outside the cell, and it is the route used when the terminal acceptor has a high redox potential. Cells lacking imcH cannot reduce Fe(III) citrate, Fe(III)-EDTA, Mn(IV) oxides or electrodes poised above about -0.1 V versus SHE, yet reduce low-potential Fe(III) oxides such as ferrihydrite and goethite normally through the CbcL- and CbcBA-dependent pathways; growth on the soluble intracellular acceptor fumarate is unaffected. The protein is constitutively expressed. Purified ImcH is reduced by the menaquinone analogue menadiol, its hemes titrate between -150 and -358 mV, and it transfers electrons to the abundant periplasmic triheme cytochrome PpcA, which it binds with higher affinity than PpcA's homologues; protons released on quinol oxidation are delivered to the same (periplasmic) side as the electrons, so the step is electroneutral, translocating no charge and so making no contribution to the membrane potential. Because the protons are nonetheless released into the periplasm, the step is proposed to contribute to the pH component of the proton motive force, which would make ImcH the only Geobacter respiratory membrane protein so far known to do so: the b-type-heme inner-membrane oxidoreductases CbcL and CbcBA are expected to release their protons on the cytoplasmic side. ImcH homologues occur throughout metal-reducing Geobacteraceae and Anaeromyxobacter and in other Fe(III)-reducing lineages, and are absent from fermentative and chlororespiratory relatives.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

electroneutral menaquinol:cytochrome c oxidoreductase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: a menaquinol + 2 Fe(III)-cytochrome c = a menaquinone + 2 Fe(II)-cytochrome c + 2 H+, in which the quinol-oxidising site and the cytochrome c acceptor are on the same side of the membrane, so that the protons released on quinol oxidation are delivered to that same side and no charge or proton is translocated across the membrane.

Justification: The only existing GO term that names both a quinol donor and a cytochrome c acceptor, GO:0008121 quinol-cytochrome-c reductase activity, is is_a GO:0015078 proton transmembrane transporter activity and GO:0015453 oxidoreduction-driven active transmembrane transporter activity, and its definition opens "Enables the transfer of a solute or solutes from one side of a membrane to the other". That parentage comes from the proton-translocating cytochrome bc1 complex and is wrong for the NapC/NirT/NrfH superfamily, whose members hold the quinol site and the cytochrome acceptor on the same (periplasmic) face: for ImcH the proton-release side was measured directly and the reaction is electroneutral. The non-transporter intermediate that would otherwise serve, GO:0016681, is obsolete, and its surviving genus GO:0016679 names no acceptor. A sibling of GO:0008121 under GO:0016679 without transporter parents would let ImcH, CymA, NrfH, TorC, TorY and their homologues be annotated to the chemistry they perform without asserting proton translocation they do not perform. An acceptable alternative upstream fix would be to loosen GO:0008121 itself by removing the transporter parents and creating a proton-translocating child for the bc1 complex.

Parent term: oxidoreductase activity, acting on diphenols and related substances as donors

Core Functions

High-potential exit from the menaquinol pool. ImcH sits in the inner membrane with its heme-bearing domain in the periplasm, is reduced by menaquinol at a binding site on the periplasmic face, and passes the electrons to the periplasmic triheme cytochrome PpcA, releasing the quinol protons on the same side so that the step is electroneutral. This is the step the cell uses whenever the extracellular acceptor sits above about -0.1 V versus SHE; below that threshold CbcL and CbcBA take over, which is why imcH deletion blocks Fe(III) citrate, Fe(III)-EDTA, Mn(IV) oxide and high-potential electrode reduction while leaving low-potential Fe(III) oxides and fumarate respiration untouched. The electron-carrier machinery is part of the same single activity rather than a second one: seven covalently bound c-type hemes, titrating from -150 to -358 mV, relay the electrons from the quinol-oxidising heme 1 pocket through the periplasm-facing domain to the solvent-exposed terminal heme, where PpcA (bound more tightly than its four homologues) collects them for onward transfer to the outer-membrane conduits and the mineral or electrode acceptor. The term carries transmembrane proton-transporter parents inherited from the cytochrome bc1 archetype that do not apply to ImcH; see the GO:0008121 row and the proposed term.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: How should GO represent an activity that is conditional on the redox potential of the terminal acceptor? ImcH, CbcL and CbcBA all oxidise the same menaquinol pool and differ only in the potential window in which they operate, and no existing term distinguishes them.

Q: Is PpcA the obligate physiological acceptor of ImcH in vivo, or can any of the five Ppc cytochromes substitute, given that they are described as physiologically equivalent but differ in expression and heme potentials?

Q: How much of the proton motive force does ImcH actually build, and in which component? The voltammetric evidence makes the step electroneutral, so it adds nothing to the membrane potential, yet the same work argues that releasing the protons into the periplasm contributes to the pH gradient and would make ImcH the only Geobacter respiratory membrane protein known to maintain a pmf. Measuring the two components separately would settle whether the energetic advantage of the high-potential pathway lies in that proton release, in the larger potential drop available to the acceptor, or in the proton-coupled electron transfer of the downstream Ppc cytochromes.

Q: Should GO:0008121 quinol-cytochrome-c reductase activity keep its transmembrane proton-transporter parents? The term is the only one naming both a quinol donor and a cytochrome c acceptor, but it is is_a GO:0015078 and GO:0015453 because the archetypal enzyme is the proton-translocating cytochrome bc1 complex, and it therefore asserts proton translocation for every NapC/NirT/NrfH-family member that holds its quinol site and its acceptor on the same face of the membrane. ImcH is measured to be electroneutral, so either the term needs an electroneutral sibling under GO:0016679 (see proposed_new_terms) or the transporter parents should move to a bc1-specific child.

Biochemistry of the purified protein (Pimenta et al. 2023, PMID:37779214)

Recombinant ImcH produced in S. oneidensis and purified: seven c-type hemes by
iron quantification, no signal-peptide cleavage, monomer/dimer equilibrium. Menadiol
(a soluble menaquinone analogue) reduces the protein, though only partially
(~2 of 7 hemes), as also seen for CymA. Hemes titrate low, from -150 to -358 mV.
Surface plasmon resonance and 1D 1H NMR identify the periplasmic triheme PpcA as the
preferred partner (K_D ≈ 2.7 µM), and cyclic voltammetry shows continuous electron
transfer from electrode-bound ImcH to PpcA in solution. Proton release is to the same
side as the electrons, i.e. the protein is electroneutral. Together these give both
half-reactions of a menaquinol:cytochrome c oxidoreductase in vitro, which is why
this review adds GO:0008121 quinol-cytochrome-c reductase activity as a NEW row —
with the caveat (recorded in the row's reason) that no single-turnover
quinol-oxidation/PpcA-reduction assay has been reported, and that the in vivo
quinone-pool step still rests on genetics plus the structural quinone site.

Pathway context (CbcL, CbcBA)

ImcH is one of at least three inner-membrane routes out of the menaquinol pool, used
in distinct potential windows: CbcL below about -0.1 V (PMID:26407054) and CbcBA near
-0.21 V (PMID:34423503). The windows matter for the annotation: ImcH is not the
Geobacter quinol dehydrogenase, it is the high-potential one, and no GO term captures
that conditionality.

Note on the CMMMech grounding

CMMMech records (asbolane, akaganeite, feitknechtite, pyrochroite,
rhodium, chromite-ore-processing-residue) cite Q747K6 as a representative protein
example with functional_role phrasings such as "required for extracellular electron
transfer to high-redox-potential Fe(III)/Mn(IV) acceptors". For Fe(III) chelates,
Mn(IV) birnessite and high-potential electrodes that is supported by the deletion
phenotypes above. Two qualifications are worth recording:

  • The akaganeite record's supports: support claim is the only one anchored to an
    experiment that actually manipulated imcH (Levar et al. 2017, akaganeite at
    pH 6.8/6.3). The rhodium and chromite-ore-processing-residue entries correctly
    flag supports: partial with the explanation that individual cytochrome genes were
    not mutated; there is no published evidence that ImcH is involved in Rh(III)
    reduction, and its role there is an extrapolation from redox potential alone.
  • "Required for extracellular electron transfer to ... acceptors" is right only inside
    the high-potential window; the same strains reduce low-potential Fe(III) oxides
    without ImcH via CbcL/CbcBA. A reader of the record alone could take ImcH to be
    required for Geobacter metal reduction generally, which the primary literature
    contradicts. No CMMMech file was edited as part of this review.

core_functions:
- description: >-
    High-potential exit from the menaquinol pool. ImcH sits in the inner membrane with
    its heme-bearing domain in the periplasm, is reduced by menaquinol at a binding
    site on the periplasmic face, and passes the electrons to the periplasmic triheme
    cytochrome PpcA, releasing the quinol protons on the same side so that the step is
    electroneutral. This is the step the cell uses whenever the extracellular acceptor
    sits above about -0.1 V versus SHE; below that threshold CbcL and CbcBA take over,
    which is why imcH deletion blocks Fe(III) citrate, Fe(III)-EDTA, Mn(IV) oxide and
    high-potential electrode reduction while leaving low-potential Fe(III) oxides and
    fumarate respiration untouched. The electron-carrier machinery is part of the same
    single activity rather than a second one: seven covalently bound c-type hemes,
    titrating from -150 to -358 mV, relay the electrons from the quinol-oxidising heme 1
    pocket through the periplasm-facing domain to the solvent-exposed terminal heme, where
    PpcA (bound more tightly than its four homologues) collects them for onward transfer
    to the outer-membrane conduits and the mineral or electrode acceptor. The term carries
    transmembrane proton-transporter parents inherited from the cytochrome bc1 archetype
    that do not apply to ImcH; see the GO:0008121 row and the proposed term.

suggested_questions:
- question: >-
    How should GO represent an activity that is conditional on the redox potential of
    the terminal acceptor? ImcH, CbcL and CbcBA all oxidise the same menaquinol pool
    and differ only in the potential window in which they operate, and no existing term
    distinguishes them.
- question: >-
    Is PpcA the obligate physiological acceptor of ImcH in vivo, or can any of the five
    Ppc cytochromes substitute, given that they are described as physiologically
    equivalent but differ in expression and heme potentials?
- question: >-
    How much of the proton motive force does ImcH actually build, and in which component?
    The voltammetric evidence makes the step electroneutral, so it adds nothing to the
    membrane potential, yet the same work argues that releasing the protons into the
    periplasm contributes to the pH gradient and would make ImcH the only Geobacter
    respiratory membrane protein known to maintain a pmf. Measuring the two components
    separately would settle whether the energetic advantage of the high-potential pathway
    lies in that proton release, in the larger potential drop available to the acceptor,
    or in the proton-coupled electron transfer of the downstream Ppc cytochromes.
- question: >-
    Should GO:0008121 quinol-cytochrome-c reductase activity keep its transmembrane
    proton-transporter parents? The term is the only one naming both a quinol donor and a
    cytochrome c acceptor, but it is is_a GO:0015078 and GO:0015453 because the archetypal
    enzyme is the proton-translocating cytochrome bc1 complex, and it therefore asserts
    proton translocation for every NapC/NirT/NrfH-family member that holds its quinol site
    and its acceptor on the same face of the membrane. ImcH is measured to be
    electroneutral, so either the term needs an electroneutral sibling under GO:0016679
    (see proposed_new_terms) or the transporter parents should move to a bc1-specific
    child.
