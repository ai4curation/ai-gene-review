Gene Description

CbcL (GSU0274) is a 619-residue inner (cytoplasmic) membrane cytochrome of the dissimilatory metal-reducing bacterium Geobacter sulfurreducens, built from two domains: a periplasmic multiheme c-type cytochrome domain carrying nine bis-histidine coordinated low-spin hemes (nine CXXCH motifs between residues 35 and 262), and a C-terminal six-helix membrane domain of the transmembrane diheme cytochrome b type. Deletion of cbcL severely impairs respiration to extracellular electron acceptors at or below about -0.1 V versus SHE, including Fe(III) (oxyhydr)oxides and low-potential electrodes, and complementation restores growth, so CbcL defines the low-potential inner-membrane route out of the menaquinone pool that operates alongside the high-potential ImcH route and the still lower-potential CbcBA route. Its periplasmic domain (apparent midpoint potential about -194 mV) transfers electrons directly to the periplasmic triheme cytochrome PpcA, forming a weak redox complex. CbcL is thought to oxidize menaquinol, but quinol oxidation has not been demonstrated. Loss of CbcL raises growth yield on Fe(III), consistent with the low-potential route conserving less energy per electron.

Core Functions

Low-potential inner-membrane electron carrier of extracellular respiration. CbcL's periplasmic nonaheme c-type cytochrome domain injects electrons into the periplasmic cytochrome network by reducing the triheme cytochrome PpcA, and CbcL is required for respiration to Fe(III) oxides and electrodes at or below about -0.1 V vs SHE. The electron source is presumed to be menaquinol at the b-heme membrane domain, which has not been shown.

Synthesis

CbcL is a two-domain inner-membrane cytochrome: a six-helix membrane domain predicted to
carry two b-type hemes, and a periplasmic nonaheme c-type cytochrome domain. Genetics
place it as necessary for respiration to extracellular acceptors at or below about
-0.1 V vs SHE (Fe(III) oxides, low-potential electrodes); biochemistry shows the
periplasmic domain can pass electrons to the periplasmic triheme cytochrome PpcA. The
working model is that CbcL takes electrons from the menaquinol pool and injects them into
the periplasmic cytochrome network, but quinol oxidation has not been shown.

GO decisions:
- GO:0016491 oxidoreductase activity (IBA): the PAINT node PTN002858933 is seeded by
S. oneidensis SirA (a catalytic sulfite reductase) and E. coli NrfB (an electron
carrier). CbcL shares only the cytochrome-c3-like multiheme domain; the activity
actually demonstrated for CbcL is cytochrome-to-cytochrome electron transfer, so
MODIFY to GO:0009055 electron transfer activity.
- GO:0030313 cell envelope (IEA, ARBA SubCell): true but general; MODIFY to
GO:0005886 plasma membrane (six Phobius TM helices plus literature "inner membrane").
- NEW: GO:0020037 heme binding (IDA, PMID:35620088), GO:0019645 anaerobic electron
transport chain (IMP, PMID:26407054). Comparator check: ImcH (Q747K6) and S. oneidensis
CymA (Q8E8S0), inner-membrane cytochromes in the same role, both carry GO:0009055
and GO:0019645 in GOA (QuickGO query, 2026-10-28), so this is the established
convention, and CbcL does part of the work (it carries the electrons).

CMMMech claims (do not edit CMMMech; notes for its curators)

CMMMech records asbolane, feitknechtite, pyrochroite, akaganeite, rhodium and
chromite-ore-processing-residue cite Q74GH2 with functional_role "Inner-membrane
b/c-type cytochrome CbcL (GSU0274) required for extracellular electron transfer to
lower-redox-potential Fe(III) phases" (or "...metal and mineral acceptors").

  • "Required ... lower-redox-potential Fe(III) phases" is supported for Fe(III)
    (oxyhydr)oxides, Fe(III)-citrate in its late, low-potential phase, and electrodes at or
    below -0.1 V (PMID:26407054, PMID:28045456).
  • "metal and mineral acceptors" (rhodium, COPR) is broader than the evidence: no cbcL
    mutant has been tested with Rh(III), Pd(II) or Cr(VI); the CMMMech evidence entries
    themselves say individual cytochrome genes were not mutated. Representative-example
    status is fine; "required" should not be read as shown for those metals.
  • For asbolane/Mn phases, Levar 2017 supports a role in late-stage Mn and Fe mineral
    reduction generally, but CbcL's direct contact with any mineral is not expected (it is
    an inner-membrane protein; outer-membrane and extracellular cytochromes mediate
    contact).
  • The "b/c-type cytochrome" description is right; whether CbcL oxidises menaquinol is
    still an inference (PMID:28045456 caveat).

CbcL (GEOSL, UniProtKB:Q74GH2) — CXXCH motif scan

Purpose

The review's GO:0020037 (heme binding) reason uses the number of CXXCH c-type
heme-binding motifs in the sequence as corroboration for the nine c-type hemes
measured on the purified periplasmic domain. This is the reproducible artifact
behind that count, replacing an asserted number with a checkable one.

The IDA evidence (pyridine hemochromogen quantitation, UV-vis/CD/NMR on the
purified 30–279 construct) carries that annotation on its own; this scan is
corroboration only, and a motif count is a sequence prediction, not a
measurement of bound heme.

Method

cxxch_scan.py reads the amino-acid sequence from the committed UniProt flat
file genes/GEOSL/cbcL/cbcL-uniprot.txt and reports every match of the regular
expression C..CH, using a lookahead so overlapping motifs (which can share a
cysteine in multiheme cytochromes) are not lost. Standard library only, no
network access. Run from the repository root:

python genes/GEOSL/cbcL/cbcL-bioinformatics/cxxch_scan.py

cysteine):

Interpretation

All nine motifs lie N-terminal to the first predicted transmembrane helix
(Phobius 286–314), i.e. in the periplasmic multiheme c domain. The count of
nine agrees with the nine c-type hemes reported for that domain, and no CXXCH
motif falls in the membrane domain — consistent with its two hemes being b-type
(non-covalently bound) rather than c-type.

The scan says nothing about the b-type hemes, which are inferred from the
NarI/FdnI-type diheme cytochrome b fold and remain unmeasured for this
protein.
