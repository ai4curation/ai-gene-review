## Enzyme and complex

- Purified enzyme from strain Delta H: high-MW aggregate of alpha, beta, gamma subunits
  [PMID:3663585 "composed of three subunits, alpha (Mr 47K), beta (Mr 31K), and gamma (Mr 26K)"].
- Cofactors of the complex: [PMID:3663585 "The tightly associated redox cofactors per 115K species are 0.6-0.7 nickel atom, 0.8-0.9 flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD), and 13-14 iron atoms in iron-sulfur centers."]
  At that time placement was unresolved: [PMID:3663585 "There is iron associated with the alpha-subunit, but placement of the nickel and FAD has not been established."]
  The alpha-subunit iron is explained by the Fe of the [NiFe] centre and a second conserved metal ion (see structure), not by an Fe-S cluster.
- Operon frhADGB; FrhD does not copurify [PMID:2207102 "encoding a polypeptide (delta) that does not copurify with the active enzyme"].
- UniProt FUNCTION: [file:METTH/frhA/frhA-uniprot.txt "Reduces the physiological low-potential two-electron acceptor coenzyme F420, and the artificial one-electron acceptor methylviologen."]

## Cofactor placement by subunit (ortholog structure, M. marburgensis)

- [PMID:24569482 "Frh is a heterotrimeric enzyme composed of the 43 kDa subunit FrhA that contains a [NiFe]-center, the 26 kDa subunit FrhG with three [4Fe4S] clusters, and the 31 kDa iron–sulphur flavoprotein FrhB, which contains the F420-binding site and has one [4Fe4S] cluster and an FAD."]
- [NiFe] ligands: [PMID:23483797 "The [NiFe] center is located near four cysteine residues, Cys 63, 66, 380, and 383, which are fully conserved in all [NiFe]-hydrogenases."]
  In P19496 the same positions (C63, C66, C380, C383) are Cys (checked against the UniProt sequence), matching the UniProt FT BINDING Ni(2+) features.
- Second conserved ion: [PMID:23483797 "The conserved residues Glu44, Glu229, and His386 coordinate the ion."] In P19496, E44, E229 and H386 are conserved at the same positions.
- C-terminal processing: [PMID:23483797 "No density is present beyond His386, and there is no room in the structure for the final 19 amino acids of the protein."] and [PMID:23483797 "We can conclude that FrhA is posttranslationally modified in the same way, likely by FrhD"]. P19496 is 405 aa with H386, so the final 19 residues (387-405) are a probable C-terminal propeptide; UniProt shows CHAIN 2..405 with no PROPEP. Worth a question to UniProt, not asserted as a defect (inferred from ortholog).
- Group 3 [NiFe]-hydrogenase, cytoplasmic: [PMID:23483797 "The cytoplasmic F420-reducing [NiFe]-hydrogenase (Frh) belongs to the group 3 [NiFe]-hydrogenases, which catalyze the reversible reduction of the soluble hydride carrier, NAD(P) or F420."]

Conclusion: FrhA carries only the H2-activating [NiFe] site. FAD and all four [4Fe4S] clusters are on FrhB and FrhG. The F420 hydride transfer happens at the FrhB FAD. So:
- nickel cation binding: true of FrhA (core).
- FAD binding, iron-sulfur cluster binding: properties of partner subunits, wrongly placed on FrhA by InterPro2GO mapping of the FrhA family entry IPR017682 -> REMOVE.
- F420 hydrogenase activity: activity of the FrhABG complex; FrhA contributes the H2 half-reaction -> contributes_to (as in Msed_1375, pattern 16).
- ferredoxin hydrogenase activity: wrong acceptor. Frh reduces F420 (and methylviologen artificially), not ferredoxin. IPR018194 (Ni-binding site PROSITE pattern shared by all [NiFe]-hydrogenase large subunits) is mapped to ferredoxin hydrogenase, which over-reaches for group 3 enzymes -> REMOVE.

## Physiology

- Role in methanogenesis: [PMID:23483797 "Four electrons are provided through the reduced form of the coenzyme F420, which is regenerated by Frh."]
- Redundancy with the Hmd-Mtd cycle (M. maripaludis): [PMID:18487331 "Their analysis demonstrates that either pathway supports growth on H2 and CO2."] So Frh catalyses a step of CO2-reducing methanogenesis but is not strictly essential where Hmd/Mtd exist; the Methanothermobacter genome also encodes Hmd and Mtd. Nickel limitation lowers Frh activity in M. marburgensis (cited in PMID:18487331).
