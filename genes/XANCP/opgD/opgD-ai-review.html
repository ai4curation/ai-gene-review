Gene Description

OpgD from Xanthomonas campestris pv. campestris ATCC 33913 is a GH186-family anomer-inverting transglycosylase. Recombinant protein converts linear beta-1,2-glucan into a cyclic 16-glucose product containing fifteen beta-1,2 linkages and one alpha-1,6 linkage. This chemistry supports its role in osmoregulated periplasmic glucan biosynthesis and differs from the hydrolysis performed by Escherichia coli OpgD. Its Tat-type signal and conserved localization support periplasmic function, although native export, cleavage and pathway flux have not been directly measured in the cited assays.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

alpha-1,6-cyclized beta-1,2-glucohexadecaose synthase activity

Definition: Catalysis of an anomer-inverting transglycosylation that converts a linear beta-1,2-glucan substrate into a cyclic 16-glucose product containing fifteen beta-1,2-glucosidic linkages and one alpha-1,6-glucosidic linkage. The reaction cleaves the donor chain and forms the alpha-1,6 ring-closing bond rather than using water as the glycosyl acceptor.

Justification: Current official GO searches did not establish a term for this substrate/product-specific cyclization. GO:0033913 specifies hydrolysis, while cyclomaltodextrin glucanotransferase acts on a different glucan linkage system. A dedicated term distinguishes the experimentally characterized XccOpgD reaction from E. coli OpgD hydrolysis and from nonspecific family-wide transfers. No invented GO or EC identifier is assigned.

Parent term: glycosyltransferase activity
