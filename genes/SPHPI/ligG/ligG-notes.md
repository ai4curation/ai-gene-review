- [PMID:12618439](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12618439/): LigG removes glutathione from the conjugate produced by LigF; alternative native activity explains the incomplete ligG knockout phenotype.
  > The ligG gene product specifically catalyzed the elimination of
glutathione from GS-HPV generated by the action of LigF. This reaction then
produces an achiral compound, beta-hydroxypropiovanillone, which is further
degraded by this strain.
- [PMID:23058289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23058289/): LigG is structurally and biochemically related to the omega class of cysteine-containing GSTs.
  > The X-ray structure and
biochemical properties of SpLigG indicate that this enzyme belongs to the omega
class of glutathione transferases.
- [PMID:26940872](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26940872/): LigG catalyzes GSH-dependent cleavage of betaR-GS-HPV, yielding HPV and GSSG.
  > The final step consists of a GSH-dependent lyase, LigG, that catalyzes the elimination of the GSH thioether linkage in (βR)-GS-HPV to produce glutathione disulfide (GSSG) and γ-hydroxypropiovanillone (HPV).
- [PMID:33244017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33244017/): Purified LigG shows about 300-fold preference for betaR-GS-HPV, while native mutant experiments distinguish overlapping LigG/LigQ activity from the LigQ-dependent betaS route.
  > Purified LigG showed an approximately 300-fold higher specific
activity for (βR)-GS-HPV than that for (βS)-GS-HPV, whereas purified LigQ showed
an approximately six-fold higher specific activity for (βS)-GS-HPV than that for
(βR)-GS-HPV. Analyses of mutants of ligG, ligQ, and both genes revealed that
SYK-6 converted (βR)-GS-HPV using both LigG and LigQ, whereas only LigQ was
involved in converting (βS)-GS-HPV.

## Annotation decisions and scope

- GO:0005737: UNDECIDED. The proximate source is TreeGrafter node PANTHER:PTN008126690, not a direct localization experiment. LigG structure, GST classification, recombinant solubility and participation in a glutathione-dependent pathway are consistent with cytoplasmic activity but do not by themselves locate the native protein. PMID:23058289 and PMID:26940872 establish enzyme structure and chemistry, not a resolved native compartment. No contradictory localization is known and no source defect is asserted; a direct localization study or documented source lineage is needed.

The proposed molecular function is GS-HPV glutathione-dependent deglutathionylase activity. A specific supported proposed term is used instead of an ill-fitting current GO term. The evidence concerns lignin-derived model dimers and downstream intermediates; it does not by itself show attack on intact lignin polymer. The description is a biological summary independent of the module audit.
