| Identity and architecture | **DMI1 (Q6RHR6)** is the *Medicago truncatula* DOES NOT/DOESN’T MAKE INFECTIONS 1 channel, corresponding to Lotus CASTOR/POLLUX and pea SYM8. It has four predicted transmembrane segments and tandem cytosolic RCK1–RCK2 regulatory domains that assemble into a tetrameric gating ring. (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000037) | DMI1 is distinct from the interacting CNGC15 Ca²⁺ channels. “CASTOR” and “POLLUX” principally denote *Lotus japonicus* homologues, so their results cannot automatically be assigned to MtDMI1. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000005) |
| Subcellular localization | A functional DMI1–GFP fusion preferentially localized to the **inner nuclear membrane**: immunogold analysis counted 151 particles across 16 nuclei, versus 20 particles across 22 control nuclei. (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000030) | Signals occurred at both nuclear membranes, but the distribution favored the inner membrane. This places DMI1 beside the nucleoplasmic Ca²⁺-release machinery rather than at the plasma membrane. (pqac-00000023, pqac-00000025) |
| Direct ion-channel evidence | In symmetrical KCl planar-bilayer recordings, MtDMI1 conductance was approximately **64 pS**, compared with approximately **175 pS** for LjCASTOR; DMI1 also had a longer mean open time than LjCASTOR (16 ± 1 versus 4 ± 1 ms). (pqac-00000024, pqac-00000029) | This is direct MtDMI1 channel evidence consistent with K⁺ conductance and a counter-ion role. The 175-pS comparator is a *Lotus* homologue, not MtDMI1. |
| Ca²⁺ selectivity controversy | A 2019 study characterized **LjCASTOR** as a Ca²⁺-regulated, Ca²⁺-selective channel, challenging the K⁺ counter-ion model; the 2024 expert review nevertheless judged the CNGC15 Ca²⁺ channel–DMI1 counter-ion–MCA8 pump model the best-supported working model. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000011, pqac-00000012) | The strongest Ca²⁺-selectivity result was obtained for truncated/reconstituted **Lotus CASTOR**, not directly for full-length MtDMI1 in its native nuclear membrane. DMI1’s physiological permeant ion therefore remains unresolved. (pqac-00000010, pqac-00000038) |
| RCK gating and CNGC15 dependence | The dominant MtDMI1 **S760N** substitution disrupts the RCK1–RCK2 inhibitory interface, causing spontaneous nuclear Ca²⁺ oscillations, symbiotic-gene expression and nodulation. CNGC15 RNAi nearly abolished the oscillations; only **3 of 37** cells retained weak activity. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000037) | Autoactive DMI1 alone is insufficient for robust oscillations: its phenotype depends on CNGC15, and mutations disrupting DMI1 gating-ring Ca²⁺ binding abolish activation. This supports DMI1 as regulator/counter-ion partner rather than the sole Ca²⁺-release pore. (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015) |
| 2024 evolutionary evidence | In bryophyte–legume tests, MtDMI1 restored arbuscular-mycorrhizal colonization in *Marchantia paleacea dmi1*. Conversely, MpaDMI1 restored nuclear Ca²⁺ oscillations—but not full colonization—in *M. truncatula dmi1-1* and caused spontaneous oscillations without added LCO. (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000046) | The bidirectional tests show ancient conservation of Ca²⁺-oscillator function but divergence in gating and host-specific outputs. The proposed RCK salt-bridge mechanism for the L503R/S760N effects is supported partly by AlphaFold2 modeling rather than an intact-channel structure. (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000046) |
| 2025 mechanistic and translational update | A K⁺-selective **DMI1TVGYG** pore variant restored Nod-factor-induced oscillations and nodulation in dmi1-1, while DMI1 Ca²⁺-binding mutations altered or abolished CNGC15-GOF oscillation frequencies. These findings support DMI1 as a frequency-setting **pacemaker**, whereas CNGC15 is the Ca²⁺-release channel. (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000033) | The field implementation engineered **CNGC15-GOF in wheat**, increasing AM colonization across five plots and in subsequent near-isogenic lines; it was not a DMI1-engineered crop. DMI1’s application remains mechanistic and a prospective engineering target. (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000035) |
