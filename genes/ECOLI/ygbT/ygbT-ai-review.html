Gene Description

ygbT, also known as cas1, encodes the CRISPR-associated protein Cas1 of the Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system. Cas1 and Cas2 are the two most widely conserved Cas proteins and together constitute the adaptation machinery that gives CRISPR immunity its heritable, adaptive character. Cas1 is a metal-dependent homodimer; two Cas1 dimers sandwich a Cas2 dimer to form a Cas1(4)-Cas2(2) heterohexamer that binds a protospacer duplex across a flat surface spanning all three dimers. Within that complex Cas1 is the catalytic subunit: it executes the integration chemistry itself as a pair of transesterification reactions, in which 3'-hydroxyl ends of the captured protospacer attack the phosphodiester backbone at the leader-proximal CRISPR repeat, producing a staggered, nicked intermediate that host replication machinery repairs into a new spacer flanked by a duplicated repeat. The reaction resembles retroviral integration and DNA transposition in mechanism and in the reverse (disintegration) activity it displays in vitro, although Cas1 does not share the RNase H fold of the retroviral integrase superfamily. It requires a divalent cation, with Mg(2+) used in vivo and Mn(2+) or Co(2+) also supporting the chemistry in vitro, and the active site is built from E141, H208 and D221 within a cluster of conserved basic residues. Sequence and structural preferences intrinsic to Cas1, together with the Cas1-Cas2 complex acting as a ruler for duplex length, direct integration to the first repeat and set the 33 bp spacer size; the host nucleoid-associated protein IHF bends the leader to orient the complex, which is what makes integration unidirectional. In vitro the purified protein also cleaves single-stranded nucleic acids and a broad range of branched DNA structures, including Holliday junctions, replication forks, 5'-flaps, 3'-flaps and splayed arms; this reflects the integrase active site acting on surrogate branch-point substrates rather than a separate resolvase activity. Cas1 is cytoplasmic, localizes to discrete nucleoid foci in a minority of cells after DNA damage, interacts physically and genetically with the RecBC and RuvAB recombination machinery, and a ygbT deletion is viable but hypersensitive to mitomycin C and UV with impaired chromosome segregation; the mechanistic basis of that phenotype remains unresolved, and RecBCD is independently implicated as the source of the DNA fragments Cas1 captures. The gene is the penultimate gene of the casABCDE-ygbT-ygbF operon, which is silenced by H-NS and induced by LeuO and by the BaeSR envelope-stress system.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

protospacer integrase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the integration of a double-stranded protospacer DNA fragment into a CRISPR array, by concerted transesterification in which the 3'-hydroxyl ends of the protospacer attack phosphodiester bonds at the boundaries of a CRISPR repeat, cleaving the target strands and covalently joining the protospacer ends to the resulting repeat 5'-phosphates. Requires a divalent metal cation.

Justification: GO has no molecular-function term for the strand-transfer chemistry that Cas1 performs. GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease activity, the term currently used, captures only the cleavage half of a concerted cleave-and-join reaction, and joining is what distinguishes integration from degradation and is what the in vitro assays actually measure. The nearest existing alternative, GO:0008907 integrase activity, is scoped to phage and retroviral integrases, and Cas1 is explicitly not a member of that superfamily - it lacks the RNase H fold - so it would be a wrong-family assertion rather than a parent. A dedicated term would also give the disintegration activity, the active-site mutant phenotypes and the Cas1-Cas2 molecular-ruler behaviour a single function to attach to. Until such a term exists, GO:0004520 is retained in core_functions as a true but partial statement rather than leaving the slot empty.

Core Functions

Metal-dependent DNA strand-transfer (integrase) activity carried out as the catalytic subunit of the Cas1(4)-Cas2(2) adaptation complex: Cas1 cleaves the phosphodiester backbone at the leader-proximal CRISPR repeat and, in the same concerted transesterification chemistry, joins the 3'-hydroxyl ends of a captured protospacer to the resulting repeat 5'-ends, producing the staggered nicked intermediate that host replication converts into a new spacer plus a duplicated repeat. The reaction requires a divalent cation and an active site built from E141, H208 and D221, and runs in reverse as a disintegration reaction in vitro.

Scope note

Cas1 is used as a reagent in engineered spacer-recording ("DNA tape recorder")
devices. That is a laboratory application, not a biological function of the gene, and
it is deliberately absent from the description and from any proposed term.

Proposed new term

GO has no molecular-function term for the DNA strand-transfer chemistry that Cas1
actually performs. GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease activity captures the cleavage half
and nothing of the joining half, yet joining is what distinguishes integration from
degradation and is what the in vitro assays measure. The closest analogue elsewhere
in GO (GO:0008907 integrase activity) is scoped to phage/retroviral integrases, and
Cas1 explicitly lacks that superfamily's fold:
PMID:25707795
is the cleanest statement that the activity is a single integrase-type activity rather
than two. I am proposing protospacer integrase activity as a top-level
proposed_new_terms entry and keeping GO:0004520 in core_functions.molecular_function
rather than leaving the slot empty, because GO:0004520 is a true (if partial)
statement and the module already depends on it.
