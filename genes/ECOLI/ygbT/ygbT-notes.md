# ygbT (cas1) — Escherichia coli K-12, UniProt Q46896

Research journal for the gene review. Every assertion below carries inline
provenance; quotes are verbatim from the cached publications in `publications/`.

## Identity

- UniProt primary gene name is **ygbT**; **cas1** is the functional synonym, and
  GOA/EcoCyc use `ygbT` as the symbol while UniProt's recommended protein name is
  "CRISPR-associated endonuclease Cas1". The gene is `b2755` / `JW2725` and sits in
  the `casABCDE-ygbT-ygbF` operon, immediately upstream of `ygbF` (cas2, P45956).
- Type I-E CRISPR-Cas system. Cas1 and Cas2 are the two universally conserved Cas
  proteins and together constitute the adaptation (spacer-acquisition) machinery:
  [PMID:27899566 "The universally conserved Cas1 and Cas2 form an integration complex that is known to mediate the protospacer invasion into the CRISPR array."]

## What Cas1 does: it is the integrase, and it does the chemistry itself

The decisive papers are Nuñez et al. 2014 and 2015.

- Cas1 is catalytic, Cas2 is not:
  [PMID:25707795 "Cas1 is the catalytic subunit and Cas2 substantially increases integration activity."]
- The reconstituted reaction is an integrase/transposase-type reaction, not a simple
  nuclease reaction:
  [PMID:25707795 "Here we show that the purified Cas1-Cas2 complex integrates oligonucleotide DNA substrates into acceptor DNA to yield products similar to those generated by retroviral integrases and transposases."]
  and
  [PMID:25707795 "CRISPR spacer integration shares mechanistic similarities with retroviral integration and DNA transposition"]
- Substrate requirements place the chemistry at a 3'-OH:
  [PMID:25707795 "Protospacer DNA with free 3'-OH ends and supercoiled target DNA are required"]
- Active-site mutants separate the two subunits cleanly:
  [PMID:25707795 "Consistent with these data, Cas1 active site mutants H208A and D221A were defective for protospacer integration in vitro, whereas the Cas2 E9Q active-site mutant supported integration"]
  and in vivo
  [PMID:25707795 "Bacteria expressing Cas1 active-site mutants, but not active-site mutants of Cas2, are incapable of acquiring new spacers in vivo, demonstrating the catalytic role of Cas1 during spacer acquisition"]
- Independently, the D221A active-site substitution abolishes array expansion in the
  Qimron adaptation assay:
  [PMID:22402487 "no expansion of the array was detected when pCas1+2 was mutated to encode Cas1D221A"]
- The nicking/joining step was mapped directly on the chromosome:
  [PMID:24920831 "The results obtained from the Southern blots presented above indicated a site-specific nicking at the first repeat in which the catalytic center of the Cas1 protein is involved."]
  with the joining geometry
  [PMID:24920831 "the non-template strand is nicked at the leader-repeat junction with subsequent joining of the resulting 5′-end of the first repeat with the 3′-end of the incoming spacer"]
- The chemistry is a transesterification, as made explicit by Rollie et al.:
  [PMID:26284603 "the 3′-end of one strand of the incoming DNA is joined to the end of the CRISPR repeat in a trans-esterification (TES) reaction"]

So: Cas1 is a metal-dependent, DNA-strand-transfer (integrase) enzyme whose
physiological output is a new spacer plus a duplicated repeat at the leader end of
the array. GO has no "DNA strand-transfer / integrase" molecular-function term that
fits; `GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease activity` is the available term and is the one the
module `crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity` asserts as the required function for the
Cas1 annoton.

## Both genes are required, in vivo

[PMID:22402487 "Strains harboring plasmids encoding Cas1 or Cas2 alone (pCas1 and pCas2, respectively) did not show observable expansion of their array"]
and
[PMID:22402487 "Induced expression of E. coli Cas1 and Cas2 resulted in acquisition of spacers, as determined by PCR amplification of the repeat-spacer units adjacent to the leader terminus in CRISPR array I of both strains"]

This is the basis of the IMP rows on `GO:0043571` for both genes.

## Architecture: Cas1 is an obligate dimer inside a Cas1(4)-Cas2(2) hexamer

- The apo protein is a dimer:
  [PMID:21219465 "The structure of the full-length YgbT showed that this protein is a homodimer"]
- The adaptation complex:
  [PMID:24793649 "The overall architecture of the asymmetric unit is a heterohexameric complex consisting of two Cas1 dimers (Cas1a-b and Cas1c-d) that sandwich one Cas2 dimer"]
- Complex formation is required for function:
  [PMID:24793649 "In addition to the catalytic function of Cas1, its ability to assemble with Cas2 is also essential for spacer acquisition."]
  and
  [PMID:24793649 "Mutations in either Cas1 or Cas2 that disrupt Cas1–Cas2 complex formation in vitro also interfere with spacer acquisition in vivo."]
- The DNA-bound complex behaves as a ruler:
  [PMID:26503043 "Our results uncover the structural basis for foreign DNA capture and the mechanism by which Cas1-Cas2 functions as a molecular ruler to dictate the sequence architecture of CRISPR loci."]
  with the measured duplex length
  [PMID:26503043 "Importantly, however, optimal substrates include a central 23 base pair helical region flanked by five single-stranded nucleotides on each 3′ end."]
  held by a shared arginine clamp
  [PMID:26503043 "The Arginine Clamp interacts with the middle of the duplex region where four Arg residues coordinate each DNA strand: Cas1 R41 and Cas2 R16, R77, R78"]

## Metal dependence and nucleic-acid binding

- [PMID:21219465 "We found a prominent divalent metal cation-dependent nuclease activity against single-stranded (ss) DNAs and a lower activity against ssRNAs"]
- Active-site/basic-residue cluster:
  [PMID:21219465 "YgbT contains a larger cluster of highly conserved basic residues (R112, R123, R138, R146, R163, K211, and K224) positioned around the potential metal-binding site (E141, H208, D221)"]
- Binding is to DNA (and, weakly, RNA) structures:
  [PMID:21219465 "Purified YgbT also bound to 5′-flaps structures, HJs, ssDNAs and ssRNAs, and produced oligomeric complexes, as detected using mobility shift assays"]

The UniProt COFACTOR block records Mg(2+), with Mn(2+) and Co(2+) also supporting the
transesterification. `GO:0046872 metal ion binding` is therefore right and
appropriately general; narrowing to magnesium would overstate the specificity.
`GO:0003676 nucleic acid binding` (IEA, InterPro) is one level too general: the
physiological ligand is DNA, and UniProt carries the `DNA-binding` keyword, so
`GO:0003677 DNA binding` is the better term.

## The branched-DNA nuclease activities: real IDA, but not three separate functions

Babu et al. 2011 is the source of three IDA/IMP rows (`GO:0008821`, `GO:0017108`,
`GO:0006974`). The experiments are genuine and directly observed:

- [PMID:21219465 "the purified Cas1 protein of Escherichia coli (YgbT) exhibits nuclease activity against single-stranded and branched DNAs including Holliday junctions, replication forks and 5'-flaps"]
- Holliday-junction cleavage with ligatable, RuvC-like products:
  [PMID:21219465 "purified YgbT displayed significant activity against the HJ1 substrate, producing a nicked duplex with electrophoretic mobility similar to that of the RuvC product"]
- but the substrate range is broad, not flap-specific:
  [PMID:21219465 "Analysis of the cleavage products by denaturing PAGE revealed that YgbT introduced a limited number of cuts into the replication fork and 5′-flap structures, but produced multiple cleavage products with the HJ3, 3′-flap, and splayed arm substrates"]
  and
  [PMID:21219465 "YgbT cleaves in vitro a broad range of branched DNA substrates (asymmetrical Holliday junction, replication fork, 5′-flap, 3′-flap and splayed arm substrates)"]

Rollie et al. then reinterpreted all of this as the integrase active site running
backwards, i.e. disintegration, not a distinct resolvase/flap-endonuclease function:

- [PMID:26284603 "Many integrases process the ends of the integrated DNA using a nuclease activity, which occurs at the same active site as the integrase activity"]
- [PMID:26284603 "It is likely that the TES reaction catalyzed by Cas1 with branched DNA substrates in vitro represents the reverse or disintegration of an integration intermediate"]
- [PMID:26284603 "Efficient TES of branched DNA substrates with a 5′-flap or duplex arm is clearly possible for both S. solfataricus and E. coli Cas1 in vitro."]

Term-by-term adjudication (this is the point the brief asked me to argue):

- **`GO:0004519 endonuclease activity`** (IBA from GO_REF:0000033 + IEA from
  GO_REF:0000120). Not an over-annotation — Cas1 really is an endonuclease — but one
  level too general when `GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease activity` is available and is
  already separately annotated by InterPro. **MODIFY** to `GO:0004520` on both rows.
  The IBA is a sound phylogenetic judgment (Cas1 is present in essentially all
  CRISPR-Cas systems and the nuclease active site is family-defining), so the
  objection is purely to the term's granularity, not to the propagation; Q46896
  appearing in its own WITH/FROM is expected, because its own experimental
  annotations are among the descendant evidences behind the IBD.
- **`GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease activity`** (IEA, InterPro IPR019851). **ACCEPT**.
  This is the closest existing MF term to the integrase chemistry and is the term the
  module requires for the Cas1 annoton.
- **`GO:0008821 crossover junction DNA endonuclease activity`** (IDA). The GO
  definition is "Catalysis of the endonucleolytic cleavage at a junction such as a
  reciprocal single-stranded crossover between two homologous DNA duplexes (Holliday
  junction)" — no exclusivity clause, and Babu et al. directly show exactly that,
  with ligatable products. The annotation is therefore **definitionally sound** and I
  will not remove an IDA I cannot contradict. But it is in-vitro activity of the
  integrase active site on a surrogate branched substrate; no HJ-resolvase role for
  Cas1 in vivo has been established, and E. coli already has RuvABC. **KEEP_AS_NON_CORE.**
- **`GO:0017108 5'-flap endonuclease activity`** (IDA). Different verdict, on the
  term definition rather than the data. GO:0017108 is defined as "Catalysis of the
  cleavage of a 5' flap structure in DNA, **but not other DNA structures**; processes
  the 5' ends of Okazaki fragments in lagging strand DNA synthesis." Both halves fail
  here: the *same* paper shows cleavage of HJs, 3'-flaps, splayed arms and replication
  forks, so the exclusivity clause is violated, and Cas1 has no role in Okazaki
  fragment processing (that is the Pol I 5'-3' exonuclease domain in bacteria, as Babu
  et al. themselves note). This is a term-fit problem, not a doubt about the
  experiment, so the right move is to generalize: **MODIFY** to `GO:0004520`.
- **`GO:0003676 nucleic acid binding`** (IEA). **MODIFY** to `GO:0003677 DNA binding`.
- **`GO:0046872 metal ion binding`** (IEA). **ACCEPT**.

## The DNA-repair story (`GO:0006974`)

The 2011 paper's second claim is a DNA-repair function:

- [PMID:21219465 "YgbT physically interacts with several key repair proteins including RecB, RecC and RuvB"]
- [PMID:21219465 "Fluorescence microscopy showed that YFP-labeled YgbT, as well as RuvABC and RecN, formed discrete fluorescent foci on the nucleoids after 120 min of MMC treatment."]
- the phenotype is MMC/UV sensitivity and a chromosome-segregation defect (UniProt
  DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE summarises this from the same paper).

I am not removing this. It is an IMP with a deliberately weak qualifier
(`acts_upstream_of_or_within`) and the phenotype is real. But the mechanism was never
resolved, and the RecBCD link was subsequently re-read in the opposite direction —
RecBCD supplies the DNA fragments that Cas1 captures, rather than Cas1 doing repair
chemistry:
[PMID:26284603 "the E. coli RecBCD helicase–nuclease complex, which processes DNA double-strand breaks for recombination and degrades foreign DNA, is implicated in the generation of viral DNA fragments captured by Cas1 and incorporated into the CRISPR locus as new spacers"]
That reading makes the MMC/UV sensitivity a downstream consequence of a shared
substrate pool rather than evidence that Cas1 performs a step of DNA repair — the
"necessary vs. participating" distinction in CLAUDE.md. So:
**KEEP_AS_NON_CORE**, not a core function, not a `REMOVE`.

## Process terms: `GO:0043571` vs `GO:0099048`

I checked the ontology rather than guessing. These sit in **disjoint** branches:

- `GO:0043571 maintenance of CRISPR repeat elements` ancestors include `GO:0043570`,
  `GO:0006259 DNA metabolic process`, `GO:0051276 chromosome organization`.
- `GO:0099048 CRISPR-cas system` ancestors include `GO:0098542 defense response to
  other organism`, `GO:0006952 defense response`, `GO:0140546`, `GO:0099046`.

Neither is an ancestor of the other, so the two rows are complementary rather than
redundant: `GO:0043571` captures the DNA-level event (array expansion, repeat
duplication) and `GO:0099048` captures the immunological system the event serves.
Both are correct for Cas1 and both are **ACCEPT**. This is the placement question the
brief flagged, and the answer is that nothing needs to move between the two genes —
both genes legitimately carry both terms.

`GO:0051607 defense response to virus` (IEA, UniRule) is *not* an ancestor of
`GO:0099048` either. It is biologically defensible at the system level but it is a
weaker and less informative statement than `GO:0099048`, and for E. coli K-12
specifically the native CRISPR system is H-NS-silenced and Babu et al. recorded that
co-expressing Cas1 with Cascade did not change phage lambda sensitivity. So this one
is an inherited family-level inference rather than a demonstrated property of this
gene: **KEEP_AS_NON_CORE**.

## Protein-binding rows

Four `GO:0005515` rows and two `GO:0042802` rows. The project's protein-binding policy
(enforced by `src/ai_gene_review/validation/protein_binding_policy.py`) permits only
`MODIFY`, `REMOVE` or `UNDECIDED` on a bare `GO:0005515` row — its defect is lack of
functional information, so `KEEP_AS_NON_CORE` and `MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED` are both
excluded. Each row therefore needs a replacement term or a removal that explicitly does
not dispute the interaction:

- `GO:0005515` / PMID:24793649 and PMID:26503043, partner P45956 (Cas2). This is the
  real, structurally defined Cas1-Cas2 interface the hexamer is built on, and it is not
  in doubt. But there is no informative replacement available *on Cas1's side*: the
  bridging role that would justify an adaptor term belongs to Cas2, and Cas1's own
  contribution is the catalysis already stated by `GO:0004520`. Complex membership is
  held by ComplexPortal CPX-996 and the interaction by IntAct. **REMOVE** on
  informativeness grounds, not on correctness.
- `GO:0005515` / PMID:21219465, partners RecB/RecC/RuvB/CasC/CasE. Same paper as the
  repair story; co-purification was reciprocally validated, but the row collapses five
  mechanistically different partners into one uninformative term, so no single
  replacement exists. **REMOVE**, with the content preserved in the `GO:0006974` review
  and in these notes.
- `GO:0005515` / PMID:24561554, partner P76339 (hprS). This comes from a
  genome-scale Y2H map
  [PMID:24561554 "we performed yeast two-hybrid screens of 3,305 baits against 3,606 preys"]
  and the cached full text does not expose this pair at all — no follow-up, no
  biological rationale, and HprS is an unrelated envelope-stress sensor kinase.
  I cannot verify it and I cannot refute it: **UNDECIDED**.
- `GO:0042802 identical protein binding` (x2). Redundant with, and less informative
  than, `GO:0042803 protein homodimerization activity`, which is an IDA from the
  crystal structure. **KEEP_AS_NON_CORE** for both — the protein-binding policy is
  scoped to `GO:0005515` only, and the self-association claim is both true and
  architecturally relevant, so demotion rather than removal is right here.
- `GO:0042803 protein homodimerization activity` (IDA, PMID:21219465). The dimer is
  the functional unit of the integrase and two dimers build the hexamer.
  **ACCEPT**.

## Localization

`GO:0005737 cytoplasm` — EXP from PMID:21219465 (YgbT-YFP microscopy) plus the
UniProt SubCell IEA. Both **ACCEPT**. The MMC-induced nucleoid foci are a
sub-localization under damage and not worth a separate annotation.

## Scope note

Cas1 is used as a reagent in engineered spacer-recording ("DNA tape recorder")
devices. That is a laboratory application, not a biological function of the gene, and
it is deliberately absent from the description and from any proposed term.

## Proposed new term

GO has no molecular-function term for the DNA strand-transfer chemistry that Cas1
actually performs. `GO:0004520 DNA endonuclease activity` captures the cleavage half
and nothing of the joining half, yet joining is what distinguishes integration from
degradation and is what the in vitro assays measure. The closest analogue elsewhere
in GO (`GO:0008907 integrase activity`) is scoped to phage/retroviral integrases, and
Cas1 explicitly lacks that superfamily's fold:
[PMID:25707795 "Cas1 is the catalytic subunit and Cas2 substantially increases integration activity."]
is the cleanest statement that the activity is a single integrase-type activity rather
than two. I am proposing `protospacer integrase activity` as a top-level
`proposed_new_terms` entry and keeping `GO:0004520` in `core_functions.molecular_function`
rather than leaving the slot empty, because `GO:0004520` is a true (if partial)
statement and the module already depends on it.
