Gene Description

ThrA is the bifunctional aspartokinase I/homoserine dehydrogenase I of Escherichia coli. Its N-terminal kinase domain uses ATP to phosphorylate L-aspartate, and its C-terminal dehydrogenase domain reduces aspartate semialdehyde to homoserine. These reactions feed the aspartate-family amino acid pathways. Both activities are threonine regulated. The protein assembles into oligomers, with active dimeric and tetrameric dehydrogenase forms observed under the tested conditions.

2026-10-26 complete native-function review

All 21 seeded rows reviewed. Raw UniProt/GOA source files and every non-review annotation payload are preserved. No NEW rows were proposed.

ThrA has two activities on one polypeptide: aspartate kinase and homoserine dehydrogenase (PMID4364333 genetic cistrons are historical functional subdivisions, not two separate modern gene products). PMID11368768 assays pathway enzyme kinetics; normal cache is abstract-only. PMID204643 directly measures a concentration-dependent dimer/tetramer equilibrium in threonine and reports both forms retain dehydrogenase activity; the inactive monomer was produced by chaotropic treatment. Retain specific self-binding and tetramer assembly as non-core rather than general protein-binding removal.

The current QuickGO definition of GO0004412 names NADP+ specifically, whereas GO0004022 is alcohol oxidation with NAD+. The normally fetched UniProt record separately curates NADP(H) and NAD(H) reactions by similarity to Arabidopsis Q9SA18. Retain the original NAD-dependent alcohol-dehydrogenase and NAD-binding rows as non-core inferred cofactor usage; do not replace them with the NADP-specific term and lose the NAD claim, and do not infer ethanol turnover. No direct target comparison establishing equal physiological NAD/NADP use was recovered. Only kinase and NADP-homoserine chemistry are highlighted as cores. Generic oxidoreductase refinement includes both existing specific activities.
