Gene Description

LptD (formerly Imp/OstA) is an essential 87 kDa integral outer-membrane protein of Escherichia coli and the beta-barrel subunit of the LptDE lipopolysaccharide (LPS) translocon. It has an N-terminal periplasmic beta-jellyroll domain, of the same OstA fold as LptA and LptC, and a C-terminal 26-stranded beta-barrel, the two linked by two interdomain disulfide bonds (Cys31-Cys724 and Cys173-Cys725), at least one of which is needed for function. The outer-membrane lipoprotein LptE sits inside the barrel as a plug. The jellyroll domain docks the C-terminus of periplasmic LptA, completing the LptB2FGC-LptA-LptD protein bridge that spans the envelope and carries LPS from the inner membrane. Arriving LPS is thought to move its core oligosaccharide and O-antigen through the barrel lumen while lipid A enters the outer leaflet through a lateral gate between barrel strands 1 and 26. LptD is folded and inserted by the BAM complex with the help of SurA, Skp, DsbA, DsbC, BepA, LptE and the lipoprotein LptM. Partial loss of LptD increases outer-membrane permeability to detergents, antibiotics, dyes, large maltodextrins and n-hexane; depletion blocks delivery of new LPS to the cell surface and is lethal. Accessory lipoproteins LptM and LptY associate with the translocon and modulate LptD conformation and the stability of its connection to the periplasmic bridge.

Core Functions

LptD is the outer-membrane beta-barrel subunit of the LptDE translocon. Its N-terminal periplasmic beta-jellyroll docks LptA and receives LPS from the periplasmic Lpt bridge. The barrel, plugged by LptE, then moves LPS across the outer membrane: the sugar moieties pass through the lumen and lipid A enters the outer leaflet laterally. This builds the LPS outer leaflet of the Gram-negative outer membrane.

description: >-
  LptD (formerly Imp/OstA) is an essential 87 kDa integral outer-membrane protein of Escherichia coli
  and the beta-barrel subunit of the LptDE lipopolysaccharide (LPS) translocon. It has an N-terminal periplasmic
  beta-jellyroll domain, of the same OstA fold as LptA and LptC, and a C-terminal 26-stranded beta-barrel,
  the two linked by two interdomain disulfide bonds (Cys31-Cys724 and Cys173-Cys725), at least one of
  which is needed for function. The outer-membrane lipoprotein LptE sits inside the barrel as a plug.
  The jellyroll domain docks the C-terminus of periplasmic LptA, completing the LptB2FGC-LptA-LptD protein
  bridge that spans the envelope and carries LPS from the inner membrane. Arriving LPS is thought to move
  its core oligosaccharide and O-antigen through the barrel lumen while lipid A enters the outer leaflet
  through a lateral gate between barrel strands 1 and 26. LptD is folded and inserted by the BAM complex
  with the help of SurA, Skp, DsbA, DsbC, BepA, LptE and the lipoprotein LptM. Partial loss of LptD increases
  outer-membrane permeability to detergents, antibiotics, dyes, large maltodextrins and n-hexane; depletion
  blocks delivery of new LPS to the cell surface and is lethal. Accessory lipoproteins LptM and LptY associate
  with the translocon and modulate LptD conformation and the stability of its connection to the periplasmic
  bridge.

core_functions:
- description: >-
    LptD is the outer-membrane beta-barrel subunit of the LptDE translocon. Its
    N-terminal periplasmic beta-jellyroll docks LptA and receives LPS from the

    the outer membrane: the sugar moieties pass through the lumen and lipid A
    enters the outer leaflet laterally. This builds the LPS outer leaflet of the
    Gram-negative outer membrane.
