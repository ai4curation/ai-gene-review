CC   -!- FUNCTION: Dehydrogenase involved in the degradation of putrescine
CC       (PubMed:15590624, PubMed:22636776). Acts as a non-specific aldehyde
CC       dehydrogenase that can catalyze three separate reactions in putrescine
CC       catabolism: the oxidation of gamma-glutamyl-gamma-aminobutyraldehyde to
CC       gamma-glutamyl-gamma-aminobutyrate (gamma-Glu-GABA), the oxidation 4-
CC       aminobutanal (gamma-aminobutyraldehyde) to 4-aminobutanoate (gamma-
CC       aminobutyrate or GABA) and the oxidation of succinate semialdehyde to
CC       succinate (PubMed:15590624, PubMed:22636776). In vitro, it exhibits
CC       broad substrate specificity for various aliphatic and aromatic
CC       aldehydes (PubMed:18668238). It can use both NAD(+) and NADP(+), but
CC       NAD(+) is significantly preferred over NADP(+) for many aldehydes
CC       (PubMed:18668238). Among the aldehydes tested, 3-hydroxypropanal (3-
CC       hydroxypropionaldehyde or 3-HPA) is the best substrate, followed by 3-
CC       methylbutanal (isovaleraldehyde), propanal (propionaldehyde), butanal
CC       (butyraldehyde), pentanal (valeraldehyde), benzaldehyde, furfural (2-
CC       furaldehyde) and acetaldehyde (PubMed:18668238). The enzyme cannot use
CC       formaldehyde (PubMed:18668238). It might play a role in propionate
CC       and/or acetic acid metabolisms in addition to its function in
CC       putrescine metabolism (PubMed:18668238). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15590624,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:18668238, ECO:0000269|PubMed:22636776}.
