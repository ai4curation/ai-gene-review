Gene Description

fruB (fpr, fruF) of Escherichia coli K-12 encodes FPr, the fructose-inducible multiphosphoryl transfer protein of the fructose phosphoenolpyruvate-dependent phosphotransferase system (PTS). The 376-residue soluble protein fuses an N-terminal fructose-type EIIA domain (phosphohistidine His62), a central M domain and a C-terminal HPr-like domain (phosphohistidine His299). FPr lacks an enzyme I region: the general enzyme I (PtsI) phosphorylates its HPr-like domain, which passes phosphate to the EIIA domain, and EIIA then phosphorylates the EIIB domains of the membrane permease FruA. FPr thereby replaces HPr in the fructose system and supplies the phosphoryl donor for fructose uptake with concomitant formation of fructose 1-phosphate. It associates peripherally with the cytoplasmic face of the membrane-bound FruA permease.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

PTS EIIA-to-EIIB phosphotransferase activity

Definition: Catalysis of phosphate transfer from the phosphorylated histidine of a phosphoenolpyruvate-dependent sugar phosphotransferase system EIIA domain to the cognate EIIB phosphoacceptor.

Justification: GO:0022877 and GO:0090563 describe the terminal transport-coupled transfer to the sugar, catalysed by the EIIB-EIIC permease; no term covers the EIIA-to-EIIB protein-to-protein step that FPr performs. The same term is proposed for the fused FruB proteins of Pseudomonas putida and is defined identically here.

Parent term: transferase activity, transferring phosphorus-containing groups

Supporting Evidence:

  • file:ECOLI/fruB/fruB-uniprot.txt : The PTS EIIA type-2 domain is phosphorylated by phospho-HPr on a histidyl residue. Then, it transfers the phosphoryl group to the PTS EIIB type-2 domain.

Core Functions

Soluble multiphosphoryl transfer protein of the E. coli fructose PTS. Its HPr-like domain is phosphorylated by enzyme I, and its fructose-type EIIA domain then phosphorylates the EIIB domains of the FruA permease, supplying the phosphoryl group for fructose import with formation of fructose 1-phosphate.
