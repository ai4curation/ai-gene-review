Gene Description

IscA is a small (107-residue) cytosolic A-type iron-sulfur cluster carrier protein of the HesB/IscA family, encoded in the E. coli iscRSUA-hscBA-fdx-iscX operon. It forms dimers and tetramers whose three invariant cysteines (Cys35, Cys99, Cys101) bind either mononuclear iron or a [2Fe-2S] cluster bridged between subunits. Purified IscA binds iron with very high affinity and can donate it for IscS-dependent cluster assembly on the scaffold IscU when free iron is limiting; in cells it is iron-loaded under aerobic growth, where oxygen promotes iron binding. IscA can also assemble a [2Fe-2S] cluster and transfer it, over multiple turnovers, to apo-ferredoxin, with which holo-IscA forms a specific complex. Genetically IscA is partially redundant with its SufA paralogue (and the A-type carrier ErpA); the iscA/sufA pair is needed for Fe-S cluster assembly under aerobic but not anaerobic growth. Copper binds IscA and blocks cluster biogenesis under anaerobic copper stress.

Core Functions

A-type carrier that binds a [2Fe-2S] cluster and transfers it to apo-acceptor proteins such as ferredoxin over multiple turnovers, acting downstream of (or in parallel with) the IscU scaffold.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Under physiological conditions does IscA act chiefly as an iron donor to IscU or as an A-type carrier of preformed clusters (from IscU) to [4Fe-4S] acceptor proteins, and does the answer change between aerobic and anaerobic growth?

Q: Which acceptor proteins depend specifically on IscA rather than on its paralogue SufA or the A-type carrier ErpA?
