Gene Description

CasC (also called Cas7 or Cse4) is the backbone subunit of Cascade, the 405-kDa CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex of the Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system, and the only subunit present in multiple copies in the catalytic core - six per complex. Each CasC adopts a hand-like fold of a modified RNA recognition motif palm, a helical fingers domain and a 30-residue thumb loop; the thumb of one subunit folds over the crRNA and inserts into a basic crease on the palm of the next, so that the six copies polymerise into a helical filament wrapped around the guide. CasC binds the crRNA through its phosphate backbone without sequence specificity, introducing a kink every six nucleotides, which divides the guide into six segments of five solvent-exposed bases presented in a pseudo A-form geometry. The arrangement simultaneously shields the guide from cellular nucleases and keeps it available for base pairing, and the bases at the kinks are flipped out of the helix so they do not participate in target recognition. When a target strand invades, CasC fingers and thumbs contact both strands of the resulting guide-target hybrid across the minor groove, stabilising its unusual ribbon conformation, and lysine-rich helices on the two PAM-proximal subunits engage the duplex during target search. CasC is required for resistance to bacteriophage lambda and for plasmid silencing, but not for maturation of the crRNA guide. It also associates with the adaptation nuclease Cas1 (YgbT) outside the Cascade complex.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex

Definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction. The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent is the Csy complex.

Justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic ancestor GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models (CPX-1005, Cascade complex, cross-referenced from this UniProt entry), and the Cas7 backbone in particular is the surface targeted by several families of phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible.

Parent term: ribonucleoprotein complex

Core Functions

Oligomerises into the helical backbone of Cascade, with each subunit's thumb folding over the guide and locking into the palm of the next, thereby building the filament that holds the crRNA, kinks it at a fixed six-nucleotide periodicity, shields it from cellular nucleases and displays it as five-nucleotide segments available for base pairing. This is the structural core of the complex and the function for which six copies are required.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: GO has no molecular-function term for crRNA-guided target recognition, so the backbone subunit that actually displays the guide for base pairing can only be annotated with generic RNA binding and DNA/RNA hybrid binding, indistinguishable from an unguided nucleic-acid-binding protein. Should a guide-RNA-dependent nucleic acid recognition term be requested?

Q: Does the reproducible CasC-Cas1 association have a role in primed spacer acquisition, as the original authors suggested but did not test? If so, is the relevant entity Cascade as a whole or CasC specifically?

Suggested experts: Mohan Babu, Andrew Emili, Alexander Savchenko

Q: Every sixth base of the guide is flipped out under a CasC thumb and does not base pair with the target. Is this a tolerated cost of the backbone architecture, or is the periodic interruption itself functional - for example in setting the fidelity threshold or in licensing primed acquisition from mismatched targets?

Suggested experts: Blake Wiedenheft, Scott Bailey

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Substitute the base-stacking thumb and palm residues identified in the structure (W199, F200, T201, L214 and the segment-ordering M166) singly and in combination, and for each variant measure Cascade assembly, dissociation constants for fully complementary targets and for targets mismatched at the fifth, sixth and seventh positions of each segment, Cas3-dependent degradation, and phage lambda resistance.

Hypothesis: The CasC thumb residues that order each guide segment set the mismatch tolerance of target recognition.

Type: structure-guided mutagenesis with quantitative binding assays

Experiment: Measure mature crRNA half-life by pulse-chase northern blotting in strains expressing full Cascade, CasCDE subcomplexes, and CasC thumb variants that fail to oligomerise, in otherwise isogenic backgrounds and in nuclease-deficient backgrounds, to separate protection from assembly.

Hypothesis: The CasC filament protects the crRNA from cellular ribonucleases in vivo.

Type: crRNA stability measurement

Experiment: Reconstitute purified Cas1-Cas2 with purified Cascade and with CasC-containing subcomplexes, measure binding by surface plasmon resonance, determine whether Cascade bound to a partially matched target enhances Cas1-Cas2 integration into a CRISPR array in vitro, and test in vivo with CasC interface variants that retain interference but lose Cas1 binding.

Hypothesis: The CasC-Cas1 interaction is a genuine physical link between interference and adaptation.

Type: in vitro reconstitution and in vivo priming assay

Session 1 (2026-10-01): initial full review

Reviewed together with casA, casB, casD and casE as one complex.

What CasC is

CasC is the Cas7 backbone subunit and the only one present in six copies
PMID:25103409.
UniProt Q46899 SUBUNIT: "The 6 CasC subunits make a helical stack forming a groove in
which the crRNA lies. Interacts directly with all of the Cas subunits."

Its fold is a modified RRM "palm" with a "fingers" domain and a 30-residue "thumb"
loop, and the oligomer is built thumb-into-palm
PMID:25103409.
Critically for how this subunit should be annotated, the crRNA contacts are not
sequence specific
PMID:25103409,
and the resulting architecture has two jobs at once
PMID:25103409.

The structural-subunit problem

CasC is the clearest case in the complex where the only GOA molecular-function rows are
RNA binding and protein binding, and where neither captures what the protein does.
My reading is that CasC has three separable, honestly assertable activities:

  1. structural constituent of the complex (GO:0005198 structural molecule activity) —
    it is the backbone; it polymerises, kinks the guide at a fixed 6-nt periodicity, and
    shields it from cellular nucleases. The quotes above support this directly, and the
    same sentence confirms it is the oligomerisation that creates the architecture. GO has
    no child of GO:0005198 for a non-ribosomal ribonucleoprotein (the children are
    ribosome, proteasome, virion, chromatin, nuclear pore and so on), so the parent is the
    correct level. I use the same term for the equivalent intra-complex protein binding
    rows on casA, casB, casD and casE.
  2. non-sequence-specific crRNA binding (GO:0003723 RNA binding) — already in GOA, and
    correct at the parent level precisely because it is non-specific.
  3. DNA/RNA hybrid binding (GO:0071667) — already in GOA, and well supported
    PMID:25123481,
    with the hybrid being stabilised by exactly this network
    PMID:25123481.

I did not invent a term for crRNA-guided target recognition; that gap is recorded in
modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml and raised in suggested_questions.

The Cas1 (YgbT) interaction — kept, but marked non-core

GOA holds a GO:0005515 IPI row for CasC with UniProtKB:Q46896 (Cas1/YgbT), plus a
part_of GO:0032991 IDA row, both from the Babu dual-function paper. The interaction is
real and was validated in both directions
PMID:21219465,
PMID:21219465,
and the authors themselves frame its significance as speculative
PMID:21219465.

How the two rows are handled:

  • the protein binding row gets REMOVE. My first pass used KEEP_AS_NON_CORE, but
    validation flagged it: keeping a bare protein-binding row preserves an annotation with
    no functional content, and the repo convention for GO:0005515 rows with no
    evidence-backed replacement is REMOVE. Unlike CasC's intra-Cascade contacts, this
    association is not a structural contribution to any defined complex, so GO:0005198
    would be wrong, and no functional consequence has been demonstrated for CasC (the
    inhibition assay in that paper used CasE, not CasC), so there is no replacement term
    to propose. The removal is about the term's emptiness, not about doubting the
    observation, and the reason field says so explicitly. The interaction belongs in
    IntAct/ComplexPortal.
  • the part_of GO:0032991 row gets MODIFY to GO:1990904, the same as the
    structure-derived row. I first split these two rows (ACCEPT here, MODIFY there) on the
    grounds that the Babu assembly is an ill-defined Cas1-CasC/CasE association rather than
    Cascade, but validation flagged the inconsistency and re-reading the sentence settled
    it the other way: the paper itself identifies CasC as a subunit of Cascade
    ("two subunits of the CRISPR-associated Cascade complex"), so both rows are about the
    same, RNA-containing complex and should be treated identically.

Requirement for immunity, and non-requirement for processing

CasC is required for phage resistance
PMID:18703739,
but like CasA and CasB it is not required for crRNA maturation — crRNA over-accumulates
in its absence
PMID:18703739.
Accordingly CasC gets no crRNA-biogenesis process term.

Complex term

Same decision as the other subunits for the Cascade-derived row: Cascade is demonstrably
a ribonucleoprotein
PMID:25103409,
PMID:23079036,
so GO:0032991 is MODIFYed to GO:1990904 ribonucleoprotein complex, and the absent
CRISPR-specific CC term is proposed. ComplexPortal does model the complex (CPX-1005,
cross-referenced from Q46899); GO does not.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex
  proposed_definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas)
    proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA
    as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I
    systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an
    R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction.
    The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition
    Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent
    is the Csy complex.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:1990904
    label: ribonucleoprotein complex
  justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms
    (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133),
    with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance
    complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic
    ancestor GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined,
    structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models (CPX-1005, Cascade
    complex, cross-referenced from this UniProt entry), and the Cas7 backbone in
    particular is the surface targeted by several families of phage-encoded
    anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and
    counter-defence curation expressible.
