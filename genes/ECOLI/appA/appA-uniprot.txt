CC   -!- FUNCTION: Catalyzes the hydrolysis of phytate (or myo-inositol
CC       hexakisphosphate, an indigestible organic form of phosphorus that is
CC       found in many plant tissues) to myo-inositol and inorganic phosphate
CC       (PubMed:10696472, PubMed:11035187, PubMed:30712472, PubMed:8387749,
CC       Ref.8). Dephosphorylates phytate in a stereospecific way by sequential
CC       removal of phosphate groups to produce myo-inositol 2-monophosphate
CC       (PubMed:11035187). Also shows phosphoanhydride phosphatase activity and
CC       hydrolyzes the distal phosphoryl residues of GTP, the 5'-beta-
CC       phosphoryl residue of the regulatory nucleotide ppGpp and
CC       tripolyphosphates (PubMed:1429631, PubMed:6282821, PubMed:8407904).
CC       Does not split most phosphomonoesters with the exception of the
CC       synthetic substrate p-nitrophenyl phosphate (pNPP), 2,3-
CC       bisphosphoglycerate and fructose 1,6-bisphosphate (PubMed:10696472,
CC       PubMed:1429631, PubMed:6282821, PubMed:8387749, PubMed:8407904, Ref.8).
