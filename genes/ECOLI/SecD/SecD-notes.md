# SecD research notes

SecD is the large multi-pass inner-membrane partner of SecF in the SecDF
accessory assembly. Genetic work first placed secD in E. coli protein export
[PMID:3029032 "Several mutations mapped in a new gene, secD."], and later
biochemistry showed that SecDF uses proton motive force rather than ATP to
complete late translocation through SecYEG [PMID:21562494 "we have shown that
SecDF is a component of the Sec machinery that utilizes the PMF to complete
protein translocation after the ATP-dependent SecA function"].

The key correction is that SecD contributes to PMF-coupled protein transmembrane
transporter activity; it is not itself a protein-transporting ATPase. SecA
provides the ATPase motor, while SecD and SecF form the PMF-coupled accessory
subcomplex.

SecD sits in the E. coli holo-translocon architecture. Purified HTL contains
SecYEG, SecDF-YajC, and YidC [PMID:24550475 "SecYEG interacts with the highly
conserved YidC and SecDF-YajC subcomplex, which facilitates translocation into
and across the membrane"], and follow-on structural work isolated the E. coli
inner-membrane complex as a SecYEG-SecDF-YajC-YidC supercomplex
[PMID:27435098 "supercomplex of SecYEG-SecDF-YajC-YidC - is a bona fide resident
of the Escherichia coli inner membrane"].

Generic protein-binding IPI rows for SecD should be removed as uninformative
interaction records, not because the reported interactions are false. The
generic SecD `membrane`, `protein transport`, and `intracellular protein
transport` rows are also less informative than the direct plasma-membrane,
Sec-complex transport, and intracellular protein transmembrane transport rows.
