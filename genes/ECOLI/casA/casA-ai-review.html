Gene Description

CasA (also called Cse1 or Cas8e) is the large subunit of Cascade, the 405-kDa CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex of the Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system. Cascade is a ribonucleoprotein built from eleven protein subunits - one CasA, two CasB (Cse2), six CasC (Cas7), one CasD (Cas5e) and one CasE (Cas6e) - assembled around a single 61-nucleotide mature crRNA. CasA occupies the tail of the seahorse-shaped complex, where an L1 loop inserts into a pore in CasD and makes base-specific contacts with the repeat-derived 5' handle of the crRNA, while a C-terminal four-helix bundle reaches the CasB dimer and so bridges the tail of the complex to the CasE-capped head. CasA also carries a four-cysteine metal-binding site. Its principal activity is discrimination of foreign from self DNA. The L1 loop scans duplex DNA for the three-nucleotide protospacer-adjacent motif (PAM), and PAM recognition on the crRNA-complementary strand licenses crRNA strand invasion and R-loop formation, during which CasA grips the displaced non-target strand. Once the R-loop has formed, the conformational change exposes a CasA surface that docks the trans-acting helicase-nuclease Cas3, which nicks, unwinds and processively degrades the invading DNA. CasA is dispensable for Cascade assembly but required for high-affinity target binding and for resistance to bacteriophage lambda.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex

Definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction. The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent is the Csy complex.

Justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic ancestor GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and its subunits are the direct targets of a whole family of phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible.

Parent term: ribonucleoprotein complex

Core Functions

Scans double-stranded DNA for a three-nucleotide protospacer-adjacent motif and, on finding one, licenses crRNA strand invasion and holds the displaced non-target strand, so that foreign sequence is distinguished from self sequence within the CRISPR array. This is the discriminating step of type I-E interference and is carried out by the CasA subunit within Cascade.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: GO has no molecular-function term for crRNA-guided, PAM-licensed target recognition, so CasA's defining activity has to be recorded as generic DNA binding, indistinguishable from that of an unguided DNA-binding protein. Should a guide-RNA-dependent nucleic acid recognition term be requested, with children for RNA-guided DNA and RNA-guided RNA recognition?

Q: Is the four-cysteine metal site in CasA (C140, C143, C250, C253) occupied by zinc? The crystal structure reports only "a metal-ion", and UniProt annotates no metal-binding feature, yet GOA carries zinc ion binding by IDA from that structure.

Suggested experts: Ryan N Jackson, Blake Wiedenheft

Q: Is CasA's recruitment of Cas3 best modelled as a molecular adaptor activity on CasA, or should the Cascade-Cas3 effector complex be curated as its own assembly with CasA as part_of? No Cas8-family protein in any organism currently carries an adaptor or recruitment term.

Suggested experts: Edze R Westra, John van der Oost

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Determine the metal content of purified CasA and of intact Cascade by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry, and collect anomalous diffraction data at the Zn K-edge on an existing Cascade crystal form. Pair this with cysteine-to-serine substitutions at C140, C143, C250 and C253, assaying target dsDNA affinity and phage lambda resistance.

Hypothesis: The CasA Cys4 site is a structural zinc site required for L1 loop positioning and therefore for PAM reading.

Type: metal analysis and site-directed mutagenesis

Experiment: Mutate the candidate Cas3-contacting residues mapped onto the CasA surface (Glu192, Glu280, Asn376, Thr383 and the loops at residues 288-294 and 318-323) and measure, for each variant, Cascade assembly, target dsDNA affinity, Cas3 co-recruitment by bimolecular fluorescence complementation, in vitro Cas3 nicking of a bound target, and phage lambda resistance. Variants that retain target binding but lose Cas3 recruitment would separate the adaptor function from the recognition function.

Hypothesis: Cas3 recruitment is mediated by a discrete CasA surface that is occluded in apo-Cascade and exposed by R-loop formation.

Type: structure-guided mutagenesis with in vivo and in vitro readouts

Experiment: Use single-molecule FRET or magnetic-tweezer assays on negatively supercoiled targets with dye-labelled CasA and Cas3 to time PAM engagement, R-loop extension and Cas3 arrival on the same molecule, comparing wild-type CasA with the PAM-blind F129A variant.

Hypothesis: The order of events is PAM reading by CasA, then R-loop propagation, then Cas3 capture.

Type: single-molecule kinetics

Session 1 (2026-10-01): initial full review

Reviewed together with casB, casC, casD and casE, because the five proteins are one
ribonucleoprotein machine and their annotations should come out mutually consistent.

What the complex is

Cascade was defined biochemically by affinity purification of individually tagged Cas
proteins from E. coli K-12 lysates
PMID:18703739.
Notably, CasA is the one subunit that could not be used as bait
PMID:18703739,
which is the first hint that CasA is peripheral to assembly rather than part of the
structural core. UniProt records the same: "CasA is not required for formation of
Cascade, but probably enhances binding to and subsequent recognition of both target
dsDNA and ssDNA" (Q46901 FUNCTION).

The crystal structure resolves the stoichiometry and the RNA
PMID:25103409,
so Cascade is a genuine ribonucleoprotein, not merely a protein-containing complex
PMID:23079036.

Where CasA sits and what it touches

CasA (called Cse1 in the structural papers) occupies the tail of the seahorse-shaped
complex, where the crRNA 5' handle is clamped
PMID:25103409.
Its docking is via a loop (L1) that enters a pore in CasD and reads the crRNA bases
directly
PMID:25103409,
and the same subunit also contacts CasD's RRM
PMID:25103409.
Its C-terminal four-helix bundle reaches the CasB dimer and thereby ties the tail of the
complex to its head
PMID:25103409.
These two contacts — to CasB (P76632) and to CasD (Q46898) — are exactly what the two
GOA protein binding IPI rows record, so those rows are about CasA's structural
integration into the complex, not about an informative binding activity.

Nucleic-acid contacts

CasA touches both the guide and the target. The L1 base-specific contacts above are the
RNA side. On the DNA side, the ssDNA-target-bound structure shows CasA gripping the
displaced strand
PMID:25123481,
with the displaced strand threading around the CasA four-helix bundle
PMID:25123481,
and the target itself lying in a groove that CasA helps form
PMID:25123481.

Self/non-self discrimination (PAM reading)

CasA is the discriminating subunit. The CasA loop is what scans for the
protospacer-adjacent motif
PMID:22521690,
and PAM dependence of target binding, plus its strand asymmetry, was shown directly
PMID:22521689.
UniProt records the supporting mutagenesis on Q46901: F129A gives an "80% increase in
phage sensitivity; 500-fold decrease in affinity for target dsDNA" and N131A a "60-fold
decrease in affinity for target dsDNA" (both ECO:0000269|PubMed:22521690).

Cas3 recruitment — the distinctive CasA function

This is the function GOA currently does not express at all. Cas3 is not recruited to
apo-Cascade
PMID:22521689,
but after target recognition Cas3 and CasA are brought into contact in vivo
PMID:22521689.
The CasA subunit is read as the docking site
PMID:22521689,
PMID:22521689,
and the independent ssDNA-bound crystal structure maps the same interface
PMID:25123481,
downstream of R-loop formation
PMID:25123481.

CasA therefore performs a step itself — it brings the trans-acting nuclease into
productive contact with the licensed R-loop — which is what GO:0030674
protein-macromolecule adaptor activity describes ("An adaptor activity that brings
together two or more macromolecules in contact, permitting those molecules to function
in a coordinated way"). I propose it as a NEW row with IPI evidence from
PMID:22521689 and UniProtKB:P38036 (Cas3) in supporting_entities.

Comparator check (as required before any NEW): I queried QuickGO for every GO

GOA holds GO:0008270 zinc ion binding for Q46901 as IDA from PMID:25103409. The cited

PMID:25103409.
UniProt Q46901 carries no BINDING feature for a metal at all, and no keyword for zinc.

  the tail of the seahorse-shaped complex, where an L1 loop inserts into a pore in
  CasD and makes base-specific contacts with the repeat-derived 5' handle of the
  crRNA, while a C-terminal four-helix bundle reaches the CasB dimer and so bridges
  the tail of the complex to the CasE-capped head. CasA also carries a four-cysteine
  metal-binding site. Its principal activity is discrimination of foreign from self
  DNA. The L1 loop scans duplex DNA for the three-nucleotide protospacer-adjacent
  motif (PAM), and PAM recognition on the crRNA-complementary strand licenses
  crRNA strand invasion and R-loop formation, during which CasA grips the displaced
  non-target strand. Once the R-loop has formed, the conformational change exposes a
  CasA surface that docks the trans-acting helicase-nuclease Cas3, which nicks,
  unwinds and processively degrades the invading DNA. CasA is dispensable for

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex
  proposed_definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas)
    proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA
    as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I
    systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an
    R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction.
    The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition
    Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent
    is the Csy complex.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:1990904

  justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms
    (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133),
    with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance
    complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic
    ancestor GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined,

    phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of
