# opgH curation notes

## 2026-11-06 evidence review

All 14 pristine seeded rows were reviewed. Raw UniProt/GOA and original annotation payloads are protected by the root seed-state and independent review baseline. Default Falcon was attempted once for this gene and returned HTTP 403 authentication failure; no provider report was fabricated. Manual primary-source review supplied the judgments. PAINT lineage checks found no IRD/IKR.

PMID:23935518 explicitly names P62517 and MG1655. Native untagged OpgH1–138 purified from BL21(DE3) inhibits FtsZ assembly and assembly-coupled GTP hydrolysis; full-length cell experiments and opgG/Rcs controls support a separate moonlighting role. Generic protein binding is refined to GO:0005095, whose definition includes reduction of GTPase activity; OpgH itself is not assigned GTPase chemistry. UDP-glucose-dependent switching and monomer sequestration remain models.

Membrane-system studies (6238027,1827116), later mdoGH resolution (7934824), and membrane topology (9352918) support UDP-glucosyltransferase activity, not purified OpgH-alone sufficiency. ACP is a required assay component; phosphopantetheine is not required for its glucan-system role (9171419). Polyprenyl-phosphate stimulation is not proof of an obligate polyprenyl-P-glucose intermediate. Translocation remains suggested by topology, so no transporter MF is added.

The cellulose IBA descends from PTN002466726 in PTHR43867; the OpgH SF5 target makes OPG-associated beta-1,2 glucan rather than beta-1,4 cellulose. This is a product/pathway scope defect, not a donor-count objection or rejection of broad GT2 hexosyltransferase chemistry. Caulobacter essentiality (39145657) is not generalized to E. coli.

No NEW biological process was proposed. No speculative biofilm, motility, pathogenicity or general stress process was added from downstream phenotypes. Abstract-only experimental localization rows are retained where independent target evidence establishes the location; absent accessible peptide tables are not treated as negative evidence.
