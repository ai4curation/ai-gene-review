Gene Description

OpgH is a multipass inner-membrane GT2 glucosyltransferase required for osmoregulated periplasmic glucan synthesis in Escherichia coli. The associated membrane enzyme system uses UDP-glucose and acyl carrier protein to elongate beta-1,2-glucan chains. OpgH also regulates nutrient-dependent cell size through its cytoplasmic N-terminal domain, which inhibits FtsZ assembly and the accompanying GTPase activity. This division-regulatory function is separable from glucan production. Translocation of nascent glucan through OpgH is a proposed role rather than independently demonstrated transport chemistry.

Core Functions

Transfers glucosyl residues from UDP-glucose as the membrane glucosyltransferase component of the OPG biosynthetic system. Membrane-system evidence supports beta-1,2-glucan elongation with ACP and additional assay requirements; it does not establish OpgH-alone sufficiency or a separately demonstrated translocation activity.

This is a product/pathway scope defect, not a donor-count objection or rejection of broad GT2 hexosyltransferase chemistry. Caulobacter essentiality (39145657) is not generalized to E. coli.

No NEW biological process was proposed. No speculative biofilm, motility, pathogenicity or general stress process was added from downstream phenotypes.
