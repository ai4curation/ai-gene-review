Gene Description

CysM (O-acetylserine sulfhydrylase B, cysteine synthase B) is one of the two pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent O-acetylserine sulfhydrylase isoenzymes of Escherichia coli K-12. This soluble, homodimeric cytosolic enzyme condenses O-acetyl-L-serine with sulfide to form L-cysteine and acetate, the terminal step of de novo cysteine biosynthesis. It can also use thiosulfate in place of sulfide, giving S-sulfo-L-cysteine, which suits it to the thiosulfate branch of sulfur assimilation; its gene lies at the end of the cysPUWAM sulfate/thiosulfate transport operon. Unlike the CysK isoenzyme, CysM does not form the cysteine synthase complex with serine acetyltransferase (CysE). Through beta-elimination of cysteine, CysM also shows a secondary L-cysteine desulfhydrase activity that contributes modestly to cysteine degradation, and in crude-extract reconstitutions it can release cysteine sulfur used for in vitro Fe-S cluster formation.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

S-sulfo-L-cysteine synthase (O-acetyl-L-serine-dependent) activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: O-acetyl-L-serine + thiosulfate = S-sulfo-L-cysteine + acetate.

Justification: CysM-type O-acetylserine sulfhydrylases use thiosulfate as the sulfur donor (EC 2.5.1.144), an activity distinct from sulfide-dependent cysteine synthase activity (GO:0004124). GO has no molecular-function term for it; the only related class, GO:0019345 (L-cysteine biosynthetic process via S-sulfo-L-cysteine), is obsolete. The activity distinguishes CysM from CysK.

Parent term: transferase activity, transferring alkyl or aryl (other than methyl) groups
