# nirD evidence notes

NirD P0A9I8 is the small cytoplasmic subunit of the E. coli NirBD enzyme. [PMID:1435259: "NirD remained associated with NirB during the initial stages of purification of the active enzyme, suggesting that NirD is a second structural subunit of the enzyme."] Native complementation/reconstitution and the PMID34323689 nitrate-only nitrogen-source growth experiment establish contribution to NADH-dependent nitrite reduction. Lack of autonomous small-chain catalysis is not a rejection criterion; the core explicitly uses `contributes_to_molecular_function`.

The same full primary paper establishes a separate activity: [PMID:34323689: "NirD directly binds the catalytic domains of RelA to balance (p)ppGpp accumulation."; "NirD can control RelA activity by directly inhibiting the rate of (p)ppGpp synthesis."] Its E50K variant retains nitrite-reductase function while losing RelA interaction. The RelA binding row is refined to enzyme inhibitor activity and represented as a second core; no redundant NEW row is needed. The nitrate module should use only the NirBD contribution.

The IPR036922 Rieske-superfamily transfer overstates [2Fe-2S] binding. The target has NirD-specific IPR017881/IPR012748 assignments and a Rieske-like fold, but a fold does not establish a metal center. [PMID:22965870, explicitly M. tuberculosis NirD: "NirD is related in three-dimensional structure and sequence to the Rieske proteins; however, it does not contain any Fe-S cluster or other cofactors that might be involved in electron transfer."] This comparator is not a native E. coli cofactor assay. The row is MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, not a claim of experimental exclusion. NirB's measured [2Fe-2S] center is not transferred onto NirD.

PMID36068114 uses artificial methyl-viologen/dithionite donors for NirB-alone activity. It does not invalidate the native NADH-dependent small-subunit contribution. PANTHER PTHR40562/PTHR40562:SF1 membership comes from target UniProt; PAINT PTN002220983 is traced as an inherited complex-membership assertion with direct target support, not a circular inference.

The E. coli panel is a physiological comparison, not an autonomous assimilatory nitrate route: nitrate uptake requires a separate upstream nitrate reductase before NirBD acts on nitrite. Assimilation is nevertheless experimentally supported. [PMID:34323689, Figure 3 supplement 1: "nitrate as the sole electron acceptor and as the sole nitrogen source"; "Under nitrate respiration, nitrate is converted to nitrite, which is subsequently converted to ammonium (NH4) by the cytosolic NADH-dependent nitrite reductase NirBD."] The study uses E. coli K-12 MG1655 derivatives; this is not evidence for cyanobacterial NirB accessory proteins.
