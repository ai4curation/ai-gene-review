| Identity | **phoA / UniProt P00634** is the classical alkaline phosphatase of *Escherichia coli* K-12; a modern study explicitly uses P00634 residue numbering. (pqac-00000007) | Accession-linked primary evidence; **strong** |
| Molecular function/reaction | Hydrolase (EC 3.1.3.1) converting a phosphate monoester plus water to the corresponding alcohol and inorganic phosphate; catalysis proceeds through a covalent phosphoseryl intermediate and can support transphosphorylation. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000008) | Biochemical and mechanistic evidence; **strong** |
| Substrate scope | Broad, relatively nonspecific activity toward phosphate monoesters, including organophosphates and 5′-ribonucleotides and probably 5′-deoxyribonucleotides. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000006) | Enzymology and review synthesis; **strong for broad scope**, substrate-dependent for individual compounds |
| Localization/biogenesis | Synthesized with a 22-residue N-terminal export signal, translocated across the inner membrane, and matured in the **periplasm** by signal-peptide removal, metal incorporation, folding, and DsbA-dependent formation of two intramolecular disulfides per subunit. Cytoplasmically retained PhoA is inactive. (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000003) | Export, mutational, biochemical, and folding experiments; **strong** |
| Oligomer/cofactors | Active enzyme is a homodimer. Each active site contains **two Zn²⁺ and one Mg²⁺**; dimerization promotes metal binding, stability, and catalysis. (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000012) | Structural and biochemical evidence; **strong** |
| Pathway regulation | A Pho-regulon effector: Pi limitation promotes PhoR autophosphorylation and phosphotransfer to PhoB; phospho-PhoB binds promoter Pho boxes and activates *phoA*. Under Pi sufficiency, PstSCAB–PhoU favors PhoR phosphatase signaling and repression. (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000015, pqac-00000018) | Genetic, biochemical, and authoritative review evidence; **strong**, although the precise starvation signal remains under study |
| Physiological role | Periplasmic PhoA hydrolyzes externally available or nontransportable organophosphates, releasing Pi that can be imported during phosphorus limitation; it is an acquisition/scavenging enzyme rather than the primary phosphate sensor. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000017) | Physiological and regulatory evidence; **strong** |
| Quantitative benchmarks | Pho regulon activation is reported below approximately **0.4 μM environmental Pi**; intracellular Pi is typically **1–10 mM**; some regulon genes are induced **>100-fold**. Wild-type PhoA showed *k*cat ≈ **65 s⁻¹** and *K*m ≈ **23 μM** for pNPP under reported assay conditions. Monomerizing T59R reduced activity **10⁴-fold** and lowered melting temperature from **97°C to 43°C**. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000018, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000009) | Quantitative regulatory and enzymology studies; **strong within stated experimental conditions** |
| Recent 2023–2024 developments/applications | A 2023 review expanded the phosphate-starvation stress-network context without revising PhoA’s core annotation. In 2024, PhoA fusion activity remained in active use for experimentally mapping membrane-protein topology, including the periplasmic orientation of TrhA termini; established applications also include localization/topology reporters and phosphate-responsive expression systems. (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000021) | Recent application and expert synthesis; **strong for reporter use**, no major 2023–2024 revision of native catalytic function identified |
