Gene Description

acnA encodes aconitase A (aconitate hydratase A, EC 4.2.1.3), a monomeric ~97 kDa cytosolic [4Fe-4S] enzyme of the aconitase/IRP family that catalyses the reversible isomerisation of citrate to isocitrate via cis-aconitate in the tricarboxylic acid cycle. E. coli has two major aconitases: AcnB is the main housekeeping enzyme during exponential growth, whereas AcnA is an aerobic stationary-phase isozyme induced by iron (Fur), sigma-38 and redox stress (SoxRS), and repressed by catabolite repression and anaerobically by ArcA/FNR. AcnA has a higher affinity for citrate, is active over a broad pH range and, unlike AcnB, retains its cluster and activity in vivo during superoxide and peroxide stress and iron chelation, allowing it to sustain TCA-cycle flux when AcnB is inactivated. Like vertebrate IRP1, cluster-free apo-AcnA binds the 3' untranslated regions of acnA and acnB mRNAs, stabilising them and increasing aconitase synthesis (a post-transcriptional positive autoregulatory switch), and it also enhances stability of the sodA transcript. acn mutants are hypersensitive to H2O2 and methyl viologen. The 2-methyl-cis-aconitate hydratase step of the 2-methylcitrate (propionate) cycle has been attributed to AcnB rather than AcnA.
