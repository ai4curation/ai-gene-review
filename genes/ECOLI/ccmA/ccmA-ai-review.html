Gene Description

CcmA is the cytoplasmic ATP-binding cassette (nucleotide-binding) subunit of the inner-membrane CcmAB complex of cytochrome c maturation system I in Escherichia coli. It associates with the membrane only together with the integral membrane partner CcmB and, through CcmB, with the heme-handling protein CcmC (and CcmD) in a CcmABCD assembly. ATP hydrolysis by CcmA is required for c-type cytochrome synthesis: in its absence, or in a Walker A (K40D) mutant, heme-loaded CcmE stays trapped with CcmC and cannot deliver heme to apocytochrome c. CcmAB was long assumed to export heme to the periplasm, but direct tests do not support that model; its function is best described as ATP-dependent energy coupling for release of holo-CcmE from CcmC, and any transported substrate remains unknown.

Core Functions

Nucleotide-binding ATPase subunit of the inner-membrane CcmAB(CD) complex whose ATP hydrolysis drives release of heme-loaded CcmE from CcmC, a step required for heme delivery to apocytochrome c in system I cytochrome c maturation.
