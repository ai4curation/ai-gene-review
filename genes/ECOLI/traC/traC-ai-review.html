Gene Description

TraC is an 875-residue F-plasmid protein required to assemble mature F-pilin subunits into the extended F pilus, the filament that establishes contact with a recipient cell at the start of conjugation. It is not an integral membrane protein: expressed alone it is cytoplasmic, and it partitions with the membrane only in the presence of the other transfer-region proteins, so its association with the inner membrane is peripheral and depends on its partners. A mutant that leaves pilin processing intact but abolishes the extended pilus places TraC at the assembly step rather than at pilin maturation, and the same mutant transfers DNA at greatly reduced frequency, linking the protein to conjugative transfer as well as to pilus biogenesis. By position in the transfer region and by broad similarity it corresponds to the VirB4-family ATPase of other type IV secretion systems, but no nucleotide binding or hydrolysis has been demonstrated for the F protein itself.

Core Functions

Assembly factor for the F conjugative pilus. TraC acts after pilin maturation to polymerise mature F-pilin subunits into the extended filament, working as a peripheral inner-membrane component recruited by the other transfer-region proteins rather than as an integral membrane protein or a component of the pilus tip. Loss of TraC leaves pilin intact but abolishes the pilus and sharply reduces conjugative DNA transfer.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Has F TraC ever been shown to bind or hydrolyse ATP? The protein is routinely called the VirB4-family ATPase of the F system, but the F literature found here covers sequencing, the traC1044 genetics and localisation only, and soluble TraC has been purified, so the experiment appears feasible and may simply never have been reported.

Q: Is the PANTHER placement of P18004 outside the VirB4 family a real evolutionary signal or a classification artefact? PANTHER puts TraC in the unnamed family PTHR38467 rather than with VirB4, which if genuine would weaken the standard family-based functional inference.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Assay purified F TraC directly for ATP binding and hydrolysis. Soluble TraC has already been purified from inclusion bodies, so the material is obtainable. Test both soluble and membrane-derived preparations, since those gave opposite answers for the pKM101 VirB4 homologue, and a result either way would close the molecular-function gap recorded here.

Experiment: Test whether the traC1044 substitution, which weakens the TraC-membrane interaction, separates pilus assembly from DNA transfer. Quantifying piliation and transfer frequency across an allelic series would establish whether the transfer defect is wholly a consequence of pilus loss or a partly independent contribution.

(traC-notes.md)

traC (F plasmid, Escherichia coli K-12) — curation notes

UniProt: P18004 (TRAC_ECOLI), 875 aa, encoded on plasmid F. UniProt gives one
functional statement, "Required for the assembly of mature F-pilin subunits
into extended F pili", and one location, "Cell inner membrane; Peripheral
membrane protein".

The sequencing paper agrees on both the length and the function
PMID:2265751,
reporting "a deduced coding region of 875 amino acids", which matches the
entry exactly.

What the genetics establishes

The traC1044 mutant is the informative allele. It abolishes F pili, leaves
pilin itself intact, and reduces DNA transfer
PMID:2885308.
Because mature pilin subunits are still present in the membrane, the defect is
in assembly rather than in processing
PMID:2885308,
and the authors read the combined phenotype as placing TraC in a
membrane-spanning complex
PMID:2885308.
Complementation confirms the assignment to traC
PMID:2885308.

So both a pilus-assembly role and a conjugative-transfer role are supported by
mutant phenotype for this protein, and neither is currently in GOA.

Membrane association is conditional

TraC is not an integral membrane protein. Expressed on its own it is
cytoplasmic; expressed from F, in the presence of the other Tra proteins, it
partitions with the membrane
PMID:1350587,
which the authors attribute to protein-protein interaction rather than to a
membrane anchor
PMID:1350587.
The same work excludes TraC from the pilus tip
PMID:1350587.
UniProt's "Peripheral membrane protein" qualifier captures this correctly.

The molecular function is unresolved, and that matters

TraC is conventionally called the VirB4-family ATPase of the F system, and
modules/bacterial_type_iv_secretion_apparatus.yaml assigns it that role by
family while deliberately grounding the ATPase molecular function on
Agrobacterium VirB4 (P0A3W0) instead. This review confirms that was the right
call. Searching the F TraC literature returns the sequencing paper, the
traC1044 genetics, and the localization study, and none of them assays
nucleotide binding or hydrolysis. GOA holds no molecular function for P18004 at
all.

Two further observations sharpen the gap rather than closing it:

  • PANTHER's own sequence classification does not place P18004 with VirB4.
    P18004 falls in PTHR38467, which PANTHER has not named, whereas VirB4
    (P0A3W0) falls in PTHR30121. The module records this split.
  • The nearest biochemical characterisation of an F-type VirB4 counterpart is of
    the pKM101 protein, confusingly also called TraB, and even there hydrolysis
    depended on the preparation (see genes/ECOLI/traB/traB-notes.md).

Conclusion: assert the pilus-assembly and conjugation processes, which mutant
phenotype supports directly, and assert no molecular function. This is a clean
MF_DARK case: a protein with a sharp, reproducible loss-of-function phenotype
and no known activity.
