Gene Description

KdpA is the potassium-binding and potassium-translocating subunit of KdpFABC, the inducible high-affinity ATP-driven potassium uptake pump of the bacterial inner membrane, which is expressed when external K+ falls to micromolar levels. KdpA is a ten-transmembrane-helix member of the superfamily of potassium transporters (SKT), built from four M1PM2 repeats around a KcsA-like selectivity filter that binds dehydrated K+ and confers the high affinity and K+/Rb+ discrimination of the system. KdpA itself has no ATPase activity: energy is supplied by the P-type ATPase subunit KdpB, and structures show an intramembrane tunnel that carries K+ from the KdpA selectivity filter to the canonical cation site of KdpB, with the accessory single-span subunits KdpC and KdpF stabilizing the complex.

Core Functions

KdpA binds periplasmic K+ in its KcsA-like selectivity filter and forms the K+-selective translocation pathway of the KdpFABC pump, contributing the substrate-handling part of the ATP-driven K+ uptake activity whose energy is supplied by KdpB.
