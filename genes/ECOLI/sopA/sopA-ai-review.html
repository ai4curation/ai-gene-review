Gene Description

SopA is the partition ATPase of the F plasmid, encoded with SopB in the sopAB operon next to the sopC centromere. It is a deviant Walker-type (ParA family) ATPase. ATP-bound SopA binds DNA non-specifically and associates with the host nucleoid; its weak intrinsic ATPase is stimulated by DNA and further by SopB bound at sopC, which supplies an arginine finger. Cycles of nucleoid binding and SopB-stimulated release position and separate the SopB-sopC partition complexes, so that each daughter cell inherits a plasmid copy. SopA also polymerizes in an ATP-dependent manner in vitro. Separately, SopA binds CTTTGC repeats in the sopAB promoter-operator and, together with SopB, represses the operon, keeping Sop protein levels in the range that partition needs.

Q: GO has no term for the non-specific, ATP-dependent DNA binding that tethers ParA-family ATPases to the nucleoid, so it is annotated here with the general DNA binding term, which a reader cannot tell apart from operator binding. Would GO accept a child of DNA binding for sequence-independent nucleoid binding by partition ATPases?

(sopA-notes.md)

sopA (P62556), F plasmid partition ATPase, Escherichia coli K-12

2026-10-30: initial review

GOA had no annotation for SopA, one of the two prototype plasmid partition ATPases.

  • ATPase: Mg-dependent, stimulated by DNA and further by SopB PMID:1406581. SopB supplies an arginine finger PMID:19740757.
  • Non-specific DNA binding is required for partition: K340A loses ATP-dependent DNA binding

    100-fold and plasmid stabilization PMID:18826408.

  • On the nucleoid when expressed alone PMID:9529520.
  • Operator binding (CTTTGC repeats) and cooperative SopA-SopB repression of sopAB [PMID:2670941;
    PMID:9529520]. Non-core.
  • Filaments: ATP-dependent SopA polymers in vitro and dynamic polymers in vivo PMID:16306264.
    Whether partition works through filaments or a diffusion-ratchet gradient is unresolved
    PMID:25107339; the review annotates only the mutant phenotype from that paper.

Decisions: NEW ATP hydrolysis activity, DNA binding, plasmid partitioning, bacterial nucleoid
(core); DNA-binding transcription repressor activity and negative regulation of transcription
(non-core).
