Gene Description

RecA is the Escherichia coli homologous recombinase and the central regulator of the SOS DNA-damage response. The protein binds single-stranded DNA and, in its ATP-bound form, polymerizes into a helical nucleoprotein filament (the presynaptic filament) that is loaded onto ssDNA by RecBCD at chi sites on broken DNA ends or by RecFOR at postreplication gaps, displacing SSB. The filament searches for homology and catalyses ATP-dependent homologous pairing and DNA strand exchange, driven by DNA-stimulated ATP hydrolysis, which underlies recombinational repair of double-strand breaks, collapsed forks and daughter-strand gaps as well as conjugational and transductional recombination. The activated filament (RecA*) also acts as a co-protease that stimulates autocleavage of the LexA repressor, derepressing the SOS regulon, and later of UmuD (to UmuD') and the phage lambda cI repressor. RecA-ATP transferred from the 3' end of RecA* is a subunit of the active DNA polymerase V mutasome (UmuD'2C-RecA-ATP) that performs mutagenic translesion synthesis. RecA is a soluble cytoplasmic protein; it also contributes to swarming motility independently of SOS induction.
