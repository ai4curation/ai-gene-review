Gene Description

RecB is the largest subunit of the heterotrimeric RecBCD enzyme (Exonuclease V), the helicase-nuclease that initiates the major pathway of homologous recombination and double-strand break repair in Escherichia coli. Its N-terminal UvrD-like superfamily 1 domains form a slow ATP-dependent 3'-5' DNA helicase/translocase that moves on the 3'-ended strand while the RecD motor moves on the 5'-ended strand, together unwinding DNA from a blunt double-strand end at high speed and processivity. Its tethered C-terminal PD-(D/E)XK domain is the only nuclease active site of the holoenzyme and requires Mg2+; it degrades the unwound strands until RecC recognizes a Chi sequence (5'-GCTGGTGG-3'), after which the attenuated enzyme leaves a 3' single-stranded tail and the RecB nuclease domain loads RecA onto it. RecBCD also destroys linear foreign DNA such as unprotected phage genomes, and recB mutants are recombination-deficient and sensitive to UV light.
