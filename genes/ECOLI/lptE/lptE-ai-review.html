Gene Description

LptE (formerly RlpB, "rare lipoprotein B") is an essential outer-membrane lipoprotein of Escherichia coli and the plug subunit of the LptDE lipopolysaccharide (LPS) translocon. It is triacylated at its N-terminal cysteine and delivered to the inner leaflet of the outer membrane by the Lol pathway. About three quarters of LptE sits inside the lumen of the 26-stranded LptD beta-barrel, forming a two-protein plug-and-barrel complex in 1:1 stoichiometry. LptE has two roles. First, it is needed for LptD biogenesis: it engages LptD while BAM assembles it, stabilizes the barrel, and drives the disulfide rearrangement that yields functional, correctly oxidized LptD. Second, it binds LPS directly through conserved basic surface loops and can disrupt LPS-LPS interactions, which may help LPS insertion into the outer leaflet. Depletion of LptE stops newly made LPS from reaching the cell surface, raises outer-membrane permeability, and is lethal. Accessory lipoproteins LptM and LptY associate with the translocon and modulate LptD conformation and the stability of its connection to the periplasmic bridge.

Suggested Experiments

    Is LptE's role in LptD assembly an assembly-chaperone activity that GO could
    capture as a molecular function, separate from its LPS-binding role in the
    mature translocon?
- question: >-
    Does LptE disaggregation of LPS, seen in vitro, occur within the assembled
    translocon in vivo, and how does it couple to opening of the LptD lateral
    gate?
suggested_experiments:
- experiment_type: separation-of-function mutagenesis
  description: >-
    Combine lptE alleles defective in LPS binding or disaggregation (e.g. R91D,
    K136D) with alleles defective in LptD assembly (e.g. lptE6). Measure
    LptD oxidation state, LptDE complex levels and pulse-labelled LPS surface
    delivery to separate LptE's assembly role from its substrate-handling
    role.
- experiment_type: in vivo LPS photo-crosslinking
  description: >-
    Incorporate pBPA at the predicted LptE LPS-binding loops in intact cells and
    test whether LPS crosslinks in an LptA-, ATP- and LptD-gate-dependent manner.
    This would show whether LptE contacts LPS in transit.
