Gene Description

NarX is a sensor histidine kinase anchored in the E. coli inner membrane by two transmembrane helices, and one of two nitrate/nitrite sensors (with the paralog NarQ) of the Nar two-component regulatory system that controls anaerobic respiratory and fermentative gene expression. Its periplasmic four-helix-bundle sensor domain, built around the conserved P-box, binds nitrate at the dimer interface; ligand binding produces a piston-type displacement of the sensor helices that is relayed through the HAMP domain to the cytoplasmic HisKA_3 dimerization/phosphotransfer and HATPase_c catalytic domains. Nitrate (strongly) and nitrite (more weakly) stimulate ATP-dependent autophosphorylation of the conserved histidine, and the phosphoryl group is transferred to the receiver aspartate of the response regulator NarL (and NarP), activating NarL-dependent transcription of genes such as narGHJI and narK. In the absence of stimulus NarX acts as a transmitter phosphatase that accelerates dephosphorylation of NarL-phosphate, a reaction that depends on a conserved DxxxQ motif adjacent to the phospho-accepting histidine. NarX and NarQ are functionally redundant for nitrate-dependent regulation but differ in how they discriminate nitrate from nitrite.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

phospho-response regulator phosphatase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the hydrolysis of the phosphoryl group from the phosphorylated aspartate residue of a two-component response regulator receiver domain, when performed by the cognate sensor (transmitter phosphatase activity).

Justification: Two-component sensors such as NarX and EnvZ have a well-characterised, mutationally separable transmitter phosphatase activity toward their phospho-aspartyl response regulators. GO currently offers only the generic GO:0004721 phosphoprotein phosphatase activity, whose descendants cover serine/threonine, tyrosine, histidine and arginine phosphatases but not phospho-aspartate on a receiver domain. The gap is not specific to sensors: the dedicated response-regulator phosphatase CheZ (E. coli P0A9H9), which dephosphorylates CheY-P, is likewise annotated only to the generic GO:0004721 (IDA and IBA in QuickGO, checked 2026-11-01).

Parent term: phosphoprotein phosphatase activity

Core Functions

Nitrate/nitrite-stimulated sensor histidine kinase in the inner membrane: ligand binding to the periplasmic P-box sensor domain stimulates ATP-dependent autophosphorylation of the conserved histidine, and the phosphoryl group is transferred to the response regulator NarL (and NarP), activating nitrate-responsive transcription.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: phospho-response regulator phosphatase activity
  proposed_definition: >-
    Catalysis of the hydrolysis of the phosphoryl group from the phosphorylated
    aspartate residue of a two-component response regulator receiver domain,
    when performed by the cognate sensor (transmitter phosphatase activity).
  justification: >-
    Two-component sensors such as NarX and EnvZ have a well-characterised,
    mutationally separable transmitter phosphatase activity toward their
    phospho-aspartyl response regulators. GO currently offers only the generic
    GO:0004721 phosphoprotein phosphatase activity, whose descendants cover
    serine/threonine, tyrosine, histidine and arginine phosphatases but not
    phospho-aspartate on a receiver domain. The gap is not specific to
    sensors: the dedicated response-regulator phosphatase CheZ (E. coli
    P0A9H9), which dephosphorylates CheY-P, is likewise annotated only to the
    generic GO:0004721 (IDA and IBA in QuickGO, checked 2026-11-01).
