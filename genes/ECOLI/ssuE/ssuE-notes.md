# E. coli ssuE: manual source-based review

SsuE is the FMN reductase of the Escherichia coli two-component alkanesulfonate monooxygenase system. It uses either NADH or NADPH with comparable measured steady-state kinetics to generate FMNH2 for the SsuD oxygenase, which releases sulfite from alkanesulfonates. SsuE also reduces FAD and riboflavin under biochemical assay conditions. Its active site spans a dimer, and a solution dimer–tetramer equilibrium shifts toward dimers upon FMN binding. SsuE–SsuD interactions promote efficient flavin transfer, while diffusion can support desulfonation under particular assay conditions.

## Acquisition and provenance

All original ordered annotation payloads and both raw files are retained. No NEW annotations were proposed. The genuine Falcon and perplexity-lite attempts failed authentication; actual FEBA attempts returned no usable mapped data (foundation job logs). These failures are not evidence about function. Publication caches were read as retrieved, never hand-edited. Primary abstracts are distinguished from normally recovered full PMC URL text. No GOA assertion or protein name is cited as biological support. Ordinary relationship fields were not used to reject a complex contribution.

The live QuickGO definition of GO:0008752 includes similar NADH/NADPH rates. Full PMID:30171650 Table 1, normally recovered at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6295896/, directly resolves this with wild-type SsuE kinetics; MsuE rows in the same table are different proteins and are not transferred. GO:0052873 remains true and is not an exclusivity claim. PMID:24816272 resolves dimer/tetramer context. PMID:11967071 stays abstract-only; the author-copy HTTPS attempt reported a certificate hostname mismatch and was not bypassed. The missing PAINT slice does not imply failure, nor is the target in WITH/FROM circular. S-313 SsuE dispensability and NADH-negative extract assays (PMID:10781557) are not native E. coli results.

dimer-tetramer equilibrium exists in solution, with FMN binding favoring the
dimer. As the active site includes residues from both subunits, at least a
dimeric association is required for the function of SsuE.”

from SsuE to SsuD requires defined protein-protein interactions, but diffusion
