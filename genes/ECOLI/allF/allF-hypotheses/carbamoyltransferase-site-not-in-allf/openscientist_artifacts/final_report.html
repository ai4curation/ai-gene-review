AIGR gene hypothesis deep research: carbamoyltransferase-site-not-in-allf

Target Escherichia coli K-12 (NCBITaxon:83333), gene allF, UniProtKB Q47208 (formerly FdrA) Partner subunits AllG UniProtKB:P77129 (formerly YlbE), AllH UniProtKB:P0AAS5 (formerly YlbF) Operon partner AllK UniProtKB:P37306 (formerly YbcF), catabolic carbamate kinase, EC 2.7.2.2 Reference context PMID: 38888336 Source genes/ECOLI/allF/allF-ai-review.yaml, free-text selector Seed hypothesis In the three-subunit oxamate carbamoyltransferase AllFGH, the carbamoyl phosphate-binding catalytic site lies in AllG and/or AllH and not in AllF. Requested test: structure-based fold assignment (Foldseek or DALI against PDB / AlphaFold DB) of AllG and AllH, reporting whether either has a carbamoyltransferase, carbamate kinase or other phosphate / carbamoyl phosphate-binding fold with its catalytic motif conserved, and whether any such motif exists in AllF.

Summary

Verdict: partially supported, and unresolved on the half that decides the curation question.

The analysis the seed hypothesis asks for was run in full. Foldseek 3Di+AA fold assignment of the AlphaFold models of AllF (mean pLDDT 92.9), AllG (97.0) and AllH (95.5) against PDB100, CATH50 and AFDB-SwissProt produced 1393 alignments. A regex scan of every target description for carbamoyltransferase, transcarbamylase, carbamate kinase, amino-acid kinase and aspartokinase keywords returned zero non-self hits for all three subunits. A sequence-motif scan found the ATCase/OTCase carbamoyl phosphate-binding loop S-x-R-T-R in E. coli PyrB (position 53) and ArgF/ArgI (position 56) and found it in none of AllF, AllG or AllH, together with the absence of the OTCase HPxQ and ATCase HCxP motifs from all three. The negative clause of the hypothesis holds: AllF has no carbamoyltransferase fold and no carbamoyltransferase catalytic motif.

The positive clause fails on the same evidence. The identical search finds no carbamoyltransferase, carbamate kinase or phosphate-binding catalytic fold in AllG or AllH either. AllG's only significant non-self structural hit in the whole search is PDB 3CLQ, a conserved protein of unknown function from Enterococcus faecalis V583 (E = 2.0e-46, 45.7% identity, 2.5 Å, no bound nonpolymer ligands). AllH has no hit at E < 1e-3 in PDB100 (best non-self E = 1.58) and none in CATH50 (best E = 0.64). Fold assignment therefore cannot place the carbamoyl phosphate site in AllG or AllH, because it places it nowhere. The hypothesis's negative half is true in the trivial sense that it is true of every subunit.

That absence is a real property of the proteins and not a failure of the search. AllK (P37306), the catabolic carbamate kinase encoded in the same allFGHK operon, run through the identical pipeline with the identical keyword filter, returned 507 alignments of which 42 matched carbamate kinase folds at E < 1e-3, best hit 8CRV Pseudomonas aeruginosa carbamate kinase at E = 4.9e-42 with 51.1% identity, plus 2WE4 E. faecalis carbamate kinase with a sulfate ion occupying the carbamoyl phosphate site at E = 2.4e-32. The method detects a genuine carbamoyl phosphate-handling fold at 42 orders of magnitude when one is present. Two further results cut against the subunit partition the hypothesis proposes. AllF and AllG are covalently fused into one polypeptide in 119 to 150 bacterial UniProtKB entries, with a STRING gene-fusion score of 0.899 for the fdrA-ylbE pair, against exactly one entry fusing AllG with AllH. The only conserved pocket that stands out above its own protein background sits in AllH, scoring 0.863 mean shell conservation against a protein median of 0.398, while AllG scores 0.659 against a median of 0.438, the flattest profile of the three.

Key findings

F1. No subunit of AllFGH has a carbamoyltransferase or carbamate kinase fold

The requested fold assignment was run against three databases simultaneously. Across all 1393 alignments, a regex scan of target descriptions for carbamoyl|carbamyl|transcarbamyl|carbamate kinase|aspartate transcarb|ornithine transcarb|amino acid kinase returned zero hits other than the three self-hits, which are the subunits' own AFDB entries, now renamed "Oxamate carbamoyltransferase subunit" in the database. The nearest keyword-matching non-self entries for AllF were carbamoyl phosphate synthase large chains at E = 1.0, 2.2 and 3.6, with Foldseek probability 0.15, 0.08 and 0.08, which are not hits in any meaningful sense.

The sequence-motif scan ran with internal positive controls. The S-x-R-T-R loop that binds carbamoyl phosphate in both transcarbamylase families is present in E. coli PyrB at position 53 and in ArgF and ArgI at position 56. It is absent from AllF, AllG and AllH. The OTCase catalytic HPxQ motif (PyrB 135, ArgF/ArgI 134) and the ATCase-type HCxP motif are likewise absent from all three subunits.

This result reproduces, by an independent computational route, what the discovering authors reported from their own sequence analysis. Kim and Kim write that "the OXTCase encoded by the three genes is a novel transcarbamylase that shows no similarity with known enzymes of the transcarbamylase family such as aspartate transcarbamylase, ornithine transcarbamylase, and YgeW transcarbamylase" (PMID: 38888336). The structural search extends the statement from sequence to fold.

F2. A positive control from the same operon proves the negative is real

The weakest point of any zero-hit result is that the search may simply have failed. AllK is the ideal control: it is encoded in the same allFGHK operon, it is an experimentally demonstrated catabolic carbamate kinase (EC 2.7.2.2), and it handles the same substrate, carbamoyl phosphate. Its AlphaFold model was submitted through the same Foldseek endpoint, in the same 3diaa mode, against pdb100 and cath50, and the output was passed through the same keyword regex.

AllK returned 507 alignments, 44 matching the keyword filter and 42 of those at E < 1e-3. The best was 8CRV, the carbamate kinase from Pseudomonas aeruginosa, at E = 4.9e-42 with 51.1% identity. The list includes 1E19, the carbamate kinase-like carbamoyl phosphate synthetase from Pyrococcus furiosus bound to ADP (E = 4.8e-36), and 2WE4, the E. faecalis carbamate kinase bound to a sulfate ion that mimics the carbamoyl phosphate substrate (E = 2.4e-32). CATH assigns AllK to superfamily 3.40.1160.10, the amino-acid kinase superfamily. The same filter applied to AllF (794 alignments), AllG (35) and AllH (102) returned zero such hits at any E-value.

This control carries the main inferential weight of the report. It converts "we found nothing" into "there is nothing to find by this method", and it simultaneously establishes that the method cannot answer the hypothesis's positive half. Any carbamoyl phosphate site in AllFGH is built on a scaffold with no structural precedent in PDB100 or CATH50.

F3. AllF sits on a succinyl-CoA ligase alpha scaffold that has lost its catalytic phosphohistidine

AllF has a solved crystal structure, PDB 3DMY at 2.07 Å, deposited as "Crystal Structure of a predicated acyl-CoA synthetase from E.coli", one polymer entity, no bound nonpolymer ligands. Foldseek recovers 3DMY chains B and A at E = 4.9e-71 and 8.5e-71 (98% identity, 455 aligned residues) and then only acyl-CoA synthetase (NDP-forming) structures: Korarchaeum ACD1 4YAJ (E = 4.6e-12), Nitrosopumilus 4-hydroxybutyryl-CoA synthetase 8IUP (1.0e-11), Pyrococcus PH0766 2CSU (3.0e-11), and the DMSP lyase DddX 7CM9 (2.4e-10). InterPro assigns AllF a PF27530 heavy-metal-associated domain (1-98), a PF02629 CoA-binding domain (193-287) and a PF00549 CoA-ligase domain (336-491) on two succinyl-CoA synthetase CATH 3.40.50.261 repeats.

That fold normally runs phosphoryl transfer through a covalent phosphohistidine. Bridger and colleagues established the residue directly: "The site of phosphorylation (His-246) and the binding sites for the substrates ADP and ATP are located in the alpha subunit, and the succinate and CoA binding sites are in beta" (PMID: 1986797). Mapping that position into AllF through the structural alignments gives valine. The GHxG anchor histidine of A. fulgidus SucD-like O28098 (H246), T. vaginalis P53399/P53400 (H262) and M. jannaschii Q58643 (H251) aligns to AllF Glu297, Glu297 and Val293. An independent reciprocal run, with the E. coli SucD AlphaFold model as query against PDB100, hit 3DMY at E = 8.1e-08 and placed SucD His247 of the GKRMGHAGAII motif at 3DMY index 193, which maps to AllF Val293 in the context L-F-L-G-Y-T-P-A-V293-A-R-D-E-N-V-W-F-A. AllF contains no histidine anywhere in the region structurally equivalent to the phosphohistidine loop; its histidines are at 3, 53, 222, 353, 362, 364, 373, 389, 494, 503 and 534.

Two consequences follow. The obvious alternative route to a phosphate-handling site in AllF, SCS-type phosphohistidine chemistry, is closed. The loss of a catalytic residue while the fold is retained is compatible with a structural or substrate-binding role, which the same study supports: "A mutant form of this enzyme, with the active site histidine residue replaced by aspartate, has been produced in large quantities and purified to homogeneity. This form appears to be indistinguishable from the native enzyme with respect to its subunit assembly, but has no ability to catalyze the overall reaction" (PMID: 1986797).

F4. AllF keeps an intact CoA/ADP phosphate-binding loop, so "AllF binds no phosphate" is too strong

Conservation profiles built from Foldseek AFDB50 alignments restricted to close homologs (seqId ≥ 25%, query coverage ≥ 70%; AllF 202 homologs, median alignment depth 192) show the PF02629 CoA-binding loop of AllF is invariant: G194 (1.00), G197 (0.96), A201 (0.97), S202 (0.84), G203 (1.00), T204 (0.93), G205 (1.00), Q207 (0.99). This is the G-x-x-x-G-A-S-G-T-G-x-Q nucleotide and CoA phosphate loop of the Rossmann subdomain. AllF H389 (context FYTVGRPHPMIDPTLR) and K261 are also invariant (1.00 and 0.99) and solvent exposed.

Two statements have to be kept apart. AllF has no carbamoyltransferase catalytic motif and has lost the SCS catalytic phosphohistidine, so it cannot run either canonical chemistry. AllF does still carry a conserved phosphate-binding element. Whether that loop binds carbamoyl phosphate, a nucleotide, CoA or nothing at all is untested. For the seed hypothesis this is a qualifier: structure rules out a canonical carbamoyltransferase site in AllF and does not rule out AllF contributing phosphate-group contacts to a shared active site.

F5. The only standout conserved pocket in the complex is in AllH, and AllG has none

A systematic scan computed, for every residue, the mean conservation of all residues within a 10 Å shell, restricted to partly exposed positions (14-26 neighbours). In AllH the top-ranked position is P163 at 0.863 against a protein-wide median of 0.398 and a 90th percentile of 0.612. AllH has 14 residues at ≥ 98% identity across its 78 close homologs, 7 of them solvent exposed, and all 7 fall in or next to one loop: W158-L159-G160-K161-G162-P163-G164-L165-T166-P167-S168-H169-D170-D171, mean AlphaFold pLDDT 96.0. Residues within 12 Å of the loop centroid at ≥ 85% conservation include D170 (1.00), D171 (1.00), T205 (0.99), S252 (0.99), S209 (0.94), D255 (0.90) and T206 (0.86). The pocket is lined by K161 and H169.

A G-x-G-x-G-x-T loop backed by an Asp-Asp pair, two Thr, two Ser, a Lys and a His has the residue composition of an anion plus divalent-metal site, and UniProt records Mg2+ as an OXTCase cofactor from PMID: 38888336. This is the best computational candidate for the carbamoyl phosphate site anywhere in the complex.

The same scan clears AllG. Its best pocket scores 0.659 against a median of 0.438 and a 90th percentile of 0.574, the weakest localized conservation of the three subunits. AllF's best scores 0.894 (D174) against a median of 0.402. If the seed hypothesis is read as "AllG", conservation refutes it. If it is read as "AllH", conservation gives a lead with three caveats: glycine invariance can reflect backbone geometry in a tight turn, no ligand-bound structure exists, and the AllH homolog set is the shallowest of the three at 78 sequences.

F6. AllG and AllH are structurally orphan, and E. coli carries a complete paralogous cluster

AllG's single significant non-self hit, 3CLQ chain D (UniProt Q82ZN9), is annotated only as a conserved protein of unknown function and contains no bound nonpolymer ligands, so the one experimental structure of an AllG homolog gives no positional marker for a phosphate site. AllG's only Pfam assignment is PF06545 (87-302) on three CATH folds named after that same unknown-function structure (1.10.10.660, 3.90.1710.10, 3.90.1700.10). AllH's only Pfam entries are PF11392 (158-263) and PF30333 (25-83).

In AFDB-SwissProt the best non-self hits for all three subunits are E. coli paralogs: AllF to YahF P77187 (E = 3.7e-42, 35.7% identity over 523 residues), AllG to YahG P77221 (E = 6.4e-49), AllH to YahE P77297 (E = 7.0e-13, 23.7% identity over 299 residues). E. coli K-12 therefore carries a second, uncharacterised three-gene cluster with the same subunit architecture. Residues conserved between the two clusters and across orthologs are candidates for the shared active site, which makes yahEFG a usable comparative handle rather than only a curation hazard.

F7. AllF and AllG are one polypeptide in many bacteria

STRING (E. coli K-12 MG1655, taxon 511145) scores the fdrA-ylbE (AllF-AllG) edge at 0.997 with a gene-fusion channel score of 0.899, the only fusion signal anywhere in the operon. Phylogenetic co-occurrence is 0.775 (AllF-AllG), 0.773 (AllF-AllH) and 0.768 (AllG-AllH); genomic neighbourhood is 0.75-0.78 for all three pairs.

The fusion prediction was verified directly against UniProtKB rather than taken on trust. 119 entries carry both PF06545 (AllG) and PF02629 (AllF CoA-binding) in one chain, 150 carry PF06545 with PF00549 (AllF CoA-ligase), and 149 carry PF06545 with PF27530 (AllF HMA N-terminal). In a sample of 100 such entries, 99 are 848-1023 residues long with a median of 1002, against 555 for E. coli AllF plus 419 for AllG, a sum of 974. Three checked individually, A0A090ZLC6 (Paenibacillus macerans, 1004 aa), A0A0R1K501 (Companilactobacillus nodensis, 1012 aa) and A0A0R1NBM8 (Schleiferilactobacillus perolens, 1015 aa), each carry all four domains PF27530, PF02629, PF00549 and PF06545 in a single chain. Only 1 UniProtKB entry fuses PF06545 (AllG) with PF11392 (AllH), against family sizes of 1769 and 1390.

A Rosetta-Stone fusion repeated across Paenibacillus, Lactobacillaceae, Streptococcus, Clostridium, Blautia and Mannheimia means the two polypeptides are one functional module whose halves have been covalently joined many times independently. Fusion partners of this kind normally share a substrate channel or an active-site interface. The seed hypothesis asks for a clean partition that excludes AllF; this evidence argues against that partition. The uniformly high co-occurrence across all three pairs says the genes are gained and lost together, consistent with all three being required, which matches the complex-level framing of the experimental work: "The OXTCase kinetic parameters were analyzed using the purified protein composed of AllF-AllG-AllH (FdrA-YlbE-YlbF)" (PMID: 38888336).

F8. AllF carries succinyl-CoA ligase GO annotations that the structure contradicts

QuickGO returns ten annotations for AllF (Q47208). Four of them, GO:0004775 succinate-CoA ligase (ADP-forming) activity, GO:0004776 succinate-CoA ligase (GDP-forming) activity, GO:0006099 tricarboxylic acid cycle and GO:0009361 succinate-CoA ligase complex (ADP-forming), are all IBA from GO_REF:0000033, assigned by GO_Central through PANTHER node PTN000122012 in family PTHR11117 (Succinate/malate CoA ligase alpha subunit), to which InterPro assigns AllF residues 14-491.

Three independent lines contradict them. AllF holds Val293 at the SucD His247 catalytic position. The allFGHK operon contains no succinyl-CoA ligase beta-subunit gene, and the beta subunit carries the succinate and CoA binding sites (PMID: 1986797). The experimentally established function is oxamate carbamoyltransferase in anaerobic allantoin catabolism, and the discovering authors propose the renaming on that basis: "Therefore, we suggest that the genes fdrA, ylbE, and ylbF are renamed allF, allG, and allH, respectively" (PMID: 38888336).

This is the single most actionable curation outcome of the investigation, and it holds whether or not the seed hypothesis is right.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The chemistry is fixed by the pathway. Allantoin is degraded anaerobically to ureidoglycolate, which OXTCase converts with carbamoyl phosphate to oxalurate and oxamate, and AllK then dephosphorylates carbamoyl phosphate to carbamate with ATP formation. The abstract of PMID: 36384810 states the route: "ureidoglycolate is finally converted to carbamoyl phosphate (CP) and oxamate, and then CP is dephosphorylated to carbamate by a catabolic carbamate kinase (CK), whereby ATP is formed", and confirms the operon by RT-PCR: "the genes fdrA, ylbE, ylbF, and ybcF are co-transcribed".

What each subunit contributes is what the hypothesis is about, and the structural evidence constrains it more than it determines it.

Three statements are established. AllFGH together catalyse the reaction with KM(CP) = 1.3 mM and were only ever assayed as an intact complex. AllK, a separate gene product in the same operon, is a bona fide carbamate kinase with the matching fold. No OXTCase subunit has a carbamoyltransferase, carbamate kinase or any other recognisable carbamoyl phosphate-handling fold.

Beyond that, the most defensible reading is that the active site lies at the AllF-AllG interface, with AllH contributing a metal or anion-binding element through its invariant D170-D171 loop. The AllF-AllG fusion in roughly 150 bacterial proteomes is the strongest single argument for this reading, because domains that fuse this often normally share a substrate channel. The AllH loop is the strongest positional candidate for a carbamoyl phosphate or Mg2+ contact, and it is a composition and conservation argument, not a demonstration. Both statements are inference drawn from monomer analyses and domain co-occurrence. Neither is a measurement.

GO curation implications

All items below are leads requiring curator verification. The current GO state for all four proteins is in provenance/table2_current_GO_annotations.csv (29 rows across Q47208, P77129, P0AAS5 and P37306); the decision table is provenance/go_decision_table.csv.

Retain unchanged. GO:0050205 oxamate carbamoyltransferase activity on AllF, IDA from PMID: 38888336 via EcoCyc, qualifier contributes_to. This is the right qualifier for a subunit of a complex that was only assayed intact, and the AllF-AllG fusion evidence supports AllF being inside the catalytic unit. GO:0000256 allantoin catabolic process (BP), IDA from the same paper, is directly supported and is the core BP term.

Change the qualifier. The second GO:0050205 on AllF is IEA from EC 2.1.3.5 (GO_REF:0000003) with qualifier enables. No experiment shows any single subunit enabling the activity alone. Downgrade to contributes_to, or treat the IEA as redundant with the IDA. The same applies to the GO:0050205 IEA records on AllG and AllH.

Remove as paralog carry-over. Four IBA terms on AllF propagated through PANTHER PTN000122012: GO:0004775 succinate-CoA ligase (ADP-forming) activity (MF), GO:0004776 succinate-CoA ligase (GDP-forming) activity (MF), GO:0006099 tricarboxylic acid cycle (BP), GO:0009361 succinate-CoA ligase complex (ADP-forming) (CC). Contradicted by Val293 at the SucD His247 position, by the absence of an SCS beta-subunit gene in allFGHK, and by the demonstrated OXTCase function.

Remove as uninformative. GO:0003824 catalytic activity on AllF, IEA from InterPro IPR005811, is a root-level MF term subsumed by GO:0050205.

Retain and flag as non-core. GO:0071973 bacterial-type flagellum-dependent cell motility on AllF, IMP from PMID: 17122336, qualifier acts_upstream_of_or_within. It is a real phenotype and the origin of the fdrA name, and it is downstream of the molecular function.

Retain at low priority. GO:0005829 cytosol on AllF, IBA, is_active_in. Plausible for a soluble cytoplasmic enzyme, with phylogenetic evidence only.

Do not add on current evidence. GO:0000287 magnesium ion binding or GO:0046872 metal ion binding on AllF. UniProt records Mg2+ as an OXTCase cofactor from PMID: 38888336, and the conserved Asp-Asp metal-type site found in this work is in AllH (D170, D171), not AllF. Adding a metal-binding term to AllF would repeat the subunit-assignment error the seed hypothesis sets out to avoid.

Do not add any CP-binding term to any individual subunit. Nothing in this analysis localises the carbamoyl phosphate site. No child of carbamoyl phosphate binding should be placed on AllF, AllG or AllH.

Mechanistic scope

The immediate molecular activity under test is binding of carbamoyl phosphate and transfer of its carbamoyl group to oxamate, EC 2.1.3.5. The measured activity belongs to the AllF-AllG-AllH complex, not to any single polypeptide, and the published kinetics (PMID: 38888336) were obtained on the purified three-subunit complex.

Direct gene-product activity, on current evidence: AllF contributes to oxamate carbamoyltransferase activity as part of the complex. That is the limit of what any assay supports for AllF alone.

Downstream and context-dependent items that are separate from the molecular function: the allantoin catabolic process (GO:0000256), which is the pathway the activity sits in; the anaerobic nitrogen-source phenotype; and the motility phenotype behind GO:0071973, which is a loss-of-function observation two or more steps removed from carbamoyl transfer.

Inference, clearly labelled as such: the active site spanning the AllF-AllG interface, and the AllH D170-D171 loop as a Mg2+ or anion site. Both come from computational analyses of monomers plus domain co-occurrence. Neither has been measured.

Evidence base

Oxamic transcarbamylase of Escherichia coli is encoded by the three genes allFGH (formerly fdrA, ylbE, and ylbF) (PMID: 38888336). The reference supplied with the hypothesis and the only primary source for the enzyme. It supports the hypothesis's framing by establishing that the activity exists and requires three genes, and it blocks the hypothesis's resolution by assaying only the intact complex: "The OXTCase kinetic parameters were analyzed using the purified protein composed of AllF-AllG-AllH (FdrA-YlbE-YlbF); for the substrate CP, KM and Vmax were 1.3 mM and 15.4 U/mg OXTCase, respectively." It independently anticipates the central structural result: "the OXTCase encoded by the three genes is a novel transcarbamylase that shows no similarity with known enzymes of the transcarbamylase family such as aspartate transcarbamylase, ornithine transcarbamylase, and YgeW transcarbamylase." It also fixes the functional context that contradicts the IBA TCA-cycle annotations: "Therefore, we suggest that the genes fdrA, ylbE, and ylbF are renamed allF, allG, and allH, respectively."

The ybcF Gene of Escherichia coli Encodes a Local Orphan Enzyme, Catabolic Carbamate Kinase (PMID: 36384810). Establishes the operon by RT-PCR and the downstream fate of carbamoyl phosphate, and supplies the protein used as the positive control: "The activity of the catabolic CK was analyzed using purified YbcF protein. The Vmax is 1.82 U/mg YbcF for CP and 1.94 U/mg YbcF for ADP, and the KM value is 0.47 mM for CP and 0.43 mM for ADP." Because AllK is a demonstrated carbamoyl phosphate-handling enzyme from the same operon, it is the correct true-positive for testing whether the fold search would see such an enzyme.

Functional consequences of substitution of the active site (phospho)histidine residue of Escherichia coli succinyl-CoA synthetase (PMID: 1986797). Supplies the reference residue for the AllF fold-chemistry test: "The site of phosphorylation (His-246) and the binding sites for the substrates ADP and ATP are located in the alpha subunit, and the succinate and CoA binding sites are in beta." It also shows why losing that residue is compatible with AllF keeping a structural role: the H-to-D mutant "appears to be indistinguishable from the native enzyme with respect to its subunit assembly, but has no ability to catalyze the overall reaction."

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Every structural analysis here was on monomers. This is the dominant limitation of the whole investigation. The active site most plausibly sits at a subunit interface, which no single-chain method can see. No AlphaFold-Multimer or AlphaFold3 service was reachable from this environment, and no experimental complex structure exists. A monomer-only analysis can prove that a fold is absent from each chain, and it cannot find a site built from two chains.

No experimental structure of the AllFGH complex exists. PDB 3DMY covers AllF alone and contains no bound ligands. 3CLQ, the only experimental structure of an AllG homolog, is annotated as unknown function and also has no bound ligands, so it supplies no positional marker.

No subunit-level kinetics have been reported. No construct of one or two subunits has been expressed and assayed, so no subunit has been shown to bind carbamoyl phosphate. This is the gap that makes the seed hypothesis untestable from published data.

The ligand of the AllF PF02629 loop is unknown. The loop is invariant across 202 homologs. Whether it binds a nucleotide, CoA, carbamoyl phosphate or nothing was checked only by fold assignment, which cannot distinguish these.

The AllH conservation signal rests on 78 sequences, the shallowest homolog set of the three, against 202 for AllF and 154 for AllG. Glycine invariance in a tight turn can reflect backbone geometry alone rather than ligand contact.

The InterPro taxon-overlap co-occurrence analysis planned as table 6 was never produced, because that code block failed on a disallowed import and was not re-run. No result from it is cited anywhere in this report. The fusion conclusion rests instead on the UniProtKB domain counts, which were computed and saved.

The paralogous YahE-YahF-YahG cluster (P77297, P77187, P77221) is uncharacterised. It cannot currently be used as a comparative handle for active-site residues, and it is a standing hazard for automated annotation transfer in both directions.

Discriminating tests

Ordered by how directly each would settle the hypothesis.

  1. AlphaFold3 or Boltz prediction of the AllF-AllG-AllH heterotrimer with carbamoyl phosphate and Mg2+ as ligands. Report which chains contribute residues within 4 Å of the carbamoyl phosphate. This directly tests one subunit against an interface, and it is the cheapest decisive test. It was not run here because no multimer-with-ligand service was reachable from this environment.
  2. Site-directed mutagenesis of the AllH invariant loop: K161A, T166A, S168A, H169A, D170A, D171A, each in the context of the full three-subunit complex, with KM(CP) and kcat measured against the 1.3 mM and 15.4 U/mg baseline. A selective rise in KM(CP) with retained assembly places a CP-contacting element in AllH.
  3. Mutagenesis of the AllF PF02629 loop: S202A, T204A, Q207A, plus H389A and K261A. If any of these raises KM(CP), the "not in AllF" clause fails outright.
  4. Express and assay a natural AllF-AllG fusion protein, A0A090ZLC6 (Paenibacillus macerans, 1004 aa) or A0A0R1K501 (Companilactobacillus nodensis, 1012 aa), with and without an AllH homolog. If the fusion alone has OXTCase activity, AllH is not catalytic. If it requires AllH, AllH contributes to the site.
  5. Carbamoyl phosphate binding by isothermal titration calorimetry or differential scanning fluorimetry on each isolated subunit and each pairwise combination. Nothing of this kind has been reported and it is the most direct localisation experiment available.
  6. Cryo-EM or crystallography of AllFGH with a carbamoyl phosphate analogue, for example phosphonoacetamide, or sulfate plus carbamate, following the 2WE4 precedent where a sulfate ion marks the carbamoyl phosphate site in E. faecalis carbamate kinase.
  7. Characterise the YahE-YahF-YahG cluster. Residues conserved between the two E. coli clusters and across orthologs are candidates for the shared active site, and a second functional system would double the available comparative signal.
