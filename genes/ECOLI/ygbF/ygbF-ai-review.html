Gene Description

ygbF, also known as cas2, encodes the small CRISPR-associated protein Cas2 of the Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system. Cas1 and Cas2 are the two most widely conserved Cas proteins and together constitute the adaptation machinery, the stage of CRISPR immunity in which a protospacer captured from invading DNA is integrated as a new spacer at the leader-proximal end of a CRISPR array. Cas2 is a 94-residue ferredoxin-like protein that forms a homodimer. Two Cas1 dimers sandwich the Cas2 dimer to build a Cas1(4)-Cas2(2) heterohexamer, within which Cas2 is the structural keystone: it is the bridge that holds the two Cas1 dimers in a fixed relative orientation, and the protospacer duplex lies across a flat surface that runs from one Cas1 dimer, over the Cas2 dimer, to the other. Cas2 does not perform the integration chemistry. The chemistry is done by Cas1, and substituting the conserved glutamate at position 9 that would be Cas2's catalytic residue leaves spacer acquisition and in vitro integration unaffected, whereas the equivalent substitutions in Cas1 abolish both. What Cas2 contributes instead is architecture and measurement: deleting the C-terminal beta6-beta7 element abolishes complex assembly, CRISPR DNA binding by the complex, and spacer acquisition; arginines R16 and R78 form part of a shared arginine clamp with Cas1 R41 that grips the middle of the protospacer duplex, and charge-reversal at those positions drastically reduces acquisition in vivo. Together these contacts make the complex a molecular ruler that selects a 23 bp duplex with short 3' single-stranded extensions and so fixes the 33 bp spacer length characteristic of this system. Cas2 shows no DNA binding on its own and contributes nothing to the branched-DNA nuclease and disintegration reactions that Cas1 catalyses alone; the complex, however, integrates protospacers far more efficiently than Cas1 by itself. Neither protein alone supports array expansion in vivo, and co-expression of the two is sufficient. The gene is the last gene of the casABCDE-ygbT-ygbF operon, which is silenced by H-NS and induced by LeuO and by the BaeSR envelope-stress system, and its disruption causes loss of plasmid silencing. Although the protein is named an endoribonuclease after RNA-cleaving activities reported for Cas2 homologues from other organisms, no nuclease activity has been demonstrated for the E. coli protein, and its putative active site is dispensable for every adaptation phenotype tested.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

protospacer length determination activity

Definition: A molecular function by which a protein or protein assembly selects a double-stranded nucleic acid fragment of a defined length by presenting a binding surface of fixed dimensions, such that only fragments matching that span are productively engaged, thereby setting the length of the product incorporated downstream.

Justification: The behaviour that distinguishes Cas2 from a generic subunit, and the Cas1-Cas2 complex from Cas1 alone, is dimensional: the bridged surface accepts a 23 bp duplex with short 3' single-stranded extensions and thereby fixes the 33 bp spacer length that defines this CRISPR system's architecture. GO has no term for this molecular-ruler behaviour. GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity, used in core_functions, captures the bridging but says nothing about measurement, and no nucleic-acid-binding term encodes length selectivity. The crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity module records this as a knowledge gap, which it genuinely is; this entry names the gap rather than filling it with an ill-fitting identifier. A term would be useful beyond CRISPR, since length-measuring assemblies recur in nucleic-acid processing. It is proposed as a property of the assembly, so a Cas2 annotation to it would properly carry the contributes_to qualifier.

Core Functions

Structural adaptor subunit of the Cas1(4)-Cas2(2) spacer-acquisition complex. The Cas2 homodimer bridges two Cas1 dimers and holds them in the orientation that creates a continuous protospacer-binding surface spanning all three dimers; arginines R16 and R78 contribute to the shared arginine clamp that grips the duplex, so that the assembled complex measures protospacer length and sets the 33 bp spacer size of this system. Cas2 performs no catalysis - its own conserved active-site glutamate is dispensable - but complex assembly through its C-terminal beta6-beta7 element is required for CRISPR DNA binding and for spacer acquisition, and the complex integrates protospacers far more efficiently than Cas1 alone.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: UniProt's recommended name for P45956 is "CRISPR-associated endoribonuclease Cas2", with EC 3.1.-.- and the Endonuclease, Nuclease and Hydrolase keywords, yet no ribonuclease activity has ever been reported for the E. coli protein; the name derives from archaeal Cas2 homologues, a Bacillus halodurans Cas2 that cleaves dsDNA rather than RNA, and a PDB deposition annotated as a putative ssRNA endonuclease. The conserved E9 "catalytic" residue is dispensable for every adaptation phenotype tested. Should the recommended name and the EC/keyword assignments be revised, and is any RNA-cleaving function of Cas2 relevant to adaptation in any organism?

Suggested experts: UniProt, CRISPR adaptation community

Q: Is GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity the right molecular function for a non-catalytic subunit whose bridging geometry is the element whose removal abolishes the complex's activity? The crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity module currently asserts no molecular function for Cas2 on the grounds that it is not catalytic. GO's molecular-function branch is not restricted to catalysis, and resolving this would settle how a large class of structural Cas and non-Cas subunits should be annotated.

Suggested experts: GO molecular function curators

Q: Does Cas2 contribute anything beyond the Cas1-Cas2 complex? Its structural homology to the VapD family of toxin-antitoxin toxins has been noted repeatedly, and a CRISPR-independent role would explain why a putative nuclease active site is conserved while being dispensable for adaptation.

Suggested experts: toxin-antitoxin community

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Engineer Cas2 variants with insertions or deletions in the bridging surface that lengthen or shorten the span between the two Cas1 dimers, reconstitute integration in vitro, and sequence the integration products to see whether the selected protospacer length shifts predictably. A shift would establish the ruler as a property of the Cas2 bridge and would be the decisive evidence for a length-determination molecular function.

Hypothesis: Cas2's contribution to adaptation is geometric: the dimensions of the bridge it forms between the two Cas1 dimers set the spacer length.

Type: biochemical

Experiment: Assay purified E. coli Cas2, alone and in the Cas1-Cas2 complex, against a panel of single-stranded RNA, double-stranded RNA, single-stranded DNA and double-stranded DNA substrates across a range of divalent cations, alongside an archaeal Cas2 as a positive control and the E9A variant as a negative. A uniformly negative result on the E. coli protein would justify revising the recommended protein name and would close off a persistent source of propagated nuclease annotations.

Hypothesis: Cas2 has no ribonuclease activity relevant to the E. coli type I-E system, and the UniProt name is a family-level holdover.

Type: biochemical

Experiment: Titrate expression of wild-type Cas2 against the beta6-beta7 deletion and the E9A active-site variant in a cas1 cas2 deletion strain, measuring both Cas1-Cas2 complex formation and spacer acquisition frequency. If acquisition tracks complex formation and is indifferent to the active site across the whole range, the adaptor assignment is secure and the nuclease assignment is excluded.

Hypothesis: Complex assembly, rather than Cas2 protein abundance, is what adaptation requires.

Type: genetic

Adjudicating the UniProt name "CRISPR-associated endoribonuclease Cas2"

The recommended name asserts an endoribonuclease activity, with EC=3.1.-.- and the
keywords Endonuclease, Hydrolase, Nuclease. The provenance of that name is
other organisms' Cas2 proteins, not E. coli Cas2:

PMID:26284603

and even within the family the reported substrate is inconsistent:

PMID:26284603

For E. coli Cas2 specifically: the only structure deposited for the isolated protein
is annotated by its depositors as a putative ssRNA endonuclease (UniProt Ref. 10,
PDB 4MAK, "Crystal structure of a putative ssRNA endonuclease Cas2"); no
ribonuclease assay on P45956 is reported in any of the cached papers; UniProt's own
FUNCTION block concedes "This subunit's probable nuclease activity is not required
for spacer acquisition"; and the E9 active-site mutants are fully functional (above).

Verdict: the endoribonuclease activity is unsubstantiated for this protein and
irrelevant to adaptation. The honest outcome is that it must not be annotated, and
GOA is already correct here — there is no nuclease, hydrolase, or RNA-binding row for
P45956 to act on. I record the adjudication in suggested_questions (so the UniProt
name is flagged rather than silently inherited) rather than inventing a REMOVE
target that does not exist in GOA. Anyone tempted to propagate GO:0004521 or
GO:0016788 onto E. coli Cas2 from the protein name should read this section first.

What Cas2 does do — and why I partly disagree with the module

The module crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity asserts no molecular function for the
cas2_adaptation_subunit annoton, with role_description "Dimeric scaffold that
bridges two Cas1 dimers and measures the protospacer duplex; no catalytic activity is
asserted, which is why this annoton carries no molecular function," and books it as a
knowledge gap.

I agree with the premise and disagree with the conclusion.

The premise — no catalytic activity — is correct and well-evidenced (previous
section). But "no catalytic function" is not the same as "no molecular function". The
GO molecular-function branch is not restricted to catalysis; it includes structural
and adaptor activities, and GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity is
defined as "An adaptor activity that brings together two or more macromolecules in
contact, permitting those molecules to function in a coordinated way." That is a
description of what Cas2 does, and the literature says so in almost those words:

PMID:26284603

The structural and functional support is at least as strong as the support for Cas1's
catalytic assignment:

  1. It is literally the bridge.
    PMID:24793649
  2. The bridging is required for function, and the requirement maps to the
    interface rather than to Cas2's presence.
    PMID:24793649
    PMID:24793649
    The clean case is the beta6-beta7 deletion (residues 79-94): UniProt records "Loss
    of spacer acquisition, no Cas1-Cas2 complex formation, loss of CRISPR DNA-binding
    by complex" for that variant, i.e. removing the bridging element removes the

  3. It measurably accelerates the reaction it is not catalysing — so it is not a

Recommendation to the module: replace "no molecular function" with
GO:0030674 protein-macromolecule adaptor activity, and retain the molecular-ruler
behaviour as the genuine knowledge gap, since GO has no term for duplex-length
measurement and the ruler is a property of the Cas1-Cas2 assembly rather than of
Cas2 alone. The knowledge gap is real; it is just a narrower gap than the module
currently claims. I have raised the ruler term as a proposed_new_terms entry so the
gap is recorded rather than papered over with an ill-fitting id.

I note the competing view honestly: a curator could reasonably hold that GO:0030674
is a near-vacuous restatement of "it is a subunit", and that is why the term is used
sparingly. My answer is that Cas2's bridging is not incidental to the complex but is
the element whose removal abolishes the activity (point 2), which is exactly the
discriminating test GO:0030674 is for.

GO:0043571 vs GO:0099048 placement across the pair

Checked against the ontology rather than assumed. The two terms are in disjoint
branches: GO:0043571 descends from GO:0043570 / GO:0006259 DNA metabolic process / GO:0051276 chromosome organization, whereas GO:0099048 descends from
GO:0098542 defense response to other organism / GO:0006952 defense response.
Neither is an ancestor of the other, so they are complementary (the DNA-level event
vs. the immune system it serves) and not redundant. Both genes legitimately carry
both, and nothing needs to move between ygbT and ygbF. ygbF's GOA is missing only the
IBA/IEA rows that ygbT has — an artefact of Cas2 being a short, poorly-conserved
family rather than a curation decision, and not something to fix by inventing NEW
rows.

  ygbF, also known as cas2, encodes the small CRISPR-associated protein Cas2 of the
  Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system. Cas1 and Cas2 are the two most
  widely conserved Cas proteins and together constitute the adaptation machinery,
  the stage of CRISPR immunity in which a protospacer captured from invading DNA is
  integrated as a new spacer at the leader-proximal end of a CRISPR array. Cas2 is a
  94-residue ferredoxin-like protein that forms a homodimer. Two Cas1 dimers
  sandwich the Cas2 dimer to build a Cas1(4)-Cas2(2) heterohexamer, within which
  Cas2 is the structural keystone: it is the bridge that holds the two Cas1 dimers
  in a fixed relative orientation, and the protospacer duplex lies across a flat
  surface that runs from one Cas1 dimer, over the Cas2 dimer, to the other. Cas2
  does not perform the integration chemistry. The chemistry is done by Cas1, and
  substituting the conserved glutamate at position 9 that would be Cas2's catalytic
  residue leaves spacer acquisition and in vitro integration unaffected, whereas the
  equivalent substitutions in Cas1 abolish both. What Cas2 contributes instead is
  architecture and measurement: deleting the C-terminal beta6-beta7 element abolishes
  complex assembly, CRISPR DNA binding by the complex, and spacer acquisition;
  arginines R16 and R78 form part of a shared arginine clamp with Cas1 R41 that grips
  the middle of the protospacer duplex, and charge-reversal at those positions
  drastically reduces acquisition in vivo. Together these contacts make the complex a
  molecular ruler that selects a 23 bp duplex with short 3' single-stranded
  extensions and so fixes the 33 bp spacer length characteristic of this system.
  Cas2 shows no DNA binding on its own and contributes nothing to the branched-DNA
  nuclease and disintegration reactions that Cas1 catalyses alone; the complex,
  however, integrates protospacers far more efficiently than Cas1 by itself. Neither
  protein alone supports array expansion in vivo, and co-expression of the two is
  sufficient. The gene is the last gene of the casABCDE-ygbT-ygbF operon, which is
  silenced by H-NS and induced by LeuO and by the BaeSR envelope-stress system, and
  its disruption causes loss of plasmid silencing. Although the protein is named an
  endoribonuclease after RNA-cleaving activities reported for Cas2 homologues from
  other organisms, no nuclease activity has been demonstrated for the E. coli
  protein, and its putative active site is dispensable for every adaptation phenotype
  tested.

  proposed_definition: >-
    A molecular function by which a protein or protein assembly selects a
    double-stranded nucleic acid fragment of a defined length by presenting a binding
    surface of fixed dimensions, such that only fragments matching that span are
    productively engaged, thereby setting the length of the product incorporated
    downstream.

    complex from Cas1 alone, is dimensional: the bridged surface accepts a 23 bp duplex
