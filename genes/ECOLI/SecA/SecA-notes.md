# SecA notes

Manual synthesis in place of provider deep research, which was unavailable in this environment.

- SecA is the peripheral ATPase motor of the E. coli Sec translocase. It receives SecB-bound precursors, binds the SecYEG channel at the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane, binds preprotein signal and mature regions, and couples ATP binding and hydrolysis to processive polypeptide movement through SecYEG. This is supported by the SecB-SecA-SecY/E binding cascade [PMID:2170023, "The binding cascade of SecB to SecA to SecY/E mediates preprotein targeting to the E. coli plasma membrane."], the purified SecY/E reconstitution study [PMID:2167176, "The SecY/E protein, reconstituted into proteoliposomes, acts together with SecA protein to support translocation of proOmpA, the precursor form of outer membrane protein A."], and the translocation ATPase study [PMID:2542029, "These data show that the SecA protein has a central role in coupling the hydrolysis of ATP to the transfer of pre-secretory proteins across the membrane."]
- SecA is partitioned between cytosol and inner membrane, and the membrane-associated fraction is functionally relevant. Cabelli et al. found that approximately half of SecA was inner-membrane associated in wild-type cells [PMID:1837021, "In a wild-type strain approximately half of the cellular SecA content was found to be associated with the inner membrane, while the remainder was soluble."].
- The C-terminal zinc site is real but supports SecB binding rather than defining a separate pathway. The zinc-binding paper concluded that one zinc atom is present in each monomeric C terminus and that zinc stabilizes the SecB-binding domain [PMID:10213615, "It is concluded that the SecB binding domain of SecA is stabilized by a zinc ion that promotes the functional binding of SecB to SecA."].
- SecA can bind ribosomes and can support a cotranslational targeting route for at least the RodZ inner-membrane substrate, but these ribosome-binding activities are accessory to the canonical SecB/SecYEG export cycle. Wang et al. reported SecA-dependent cotranslational RodZ targeting [PMID:28928132, "SecA is necessary and sufficient to direct the targeting and translocation of RodZ to the bacterial plasma membrane in an obligatorily cotranslational mechanism."], and Knupffer et al. mapped SecA on the ribosomal protein uL23 surface near the tunnel exit [PMID:31409676, "We have mapped the interaction of SecA with translating and nontranslating ribosomes and demonstrate that the N terminus and the helical linker domain of SecA bind to an acidic patch on the surface of the ribosomal protein uL23."].
- SecA self-association and binary contacts with SecB, SecYEG subunits, FtsY, and model substrates are mechanistically plausible, but generic GO:0005515 protein-binding rows do not state SecA's specific ATPase, preprotein-binding, SecYEG-binding, or targeting activity. Generic protein-binding IPI rows for SecA should be removed as uninformative interaction records, not because the reported interactions are false.
