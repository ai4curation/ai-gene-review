CC   -!- FUNCTION: The RuvA-RuvB-RuvC complex processes Holliday junctions
CC       during genetic recombination and DNA repair (PubMed:6374379).
CC       Endonuclease that resolves Holliday junction (HJ) intermediates.
CC       Cleaves cruciform DNA by making single-stranded nicks across the
CC       junction at symmetrical positions within the homologous arms, leaving a
CC       5'-phosphate and a 3'-hydroxyl group; requires a central core of
CC       homology in the junction (PubMed:10471285, PubMed:1661673,
CC       PubMed:1758493, PubMed:1829835, PubMed:36000732, PubMed:8001122,
CC       PubMed:8106500, PubMed:8195150, PubMed:9000618, PubMed:9135161,
CC       PubMed:9160752). The consensus cleavage sequence is 5'-(A/T)TT(C>G/A)-
CC       3'. Cleavage occurs on the 3'-side of the TT dinucleotide at the point
CC       of strand exchange, although there is some flexibility in the position
CC       cleaved (PubMed:10471285, PubMed:8001122, PubMed:8195150,
CC       PubMed:9135161). The cleavage reactions can be uncoupled; incision
CC       requires the presence of two consensus cleavage sequences, although
CC       they do not have to be identical (PubMed:9135161). The presence of a
CC       5'-phosphate in a half-cut site accelerates cleavage of the second
CC       site, ensuring the second cleavage occurs within the lifetime of a
CC       single RuvC-HJ complex (PubMed:19399178). Binds to cruciform DNA in a
CC       sequence non-specific manner (PubMed:10471285, PubMed:8106500,
CC       PubMed:8195150). {ECO:0000255|HAMAP-Rule:MF_00034,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:10471285, ECO:0000269|PubMed:1661673,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:1758493, ECO:0000269|PubMed:1829835,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:19399178, ECO:0000269|PubMed:36000732,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6374379, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8001122,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8106500, ECO:0000269|PubMed:8195150,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9000618, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9135161,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9160752}.
CC   -!- FUNCTION: An in vitro resolvase system that forms and processes HJ has
CC       been reconstituted with DNA substrates, RuvA, RuvB and RuvC. RuvA-RuvB
CC       increases the rate of strand exchange (branch migration), dissociates
CC       the RecA filament and allows RuvC to cleave in both orientations at the
CC       cruciform junction (PubMed:10421637, PubMed:9160752). HJ-RuvA-RuvB-RuvC
CC       complexes resolve Holliday junctions and also undergo branch migration,
CC       providing evidence for a coupled branch migration/HJ resolution
CC       reaction (PubMed:10421637). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10421637,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9160752}.

CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homodimer which binds Holliday junction (HJ) DNA. The HJ
CC       becomes 2-fold symmetrical on binding to RuvC with unstacked arms; it
CC       has a different conformation from HJ DNA in complex with RuvA. In the
CC       full resolvosome a probable DNA-RuvA(4)-RuvB(12)-RuvC(2) complex forms
CC       which resolves the HJ (By similarity). A mix of monomer and homodimer
CC       in solution (PubMed:8106500). Homodimer which binds DNA; there is
CC       probably a monomer-dimer equilibrium during DNA-binding
CC       (PubMed:10471285, PubMed:1661673, PubMed:7638215, PubMed:7923356,
CC       PubMed:9135161). Can form complexes with RuvA and Holliday junction
CC       (HJ) DNA, in which the resolvase activity is inhibited
CC       (PubMed:9000618). Forms a complex with RuvB without DNA; RuvA-RuvC
CC       complexes were not detected in the absence of DNA (PubMed:9160752). In
CC       the presence of HJ DNA a DNA-RuvA-RuvB-RuvC complex forms with 2 rings
CC       of RuvB that resolves upon addition of ATP. Upon resolution the protein
CC       complex dissociates from DNA (PubMed:10421637, PubMed:9637927).
CC       {ECO:0000255|HAMAP-Rule:MF_00034, ECO:0000269|PubMed:10421637,

CC   -!- DOMAIN: Each subunit has a cleft approximately 20-25 Angstroms wide and
CC       20 Angstroms deep, which is sufficient to accommodate dsDNA. Residues

CC       142) line this cleft. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:7923356}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Loss of Holliday junction endonuclease activity
CC       (PubMed:1829835). Increased sensitivity to mitomycin and UV light,
CC       almost normal conjugative recombination in wild-type backgrounds
CC       (PubMed:2164626). Suppresses lethality in dnaB temperature-sensitive
CC       mutants; ruvC cannot be deleted in recB-recC temperature sensitive
CC       mutants (PubMed:9814711). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:1829835,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:2164626, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9814711}.
CC   -!- MISCELLANEOUS: The dual incision reaction has defined structure and
CC       sequence requirements which must be satisfied for efficient nuclease
CC       activity. These requirements are biologically important since they
CC       serve as a control mechanism that prohibits the nuclease from cutting
CC       DNA at structures that could bear a physical resemblance to a Holliday
CC       structure. {ECO:0000269|PubMed:9135161}.
