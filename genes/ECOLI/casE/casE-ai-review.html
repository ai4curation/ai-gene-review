Gene Description

CasE (also called Cas6e or Cse3) is the CRISPR RNA processing endoribonuclease of the Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system and, after catalysis, a structural subunit of the complex whose assembly it nucleates. It is built from two tandem RNA recognition motifs connected by a short linker, whose beta-sheets face each other to create a V-shaped cleft on one side of the protein. The opposite face, together with a positively charged groove-loop on the C-terminal motif, grips the stem-loop formed by each CRISPR repeat and makes base-specific contacts both within the stem-loop and in the nucleotides flanking it, which is how a single enzyme recognises every repeat in an array regardless of the intervening spacer. CasE cleaves the primary array transcript within each repeat, immediately upstream of the hairpin, releasing single spacer guides that carry an 8-nucleotide repeat-derived 5' handle and a 3' hairpin; the reaction requires no divalent metal ion and no ATP, and leaves a 5'-hydroxyl and a 2',3'-cyclic phosphate. A conserved histidine is required for catalysis but not for incorporation into the complex. CasE does not release its product. It stays bound to the 3' hairpin of the mature guide and is tethered to the Cas7 backbone by a helix from the first backbone subunit inserting into its V-shaped cleft, so the enzyme-product pair caps the head of Cascade and serves as the platform for ordered assembly of the remaining ten subunits. Loss of CasE abolishes pre-crRNA cleavage, resistance to bacteriophage lambda and plasmid silencing. CasE also associates with the adaptation nuclease Cas1 (YgbT) and reduces its cleavage of Holliday junctions in vitro.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex

Definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction. The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent is the Csy complex.

Justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic ancestor GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and its subunits are the direct targets of a whole family of phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible. For CasE in particular, such a term is what would let the dual role be stated properly - a processing enzyme that becomes a subunit of the complex assembled on its own product.

Parent term: ribonucleoprotein complex

Core Functions

Cleaves the primary CRISPR array transcript within each repeat, immediately upstream of the repeat hairpin, converting it into mature single-spacer guide RNAs that carry an 8-nucleotide repeat-derived 5' handle and a 3' hairpin. The reaction is metal- and ATP-independent and depends on a conserved active-site histidine; purified CasE performs it alone, and only on its cognate repeat sequence.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: GO has no term for crRNA processing or crRNA biogenesis, so the activity has to be recorded as generic RNA processing, and the CRISPR-specific alternative, GO:0043571 maintenance of CRISPR repeat elements, is an array-level term that stretches from spacer acquisition to array transcription. Should a crRNA maturation process term be requested as a child of RNA processing?

Q: Does the in vitro inhibition of Cas1 Holliday-junction cleavage by CasE have a physiological role - for example throttling Cas1's recombination-associated activity when Cascade is engaged - or is it an artefact of the concentrations used?

Suggested experts: Mohan Babu, Andrew Emili, Alexander Savchenko

Q: Is CasE catalytically active only once per guide, or does it turn over? The enzyme stays bound to its product, which implies product inhibition, yet a single CasE must process a multi-repeat array.

Suggested experts: Stan J J Brouns, John van der Oost, Blake Wiedenheft

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Measure single-turnover and multiple-turnover kinetics of purified CasE on substrates containing one, two and four repeat-spacer units, with and without stoichiometric CasC and CasD, and follow product release directly by fluorescence anisotropy of a labelled hairpin. If Cas7 loading is what frees the enzyme, turnover should depend on CasC concentration.

Hypothesis: CasE turns over on a multi-repeat array despite remaining bound to each product, because downstream Cas7 loading displaces it onto the assembling complex.

Type: enzyme kinetics with product-release readout

Experiment: Complement a casE deletion with the H20A allele while supplying mature crRNA from a separate, pre-processed transcript that needs no cleavage, then test phage lambda resistance and target dsDNA binding. Restoration of immunity would confirm that the histidine's only essential role is catalytic.

Hypothesis: The CasE active-site histidine is required for immunity only through processing, not through any role in the assembled complex.

Type: genetic complementation with bypass substrate

Experiment: Map the CasE surface that contacts Cas1 by crosslinking mass spectrometry, generate separation-of-function CasE variants that retain processing and Cascade assembly but lose Cas1 binding, and compare them with wild type for rates of primed spacer acquisition, chromosomal integrity, and sensitivity to DNA damaging agents.

Hypothesis: The CasE-Cas1 interaction regulates Cas1 activity in vivo during primed acquisition.

Type: interface mapping and separation-of-function genetics

Session 1 (2026-10-01): initial full review

Reviewed together with casA, casB, casC and casD as one complex. CasE is the only one of
the five with a demonstrated catalytic activity, and it is dual-role: it is both the
pre-crRNA processing enzyme and a structural subunit of the complex it builds. Both have
to survive the review, and the catalysis must not be buried under the complex membership.

The catalysis is subunit-intrinsic

Of the five subunits, only casE is required for pre-crRNA cleavage. Brouns deleted the
genes individually in the native strain and then, to rule out polar effects on the
overlapping casD/casE genes, rebuilt Cascade subunit by subunit in a host with no cas
genes
PMID:18703739.
Purified CasE alone, as a MalE fusion, cleaved the cognate pre-crRNA
PMID:18703739,
and the chemistry needs no cofactor
PMID:18703739,
consistent with the family as a whole
PMID:25103409.
The active-site assignment rests on a conserved histidine, and the mutant separates
catalysis from assembly cleanly
PMID:18703739.
That same H20A allele also shows the processing step is mechanistically required for
immunity, not just correlated with it
PMID:18703739.

So GO:0004521 RNA endonuclease activity (IDA, PMID:18703739) is ACCEPTed as a core
function, and GO:0006396 RNA processing (IMP) is ACCEPTed as the matching process. I
keep GO:0006396 at the parent level because GO has no crRNA-processing child; a QuickGO
ontology search for "crRNA" returns zero terms and for "CRISPR" returns only five, all
biological_process.

Adding GO:0043571, with the comparator check done first

CasE is missing GO:0043571 maintenance of CRISPR repeat elements, whose definition
explicitly includes "transcription of the CRISPR repeat arrays into RNA and processing".
Before proposing it as NEW I ran the required checks:

  • Who performs the step? CasE itself cleaves the array transcript, shown as a
    purified protein acting alone on its cognate substrate. This is not a necessity
    argument from a knockout; it is the enzyme in a tube with the RNA.
  • Comparator check. QuickGO returns 14 non-IEA annotations to GO:0043571. Among them:
    UniProtKB:Q02MM2 cas6f IMP PMID:26586803 — the type I-F orthologue of casE — and
    UniProtKB:Q8U1S4 cas6 IDA PMID:19141480, a standalone type III Cas6. Within this
    repository, genes/PSEAB/cas6f/cas6f-ai-review.yaml ACCEPTs GO:0043571 on both its IEA
    and IMP rows. So Cas6-family processing enzymes do carry this term, and casE's absence
    is an uncurated gap in a populated term, not a convention excluding it.
  • Not an ancestor or descendant of anything casE already carries. GO:0043571 sits in
    a different part of the process branch from GO:0006396 and GO:0099048.
  • Mirror check against casD. casD carries GO:0043571 as an IEA from
    InterPro:IPR021124. I mark that one KEEP_AS_NON_CORE precisely because casD does not
    do the cleaving (see above), which is the same test applied in the opposite direction.

The structural role, which must not be lost either

Cleavage does not release the enzyme. CasE stays on its own product
PMID:25103409,
and is tethered into the complex by a helix from the first backbone subunit entering the
cleft on its non-RNA face
PMID:25103409,
so the enzyme-product pair is read as the seed of assembly
PMID:25103409.
Its RNA recognition is sequence-specific, unlike the backbone's
PMID:25103409.

Hence the intra-complex GO:0005515 row is MODIFYed to GO:0005198 structural molecule
activity, the same replacement used for casA, casB, casC and casD. This is what keeps the
dual role visible: GO:0004521 for the chemistry, GO:0003723 for the sequence-specific
3' hairpin recognition, GO:0005198 for capping the head of the complex.

  The opposite face, together with a positively charged groove-loop on the
  C-terminal motif, grips the stem-loop formed by each CRISPR repeat and makes
  base-specific contacts both within the stem-loop and in the nucleotides flanking
  it, which is how a single enzyme recognises every repeat in an array regardless of
  the intervening spacer. CasE cleaves the primary array transcript within each
  repeat, immediately upstream of the hairpin, releasing single spacer guides that
  carry an 8-nucleotide repeat-derived 5' handle and a 3' hairpin; the reaction
  requires no divalent metal ion and no ATP, and leaves a 5'-hydroxyl and a
  2',3'-cyclic phosphate. A conserved histidine is required for catalysis but not
  for incorporation into the complex. CasE does not release its product. It stays
  bound to the 3' hairpin of the mature guide and is tethered to the Cas7 backbone
  by a helix from the first backbone subunit inserting into its V-shaped cleft, so
  the enzyme-product pair caps the head of Cascade and serves as the platform for
  ordered assembly of the remaining ten subunits. Loss of CasE abolishes pre-crRNA
  cleavage, resistance to bacteriophage lambda and plasmid silencing. CasE also
  associates with the adaptation nuclease Cas1 (YgbT) and reduces its cleavage of
  Holliday junctions in vitro.

- proposed_name: CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex
  proposed_definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas)
    proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA
    as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I
    systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an
    R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction.
    The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition
    Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent
