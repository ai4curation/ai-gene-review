Gene Description

PaaF is the 2,3-dehydroadipyl-CoA hydratase of the aerobic, CoA-thioester-based ("box") phenylacetate catabolic pathway of Escherichia coli, encoded in the paaABCDEFGHIJK operon. In the beta-oxidation-like lower part of the pathway it reversibly hydrates the C6 dicarboxylic enoyl-CoA 2,3-dehydroadipyl-CoA, produced by PaaJ thiolysis and PaaG isomerization, to 3-hydroxyadipyl-CoA, the substrate of the PaaH dehydrogenase. PaaF is a crotonase-superfamily (enoyl-CoA hydratase/isomerase family) enzyme most similar to mitochondrial enoyl-CoA hydratase, with the two catalytic glutamates (Glu-109, Glu-129) conserved. It forms homotrimers that stack into a hexamer, which associates with two PaaG trimers to form a stable four-layered PaaF-PaaG hydratase-isomerase complex. paaF deletion mutants cannot grow on phenylacetate and accumulate delta3-dehydroadipate. Operon expression is repressed by PaaX and induced by phenylacetyl-CoA.

GO:0006635 fatty acid beta-oxidation
IBA
GO_REF:0000033 		MARK AS OVER ANNOTATED
Summary: Family-level propagation of fatty acid beta-oxidation from mitochondrial enoyl-CoA hydratases (ECHS1) to the phenylacetate-pathway paralog.
Reason: The PAINT node groups PaaF with mitochondrial short-chain enoyl-CoA hydratases, and the chemistry is indeed beta-oxidation-like. However, GO:0006635 describes complete oxidation of a long-chain fatty acid. E. coli PaaF is encoded in the paa operon, acts on the C6 dicarboxylic intermediate 2,3-dehydroadipyl-CoA derived from the aromatic ring, and has an active site adapted to Paa-pathway intermediates rather than fatty acyl chains. It also does not form a FadBA-like beta-oxidation complex with PaaH and PaaJ. No experiment shows that PaaF contributes to fatty acid degradation in E. coli, which uses the FadB/FadJ multifunctional enzymes for this purpose. The term is not removed outright because crotonase-family hydratases often accept short acyl-CoAs in vitro.
