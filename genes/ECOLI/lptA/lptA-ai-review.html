Gene Description

LptA is an essential, signal-peptide-bearing periplasmic protein of the seven-component lipopolysaccharide transport (Lpt) machinery of Escherichia coli. Its mature chain folds into a twisted beta-jellyroll with a hydrophobic groove, and it binds LPS through the lipid A moiety. LptA docks by its N-terminal edge onto the C-terminal edge of the beta-jellyroll of the inner-membrane protein LptC, and by its C-terminal edge onto the N-terminal periplasmic domain of the outer-membrane translocon LptD. One or a few head-to-tail LptA molecules thereby complete a continuous trans-envelope protein bridge. LPS extracted from the inner membrane by the ATP-driven LptB2FG transporter is passed from LptC to LptA and along the bridge to LptDE, which inserts it into the outer leaflet of the outer membrane. Bridge assembly is gated by correct LptDE assembly, and LptA is unstable when the bridge cannot form. Depletion of LptA blocks LPS delivery to the outer membrane, causes LPS to accumulate in the inner membrane and is lethal. Expression of the lptA-lptB operon is controlled by sigma-E. Bridge assembly and LPS occupancy couple displacement of the LptC transmembrane helix to increased ATP binding and hydrolysis by the inner-membrane motor.

Core Functions

Periplasmic beta-jellyroll bridge protein that receives LPS from the inner-membrane LptC and conveys it along a continuous protein bridge to the N-terminal domain of the outer-membrane translocon LptD, enabling ATP-driven LPS delivery to the cell surface.
