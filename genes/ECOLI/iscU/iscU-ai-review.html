Gene Description

IscU is the small cytosolic scaffold of the E. coli Isc iron-sulfur cluster assembly machinery. It docks onto IscS, accepts persulfide sulfur through conserved cysteines, binds ferrous iron under reconstitution conditions, and assembles transient clusters. Native mass spectrometry supports both iron-loaded and sulfur-loaded intermediates; the obligatory physiological order and principal native iron-delivery route remain unresolved. Purified IscU carries [2Fe-2S] clusters and can form [4Fe-4S] clusters under reductive assembly conditions. Its structured and dynamically disordered conformations are recognized differently by IscS, HscB and HscA. HscB targets cluster-loaded IscU to HscA, whose ATPase cycle promotes cluster transfer to acceptor proteins. IscU performs repeated cluster-transfer cycles in vitro and supports maturation of defined native clients, including the Fur iron regulator. Zinc stabilizes the scaffold but can inhibit iron loading, and copper can disrupt its assembly chemistry.

Core Functions

Cytosolic scaffold and carrier that accepts IscS-derived sulfur and iron, assembles transient Fe-S clusters, and delivers cluster cargo to acceptor proteins. Multiple-turnover [2Fe-2S] transfer is measured; [4Fe-4S] formation is demonstrated under reductive reconstitution conditions without establishing a universal native delivery route.
