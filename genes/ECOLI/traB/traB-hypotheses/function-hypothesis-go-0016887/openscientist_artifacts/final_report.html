AIGR Deep Research Report: ATP Hydrolysis Activity (GO:0016887) on F-plasmid TraB (P41067)

Gene: traB (ECOLI) · UniProt: P41067 (TRAB1_ECOLI) · Organism: Escherichia coli K-12 (NCBITaxon:83333) Focus type: function_assignment · Seed hypothesis: traB has ATP hydrolysis activity (GO:0016887) Term under review: GO:0016887 (ATP hydrolysis activity), evidence IDA, original reference PMID:20172994

Summary

The GO:0016887 "ATP hydrolysis activity" annotation on P41067 is REFUTED as an over-annotation caused by a TraB name collision. The seed hypothesis — that the F-plasmid conjugation protein TraB (P41067) directly catalyzes ATP hydrolysis — is not supported by any direct evidence pertaining to this protein. Instead, the annotation traces to a conflation of two unrelated conjugation proteins that happen to share the colloquial name "TraB."

P41067 (TRAB1_ECOLI, 475 aa) is the F-plasmid TraB, a member of the VirB10/TraB/TrbI family (Pfam PF03743 "TrbI", InterPro IPR005498). This family is the non-catalytic, envelope-spanning structural scaffold of the type IV secretion system (T4SS). Its UniProt-curated function is "Involved in F pilus assembly," and its sequence contains no Walker-A / P-loop motif (GxxxxGK[S/T]) — the diagnostic signature required for ATP hydrolysis. By contrast, the IDA reference PMID:20172994 is explicitly titled "Biochemical dissection of the ATPase TraB, the VirB4 homologue of the Escherichia coli pKM101 conjugation machinery." That paper assayed a different protein: the ~866-aa VirB4-family TraB of plasmid pKM101 (UniProt Q46698), a bona-fide P-loop ATPase with a canonical Walker-A motif (GMSGEGKT, ~residue 498).

The two "TraB" proteins differ in essentially every discriminating feature — length (475 vs 866 aa), domain family (VirB10 scaffold vs VirB4 ATPase), presence of nucleotide-binding motifs (absent vs present), and documented biochemical role (structural vs catalytic). Independent primary evidence (PMID:11722740) confirms that the F-plasmid TraB is an inner-membrane structural component of the envelope-spanning TraV–TraK–TraB complex, with no catalytic activity demonstrated. The only GO:0016887 annotation on P41067 is a single IDA:CACAO community-curation entry, the typical vector for name-collision propagation. Recommended curation action: remove GO:0016887 from P41067 (optionally re-home the evidence to Q46698), pending curator verification.

Executive Judgment

Verdict: REFUTED (over-annotation via name collision).

The ATP-hydrolysis evidence is real, but it belongs to the pKM101 VirB4-TraB (Q46698), not to the F-plasmid VirB10-TraB (P41067). The refutation rests on four convergent, orthogonal lines of evidence:

  1. The IDA reference assayed a different protein. 20172994 characterizes the VirB4 homologue TraB of pKM101 (~866 aa), explicitly named as "the ATPase TraB."
  2. Domain family is non-catalytic. P41067 is VirB10/TraB/TrbI (PF03743, IPR005498) — a structural T4SS scaffold family, not a VirB4/VirB11/VirD4 ATPase family.
  3. No catalytic apparatus. P41067 lacks a Walker-A / P-loop motif; ATP hydrolysis is mechanistically impossible without a nucleotide-binding fold.
  4. Positive alternative function. 11722740 shows P41067 is an inner-membrane structural component of the TraV–TraK–TraB complex — the canonical VirB10 scaffold role.

Most important caveat: This is a high-confidence refutation of the attribution. Confirmation of the exact UniProt accession assayed in 20172994 (inferred to be Q46698) from the paper's full-text methods would fully close the loop before the IDA is re-homed.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — The GO:0016887 IDA on P41067 is a TraB name-collision mis-annotation

P41067 (TRAB1_ECOLI, 475 aa) is the F-plasmid "Protein TraB." Its UniProt-curated molecular function is "Involved in F pilus assembly," and its domain architecture places it unambiguously in a structural, not catalytic, protein family: Pfam PF03743 (TrbI) and InterPro IPR005498 ("Type IV secretion system VirB10/TraB/TrbI family"). The VirB10/TraB/TrbI family forms the inner-membrane, envelope-spanning scaffold of the T4SS core complex. It is energized by — but does not itself perform — ATP hydrolysis.

Critically, P41067's sequence contains no Walker-A (P-loop) motif (GxxxxGK[S/T]), the diagnostic nucleotide-binding signature of ATPases. A protein lacking a P-loop cannot be assumed to hydrolyze ATP, and VirB10-family members are canonically non-catalytic.

The only GO:0016887 annotation on P41067 is a single IDA:CACAO community-curation entry. Its cited primary reference, PMID:20172994 (Durand, Oomen & Waksman, 2010), explicitly characterizes "the ATPase TraB, the VirB4 homologue of the E. coli pKM101 conjugation machinery." That protein is in the VirB4 family (not VirB10), is ~866 aa (not 475 aa), and corresponds to UniProt Q46698 (Q46698_ECOLX) — not P41067. Every discriminating feature indicates the ATP-hydrolysis evidence describes the pKM101 VirB4-TraB, not the F-plasmid VirB10-TraB P41067. The two proteins share only the colloquial label "TraB," a conjugation-gene name reused across independent plasmid transfer systems. Notably, in the F system the VirB4 ATPase role is filled by a differently named protein (TraC), while "TraB" denotes the VirB10 scaffold — the inverse of the pKM101 nomenclature, which is precisely how the cross-assignment error occurred.

Finding 2 — F-plasmid TraB (P41067) is an inner-membrane structural scaffold, not an ATPase

Independent primary literature corroborates the structural (non-catalytic) role of P41067. PMID:11722740 (Harris, Hombs & Silverman, 2001) used yeast two-hybrid interaction mapping and membrane fractionation to show that F-plasmid TraB is an inner-membrane protein that assembles with TraK (periplasm) and TraV (outer-membrane lipoprotein) into an envelope-spanning TraV–TraK–TraB complex. The authors state directly that "TraV, TraK and TraB form a complex in Escherichia coli that spans the cell envelope from the outer membrane (TraV) through the periplasm (TraK) to the inner membrane (TraB)."

TraB fractionates with the inner membrane, and the assembled complex bridges outer membrane → periplasm → inner membrane. This is precisely the canonical VirB10-family structural/scaffolding role, consistent with P41067's Pfam PF03743 / InterPro IPR005498 assignment. No catalytic or ATP-hydrolysis activity is demonstrated or implied for TraB in this work.

Combined with the absence of any P-loop/Walker-A motif in P41067 (475 aa) versus the genuine VirB4-TraB ATPase of pKM101 (Q46698; ~866 aa; P-loop NTPase with Walker-A motif GMSGEGKT near residue 498 — the protein actually assayed in 20172994), this independently confirms that the GO:0016887 IDA on P41067 is a mis-assignment. Two orthogonal lines of evidence — domain-family/sequence analysis and direct biochemical/cell-biological characterization — converge on the same conclusion.

The F-plasmid TraB (P41067) is the VirB10-family channel/scaffold component that physically couples the outer-membrane and periplasmic subassemblies to the inner membrane. In T4SS architecture the ATP-hydrolyzing engines are the VirB4, VirB11, and VirD4 family NTPases — distinct proteins bearing P-loop motifs. The energy-providing ATPase role and the structural scaffold role are performed by different proteins. The IDA annotation conflated the two because the pKM101 VirB4 homologue is also named "TraB."

Why the mis-annotation is mechanistically implausible

ATP hydrolysis requires a nucleotide-binding fold — minimally a Walker-A/P-loop (GxxxxGK[S/T]) and usually a Walker-B motif. P41067 has neither. VirB10-family proteins are not NTPases; they respond to ATP binding/hydrolysis by other T4SS components (conformational "energy sensing") but do not catalyze the reaction themselves. Assigning a direct catalytic MF term to a scaffold protein lacking the catalytic apparatus is biochemically unsupportable.
