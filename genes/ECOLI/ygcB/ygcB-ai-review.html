Gene Description

Cas3 (gene ygcB/cas3) is the 888-residue helicase-nuclease of the Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system and the enzyme that destroys the invading DNA. It is a two-module protein: an N-terminal metal-dependent HD nuclease domain (residues 20-231, with Mg(2+) ligands at His75 and Asp160) fused to a superfamily-2 helicase/ATPase module (ATP-binding domain 301-504 with a Walker A motif at 314-321 and a DEAH box at 452-455, plus a helicase C-terminal domain 556-735). Cas3 does not find its own targets. The crRNA-loaded Cascade surveillance complex locates a protospacer in negatively supercoiled DNA, verifies the adjacent PAM and forms an R-loop; the Cascade large subunit Cse1/CasA then recruits Cas3 to the licensed site. The HD domain nicks the displaced single strand, and the ATP-driven helicase and the Mg(2+)-dependent nuclease then act together to unwind the duplex and degrade the target processively in the 3'-to-5' direction, giving complete destruction of phage and plasmid DNA. On model substrates the purified enzyme shows endonucleolytic and 3'-to-5' exonucleolytic cleavage restricted to single-stranded regions, ATP-dependent unwinding of the RNA strand of an RNA:DNA hybrid, and, in the absence of ATP, the reverse reaction of annealing RNA into duplex DNA to form R-loops. Cas3 is required together with Cascade for resistance to bacteriophage lambda and for curing of plasmids carrying a protospacer; HD-domain and helicase-motif point mutants abolish immunity in vivo, and the helicase mutant K320N uncouples nicking from degradation. The protein has no known activity outside CRISPR interference.

Core Functions

Metal-dependent HD-domain single-strand endonuclease that nicks the Cascade-licensed target. Recruited by the Cascade large subunit Cse1/CasA once an R-loop has formed at a PAM-flanked protospacer, Cas3 cleaves within the displaced single strand. This is the committed, immunity-essential step of type I-E interference: HD-domain point mutants H74A, D75A, K78A and D452N abolish lambda immunity and plasmid curing in vivo.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does the ATP-independent RNA:DNA annealing activity of purified Cas3 have any role in vivo, or is it simply the reverse of the helicase reaction observed under conditions where ATP is absent? Cascade forms R-loops on its own, which leaves no obvious requirement for Cas3 to do so.

Q: Should GO add a molecular-function term for crRNA-guided, PAM-licensed target recognition? Cas3 itself needs no such term - all three of its activities map onto existing ids - but the Cascade subunits that recruit it currently have to stand in double-stranded DNA binding for that function.

The distinction decides whether GO:0008310 or the broader GO:0008296 is the right annotation for Cas3 in situ.

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Assay purified E. coli Cas3 side by side with E. coli DeaD/CsdA (P0A9P6) on a matched panel of substrates - a blunt and a 3'-overhang RNA duplex, an RNA:DNA hybrid, and a duplex DNA fork - under identical ATP and Mg(2+) conditions, scoring unwinding by gel shift. A clean split, with DeaD active on the RNA duplex and Cas3 active only on the hybrid, settles the term choice directly.

Hypothesis: The Cas3 helicase module has lost the ability to unwind duplex RNA that its DEAD-box relatives retain, which would confirm that the GO:0003724 IBA reflects family-level lumping rather than a conserved activity.

Experiment: Reconstitute Cascade with wild-type Cas3, the HD mutant D452N and the Walker A mutant K320N on a negatively supercoiled plasmid target, and follow the reaction by time-resolved topology mapping plus sequencing of the 3' ends generated. This gives the position of the initiating nick and the extent of exonucleolytic resection for each allele separately.

Hypothesis: Nicking and processive degradation are catalysed by the same HD active site but gated by the helicase, so helicase-dead and nuclease-dead alleles should fail at different points in the same reaction.

actually measured, and the root cause is the family-level lumping of the Cas3 helicase
module with DEAD-box RNA helicases, i.e. term scoping on the ancestral node rather than a
bad source annotation.

a consequence of the hybrid-directed activities rather than an independent core function.

Two problems, one minor and one substantive.

  1. The evidence item cites the GO term as evidence for itself. source_id: GO:0008296
    restates the annotation rather than grounding it. The grounding exists and is a cached
    full-text paper: PMID:22521689, which contains the direct assay. An EvidenceItem with
    a literature source_id plus supporting_text is verbatim-checked by the module
    validator, so the quote above can be dropped in as-is.
  2. GO:0008296 is one level too general, and it is the wrong one of Cas3's two nuclease
    activities to stand alone.     The measured exonuclease acts "on the single stranded
    region of the DNA substrate", so GO:0008310 single-stranded DNA 3'-5' DNA exonuclease
    activity is the accurate id. More importantly, the committed destruction step the
    annoton's own role_description describes ("processively unwinds and degrades the
    target") is a coupled activity: the HD domain first nicks the displaced strand
    (GO:0000014, the step that commits the target and the one abolished by the
    immunity-null HD mutants H74A/D75A/K78A/D452N), and the helicase then feeds ssDNA to the
    nuclease (GO:0033677). The K320N helicase mutant separates them cleanly — it "Nicks
    target plasmid DNA but does not degrade it" [file:ECOLI/ygcB/ygcB-uniprot.txt].

So the honest single-function choice for this annoton is GO:0000014 (the licensing,
committed, immunity-essential step), with GO:0008310 and GO:0033677 as the coupled
activities noted in the description — rather than GO:0008296 as the function with
GO:0033677 merely mentioned in an evidence sentence. If a single catalytic id must
carry the degradation, GO:0008310 should replace GO:0008296.

Nothing else about the annoton needs changing: the family grounding
(InterPro:IPR006474 Helicase Cas3, CRISPR-associated, core) contains both representative
members, and the role_description is accurate.

Not annotated, deliberately

  • Cas3 is a workhorse of type I genome-editing/"DNA-shredding" reagents. That is a
    laboratory use, not a function of the gene in its host, and is kept out of the review
    entirely per project scope discipline.
  • GO:0097098 DNA/RNA hybrid annealing activity is real and directly observed, but the
    authors themselves describe annealing and ATP-driven unwinding as "apparently
    antagonistic roles" in the abstract, and the in vivo role of the annealing reaction is
    unresolved (Cascade also forms R-loops). It is kept as a non-core activity rather than
    promoted into core_functions.
  • No new ontology term is proposed. Unlike Cascade — which needs a crRNA-guided target
    recognition MF that GO lacks — every activity Cas3 has been shown to perform maps onto an
    existing GO term.
