# SecG notes

Manual synthesis in place of provider deep research, which was unavailable in this environment.

- SecG is the small accessory subunit of the SecYEG inner-membrane channel. SecY and SecE form the core pore and clamp, while SecG stabilizes or stimulates the translocase and contributes to SecYEG protein transmembrane transporter activity. Brundage et al. established SecY, SecE, and band 1/SecG as the associated active membrane domain [PMID:1531482, "These data establish that the subunits of SecY/E are firmly associated and that it is the associated complex which is active for translocation."], and Hanada et al. showed that depleting all three subunits removed SecYEG activity [PMID:7650029, "These data establish that SecY, SecE, and SecG constitute the integral membrane domain of preprotein translocase."].
- SecG is not the ATPase motor of protein transport. ATP hydrolysis in the Sec translocase belongs to SecA, while SecG is a membrane channel accessory subunit; the InterPro IEA to GO:0015450 protein-transporting ATPase activity conflates SecG with the SecA motor.
- SecG undergoes SecA-linked topological rearrangements during protein translocation. Nagamori et al. found increased SecA-SecG cross-linking during the translocation cycle [PMID:12359079, "Crosslinking analyses revealed that the interaction between SecG and SecA in membranes markedly increases when SecA and SecG undergo membrane-insertion and topology inversion, respectively."].
- Like SecY and SecE, SecG acts in the SecYEG pore used by both SecA/SecB post-translational export and SRP-dependent cotranslational insertion. Koch et al. showed that both routes meet at the channel [PMID:10397756, "Both targeting pathways intersect at the translocation pore because they are equally affected by a blocked translocation channel."], and Valent et al. found SecG in the Sec translocon engaged by SRP-targeted nascent chains [PMID:9564033, "Upon release of the SRP at the membrane, the targeted nascent proteins insert into a translocon that contains at least SecA, SecY and SecG."].
- Generic GO:0005515 protein-binding rows for SecG should be removed as uninformative interaction records. The SecG-SecA and SecG-SecY contacts are real, but they support SecYEG channel membership and conformational stimulation rather than a standalone molecular function.
