Is AslA the maturation substrate of E. coli AslB? A hypothesis-level evidence report

Target: Escherichia coli K-12 AslB (UniProt P25550, b3800), NCBITaxon:83333 Focus: free_text, hypothesis slug kgap-aslb-substrate-assignment Source: genes/ECOLI/aslB/aslB-ai-review.yaml, selector free-text Computed provenance: provenance/aslB-substrate-analysis-tables.md, figures plot_1 to plot_5

Summary

AslA carries serine at the position that becomes formylglycine. The residue is Ser136, in the motif SSPTR, sequence context ILTSAYSQPS[S]SPTRATILTG. E. coli K-12 encodes exactly three type I sulfatases: aslA/b3801 (P25549, Ser136, SSPTR), ydeN/b1498 (P77318, Ser132, SGPSR) and yidJ/b3678 (P31447, Cys52, CTPAR). AslA and YdeN are Ser-type, YidJ is the single Cys-type sulfatase in the genome. The other nine proteins returned by a Pfam PF00884 query against taxon 83333 (EptA/b4114, EptB/b3546, EptC/b3955, OpgB/b4359, OpgE/b0815, PbgA/b2188, YhbX/b3173, plus two strain-variant entries) are alkaline-phosphatase-superfamily phosphotransferases with no (C/S)XPXR in a sulfatase context, so a sulfatase maturase has nothing to act on in them.

The genomic-context argument that the hypothesis proposes to test fails on inspection. In GenBank U00096.3, aslB is CDS 959..2194 on the plus strand (genome 3,982,958-3,984,193) and aslA is CDS complement(2353..4008) on the minus strand (genome 3,984,352-3,986,007). The two genes point towards each other with 158 bp between their stop codons. Convergent genes lie in separate transcription units by definition, so aslB and aslA cannot be in one operon. The characterised Ser-type precedent, Klebsiella pneumoniae atsB-atsA (GenBank AJ131525), is co-oriented with 44 bp of intergenic DNA and atsB first. The second E. coli maturase and sulfatase pair reproduces the precedent and the aslB-aslA pair does not: ydeN and ydeM are co-oriented 51 bp apart on the minus strand. No published proteomic or transcriptomic dataset shows aslB and aslA co-expressed, and no dataset reports AslA Ser136 as formylglycine-modified. All four proteins (AslB, AslA, YdeN, YidJ) sit at UniProt protein-existence level 3, inferred from homology, with PaxDb as the only proteomics cross-reference and no PeptideAtlas, PRIDE or jPOST record for AslA or AslB.

Verdict: unresolved, with one sub-claim refuted. Sequence and genomic context cannot establish the substrate of AslB. The Ser/Cys typing is compatible with an AtsB-like maturase acting on AslA but discriminates nothing, because characterised anSMEs are dual-substrate (PMID: 18408004), recognise substrates through backbone hydrogen bonding with decreased sequence specificity (PMID: 23650368), and AslB itself raised the activity of both Cys-type and Ser-type heterologous sulfatases (PMID: 28882567). The typing also fails to select AslA over YdeN, since both are Ser-type. For curation, AslB should keep family-level radical SAM and iron-sulfur cluster terms and should not carry a substrate-specific statement naming AslA.

Executive judgment

Unresolved, with one sub-claim refuted.

The hypothesis asks whether the two proteins' sequences decide that AslB is positioned to mature AslA. The analysis answers both halves of the question, and they point in different directions.

The sequence half has a clean answer that is compatible with the hypothesis. AslA is an intact Ser-type type I sulfatase whose nucleophile position aligns exactly onto the experimentally demonstrated formylglycine of three reference sulfatases in 8 of 9 pairwise global alignments. AslA retains 8 of the 10 catalytic and metal-binding residues defined crystallographically in human ARSA, the same score as two bacterial arylsulfatases with measured activity. If AslA is ever expressed as an active enzyme, some maturase must convert Ser136, and the only anSME-family candidates in the K-12 genome are AslB, YdeM and YidF. That is an argument from necessity across a set of three.

The genomic half refutes the specific claim it was asked to test. aslB and aslA are convergent, 158 bp apart, so they lie in separate transcription units and there is no operon. The precedent the hypothesis names (atsB-atsA) is co-oriented at 44 bp. The E. coli pair that actually reproduces the precedent is ydeM-ydeN. Convergent adjacency in an enteric genome is common by chance and carries no operon-based inference.

The remaining steps of the inference also fail to discriminate. Ser/Cys typing excludes nothing, because anSMEs modify both cysteinyl and seryl active sites and AslB is documented acting on both types. The apparent STRING functional link between aslB and aslA is carried by textmining (0.865) against coexpression 0.186 and conserved neighbourhood 0.083, which traces back to the shared "arylsulfatase-like" naming and physical adjacency. That is database carry-over.

The most important caveats. E. coli K-12 has no observed arylsulfatase activity (PMID: 17303125), and UniProt P25549 records "No E.coli strains have been observed to have an arylsulfatase activity", so assigning AslA as the physiological substrate of AslB assigns a maturation reaction onto a protein whose own catalytic function is unverified. The one in vivo test of AslB in K-12 deleted aslB, ydeM and yidF and found Cys-type sulfatase maturation unimpaired, which leaves the physiological substrate of AslB unidentified. A negative genomic result does not refute the biochemical possibility: convergent genes can still encode an enzyme and its substrate.

Key findings

Finding 1: E. coli K-12 encodes exactly three type I sulfatases, and AslA is Ser-type

A UniProt query for taxonomy_id:83333 AND xref:pfam-PF00884 returns 12 proteins. Intersecting with InterPro IPR024607, the sulfatase conserved site, leaves exactly three: AslA/b3801 (P25549, 551 aa), YdeN/b1498 (P77318, 560 aa) and YidJ/b3678 (P31447, 497 aa). The nine excluded proteins (EptA/b4114, EptB/b3546, EptC/b3955, OpgB/b4359, OpgE/b0815, PbgA/b2188, YhbX/b3173 and two strain-variant entries) belong to the alkaline-phosphatase superfamily and carry no (C/S)XPXR in a sulfatase context. They are phosphoethanolamine and glucan phosphotransferases.

A regex scan of each sulfatase sequence for the signature (C/S)XPXR gives exactly one N-terminal-domain hit per protein:

The signature rule comes from primary literature. A new type of bacterial sulfatase reveals a novel maturation pathway in prokaryotes states that "this conversion depends on a strictly conserved sequence called 'sulfatase signature' (C/S)XPXR" (PMID: 16766528). The residue it locates is the catalytically essential one: Formylglycine, a post-translationally generated residue with unique catalytic capabilities states that "formylglycine (fGly) is a catalytically essential residue found almost exclusively in the active sites of type I sulfatases" (PMID: 25514000).

To confirm that the regex hits are positionally equivalent to real formylglycines rather than coincidental motif matches, each E. coli sulfatase was aligned by Needleman-Wunsch global alignment with affine gaps (BLOSUM62, gap open -11, extend -1) against the three reference sulfatases whose formylglycine carries experimental evidence. On substrate typing alone, a strictly Ser-specific maturase could act on AslA and YdeN and could not act on YidJ. That restriction does not hold in practice, as Finding 3 shows.

Finding 2: aslB and aslA are convergently transcribed, so they cannot form an operon

Coordinates were read from the primary GenBank feature tables. For region 3,982,000-3,987,000 of U00096.3 (E. coli K-12 MG1655), the raw feature lines are CDS 959..2194 /gene="aslB" and CDS complement(2353..4008) /gene="aslA". aslB therefore occupies genome coordinates 3,982,958-3,984,193 on the plus strand and aslA occupies 3,984,352-3,986,007 on the minus strand. Convergent genes lie in separate transcription units by definition, so there is no shared transcript to argue from. The characterised Ser-type precedent that the hypothesis names, K. pneumoniae atsB-atsA in GenBank AJ131525, is a textbook operon: both genes on the plus strand, maturase first, 44 bp of intergenic DNA. The E. coli pair that reproduces that arrangement is ydeM-ydeN, co-oriented with 51 bp of spacing and transcription order ydeN then ydeM. The third pair, yidF-yidJ, is co-oriented but separated by 1,299 bp containing yidG, yidH and the oppositely oriented yidI, so it is not a tight pair either.

Answer to sub-question 4. aslB and aslA are not in one operon. Their orientation is convergent and their spacing is 158 bp, against the co-oriented 44 bp of the atsB-atsA precedent. If genomic context is used to nominate a substrate for an E. coli anSME homolog, it nominates YdeN for YdeM.

Finding 3: Ser/Cys typing cannot discriminate, because anSMEs are dual-substrate

The sub-question "which of those substrates could a Ser-type maturase act on" has no restrictive answer in practice. Anaerobic sulfatase-maturating enzymes, first dual substrate radical S-adenosylmethionine enzymes characterised two bacterial anSMEs physiologically responsible for Cys-type and Ser-type maturation and found that both oxidise cysteine and serine in vitro. The paper states the categorisation it then undermines: "each enzymatic system is currently considered to be dedicated to the modification of either cysteine or serine residues encoded in the sulfatase-active site and has been accordingly categorized as Cys-type and Ser-type sulfatase-maturating enzymes" (PMID: 18408004). Quantitative assays later showed stoichiometric coupling of AdoMet cleavage and formylglycine formation on peptide substrates carrying either a cysteinyl or a seryl active-site residue (PMID: 20218986).

The structural reason is known. The anSME crystal structure shows that "anSMEs differ from their aerobic counterparts in using backbone-based hydrogen-bonding patterns to interact with their peptidyl-substrates, leading to decreased sequence specificity" (PMID: 23650368). An enzyme that reads substrate backbone rather than side-chain sequence cannot have its physiological substrate deduced from substrate sequence.

AslB itself has been shown to act across the type boundary. Co-expressing AslB in E. coli BL21(DE3) with human GALNS in both Cys- and Ser-type versions and with IDS gave activity increases of 2.0-, 1.5- and 4.6-fold: "the co-expression of AslB with GALNS Cys- and Ser-type presented an activity increment of 2.0- and 1.5-fold compared to the control cultures, lacking overexpressed AslB" (PMID: 28882567).

There is a second consequence for the hypothesis. E. coli K-12 has no observed sulfatase activity: "although deprived of sulfatase activity, Escherichia coli K12 can efficiently mature heterologous Cys-type sulfatases" (PMID: 17303125), and UniProt P25549 records "No E.coli strains have been observed to have an arylsulfatase activity." Naming AslA the physiological substrate of AslB assigns a maturation reaction onto a protein whose own catalytic function is itself unverified.

The divalent-metal ligands (ARSA D29, D30, D281, N282) and the sulfate-binding lysine and histidine pair (K123, H125, H229, K302) are all present in AslA. AslA scores identically to two bacterial arylsulfatases with measured activity, so it is a structurally intact sulfatase and not a degraded pseudo-enzyme. YidJ, with 6/10 and two gapped positions, is the most eroded of the three.

This keeps the premise of the hypothesis alive without settling it. Because "arylsulfatases require a maturating enzyme to perform a co- or posttranslational modification to form a catalytically essential formylglycine (FGly) residue" (PMID: 23650368), an intact AslA would need Ser136 converted for the protein to be catalytically competent. If AslA is ever made as an active enzyme in E. coli, some maturase must do the work, and the only anSME-family candidates in the genome are AslB, YdeM and YidF. That is an argument from necessity across a set of three, not from pairing to one.

Finding 6: comparative genomics cannot rescue the pairing argument

Three attempts to turn the aslB-aslA adjacency into evidence of a maturase and substrate relationship returned null or circular results.

KEGG REST returns no KO assignment for eco:b3800 (aslB), b3801 (aslA), b1497 (ydeM), b1498 (ydeN) or b3678 (yidJ), so orthology-group based gene-neighbourhood analysis is not available for these genes. The same query revealed that b3677 is yidI, not yidF.

A UniProt ordered-locus-name proximity test across Enterobacteriaceae (taxonomy_id:543) found only 4 comparable organism entries carrying both the aslA and aslB gene names: E. coli K-12 b3800/b3801, E. coli CFT073 c4718/c4719, E. fergusonii EFER_3706/EFER_3705, plus one non-comparable JW pair, and 4 entries for ydeM/ydeN. All informative organisms fall within the genus Escherichia, and the gene names were themselves propagated from E. coli K-12 by homology, so the test is circular and has no power to distinguish conserved functional coupling from a single ancestral insertion.

The one independent large-scale measure available is the STRING conserved-neighbourhood channel computed across thousands of genomes, which gives aslB-aslA 0.083 against ydeM-ydeN 0.631. The STRING phylogenetic-profile channel does not discriminate either: aslB-aslA 0.665, ydeM-ydeN 0.713, yidF-aslB 0.772, ydeN-aslB 0.719, yidJ-aslB 0.652, ydeM-aslA 0.636, yidF-aslA 0.656. Every maturase and sulfatase combination co-occurs across genomes at a similar level, because all sulfatase and anSME genes appear and disappear together as a block.

A separate observation from the literature lowers the prior for one-to-one pairing. Two marine Kiritimatiellales isolates encode 521 and 480 sulfatases respectively (8.0% and 8.4% of all coding sequences), and "both encoded a single anaerobic sulfatase-maturating enzyme which could be responsible for post-translational modification of formylglycine-dependent sulfatases" (PMID: 30833937). One maturase commonly serves many substrates, so gene adjacency is a weak indicator of a dedicated pair.

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The immediate molecular function being evaluated is one reaction: AslB, a radical SAM enzyme with a SPASM domain and auxiliary [4Fe-4S] clusters, abstracts the Cβ hydrogen atom from the active-site serine or cysteine of a type I sulfatase using a 5'-deoxyadenosyl radical, converting it to Cα-formylglycine. The mechanism is established for the enzyme family: anSMEs use a 5'-deoxyadenosyl radical to catalyse direct H-atom abstraction from the substrate, and they are the first radical AdoMet enzymes shown to catalyse a post-translational modification involving Cβ H-atom abstraction from an active-site cysteinyl or seryl residue (PMID: 19489556). Three almost isopotential [4Fe-4S] clusters are each required for formylglycine generation in vitro, and the two auxiliary clusters are needed for efficient reductive cleavage of AdoMet (PMID: 20218986).

What is in question is the identity of the peptide that AslB acts on in E. coli K-12. The chain of inference the hypothesis proposes, and where it breaks:

The three arrows into the target node are all dashed. The sequence evidence establishes compatibility, which is a necessary condition and not a sufficient one, and the same condition is satisfied by YdeN. The Ser-type specificity premise that would narrow the candidate set is removed by three independent results: anSMEs are dual-substrate in vitro, they recognise substrates through backbone hydrogen bonding, and AslB specifically raised the activity of Cys-type GALNS 2.0-fold alongside Ser-type GALNS 1.5-fold. The genomic premise is refuted outright by the convergent orientation.

What survives is a two-part statement. AslB is an anSME-family radical SAM enzyme with intact cluster ligands and SAM contacts, which supports family-level molecular-function annotation. The substrate on which it acts in E. coli K-12 is unknown, and the organism has never been shown to have arylsulfatase activity, so there may be no physiological substrate under laboratory conditions.

GO curation implications

These are leads requiring curator verification.

Retain family-level molecular function terms on AslB. The structural and mechanistic basis for AslB being a radical SAM enzyme with auxiliary iron-sulfur clusters is not in question and was explicitly excluded from re-derivation by the hypothesis. Terms in the region of GO:0051539 (4 iron, 4 sulfur cluster binding), GO:0046872 (metal ion binding) and a catalytic-activity term covering S-adenosyl-L-methionine-dependent radical chemistry are supported by sequence conservation of the cluster ligands, the SAM contacts and the catalytic aspartate, together with the characterised biochemistry of the anSME family (PMID: 19489556, PMID: 20218986, PMID: 23650368). Mechanistic scope

The direct gene-product activity being evaluated is the conversion of a peptidyl serine or cysteine to Cα-formylglycine by AslB, a single chemical step performed by a radical SAM enzyme carrying three [4Fe-4S] clusters, one at the radical SAM site and two in the SPASM domain. The auxiliary clusters of SPASM-domain radical SAM enzymes have been characterised directly in related systems: PqqE carries a 2Fe-2S cluster at AuxI and a 4Fe-4S cluster at AuxII with aspartate ligation (PMID: 29405700, PMID: 31769977), the sactipeptide enzyme CteB shows one SPASM cluster with an open coordination site that becomes occupied by a substrate cysteine (PMID: 28704043), and in SuiB a redox-tuned [4Fe-4S]2+ auxiliary cluster was shown to oxidise a trapped reaction intermediate (PMID: 34001621). These establish the catalytic machinery that AslB is inferred to use, without identifying its substrate.

Separate from that single step are three things the evidence does not reach.

The downstream pathway consequence, sulfate ester hydrolysis by a matured sulfatase, has never been observed in E. coli K-12, which has no measurable arylsulfatase activity. AslA's own catalytic function is an inference from its intact active site, not an observation.

The loss-of-function phenotype attributed to aslA in other contexts belongs to a different strain and a different process. In E. coli K1, aslA mutants showed decreased invasion of brain microvascular endothelial cells, with 32% of animals infected with mutant 27A-6 developing meningitis against 82% for the parent strain (PMID: 10948126). That is a pathogenesis phenotype in a pathogenic isolate, with no sulfatase activity measured and no bearing on whether AslB matures AslA in K-12.

The heterologous activity of AslB reported with human GALNS and IDS reports capability under overexpression in BL21(DE3) (PMID: 28882567). It demonstrates that AslB can perform the chemistry on substrates of both types, which constrains the specificity argument, and it says nothing about the native substrate.

Conflicts and alternatives

The competing pair. YdeM and YdeN are the E. coli maturase and sulfatase pair that reproduces the atsB-atsA arrangement: co-oriented, 51 bp apart, conserved neighbourhood 0.631, coexpression 0.637. YdeN is Ser-type (Ser132, SGPSR) and retains the same 8 of 10 ARSA catalytic residues as AslA. Every criterion the hypothesis proposes for nominating AslA is satisfied at least as well, and in the genomic criteria better, by YdeN. A curator reading a genomic-context argument for an E. coli anSME substrate should see the YdeM/YdeN pair alongside it.

Database carry-over. The aslB and aslA gene names both derive from "arylsulfatase-like" annotation, and the two genes are physically adjacent. STRING textmining (0.865) aggregates co-mentions that follow from those two facts rather than from independent observation, and the experiments and databases channels are both zero. Any downstream resource that reports an AslB-AslA functional association without channel decomposition is restating the naming convention.

Organism-specific difference. The AslA invasion phenotype comes from E. coli K1, a pathogenic isolate, not from K-12 (PMID: 10948126). That study measured invasion of brain microvascular endothelial cells and meningitis incidence, with no assay of sulfatase activity. It should not be read as evidence that AslA functions as a sulfatase in K-12.

Maturation is not always required for activity. Recombinant Ensifer (Sinorhizobium) meliloti choline-O-sulfatase lacking the formylglycine modification at Cys54 remained a multiple-turnover enzyme, with kcat 2.7 × 10⁻¹ s⁻¹ and KM 11.1 mM against choline-O-sulfate (PMID: 30338300). The absence of a maturation effect in a given host therefore does not by itself prove the maturase and substrate relationship is absent, which cuts against over-reading the triple-deletion result in either direction.

One maturase, many substrates. The marine Kiritimatiellales isolates with 480-521 sulfatase genes each encode a single anSME (PMID: 30833937). The architecture of the system is many substrates per maturase, which weakens the prior that a neighbouring sulfatase is the dedicated substrate of a neighbouring maturase.

A negative genomic result is not a biochemical one. Convergent orientation refutes the operon claim. AslB can still mature AslA without genomic linkage. The report refutes the stated genomic argument and leaves the biochemical question open.

Discriminating tests

In vitro peptide panel against reconstituted AslB. Express AslB with a strep or His tag, reconstitute the three [4Fe-4S] clusters anaerobically, and assay formylglycine formation on synthetic peptides spanning the three native active sites: AslA ILTSAYSQPSSSPTRATILTG (Ser136), YdeN FTNGYVAHGVSGPSRAAIMTG (Ser132) and YidJ FNSAYTCSPVCTPARAGLFTG (Cys52). Quantify AdoMet cleavage and FGly production as in PMID: 20218986. A clear kcat/KM preference for the AslA peptide over the YdeN peptide is the strongest available support for the hypothesis. Comparable rates on all three would confirm that specificity is not encoded in the substrate sequence, matching the backbone-recognition result of PMID: 23650368. Run YdeM and YidF through the same panel to build a 3 × 3 matrix.

Targeted mass spectrometry of AslA Ser136. Express AslA from its native locus with an affinity tag in wild-type K-12 and in ΔaslB, ΔydeM, ΔyidF single mutants and the triple mutant, under anaerobic and aerobic conditions. Detect the +14 Da mass shift or the hydrazide-captured aldehyde at Ser136 by parallel reaction monitoring. Loss of the modification specifically in ΔaslB would establish the pairing in vivo. This is the single most decisive experiment.

Transcription start site mapping across the aslB-aslA intergenic region. Use dRNA-seq or 5' RACE to locate promoters in the 158 bp between the two stop codons and upstream of each gene. This settles whether the genes share any regulatory input despite lying in separate transcription units, and it would also detect antisense overlap, which convergent gene pairs sometimes show.

Conditional transcriptomics. RNA-seq across sulfur starvation, alternative sulfur sources (choline-O-sulfate, aryl sulfates, sulfated glycans), anaerobiosis and stationary phase, scoring aslB, aslA, ydeM, ydeN, yidF and yidJ. Correlated induction of aslB and aslA under any condition would restore a contextual argument that the STRING coexpression channel (0.186) does not currently provide.

Cross-species neighbourhood analysis outside genus Escherichia. The ordered-locus test failed because it was confined to Escherichia. Build a proper analysis by retrieving anSME-family and PF00884 sulfatase gene coordinates from several hundred diverse bacterial genomes directly from NCBI, then test whether anSME genes are preferentially co-oriented with a nearby sulfatase. This gives a background rate against which the convergent aslB-aslA arrangement can be scored, and it avoids the circularity of homology-propagated gene names.

Complementation across the three systems. Express AslA with each maturase individually in a heterologous host lacking any anSME, then assay arylsulfatase activity. If AslA is activated only by AslB, the pairing is supported. If YdeM or YidF activates it equally, the one-to-one model fails. This tests the full-length protein rather than a peptide and complements the in vitro panel.

Answer to the hypothesis, stated plainly

Sequence and genomic context cannot establish the substrate of AslB.

They answer the sequence question cleanly: AslA carries serine at the formylglycine position, Ser136 in the motif SSPTR, so it is a Ser-type sulfatase, as is YdeN (Ser132, SGPSR), while YidJ (Cys52, CTPAR) is the only Cys-type sulfatase in E. coli K-12.

They answer the genomic question in the negative: aslB and aslA are convergently transcribed with 158 bp between their stop codons, so they are not in one operon, and they do not reproduce the co-oriented 44 bp atsB-atsA arrangement that the hypothesis names as the precedent.

What remains is physical adjacency plus compatibility of substrate type, and both are equally satisfied by YdeN, whose pairing with ydeM does reproduce the precedent. The AslA-substrate hypothesis is unresolved, and its operon sub-claim is refuted.
