Gene Description

CysK (O-acetylserine sulfhydrylase A, OASS-A) is the major cytosolic pyridoxal 5'-phosphate-dependent cysteine synthase of Escherichia coli. The homodimeric enzyme condenses O-acetyl-L-serine with sulfide to form L-cysteine and acetate, the second and final step of cysteine biosynthesis from serine. It associates with serine acetyltransferase (CysE), whose C-terminal tail docks into the CysK active site, to form the cysteine synthase complex; this is a regulatory and structural association, because complex formation lowers the activity of the bound CysK, so the productive sulfhydrylase is the free enzyme in excess over CysE. CysK also shows secondary L-cysteine desulfhydrase activity and, during contact-dependent growth inhibition by uropathogenic E. coli, binds and activates the tRNase toxin CdiA-CT independently of its catalytic activity. The paralog CysM (OASS-B) provides a second O-acetylserine sulfhydrylase that also uses thiosulfate.

PLP-dependent condensation of O-acetyl-L-serine with sulfide to form L-cysteine and acetate, the terminal step of cysteine biosynthesis, carried out by free cytosolic CysK; the CysK bound in the cysteine synthase complex with CysE is a regulatory reservoir whose activity is lowered by complex formation.
