## Key findings
- Gph identified as PGPase; in the dam superoperon
  [PMID:10572959 "Here we present evidence that the 27 kDa protein has 2-phosphoglycolate phosphatase activity, and we name the gene gph."];
  no physiological effect of deletion seen then
  [PMID:10572959 "We found no physiological effect of deleting gph and its function in E. coli remains unclear."].
- Purified enzyme, high specificity and efficiency
  [PMID:13129953 "2-Phosphoglycolate concentration kinetics determinations with purified preparations yielded a K m of 210 μM and a V max of 208 μmol min −1 mg −1 of protein from the Lineweaver-Burk plot."];
  no activity on phosphoglycerates, sugar phosphates, PEP, G3P
  [PMID:13129953 "No phosphatase activity was detected on the following phosphorylated compounds tested at concentrations of up to 5 mM"].
- Mg2+ and Cl- cooperative cofactors
  [PMID:13129953 "whereas the addition of Cl − alone (KCl) increased the activity by 11-fold, indicating a cooperative effect between the two cofactors"].
- Monomer [PMID:13129953 "indicates a monomeric structure for this enzyme"].
- Physiological role: salvage of 2-phosphoglycolate from repair of 3'-phosphoglycolate
  DNA ends
  [PMID:13129953 "We thus provide evidence for a salvage function of 2-phosphoglycolate phosphatase in the metabolism of a two-carbon compound generated by the cellular DNA repair machinery."].
- gph mutation does not alter DNA damage/survival after bleomycin or H2O2
  [PMID:13129953 "suggesting that the mutation in gph did not change the susceptibility of these cells to the oxidizing agents"],
  but mutants accumulate 2-phosphoglycolate and regrow more slowly.
- HAD screen: broad secondary activities among E. coli HADs, many likely
  non-physiological
  [PMID:16990279 "Many of the secondary activities of HADs might have no immediate physiological function but could comprise a reservoir for evolution of novel phosphatases."];
  UniProt gives Gph Km 8.9 mM for acetyl phosphate and 0.13 mM for imidodiphosphate.
  Cached abstract only.
