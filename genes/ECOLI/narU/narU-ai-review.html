Gene Description

NarU is a multipass nitrate/nitrite transporter of the major facilitator superfamily in the Escherichia coli inner membrane. It supports nitrate uptake and nitrite uptake and export, with a particular physiological advantage during nutrient starvation or slow growth. NarU can supply substrate for cytoplasmic respiratory nitrate reduction; nitrate reduction and subsequent nitrite reduction require separate enzymes. Its precise transport coupling mechanism remains unresolved.

Core Functions

Transports nitrate into the cell across the cytoplasmic membrane, supplying substrate for nitrate reduction. NarU contributes to growth under nitrate-respiring conditions and is advantageous during slow growth or starvation.

narU evidence notes

NarU P37758 is a multipass inner-membrane nitrate/nitrite transporter, not a nitrate reductase. [PMID:11967075: "Single mutants that lack either NarK or NarU were competent for nitrate-dependent anaerobic growth on a non-fermentable carbon source, glycerol."] Those engineered strains eliminate nitrate reductase Z and periplasmic nitrate reductase, so substrate supply to cytoplasm-facing nitrate reductase A is tested. This demonstrates transport in a respiratory background, not an autonomous NarU/NirBD assimilation route.

[PMID:18691156: "NarK and NarU, catalyse nitrate uptake, nitrite export and nitrite uptake across the Escherichia coli cytoplasmic membrane by an unknown mechanism."] The direction-neutral nitrite transporter annotation is retained because efflux-only refinement would lose uptake. Both nitrate and nitrite specificity are retained when refining generic transporter/process annotations.

Coupling remains unresolved. [PMID:18691156: "We propose that NarU functions as a single channel for nitrate uptake and nitrite expulsion, either as a nitrate-nitrite antiporter, or more likely as a nitrate/H+ or nitrite/H+ channel."] [PMID:41649193: "NarK operates as a nitrate/nitrite antiporter, whereas NarU's mechanism remains unresolved, with evidence suggesting potential symport activity."] The latter is molecular simulation, not a direct stoichiometric transport experiment. The ARBA secondary-active annotation is UNDECIDED; neither a proven antiporter nor a proton symporter is invented. PMID23523348 supplies structural context, but only its abstract was available and does not settle coupling.

Native Myc-tagged NarU is found in membrane fractions [PMID:16804183: "A commercial anti-Myc antibody was used to detect NarK and NarU in membrane fractions."]. That source also demonstrates selective advantage during starvation or slow growth. The topology papers support localization; lack of visible target rows in an abstract/supplement is not treated as a curation error. UniProt's experimental CC location and multipass topology independently corroborate the assignments. PANTHER PTHR23515 is the actual target cross-reference despite its plant-dominated family name; no plant-like coupling is inferred from that name. Observed PTN009073875 sources are traced without claiming a new tree reconstruction.

The E. coli panel is a physiological comparison, not an autonomous assimilatory nitrate route: nitrate uptake requires a separate upstream nitrate reductase before NirBD acts on nitrite. Assimilation is nevertheless experimentally supported. [PMID:34323689, Figure 3 supplement 1: "nitrate as the sole electron acceptor and as the sole nitrogen source"; "Under nitrate respiration, nitrate is converted to nitrite, which is subsequently converted to ammonium (NH4) by the cytosolic NADH-dependent nitrite reductase NirBD."] The study uses E. coli K-12 MG1655 derivatives; this is not evidence for cyanobacterial NirB accessory proteins.
