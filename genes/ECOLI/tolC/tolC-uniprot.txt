CC   -!- FUNCTION: Outer membrane channel, which is required for the function of
CC       several efflux systems such as AcrAB-TolC, AcrEF-TolC, EmrAB-TolC and
CC       MacAB-TolC (PubMed:11274125, PubMed:15228545, PubMed:18955484,
CC       PubMed:28355133, PubMed:28504659, PubMed:31201302, PubMed:40083904,
CC       PubMed:40461577, PubMed:6337123). These systems are involved in export
CC       of antibiotics and other toxic compounds from the cell
CC       (PubMed:11274125, PubMed:18955484, PubMed:28504659). As part of the
CC       system, involved in the efflux of the immediate heme precursor,
CC       protoporphyrin IX (PPIX), which is probably an endogenous substrate
CC       (PubMed:25257218). Plays a role in substrate specificity during efflux
CC       (PubMed:32850959). TolC is also involved in import of colicin E1 into
CC       the cells (PubMed:23176499, PubMed:35199644).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:11274125, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15228545,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:18955484, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23176499,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:25257218, ECO:0000269|PubMed:28355133,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:28504659, ECO:0000269|PubMed:31201302,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:32850959, ECO:0000269|PubMed:35199644,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:40083904, ECO:0000269|PubMed:40461577,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:6337123}.
CC   -!- ACTIVITY REGULATION: In vitro, inhibited by hexaamminecobalt(3+).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15342230}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Homotrimer (PubMed:15342230, PubMed:16079137, PubMed:9044294).
CC       Part of tripartite efflux systems, which are composed of an inner
CC       membrane transporter, a periplasmic membrane fusion protein, and an
CC       outer membrane component, TolC (PubMed:15228545, PubMed:16079137,
CC       PubMed:18955484, PubMed:19342493, PubMed:28355133, PubMed:31201302,
CC       PubMed:40083904). The complexes form a large protein conduit and can
CC       translocate molecules across both the inner and outer membranes
CC       (PubMed:15228545, PubMed:18955484, PubMed:40083904). TolC interacts
CC       with the membrane fusion proteins AcrA, EmrA and MacA (PubMed:15228545,
CC       PubMed:18955484, PubMed:19805313, PubMed:21325274). Component of the
CC       AcrAB-TolC multidrug efflux complex, composed of six AcrA subunits
CC       forming a hexameric tube, binding to an AcrB trimer, which interact
CC       with the trimeric TolC outer membrane channel protein (PubMed:15228545,
CC       PubMed:16079137, PubMed:28355133, PubMed:31201302). TolC is thought to
CC       not contact AcrB stably; instead, AcrA joins AcrB and TolC by forming a
CC       funnel-like hexamer anchored to the inner membrane (PubMed:15228545,
CC       PubMed:28355133). Contact between AcrA-AcrB and TolC may prompt a
CC       conformational change in the presence of substrate, allowing the
CC       periplasmic gate to open (PubMed:28355133, PubMed:31201302). It is
CC       thought that, under high intracellular substrate concentration, AcrB
CC       ejects substrate into the tunnel formed by AcrA-TolC; as the substrate
CC       level declines, conformational changes in AcrB cause efflux to reduce
CC       and stop and the complex shifts to the closed state (PubMed:31201302).
CC       Component of the AcrABZ-TolC efflux pump complex, composed of six AcrA
CC       subunits forming a hexameric tube binding to an AcrB trimer, which
CC       interact with the trimeric TolC outer membrane channel protein; AcrZ
CC       interacts directly with AcrB, probably in 1:1 stoichiometry
CC       (PubMed:28355133). Component of the MacAB-TolC efflux pump complex,
CC       composed of an inner membrane ABC-type transporter, MacB, a periplasmic
CC       membrane fusion protein, MacA, and an outer membrane component, TolC
CC       (PubMed:28504659, PubMed:40083904, PubMed:40461577). In the absence of
CC       substrate, MacA and TolC may form a bipartite complex (PubMed:40083904,
CC       PubMed:40461577). Interacts with colicin E1 (via N-terminal region);
CC       interaction can inhibit efflux and potentiate the effects of
CC       antibiotics on E.coli strain BW25113 (PubMed:35199644).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15228545, ECO:0000269|PubMed:15342230,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:16079137, ECO:0000269|PubMed:18955484,

CC       proximal end of the tunnel may be sealed by sets of coiled helices
CC       (PubMed:10879525). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10879525}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Cannot grow on efflux substrates novobiocin or
CC       fusidic acid (PubMed:15228545). Cell envelope weakening and increases
CC       outer membrane permeability (PubMed:38628966). Acquires sensitivity to
CC       chemical inhibitors of ATP-dependent lipid A-core flippase MsbA
CC       (PubMed:38628966). Impairs growth in combination with CRISPRi-mediated
CC       knockdown of msbA (PubMed:38628966). Accumulates 9 times more
CC       protoporphyrin IX (PPIX) than the corresponding complemented strain; in
CC       the presence of overexpression of the Dyp peroxidase, yfeX
CC       (PubMed:25257218). Total intracellular porphyrins increase about 25-
CC       fold in the presence of iron chelators, in a combined tolC and entF
CC       mutant background (PubMed:25257218). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:15228545,
