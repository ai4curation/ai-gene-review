Gene Description

CcmF is the large polytopic inner-membrane subunit of the cytochrome c synthetase of the Escherichia coli cytochrome c maturation (Ccm) system I. It has about 13 transmembrane segments, a periplasmic tryptophan-rich WWD domain flanked by conserved periplasmic histidines that is proposed to receive heme from the chaperone holo-CcmE, and a stably bound b-type heme ligated by conserved transmembrane histidines (His261, His491). CcmF forms a complex with CcmH and interacts with holo-CcmE; together with CcmH and the thioredoxin-like CcmG, CcmF is sufficient to attach heme covalently to the CXXCH motifs of periplasmic apocytochromes c when heme is available. In vitro its b-heme is reduced by quinols, suggesting a role in reducing the heme iron before thioether bond formation, although the proposed quinone site is not essential in vivo. CcmF is required for all periplasmic c-type cytochrome synthesis in E. coli.

Core Functions

Heme-binding integral membrane subunit of the CcmF/CcmH holocytochrome c synthase: binds a stable b-heme and receives heme from holo-CcmE, and together with CcmH (and reduced apocytochrome supplied via CcmG) covalently attaches heme to apocytochrome CXXCH motifs in the periplasm.

Biology

  • CcmF is a cytochrome b with a stoichiometric b-heme, not derived from holo-CcmE PMID:19629033; His261 is an axial ligand, and His491 also contributes PMID:22066495.
