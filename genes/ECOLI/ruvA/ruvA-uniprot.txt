CC   -!- FUNCTION: The RuvA-RuvB-RuvC complex processes Holliday junction (HJ)
CC       DNA during genetic recombination and DNA repair (PubMed:21531731,
CC       PubMed:6374379). The RuvA-RuvB complex plays an important role in the
CC       rescue of blocked DNA replication forks via replication fork reversal
CC       (RFR); RFR and homologous recombination required for UV light survival
CC       can be separated (PubMed:16424908, PubMed:18942176, PubMed:21531731,
CC       PubMed:9814711). The RuvA-RuvB complex promotes Holliday junction (HJ)
CC       branch migration. RuvA binds to HJ cruciform DNA, conferring on it an
CC       open structure (PubMed:10890893, PubMed:7885479, PubMed:9628481). In
CC       the presence of RuvB, ATP and Mg(2+) the junction is dissociated;
CC       hydrolyzable (d)NTPs can replace ATP but other analogs cannot
CC       (PubMed:10772859, PubMed:10890893, PubMed:1608954, PubMed:1617728,
CC       PubMed:1833759, PubMed:7885479, PubMed:8393934, PubMed:9628481). The
CC       RuvB hexamer acts as a pump, pulling dsDNA into and through the RuvAB
CC       complex (PubMed:9078376). Can bypass UV-induced lesions
CC       (PubMed:1617728) and physically cross-linked DNA strands
CC       (PubMed:10662672). RuvA gives specificity by binding to cruciform
CC       junctions, while the RuvB ATPase provides the motor force for branch
CC       migration; excess RuvB can promote branch migration in the absence of
CC       RuvA (PubMed:10662672, PubMed:1617728). Overexpression of RuvA alone
CC       leads to UV sensitivity (PubMed:2164626). RuvA stimulates the weak
CC       ATPase activity of RuvB in the presence of DNA; the addition of HJ DNA
CC       further stimulates ATPase about 10-fold (PubMed:1435721,
CC       PubMed:1833759, PubMed:8393934). Inhibits RuvC endoDNase activity by
CC       binding to HJ DNA, including DNA to which RuvC is already bound
CC       (PubMed:9000618). Does not bind dsDNA (PubMed:11080172). May act as a
CC       collar that slides at HJ which promotes branch migration while
CC       inhibiting other DNA remodeling activities (Probable).

CC       tetramers at cruciform DNA structures (PubMed:7885479, PubMed:9047358).
CC       In E.coli X-ray structures only 1 RuvA tetramer binds to HJ (complex
CC       I); however in M.leprae and T.thermophilus, 2 RuvA tetramers bind the
CC       HJ (complex II) (PubMed:10890893, PubMed:9628481). Mutagenesis
CC       experiments that prevent RuvA complex II formation on HJ DNA have
CC       impaired function, suggesting both complex I and complex II are
CC       physiologically important. Y-junction resolution is normal in these
CC       mutants, further suggesting each RuvA tetramer supports the activity of
CC       one RuvB hexamer. Mutants that don't make complex II are impaired in
CC       replication fork reversal (RFR), suggesting complex II exists in vivo
CC       (PubMed:15556943, PubMed:21531731). Forms a complex with RuvB without
CC       DNA; RuvA-RuvC complexes were not detected in the absence of DNA
CC       (PubMed:9160752). Can form complexes with RuvC and HJ DNA which
CC       inhibits RuvC activity (PubMed:15556943, PubMed:21531731,
CC       PubMed:9000618, PubMed:9637927). In the presence of HJ DNA a DNA-RuvA-
CC       RuvB-RuvC complex forms with 2 rings of RuvB that resolves upon
CC       addition of ATP. Upon HJ resolution the protein complex dissociates
CC       from DNA (PubMed:10421637, PubMed:9637927).

CC       is induced by damage to DNA and is regulated by LexA as part of the SOS
CC       response. RuvA and RuvB are also involved in mutagenesis induced by UV
CC       and X irradiation and by some chemicals (PubMed:2842314,
CC       PubMed:3279394). Induced by hydroxyurea (PubMed:20005847).
CC       {ECO:0000269|PubMed:20005847, ECO:0000269|PubMed:2842314,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:3279394}.
CC   -!- DOMAIN: Has three domains with a flexible linker between the domains II
CC       and III, and assumes an 'L' shape. Domain III has weak interactions
CC       with the adjacent subunit (PubMed:10890893, PubMed:8832889,
CC       PubMed:9493263, PubMed:9628481). In complex with Holliday junction (HJ)
CC       DNA, an approximately square planar DNA molecule is bound to the
CC       tetramer's concave face, with the DNA accessible to solution on one
CC       side. Domain III moves 4-5 Angstroms toward the protein-DNA interface
CC       upon DNA binding (PubMed:10890893, PubMed:9628481). The isolated N-
CC       terminus (domains I and II) forms tetramers and binds HJ DNA but does
CC       not bind RuvB (PubMed:10772859, PubMed:9493263). Alterations of bulky
CC       residues on the surface of domain III prevent binding to RuvB
CC       (PubMed:9493263). Domains I and II crystallize with the same tetrameric
CC       structure and are dominant negative to wild-type in vivo. In intact
CC       protein, domain III is quite flexible in solution. Isolated domain III
CC       inhibits the ATPase activity of RuvB (PubMed:10772859). The acidic pin
CC       (Glu-55 and Asp-56) seems to push the DNA backbone away from the
CC       HJ/RuvA complex center. It prevents binding to non-HJ dsDNA and
CC       constrains the rate of branch migration (PubMed:10890893,
CC       PubMed:11080172). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10772859,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:10890893, ECO:0000269|PubMed:11080172,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:8832889, ECO:0000269|PubMed:9493263,
CC       ECO:0000269|PubMed:9628481}.
CC   -!- DISRUPTION PHENOTYPE: Sensitive to radiation, filamentous growth after
CC       transient inhibition of DNA synthesis, little effect on conjugal
CC       recombination in wild-type strains (PubMed:6374379). Increased
CC       sensitivity to mitomycin and UV light (PubMed:10772859,
CC       PubMed:18942176, PubMed:2164626, PubMed:2693946, PubMed:9493263).
CC       Suppresses lethality in recB-recC and dnaB temperature-sensitive
CC       mutants (PubMed:9814711). {ECO:0000269|PubMed:10772859,
