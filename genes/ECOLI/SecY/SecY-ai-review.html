Gene Description

SecY is the pore-forming subunit of the Escherichia coli SecYEG protein-conducting channel in the inner membrane. Its ten-transmembrane-helix core forms the gated aqueous pore and lateral gate used for post-translational SecA/SecB-dependent export of secretory preproteins and for cotranslational insertion of inner-membrane proteins delivered by SRP. SecY directly contacts signal peptides, SecA, SecE, SecG, ribosomes, and accessory translocon factors during Sec-dependent protein movement into or across the plasma membrane.

SecY notes

Manual synthesis in place of provider deep research, which was unavailable in this environment.

  • SecY is the pore-forming alpha subunit of the SecYEG inner-membrane channel. Together with SecE and SecG, it forms the membrane-embedded domain that translocates secretory preproteins and cotranslational inner-membrane-protein substrates. Brundage et al. established SecY, SecE, and band 1/SecG as the associated active membrane domain [PMID:1531482, "These data establish that the subunits of SecY/E are firmly associated and that it is the associated complex which is active for translocation."], and Hanada et al. depleted SecY, SecE, and SecG together in reconstitution experiments [PMID:7650029, "These data establish that SecY, SecE, and SecG constitute the integral membrane domain of preprotein translocase."].
  • SecY directly contributes the protein-conducting pore and signal-peptide-interacting lateral gate. Wang et al. used a synthetic photoaffinity-labeled signal peptide and found specific saturable SecY binding [PMID:15476412, "The signal peptide was found to specifically bind SecY in a saturable manner and at levels comparable to those that stimulate SecA ATPase activity."].
  • The SecYEG pore is used by both the post-translational SecA/SecB route for secretory proteins and the SRP route for cotranslational membrane-protein insertion. Koch et al. reconstituted SRP and SecA/SecB pathways and found that both targeting pathways intersect at the pore [PMID:10397756, "Both targeting pathways intersect at the translocation pore because they are equally affected by a blocked translocation channel."], while Valent et al. showed that SRP-targeted nascent proteins insert into a Sec-containing translocon [PMID:9564033, "Upon release of the SRP at the membrane, the targeted nascent proteins insert into a translocon that contains at least SecA, SecY and SecG."].
  • SecY can also associate with SecDF-YajC-YidC in a larger holo-translocon. Schulze et al. purified an E. coli holo-translocon containing single copies of SecYEG and SecDF-YajC-YidC [PMID:24550475, "the HTL is a hetero-dimer composed of single copies of SecYEG and SecDF-YajC-YidC."].
  • Generic GO:0005515 protein-binding rows for SecY should be removed as uninformative interaction records. The reported contacts with SecA, SecE, SecG, ribosome, YidC, and PpiD reflect channel assembly or engagement by accessory factors, not a standalone molecular function separable from SecYEG channel activity.
