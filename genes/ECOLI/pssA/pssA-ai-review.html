Gene Description

pssA encodes CDP-diacylglycerol--serine O-phosphatidyltransferase (phosphatidylserine synthase), a type-I phosphatidylserine synthase of the phospholipase D superfamily. It transfers the phosphatidyl group of CDP-diacylglycerol to L-serine, through a covalent phosphatidyl-enzyme intermediate formed at its two HKD motifs, producing phosphatidylserine and CMP. Phosphatidylserine is then decarboxylated by Psd, so PssA catalyzes the first step of phosphatidylethanolamine biosynthesis, the major membrane phospholipid of E. coli. PssA is a peripheral membrane protein: the monomer binds the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane, with binding promoted by anionic lipids, and this membrane-bound form is the active one, whereas the cytosolic monomer-dimer pool is inactive.

Interpretation

PssA is a type-I phosphatidylserine synthase (PLD superfamily, two HKD motifs,
PTHR12586). The InterPro IPR016270 (PGS1) mapping to GO:0008444 and
GO:0032049 transfers the eukaryotic phosphatidylglycerophosphate synthase
function of the same superfamily lineage; E. coli uses PgsA (a CDP-alcohol
phosphatidyltransferase) for that reaction, so these rows are removed.
