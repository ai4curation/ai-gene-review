# SecF research notes

SecF is the smaller SecDF membrane partner that works with SecD in the E. coli
SecDF-YajC accessory assembly. SecDF uses proton motive force rather than ATP to
complete late translocation through the SecYEG channel [PMID:21562494 "we have
shown that SecDF is a component of the Sec machinery that utilizes the PMF to
complete protein translocation after the ATP-dependent SecA function"], and
structural synthesis describes SecDF as a protein-translocation factor distinct
from the ATP-driven SecA motor [PMID:29718185 "SecDF is a
protein-translocation factor, which is different from the ATP-driven SecA
motor"].

The key correction is that SecF contributes to PMF-coupled protein transmembrane
transporter activity; it is not itself a protein-transporting ATPase. SecA
provides the ATPase motor, while SecD and SecF form the PMF-coupled accessory
subcomplex.

Purified holo-translocon work places SecF with SecD and YajC in the
SecYEG-SecDF-YajC-YidC assembly [PMID:24550475 "SecYEG interacts with the highly
conserved YidC and SecDF-YajC subcomplex, which facilitates translocation into
and across the membrane"]. A later structural study likewise treated the E. coli
holo-translocon as a SecYEG-SecDF-YajC-YidC inner-membrane supercomplex
[PMID:27435098 "supercomplex of SecYEG-SecDF-YajC-YidC - is a bona fide resident
of the Escherichia coli inner membrane"].

The generic SecF `membrane`, `protein transport`, and `intracellular protein
transport` rows are less informative than the direct plasma-membrane,
Sec-complex transport, and intracellular protein transmembrane transport rows.
