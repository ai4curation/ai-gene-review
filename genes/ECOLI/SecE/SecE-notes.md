# SecE notes

Manual synthesis in place of provider deep research, which was unavailable in this environment.

- SecE is the essential gamma/clamp subunit of the E. coli SecYEG channel. It binds SecY, holds the two halves of SecY together, and contributes to the SecYEG protein transmembrane transporter rather than forming an independent pore. Brundage et al. established SecY, SecE, and band 1/SecG as the associated active membrane domain [PMID:1531482, "These data establish that the subunits of SecY/E are firmly associated and that it is the associated complex which is active for translocation."], and Hanada et al. showed that depleting SecY, SecE, and SecG removed preprotein translocase activity [PMID:7650029, "These data establish that SecY, SecE, and SecG constitute the integral membrane domain of preprotein translocase."].
- The direct SecY-SecE interface is supported by multiple cross-linking studies. Harris and Silhavy mapped periplasmic contact sites [PMID:10348856, "Together, these two points of contact allow the modeling of a limited region of quaternary structure, establishing the first characterized site of interaction between these two proteins."], and Satoh et al. identified a cytosolic SecY-SecE interface [PMID:12809500, "We suggest that the cytosolic and intramembrane interactions bring these two subunits together, forming a functionally crucial SecYE interface involving the SecY C5 region and the conserved cytosolic segment of SecE."].
- Like SecY and SecG, SecE acts in the plasma membrane SecYEG pore used by both SecA/SecB post-translational export and SRP-dependent cotranslational insertion. Koch et al. showed that the SRP/FtsY and SecA/SecB routes are independent targeting pathways that intersect at the same translocation pore [PMID:10397756, "Both targeting pathways intersect at the translocation pore because they are equally affected by a blocked translocation channel."].
- Generic GO:0005515 protein-binding rows for SecE should be removed as uninformative interaction records. The SecE-SecY and SecE-SecA contacts are real, but they are evidence for channel-complex assembly and SecYEG/SecA translocase function rather than a specific independent binding activity.
