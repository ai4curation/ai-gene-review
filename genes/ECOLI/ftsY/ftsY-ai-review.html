Gene Description

FtsY is the Escherichia coli signal recognition particle (SRP) receptor, the single-subunit bacterial homolog of the eukaryotic SRP receptor alpha subunit. It has an acidic, largely disordered N-terminal A domain carrying lipid-binding helices that attach it peripherally to anionic phospholipids on the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane, followed by N and G domains homologous to those of Ffh. Membrane-bound FtsY captures the SRP-ribosome-nascent-chain complex; GTP-dependent heterodimerization of the FtsY and Ffh NG domains creates a shared active site in which each protein stimulates the other's GTP hydrolysis. Rearrangements of this complex hand the ribosome-nascent chain to the SecYEG translocon, with which FtsY also interacts, and GTP hydrolysis dissociates SRP and receptor. FtsY is essential for cotranslational inner-membrane protein insertion; it can also homodimerize at the membrane and is inhibited by (p)ppGpp.

ftsY (E. coli K-12) review notes

2026-11-02

Reviewed as an experimentally characterized exemplar for the bacterial SRP
cotranslational targeting module. No deep-research provider run was made for
this gene in this session (no provider credentials available); the review rests
on the Swiss-Prot record and the cached publications cited by GOA. All seeded
GOA rows were reviewed against the cached abstracts or full texts.

Key evidence:

  • FtsY binds SRP like the mammalian receptor [PMID:8107852, "Here we show that
    the Ffh/4.5S ribonucleoprotein binds tightly to FtsY in a GTP-dependent
    manner."] and is required for export [PMID:8194520, "Depletion of FtsY is
    shown to cause the accumulation of the precursor form of beta-lactamase, OmpF
    and ribose binding protein in vivo"].
  • The SRP and receptor GTPases share an active site [PMID:17682051, "form a
    unique complex in which hydrolyses of both guanosine triphosphates (GTP) are
    activated in a shared active site"].
  • FtsY is almost entirely membrane-bound in vivo [PMID:19912622, "the vast
    majority of FtsY was localized to the inner membrane and that soluble FtsY
    constituted only a negligible species in vivo"], so the cytosol row is kept as
    non-core.
  • (p)ppGpp inhibits FtsY rather than FtsY carrying out the stringent response
    [PMID:35217658, "(p)ppGpp binds to the SRP GTPases Ffh and FtsY, and inhibits
    the formation of the SRP receptor-targeting complex"].

description: FtsY is the Escherichia coli signal recognition particle (SRP) receptor, the single-subunit
  bacterial homolog of the eukaryotic SRP receptor alpha subunit. It has an acidic, largely disordered
  N-terminal A domain carrying lipid-binding helices that attach it peripherally to anionic phospholipids
  on the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane, followed by N and G domains homologous to those of Ffh.
  Membrane-bound FtsY captures the SRP-ribosome-nascent-chain complex; GTP-dependent heterodimerization
  of the FtsY and Ffh NG domains creates a shared active site in which each protein stimulates the other's
  GTP hydrolysis. Rearrangements of this complex hand the ribosome-nascent chain to the SecYEG translocon,
  with which FtsY also interacts, and GTP hydrolysis dissociates SRP and receptor. FtsY is essential for
  cotranslational inner-membrane protein insertion; it can also homodimerize at the membrane and is inhibited
  by (p)ppGpp.
