# sucD (P0AGE9) notes

## 2026-11-11 first review

Written as part of the evolutionary review of the succinyl-CoA synthetase families; the E. coli subunits are PAINT seeds for both families. See genes/yeast/LSC2/LSC2-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md.

- Genes and operon [PMID:3543212 "The newly defined genes express polypeptides of 41 kDa (sucC) and 31 kDa (sucD), corresponding to the beta and alpha subunits of succinyl-CoA synthetase, respectively."].
- Structure: alpha2beta2 [PMID:8144675 "The quaternary organization of the E. coli enzyme is an alpha 2 beta 2 heterotetramer."]; CoA and the phosphohistidine on the alpha subunit [PMID:8144675 "One molecule of coenzyme A is bound to each alpha-subunit at a typical nucleotide-binding motif, and His-246 of each alpha-subunit is phosphorylated."]; nucleotide on the beta subunit [PMID:10625475 "X-ray diffraction data revealed an ADP-Mg(2+) complex bound in the ATP-grasp fold of the N-terminal domain of each beta-subunit"].
- Nucleotide use: ATP preferred, GTP accepted [PMID:22751660 "This contrasts with Escherichia coli SCS, which uses GDP/GTP but prefers ADP/ATP"]. UniProt gives Km 70 uM for ATP and 394 uM for GTP, kcat 2684 and 1471 per minute (attributed to PMID:10353839), so kcat/Km with GTP is about a tenth of that with ATP. GDP-forming activity is therefore kept as a non-core secondary activity, not accepted as core and not removed.
- Position 20 of the beta subunit is Pro; changing it to Gln, the residue of GTP-specific animal subunits, improves GTP use [PMID:10353839 "A mutant protein designed to probe nucleotide specificity (P20betaQ) had a Km(app) for GTP that was more than 5 times lower than that of wild-type SCS"].
- Evolutionary reading: the E. coli enzyme shows the dual-specificity bacterial state. It is the reason PAINT can defend GDP-forming activity at the LUCA node of the alpha family, and it is not a guide to S. cerevisiae, whose enzyme does not use GTP [PMID:9874242 "utilize ATP but not GTP for succinyl-CoA synthesis"]; other fungal enzymes are unassayed.
- cytoplasm (HDA) modified to cytosol; GO:0042709 marked too general beside GO:0009361; protein binding rows with the partner subunit removed under the policy.
- GO:0046777 protein autophosphorylation (IMP) marked over-annotated: His246 phosphorylation is the catalytic intermediate [PMID:8144675 "This phosphohistidine, a catalytic intermediate, is stabilized by two helix dipoles"]. The experiment itself is sound [PMID:1986797 "As expected, the His-246 alpha----Asp mutant is incapable of undergoing phosphorylation."].
- One background statement in PMID:1986797 is partly overturned by the later structures and is flagged on the finding [PMID:1986797 "The site of phosphorylation (His-246) and the binding sites for the substrates ADP and ATP are located in the alpha subunit, and the succinate and CoA binding sites are in beta."]. The nucleotide and CoA assignments are overturned (CoA on alpha, nucleotide on beta). The succinate assignment to the beta subunit and the phosphohistidine assignment stand.

## Retrospective audit corrections (2026-11-12)

A retrospective audit of the succinyl-CoA synthetase reviews found the points below. No annotation action changed in this file. Earlier statements in these notes that were wrong have been corrected in place and are listed here.

- Fungal generalisation corrected. The earlier note said fungi have a single beta subunit that does not use GTP. Only the S. cerevisiae enzyme has been assayed [PMID:9874242 "utilize ATP but not GTP for succinyl-CoA synthesis"], and some early-diverging fungi keep a SUCLG2-type beta gene [file:yeast/LSC2/LSC2-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md "Early-diverging fungi (Mortierellomycota, Basidiobolus, Rozella) keep a SUCLG2-type beta gene with Gln at position 20, so the G-type beta gene was lost within fungi and not in the common ancestor of all fungi."]. The GO:0004776 reasons now name the S. cerevisiae enzyme only.
- Position 20 is not a predictor of nucleotide preference. Thermus aquaticus has Pro at this position and prefers GDP/GTP [PMID:22751660 "A water molecule and Pro20β in T. aquaticus take the place of Gln20β"]. What E. coli shares with the ADP-forming eukaryotic lineage is ADP use [file:yeast/LSC2/LSC2-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md "E. coli SCS has Pro20 and uses both nucleotides, with a preference for ADP (PMID:22751660), so what the A-type lineage shares with the bacterial enzyme is ADP use, not ADP specificity."].
- "ADP-forming activity is the ancestral state" removed from the GO:0004775 IBA reason. PAINT places the ADP-forming term at the root of the beta family (PTN000212531) and both the ADP- and GDP-forming terms at the LUCA node of the alpha family (PTN000122012, which is one step below the alpha-tree root). Whether the ancestor also used GDP stays an open question.
- Reverse direction in vivo. Two papers were cached and added. sucCD can be deleted (the deletion strain grows in LB) because sucAB is a second route to succinyl-CoA [PMID:16445752 "either sucAB or sucCD is enough to produce succinyl-CoA that is essential for cell viability"]. Insertions that abolish succinyl-CoA synthetase prevent aerobic growth on acetate or 2-oxoglutarate, and anaerobic growth needs methionine and lysine [PMID:2496297 "unable to grow aerobically on acetate or alpha-ketoglutarate"]. The 1989 paper also reports a 5-aminolevulinate requirement and attributes it to succinyl-CoA; that explanation is not used, because E. coli makes 5-aminolevulinate from glutamate [PMID:2254275 "exclusively from glutamate via the five-carbon pathway, which also occurs in plants and some bacteria including Escherichia coli"].
- The OpenScientist report is now marked UNVERIFIED. Its mechanism scheme places the succinate site on the alpha subunit, and it lists tetrapyrrole and heme synthesis among the uses of succinyl-CoA, which is wrong for E. coli (see above). The earlier statement that it agrees with every action is still true of the actions; it is not a statement that the report is free of errors.
- cytoplasm (HDA, PMID:16858726). The earlier summary said the subunits were identified in the cytosolic fraction. The cached abstract does not name the subunits or a fraction for them [PMID:16858726 "the cytosolic and membrane protein complexes of Escherichia coli were separated"]. The summary and reason now say so; the MODIFY to cytosol rests on the IDA cytosol rows.
- Paper titles are no longer used as supporting quotes (PMID:19402753).
- Reference verdicts for the high-throughput papers in which the subunits are not visible in the cached text (PMID:16606699, PMID:16858726, PMID:18304323, PMID:19402753, PMID:15911532) changed from VERIFIED to UNVERIFIED.
- Succinate site. Succinate binds the C-terminal domain of the beta subunit; the structural evidence is from the pig GTP-specific enzyme [PMID:27487822 "Succinate binds in the carboxy-terminal domain of the β-subunit."].
- Description: "conserved as a single gene from bacteria to animals" changed to "conserved from bacteria to animals; in animals a single alpha subunit serves separate ADP- and GDP-specific beta subunits". Some genomes have more than one alpha gene [file:yeast/LSC2/LSC2-bioinformatics/RESULTS.md "The PANTHER alpha tree has one or two alpha genes per fungal genome; S. cerevisiae has exactly one alpha (LSC1) and one beta (LSC2)."].
- GO:0046777 protein autophosphorylation stays MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, with a corrected reason. The earlier reason required a regulatory or signalling outcome, which the GO definition does not. The reason now rests on the phosphohistidine being a transient catalytic intermediate. GO usage is mixed (QuickGO, checked 2026-11-12): E. coli ndk, gpmA, ptsI and ppsA and human SUCLG1 and ACLY carry no autophosphorylation row; E. coli ppk and glmM and P. aeruginosa SucC do.
- PMID:1986797 background statement split into two findings. The nucleotide and CoA assignments are overturned by the crystal structures. The succinate assignment to the beta subunit stands and is marked CORROBORATED.
- The quote from the bioinformatics RESULTS.md on the GO:0004776 IBA row was removed. The sentence it quoted ("Fungi lost the G-type beta paralog") was wrong at kingdom level and no longer exists; the row is supported by the kinetic constants and PMID:22751660.
- GO:0009361 IBA reason reworded: SucD is the seed. An ATP-forming succinate-CoA ligase complex of alpha and beta subunits is found in bacteria (alpha2beta2 in E. coli) and in eukaryotes that have an A-type beta subunit (an alpha-beta heterodimer in the animal and yeast enzymes).
- Review status changed from INITIALIZED to DRAFT; no row is pending.
