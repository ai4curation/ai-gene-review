Gene Description

CasB (also called Cse2) is a small, all-helical subunit of Cascade, the 405-kDa CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex of the Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system. Two copies of CasB form a head-to-tail dimer that runs along the belly of the complex, contacting the thumb and web surfaces of the CasC (Cas7) backbone and connecting the CasE-capped head to the CasA tail. CasB is a non-sequence-specific nucleic acid binding protein that engages both duplex DNA and single-stranded RNA through a conserved basic surface patch, with a preference for duplex nucleic acid. Within the complex this surface serves target interrogation and R-loop stabilisation rather than guide binding. When crRNA invades a protospacer, CasB residues stack with and hydrogen-bond to individual nucleotides of the displaced non-target strand, and the CasB dimer forms one wall of the basic groove that accommodates that strand; target binding also repositions the CasB subunits, which unblocks the distal end of the guide for base pairing. CasB is required for resistance to bacteriophage lambda and for plasmid silencing, but not for maturation of the crRNA guide.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex

Definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction. The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent is the Csy complex.

Justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic ancestor GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and its subunits are the direct targets of a whole family of phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible.

Parent term: ribonucleoprotein complex

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: CasB's specific contribution is stabilisation of the displaced strand of an R-loop, for which GO has no term - neither a molecular function for crRNA-guided target recognition nor a process term for R-loop formation by a guide-RNA effector. Should such terms be requested, and if so should R-loop stabilisation be a function of the subunit or only of the complex?

Q: The crystal structure states that CasB makes no direct contact with the crRNA, whereas the UniProt SUBUNIT annotation for P76632, based on earlier biochemistry, records a direct crRNA interaction. Is the apparent crRNA contact an artefact of crosslinking or subcomplex assays, or is it a state not captured in the crystal?

Suggested experts: Ryan N Jackson, Blake Wiedenheft, Ailong Ke

Q: CasB over-accumulates mature crRNA when deleted. Does CasB contribute to crRNA turnover or to release of mature crRNA from the processing subcomplex, or is the effect purely a consequence of failing to assemble functional Cascade?

Suggested experts: Stan J J Brouns, John van der Oost

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Introduce the E. coli equivalents of the CasB basic-patch alanine substitutions characterised in Thermobifida fusca (the Arg/Lys clusters corresponding to R45/R46, R130/K131/R132 and R148/R150) into P76632, then measure for each variant Cascade assembly and stoichiometry by size exclusion chromatography, affinity for a PAM-flanked dsDNA target, R-loop formation on a negatively supercoiled plasmid, Cas3-dependent degradation, and phage lambda resistance.

Hypothesis: The conserved basic patch of CasB is required specifically for R-loop stabilisation, not for Cascade assembly.

Type: structure-guided mutagenesis with assembly, binding and in vivo readouts

Experiment: Use single-molecule FRET with dyes on the two CasB subunits and on the PAM-distal end of the crRNA to follow the conformational change during R-loop propagation on targets with mismatches placed at increasing distance from the PAM, testing whether CasB motion precedes or follows distal base pairing.

Hypothesis: CasB repositioning on target binding is the step that unblocks the PAM-distal end of the guide.

Type: single-molecule FRET

Experiment: Perform in vivo crosslinking of tagged CasB followed by sequencing of the crosslinked DNA to map the displaced-strand footprint at a chromosomal protospacer, comparing wild-type CasB with basic-patch variants and comparing strains with and without Cas3.

Hypothesis: CasB contacts the displaced strand in vivo and protects it from cellular single-strand nucleases.

Type: in vivo crosslinking and sequencing

Session 1 (2026-10-01): initial full review

Reviewed together with casA, casC, casD and casE as one complex.

Position in Cascade

CasB is present in two copies and forms a dimer that runs along the belly of the
complex, bridging the CasE-capped head and the CasA tail
PMID:25103409,
contacting the CasC (Cas7) backbone
PMID:25103409.
UniProt P76632 SUBUNIT records the same: "Homodimer. Part of the Cascade
ribonucleoprotein complex ... Interacts directly with crRNA, CasA, CasC and CasE."

Note the discrepancy worth recording: the crystal structure explicitly states that CasB
does not touch the crRNA
PMID:25103409,
whereas UniProt's SUBUNIT line (based on earlier biochemistry) says it interacts
directly with crRNA. I do not need to adjudicate this, because GOA holds no crRNA-binding
row for CasB; its nucleic-acid rows are about DNA and about generic nucleic acid.

What CasB actually binds

The CasB-specific structural/biochemical paper assayed four CasB orthologues, including
E. coli P76632, by EMSA
PMID:23079036,
and found the binding to be non-selective
PMID:23079036,
with a preference for duplex
PMID:23079036.
The binding surface is a conserved basic patch, confirmed by mutagenesis
PMID:23079036.

This is why I keep GOA's GO:0003676 nucleic acid binding at the parent level rather
than pushing it down to DNA binding: the experiment that supports it deliberately
establishes non-specificity across DNA and RNA, so the parent is the honest term. The
separate GO:0003677 DNA binding row rests on different, structural evidence and is kept
as well — the two rows are not redundant duplicates but complementary statements at
different levels from different experiments.

The structural DNA evidence is specific. In the target-bound complex CasB residues grip
individual displaced (non-target-strand) nucleotides
PMID:25123481,
CasB lines the basic groove that accommodates the displaced strand
PMID:25123481,
and the CasB dimer forms one wall of the channel the target sits in
PMID:25123481.
So CasB's job is R-loop stabilisation: it holds the strand that crRNA invasion displaces.

The structural-subunit problem, and how I resolved it

GOA gives CasB two GO:0005515 protein binding IDA rows (one from the Cascade
purification, one from the crystal structure) and nothing else about its role in the
complex. CLAUDE.md forbids treating protein binding as informative. The options were:

  • nucleic-acid-binding only — loses the fact that CasB is two-thirds of the
    head-to-tail connection in the complex;
  • an explicit ontology gap — overkill, because a fitting term does exist;
  • structural constituent — GO:0005198 structural molecule activity, "The action of a
    molecule that contributes to the structural integrity of a complex". There is no child
    for a non-ribosomal RNP (the children are ribosome, proteasome, virion, chromatin,
    nuclear pore, etc.), so the parent is the correct level.

I took the third option, and applied the same replacement to the equivalent
intra-complex protein binding rows on casA, casC, casD and casE, so the five subunits
come out consistent. CasB's structural role is directly stated in the structure
PMID:25103409.

I did not invent a term for crRNA-guided target recognition or for R-loop
stabilisation; those gaps are recorded in suggested_questions and in
modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml.

Requirement for immunity

CasB is essential for the pathway, not merely a passenger
PMID:23079036,
consistent with Cascade as a whole
PMID:18703739.
Brouns also noted that crRNA accumulates in the casB knockout
PMID:18703739,
i.e. CasB is not needed for processing; it is needed for interference. That is why CasB
gets no crRNA-biogenesis process term here.

Complex term

Same decision as the other four subunits: GO:0032991 is the bare root of the complex
branch, and Cascade is demonstrably a ribonucleoprotein
PMID:23079036,
PMID:25103409,
so I MODIFY to GO:1990904 ribonucleoprotein complex. GO has no CC term for Cascade or
any CRISPR surveillance complex — a QuickGO ontology search for "CRISPR" returns five
terms, all biological_process — so I also propose the missing term.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex
  proposed_definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas)
    proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA
    as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I
    systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an
    R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction.
    The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition
    Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent
    is the Csy complex.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:1990904
    label: ribonucleoprotein complex
  justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms
    (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133),
    with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance
    complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic
    ancestor GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined,
    structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models (CPX-1005, Cascade
    complex), and its subunits are the direct targets of a whole family of
    phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of
    structural and counter-defence curation expressible.
