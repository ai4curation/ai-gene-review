Gene Description

KdpC is the small accessory subunit of KdpFABC, the inducible high-affinity ATP-driven potassium uptake pump of the bacterial inner membrane, and is required together with KdpA and KdpB for a functional system. KdpC has a single transmembrane helix and a soluble domain of novel fold; crystal and cryo-EM structures place this domain on the periplasmic side, where it is held against periplasmic loops of the K+-selective subunit KdpA near the entrance of its selectivity filter, and KdpC (with KdpF) stabilizes the KdpA-KdpB partnership. KdpC neither hydrolyzes ATP (a KdpB function) nor forms the K+ pathway (a KdpA function). Earlier biochemical work proposed that KdpC acts as a "catalytic chaperone" that binds ATP and raises the nucleotide affinity of KdpB, but the periplasmic placement of its soluble domain in the structures is difficult to reconcile with direct contact with the cytoplasmic nucleotide-binding domain of KdpB.

Core Functions

KdpC is an essential single-pass accessory subunit of the KdpFABC K+ pump. Its periplasmic soluble domain packs against KdpA near the selectivity-filter entrance and, with KdpF, stabilizes the KdpA-KdpB assembly, contributing to the complex's ATP-driven K+ uptake activity without itself catalysing ATP hydrolysis or forming the K+ pathway.
