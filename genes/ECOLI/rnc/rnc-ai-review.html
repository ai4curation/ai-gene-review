Gene Description

Ribonuclease III (RNase III, gene rnc, EC 3.1.26.3) is the founding member of the RNase III superfamily that also contains the eukaryotic enzymes Dicer and Drosha. The 226-residue Escherichia coli protein is a homodimer in which each subunit contributes an N-terminal nuclease domain (residues 6-128, catalytic Asp45 and Glu117) and a C-terminal double-stranded RNA-binding domain (155-225); the two half-sites form a composite catalytic centre that uses two Mg(2+) ions per phosphodiester hydrolysis. The enzyme is a double-strand-specific endoribonuclease: it cleaves duplex RNA elements of roughly 20 base pairs formed within otherwise single-stranded substrates, leaving 5'-phosphate and 3'-hydroxyl ends, and does not act on RNA-DNA hybrids. Its central housekeeping role is ribosome biogenesis - it makes the first cuts that excise the 16S and 23S precursors from the single 30S primary rRNA transcript, a step that is obligate for 23S maturation although ribosomes carrying unprocessed 23S still support growth. It also processes phage transcripts (notably the T7 early polycistronic mRNA), at least one tRNA, and a set of cellular mRNAs, where cleavage of the 5' leader initiates decay and thereby sets transcript abundance; the pnp message encoding polynucleotide phosphorylase is the archetype, and the enzyme processes its own transcript as well. RNase III is cytoplasmic and loosely ribosome-associated, is encoded in the rnc-era-recO operon, and is itself regulated: the macrodomain protein YmdB binds its catalytic region and down-regulates cleavage during cold shock, with a separate YmdB-independent mechanism operating on entry into stationary phase. Loss of the gene is tolerated, giving slow growth, transient accumulation of the 30S rRNA precursor, extended 23S rRNA in the ribosome and stabilisation of its mRNA substrates. Being a widely conserved host enzyme that acts on any duplex RNA presented to it, RNase III is also recruited by pathways that do not encode their own nuclease: type II CRISPR-Cas systems of other bacteria use the host enzyme to cut the pre-crRNA:tracrRNA duplex during guide RNA maturation.

Core Functions

Double-strand-specific endoribonuclease that makes the initial cleavages excising the 16S and 23S rRNA precursors from the single 30S primary transcript, the committing step of ribosome biogenesis downstream of transcription. The enzyme recognises duplex elements of around 20 base pairs within otherwise single-stranded substrates and cleaves site-specifically using two Mg(2+) ions, leaving 5'-phosphate and 3'-hydroxyl ends. Loss of the enzyme abolishes 23S maturation entirely, although the extended 23S species still assemble into functional 50S subunits, which is why the gene is dispensable for growth.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should RNase III carry GO:0006402 mRNA catabolic process alongside GO:0006397 mRNA processing? It makes the committing endonucleolytic cleavage of pnp mRNA turnover itself, and RNase E (P21513) carries GO:0006402 and GO:0006401 for the same kind of decay-initiating role. The complication is that GO:0010468, already annotated here by IBA, is an ancestor of GO:0006402, so this is a refinement of an existing row for a curator to make rather than a missing annotation.

Q: Does RNase III have any role in CRISPR-Cas immunity in Escherichia coli K-12 itself? The enzyme can substitute for the Streptococcus RNase III when a type II system is transplanted into E. coli, but E. coli K-12's native system is type I-E and processes pre-crRNA with the Cas6e/CasE subunit of Cascade, so no CRISPR process term is annotated here. A negative result - showing that an rnc deletion leaves type I-E immunity intact in K-12 - would settle it definitively.

Suggested experts: Virginijus Siksnys, Emmanuelle Charpentier

Q: How many of the roughly two dozen transcripts stabilised in an rnc null are direct substrates rather than secondary consequences of disturbed ribosome biogenesis? The distinction decides how much of the regulation of gene expression annotation reflects direct cleavage.

Q: What determines which duplex elements of around 20 base pairs are cleaved and which are merely bound? The ionic-strength dependence of site fidelity suggests that specificity is partly thermodynamic rather than encoded in sequence, which would explain how the enzyme can be borrowed by a heterologous pathway such as type II crRNA maturation.

Core activity

A double-strand-specific endoribonuclease that cleaves duplex RNA, leaving
5'-phosphate/3'-OH ends, and acts as a homodimer so that two half-sites form the
composite catalytic centre.

  • PMID:15699182
  • End chemistry, measured directly:
    PMID:1091644 The same preparation study separated the
    dsRNA-cleaving/T7-processing activity from a contaminating activity on the RNA strand of
    DNA:RNA hybrids PMID:1091644, and UniProt records the resulting specificity as
    "Digests double-stranded RNA formed within single-strand substrates, but not RNA-DNA
    hybrids" [file:ECOLI/rnc/rnc-uniprot.txt]. That separation is worth noting, because it
    is exactly the opposite of the Cas3 case reviewed alongside this gene: Cas3's hybrid
    activity is real, RNase III's is a contaminant.
  • Homodimer: PMID:932008
  • Two catalytic metal ions, by kinetics and by a two-metal-ion-specific inhibitor:
    PMID:15699182

    disruption phenotype: slower growth, extended 23S in the ribosome)

The CRISPR connection, and why it is NOT annotated here

This gene is the mirror image of ygcB/Cas3. Cas3 is a dedicated Cas protein with no
function outside CRISPR. RNase III is a housekeeping enzyme that a different class of
CRISPR system borrows. The module's own framing is right on this point: it "is not a Cas
protein and is not encoded in the cas locus"
[file:modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml].

What the literature actually establishes, in order of how close it gets to E. coli:

  1. Type II crRNA maturation needs host RNase III — demonstrated in Streptococcus
    pyogenes    .     PMID:21455174 The paper is explicit that this is a host factor being
    recruited: PMID:21455174 The organism is S. pyogenes, not E. coli.
  2. The E. coli protein can do the job, in a heterologous host. Karvelis et al.
    transplanted the S. thermophilus DGCC7710 CRISPR3 (type II) system into E. coli and
    compared rnc+ and rnc- hosts:
    PMID:23535272. The result was conditional:
    PMID:23535272
    UniProt records both halves of this as experimental findings of the entry: complementing
    the pre-crRNA processing defect of an S. pyogenes rnc deletion, and loss of
    plasmid immunity in E. coli HT115 [file:ECOLI/rnc/rnc-uniprot.txt].
  3. E. coli K-12's own CRISPR system does not use RNase III. E. coli K-12 has a type
    I-E system, in which the repeat cleavage is done by the Cas6e/CasE subunit of Cascade,
    not by a host RNase: PMID:23535272 UniProt makes the same point about the complementation experiment —
    the E. coli strain used "does not have the corresponding CRISPR locus"
    [file:ECOLI/rnc/rnc-uniprot.txt].

Conclusion: no CRISPR process term on P0A7Y0. Point 2 is a demonstration of
capability in an engineered context, not of a role in E. coli K-12 biology. A
GO:0099048 or crRNA-processing annotation on this protein would assert that E. coli
RNase III participates in CRISPR-Cas immunity in E. coli, which point 3 contradicts: the
native system routes that step through Cas6e. GOA carries no CRISPR annotation for rnc
and should not — this is a curator decision to respect, not a gap. The capability is
recorded here, in suggested_questions, and in the review.reason of the
ribonuclease III activity rows, which is where scoped prose belongs.

Note also that this is not a case of adding an annotation because "every other
participant has it": the other participants in type II maturation are Cas9/Csn1 and the
tracrRNA, and the relevant comparator for the host enzyme is the S. pyogenes RNase III,
which is the protein the S. pyogenes result is about.
