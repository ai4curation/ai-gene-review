Gene Description

LptB (formerly YhbG) is the nucleotide-binding domain (NBD) subunit of the LptB2FG ABC transporter of the Escherichia coli inner membrane. Two LptB copies form a homodimeric ATPase that sits on the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane, docked onto the coupling helices of the transmembrane subunits LptF and LptG; the bitopic protein LptC associates with LptB2FG to form the LptB2FGC inner membrane complex. ATP binding and hydrolysis by LptB drive conformational changes in LptFG that extract newly synthesized lipopolysaccharide (LPS) from the outer leaflet of the inner membrane (after MsbA has flipped it) and push it into the periplasmic LptC-LptA-LptD protein bridge, which delivers LPS to the LptDE translocon for insertion into the outer leaflet of the outer membrane. LptB is essential; its depletion or catalytic inactivation (E163Q) blocks LPS transport and outer membrane biogenesis. Unlike classical ABC transporters, LptB2FG does not move its substrate across the bilayer but extrudes it out of the membrane. Bridge assembly and LPS occupancy couple displacement of the LptC transmembrane helix to increased ATP binding and hydrolysis by the inner-membrane motor.

Core Functions

ATPase (nucleotide-binding domain) subunit of the LptB2FG(C) ABC transporter; an LptB homodimer on the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane binds and hydrolyzes ATP and, through the LptF/LptG coupling helices bound in its groove, powers extraction of LPS from the outer leaflet of the inner membrane into the periplasmic Lpt bridge.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Should the LptB2FG complex be given a transporter-class molecular function? GO:0140359 ABC-type transporter activity and GO:0042626 are defined as transport across a membrane, GO:0015437 lipopolysaccharide floppase activity describes the MsbA step, and GO:0140332 lipopolysaccharide transfer activity is currently applied to the LPS-binding Lpt pocket proteins. Is a new term for ATP-driven extraction of a lipid from a membrane leaflet (an ABC-type lipid extractase) needed, to which LptB would contribute?

Q: How do native bridge turnover and LPS concentration tune the LptB ATPase cycle in vivo beyond the bridge-dependent LptC-helix displacement established in PMID42844470?
