Gene Description

CcmH is an inner-membrane component of cytochrome c maturation system I in Escherichia coli. The 350-residue precursor carries an N-terminal periplasmic CcmH/CycL/NrfF domain with a conserved CXXC (RCPKC) thiol motif, two transmembrane segments, and a C-terminal periplasmic tetratricopeptide-repeat domain homologous to the separate CcmI/CycH protein of other Gram-negative bacteria; in E. coli only the N-terminal redox domain is required for cytochrome c maturation. Both cysteines of the CXXC motif are needed for holocytochrome c formation, and their loss is bypassed by an exogenous thiol, consistent with CcmH keeping the apocytochrome CXXCH heme-binding site reduced through thiol-disulfide exchange. CcmH associates with the heme-binding membrane protein CcmF, and the purified CcmF/CcmH complex forms the holocytochrome c synthase (heme lyase) that receives heme from holo-CcmE and covalently attaches it to apocytochromes c in the periplasm. Loss of ccmH abolishes formation of all c-type cytochromes.

Core Functions

Redox-active subunit of the CcmF/CcmH holocytochrome c synthase at the periplasmic face of the inner membrane. Its N-terminal CXXC domain keeps the apocytochrome CXXCH heme-binding site reduced through thiol-disulfide exchange (with CcmG as partner in the redox relay), and in complex with CcmF it contributes to stereospecific covalent attachment of heme to apocytochromes c.
