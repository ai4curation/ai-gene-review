Gene Description

CheW is a small (167 aa) soluble cytoplasmic coupling protein of the Escherichia coli chemotaxis system, composed of a single CheW-like domain of two SH3-like beta-barrel subdomains, homologous to the P5 domain of the histidine kinase CheA. CheW binds directly to the cytoplasmic hairpin tips of methyl-accepting chemoreceptors (Tar, Tsr, Trg, Tap, Aer) and to the P5 domain of CheA, physically linking the kinase to the receptors. Receptor trimers of dimers, CheW and CheA form ternary core signaling units (two receptor trimers, two CheW and one CheA dimer) that network into hexagonal arrays at the cell poles; CheW binding promotes receptor clustering and CheA recruitment. Within these arrays CheW is required for receptor-mediated activation and stimulus control of CheA autophosphorylation and thus of phosphotransfer to CheY and CheB, and cheW mutants swim smoothly and are non-chemotactic.

Core Functions

Molecular adaptor that physically bridges chemoreceptor cytoplasmic tips and the CheA histidine kinase, assembling the receptor-CheW-CheA core signaling unit and placing CheA autophosphorylation under receptor control. Binding to receptor and to CheA are each essential for active ternary complexes and for chemotaxis.

description: CheW is a small (167 aa) soluble cytoplasmic coupling protein of the Escherichia coli
  chemotaxis system, composed of a single CheW-like domain of two SH3-like beta-barrel subdomains,
  homologous to the P5 domain of the histidine kinase CheA. CheW binds directly to the cytoplasmic
  hairpin tips of methyl-accepting chemoreceptors (Tar, Tsr, Trg, Tap, Aer) and to the P5 domain of
  CheA, physically linking the kinase to the receptors. Receptor trimers of dimers, CheW and CheA
  form ternary core signaling units (two receptor trimers, two CheW and one CheA dimer) that network
  into hexagonal arrays at the cell poles; CheW binding promotes receptor clustering and CheA
  recruitment. Within these arrays CheW is required for receptor-mediated activation and stimulus
  control of CheA autophosphorylation and thus of phosphotransfer to CheY and CheB, and cheW mutants
  swim smoothly and are non-chemotactic.
