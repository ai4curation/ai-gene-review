Gene Description

MurQ is the N-acetylmuramic acid 6-phosphate (MurNAc 6-phosphate) etherase of E. coli (EC 4.2.1.126), a cytoplasmic homodimeric enzyme with a SIS-type sugar-phosphate binding fold related to the N-terminal domain of glucokinase regulatory protein. It cleaves the D-lactyl ether substituent at C3 of MurNAc 6-phosphate, giving N-acetylglucosamine 6-phosphate (GlcNAc 6-phosphate) and D-lactate, by syn elimination of lactate to an unsaturated aldehyde intermediate followed by addition of water; Glu83 and Glu114 are essential catalytic residues. MurQ is the only MurNAc 6-phosphate etherase in E. coli and is the point where two pathways merge: catabolism of external MurNAc taken up and phosphorylated by the PTS transporter MurP, and recycling of 1,6-anhydro-MurNAc released from the cell's own peptidoglycan and phosphorylated by the kinase AnmK. The GlcNAc 6-phosphate produced feeds the nag pathway to fructose 6-phosphate or amino sugar reuse. murQ is the first gene of the murQP operon, repressed by MurR and induced by MurNAc 6-phosphate, with additional activation by cAMP-CAP; low basal expression suffices for recycling, whereas growth on MurNAc as sole carbon source requires induction.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

N-acetylmuramic acid 6-phosphate etherase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: N-acetyl-D-muramate 6-phosphate + H2O = N-acetyl-D-glucosamine 6-phosphate + (R)-lactate.

Justification: MurQ (EC 4.2.1.126, RHEA:26410) is a well-characterized enzyme of bacterial peptidoglycan recycling and MurNAc catabolism, found in many bacteria (HAMAP MF_00068, NCBIfam TIGR00274), but GO has no term for it; annotations currently fall back to ether hydrolase activity or carbon-oxygen lyase activity. EC places the reaction under hydro-lyases, but the mechanism is elimination of lactate followed by hydration rather than release of water, so carbon-oxygen lyase activity (GO:0016835) is proposed as the parent.

Parent term: carbon-oxygen lyase activity

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:15983044 : Purified MurQ-His6 fusion protein and extracts of cells expressing murQ both catalyze the cleavage of MurNAc 6-phosphate, with GlcNAc 6-phosphate and D-lactate being the primary products.
  • file:ECOLI/murQ/murQ-uniprot.txt : Reaction=N-acetyl-D-muramate 6-phosphate + H2O = N-acetyl-D-glucosamine 6-phosphate + (R)-lactate

murQ (yfeU, b2428, P76535) notes

2026-10-31 — initial review

No deep-research file. Sources: UniProt P76535, PMID:15983044, PMID:16452451,
PMID:18837509, PMID:15901686 (abstracts only in cache), PMID:18723630 (full text).

Activity

  • MurNAc 6-phosphate etherase (EC 4.2.1.126; RHEA:26410): MurNAc-6P + H2O ->
    GlcNAc-6P + D-lactate PMID:15983044.
  • Mechanism: syn elimination of lactate to an alpha,beta-unsaturated aldehyde, then syn
    addition of water; C3-O bond cleaved; Glu83 (proton donor) and Glu114 essential
    PMID:18837509.
    KM 1.2 mM, kcat 5.7 s-1; homodimer (UniProt, from PMID:18837509).
  • Net reaction is hydrolysis of an ether bond; mechanism and EC class are lyase. Both
    existing MF terms (GO:0016803 ether hydrolase, GO:0016835 carbon-oxygen lyase) are
    true but general. GO has no term for EC 4.2.1.126 (checked go.db for EC/RHEA xrefs and
    label "etherase"): NTR proposed.
  • SIS domain (55-218); GCKR-like family, MurNAc-6-P etherase subfamily; homolog of the
    N-terminal domain of human glucokinase regulatory protein.

Pathways

  • Required for growth on MurNAc as sole carbon source; ΔmurQ accumulates MurNAc-6P
    PMID:15983044.
  • Only MurNAc-P etherase in E. coli; with AnmK required for anhMurNAc from the medium or
    own murein PMID:16452451.
  • AnmK phosphorylates anhMurNAc to MurNAc-P, then the etherase gives GlcNAc-P
    PMID:15901686. GlcNAc-6P enters the nag pathway (NagA, NagB) to fructose-6P or is
    reused for UDP-GlcNAc synthesis.
  • Low constitutive murQ expression suffices for recycling; high-level expression (after
    MurR release by MurNAc-6P, plus cAMP-CAP) needed for growth on MurNAc PMID:18723630.

GO term notes

  • GO:0097173 (MurNAc catabolic) and GO:0097175 (anhMurNAc catabolic) are not under
    amino sugar catabolic process (GO:0046348) in GO; they sit under carboxylic acid
    catabolic process. So the amino sugar rows are MODIFIED to these terms rather than
    TOO_GENERAL.
  • No "peptidoglycan recycling" term; GO:0009254 peptidoglycan turnover is the
    existing term.
  • IPI protein binding (PMID:18837509, EcoliWiki): almost certainly the homodimer;
    UniProt SUBUNIT "Homodimer" is attributed to this paper. MODIFY to GO:0042803.
