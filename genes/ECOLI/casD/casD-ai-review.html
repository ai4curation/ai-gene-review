Gene Description

CasD (also called Cas5e) is the single Cas5 subunit of Cascade, the 405-kDa CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex of the Escherichia coli K-12 type I-E CRISPR-Cas system, and it caps the 5' end of the guide at the tail of the complex. Its fold is a right-handed fist built on a modified RNA recognition motif, with a 50-residue insertion forming a thumb that arches over the top. CasD recognises the repeat-derived 8-nucleotide 5' handle of the mature crRNA sequence-specifically, threading the last seven nucleotides along the arch of its thumb into base-specific pockets on a glycine-rich helix, and flipping the terminal base out of the helical display in the same way a CasC thumb does. Three structural consequences follow. Cas7 oligomerisation stops at the 5' end of the guide rather than running off it, the finger domain of the adjacent CasC subunit is rotated to open a channel wide enough for duplex DNA, and the arch creates a pore that the CasA L1 helix plugs into, so CasD is the platform on which CasA is recruited to the tail. CasD thumb residues also make van der Waals contacts with the first, PAM-proximal segment of the crRNA-DNA hybrid, the seed region where complementarity matters most. CasD is required for resistance to bacteriophage lambda and for plasmid silencing, and its loss lowers mature crRNA levels, but it is not the subunit that cleaves pre-crRNA.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex

Definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction. The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent is the Csy complex.

Justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic ancestor GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and its subunits are the direct targets of a whole family of phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible.

Parent term: ribonucleoprotein complex

Core Functions

Recognises the repeat-derived 5' handle of the mature crRNA sequence-specifically, seating its terminal bases in dedicated pockets on a glycine-rich helix and threading the handle along the arch of its thumb. This is the anchor that defines the 5' boundary of the assembled complex and the molecular signal that initiates assembly of the tail.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: GO has no molecular-function term for crRNA-guided target recognition, so the subunit that anchors the guide and stabilises the seed segment of the hybrid can only carry generic RNA binding and DNA/RNA hybrid binding terms. Should a guide-RNA-dependent nucleic acid recognition term be requested?

Q: Is GO:0043571 maintenance of CRISPR repeat elements the right process term for a Cas5 protein that binds the mature 5' handle but does not cleave the array? Its definition stretches from spacer acquisition to array transcription and processing, and it is applied to Cas1, Cas2, Cas3, Cas5, Cas6, Cas7 and Cas9 alike, which makes it hard to use as a statement about any one subunit.

Q: Why do mature crRNA levels fall when casD is disrupted, given that CasE alone is sufficient for cleavage? Is CasD required to release or stabilise the cleaved product, or is the effect a consequence of the casD-casE gene overlap?

Suggested experts: Stan J J Brouns, John van der Oost

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Express pre-crRNA with defined subsets of Cascade subunits in a cas-free host and measure, in parallel, cleavage of pre-crRNA (northern blot for precursor and product) and the half-life of the mature product by pulse-chase, for CasE alone, CasCE, CasCDE and full Cascade. Separating a cleavage defect from a stability defect would settle why casD disruption lowers crRNA.

Hypothesis: CasD stabilises mature crRNA after cleavage rather than contributing to cleavage itself.

Type: reconstitution and RNA stability measurement

Experiment: Substitute the residues lining the -8A, -7U and -6A pockets on the glycine-rich helix, and the thumb residue Arg108 that hydrogen-bonds C7, and test whether the resulting complexes can be loaded with a chimeric guide carrying a non-cognate 5' handle; read out complex assembly, target dsDNA affinity, and phage lambda resistance.

Hypothesis: The CasD base-specific pockets for the 5'-handle nucleotides enforce loading of repeat-derived guides only.

Type: structure-guided mutagenesis and guide-swap assay

Experiment: Mutate CasD Tyr85 and Gln105 and measure dissociation constants for targets mismatched at individual seed positions (1 to 5) against a fully complementary control, together with Cas3-dependent degradation and the rate of primed spacer acquisition from mismatched targets.

Hypothesis: CasD thumb contacts with the seed segment set the fidelity threshold for target discrimination.

Type: quantitative binding and interference assays

Session 1 (2026-10-01): initial full review

Reviewed together with casA, casB, casC and casE as one complex.

Position and role

CasD is the single Cas5 subunit, and it caps the 5' end of the guide at the tail of the
complex
PMID:25123481,
where it is one of three proteins sandwiching that end
PMID:25103409.
Its fold is a modified RRM "fist" with a thumb that arches over the top, and it performs
three distinguishable jobs at once
PMID:25103409:
it reads the repeat-derived 5' handle, it terminates the CasC filament, and it provides
the docking platform for CasA.

Nucleic-acid binding

Unlike CasC, CasD reads sequence
PMID:25103409,
with the 5' handle threaded along the thumb arch
PMID:25103409,
and specific hydrogen bonds to individual bases
PMID:25123481.
So the two GOA RNA binding IDA rows are solid and are, if anything, under-specific.

CasD also carries a GO:0071667 DNA/RNA hybrid binding IDA row. This is directly
supported by the target-bound structure
PMID:25123481.
Note the significance: positions 1-5 are the PAM-proximal "seed", the segment where
complementarity matters most, so CasD's hybrid contact is at the point where target
recognition is initiated.

The structural-subunit problem

CasD's single GO:0005515 protein binding IDA row is about intra-complex contacts with
CasA and CasC (UniProt Q46898 SUBUNIT: "Interacts directly with CasA and CasC"). As for
casA, casB, casC and casE, I MODIFY it to GO:0005198 structural molecule activity, which
is exactly what the structure supports — CasD terminates the filament, remodels the
adjacent Cas7.6 finger domain, and creates the pore that CasA's L1 helix plugs into
PMID:25103409.
GO has no child of GO:0005198 for a non-ribosomal ribonucleoprotein, so the parent is
the correct level, and the same term is used for all five subunits.

The GO:0043571 IEA row — kept, but non-core

GOA carries involved_in GO:0043571 maintenance of CRISPR repeat elements as an IEA from
InterPro:IPR021124 (CRISPR-associated protein, Cas5). GO:0043571's definition is broad
and does include processing: "transcription of the CRISPR repeat arrays into RNA and
processing".

Comparator check. Querying QuickGO for non-IEA annotations to GO:0043571 returns 14 rows,
and they include UniProtKB:D4GQN7 cas5 IMP PMID:24459147 — a Cas5 protein with
experimental support for this term — alongside Cas1, Cas2, Cas3, Cas6f, Csy3 and Cas9.
So the term is used for Cas5-family proteins by convention, and the IEA is not an
out-of-family mis-mapping.

But for E. coli CasD the direct evidence argues it is not core. Brouns first saw crRNA
lost in the casD knockout
PMID:18703739,
explicitly flagging the overlap of casD and casE as a confound, and the reconstitution in
a cas-free host settled it
PMID:18703739,
PMID:18703739.
CasD therefore is not required for pre-crRNA cleavage; it binds and stabilises the
already-cleaved 5' handle. UniProt records the consequence as "Decreased levels of
crRNA" rather than failure of cleavage. Hence KEEP_AS_NON_CORE: the term is within
family convention and not wrong, but CasD's core job is interference, not array
maintenance.

By the same logic I do not propose GO:0043571 as a new annotation for CasD, and I do
propose it for CasE, which does the cleaving.

Redundant IEA rows

GO:0003723 RNA binding IEA (IPR010147, CRISPR-associated protein CasD) and
GO:0051607 defense response to virus IEA (same signature) are both duplicated by
experimental rows on the same protein. They are correct and consistent with the direct
evidence, so I ACCEPT them rather than calling them redundant; an IEA that agrees with
an IDA is corroboration, not noise.

Same as the other four subunits: Cascade is demonstrably a ribonucleoprotein
PMID:25103409,
PMID:23079036,
so GO:0032991 is MODIFYed to GO:1990904 ribonucleoprotein complex and the missing

terms, all biological_process; there is no CC term, though ComplexPortal models the

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex
  proposed_definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas)
    proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA
    as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I
    systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an
    R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction.
    The Escherichia coli type I-E complex, Cascade, has the composition
    Cas8e(1)/Cse2(2)/Cas7(6)/Cas5(1)/Cas6e(1) with one crRNA; the type I-F equivalent
    is the Csy complex.
