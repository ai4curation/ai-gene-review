Gene Description

TrmA is an Escherichia coli K-12 RNA methyltransferase that uses SAM to form m5U54 in tRNA and m5U341 in the tRNA-like domain of tmRNA. It also promotes tRNA folding independently of methyl-group transfer through its RNA-binding domain. TrmA forms stable covalent complexes with undermodified tRNAs and with a 3-prime fragment of 16S rRNA; this latter association does not establish rRNA methylation or stable ribosomal membership. Cytosolic localization is supported by independently curated fractionation/proteomic experiments. The physiological consequences of catalysis and RNA-chaperone function depend on growth conditions and are not interchangeable with an absolute essentiality claim.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

tmRNA (uracil-C5)-methyltransferase activity, S-adenosyl methionine-dependent

Definition: Catalysis of transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosyl-L-methionine to carbon 5 of a uridine residue in the tRNA-like domain of transfer-messenger RNA (tmRNA), producing 5-methyluridine and S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine.

Justification: The existing tRNA U54 term excludes tmRNA, while the general RNA methyltransferase term loses the established base, methylation position and donor specificity. PMID:23603891 assigns tmRNA m5U formation to the characterized SAM-dependent enzyme TrmA. The proposed name avoids imposing E. coli residue numbering on all tmRNAs. This formalizes an already-supported substrate activity; no new gene-annotation row is proposed. Purified tRNA-enzyme evidence in PMID:6997293 establishes the SAM-dependent TrmA chemistry; the tmRNA deletion experiment does not independently measure donor dependence.

Parent term: RNA methyltransferase activity

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:23603891 : The evidence comes from the lack of m (5)U in purified tmRNA or TLD variants recovered from an Escherichia coli mutant strain deleted of the trmA gene.
  • PMID:6997293 : The tRNA-modifying enzyme, S-adenosylmethionine: tRNA (uridine-5)-methyltransferase, has been purified essentially to homogeneity from an Escherichia coli strain containing an elevated level of this enzyme.
