Which Brugia malayi beta-tubulin isotype is the benzimidazole-engaged target?

Target gene: bma-ben-1, UniProt A0A1U7F2B2, WormBase ParaSite locus Bm4733, RefSeq XP_001896615.1, 448 aa, Brugia malayi (NCBITaxon:6279). Hypothesis slug: kgap-bmaben1-isotype-target. Source: genes/BRUMA/bma-ben-1/bma-ben-1-ai-review.yaml, free-text selector. Verdict: Unresolved, with bma-ben-1 weakly to moderately favoured.

Summary

Four lines of analysis were run against the seed hypothesis, and they do not converge on a single gene. Brugia malayi encodes exactly four beta-tubulins. On pairwise identity, bma-ben-1 (Bm4733) is the Brugia isotype closest to C. elegans BEN-1 at 93.69%, ahead of Bm12766 (89.86%), Bm8661 (88.69%) and Bm9698 (87.61%), a margin of 3.83 percentage points, and it is the reciprocal best hit. The other three map cleanly onto different C. elegans paralogs: Bm12766 to TBB-4 (95.72%), Bm8661 to MEC-7 (92.74%), and Bm9698 to nothing above 87.61% despite its WormBase name Bma-tbb-6 (it is only 55.3% identical to C. elegans TBB-6). Comparative genomics gives the one definite answer the seed asked for: every filarial beta-tubulin that has actually been used in benzimidazole-resistance genotyping is the orthologue of this locus. The B. malayi reference AY705382 from PMID: 16103581 is 99.8% identical to bma-ben-1 and 85.5 to 87.9% to the other three, and the O. volvulus and D. immitis cDNAs from PMID: 9576511 are 98.4% identical to it.

The pharmacology leg returns a clean negative, then a partial recovery. All four Brugia isotypes carry F167, E198 and F200, the three canonical resistance codons, so those positions discriminate nothing. The same alignment separates C. elegans TBB-1 and TBB-2 (Y200), MEC-7 (S198) and human TUBB (Y200) from BEN-1, so the method has power where a difference exists. Widening the inventory from three codons to 27 colchicine-site pocket positions cuts the candidate set from four to two: bma-ben-1 and Bm9698 match H. contortus isotype-1 at every one of the 27, while Bm12766 carries S165N plus M316I and Bm8661 carries S165T plus M316I. Pocket sequence therefore narrows the field but cannot separate bma-ben-1 from Bm9698.

Expression excludes nobody, and phylogeny declines to confirm the orthology the gene symbol implies. All four loci are transcribed (8-mer assignment of 70 NCBI tubulin records put 4, 2, 2 and 2 records on them) and all four UniProt entries are PE=3; the only PE=2 B. malayi beta-tubulin is Q66T51, which is the bma-ben-1 locus itself. EBI Expression Atlas holds 0 Brugia malayi experiments and WormBase ParaSite REST timed out on three attempts, so no isotype-resolved stage matrix was obtained, and this is stated as a retrieval failure rather than a biological negative. Both UPGMA and neighbour joining fail to nest bma-ben-1 inside the ben-1 clade, and the NJ tree additionally places human TUBB inside a nematode paralog clade, which is provably wrong. The 93.69% figure is a distance ranking, not a resolved 1:1 orthology. The seed hypothesis is confirmed in its central claim: the target isotype is unresolved, and no benzimidazole or drug-response GO term should be attached to this protein.

Executive judgment

Unresolved. The evidence favours A0A1U7F2B2 (bma-ben-1, Bm4733) as the benzimidazole-engaged beta-tubulin of B. malayi, with weak to moderate strength. It is the only isotype that sits in the intersection of the two informative evidence sets: the identity ranking against C. elegans BEN-1, and the benzimidazole pocket inventory. The favouring rests on orthology and reagent provenance, not on any functional test in B. malayi.

Reasoning, leg by leg:

  • Inventory and identity ranking give a clear answer. Four genes, one ranking, 3.83 pp margin, reciprocal best hit, and three of the four assigned to other paralog families.
  • The three canonical resistance codons give no answer. F167, E198 and F200 are identical in all four Brugia isotypes. Sequence at those positions cannot discriminate them.
  • The wider drug pocket gives a partial answer. 27 positions split the four into two pairs. bma-ben-1 and Bm9698 are indistinguishable from each other and from the drug-sensitive H. contortus isotype-1 reference.
  • Expression gives no answer. All four loci are transcribed, all four are PE=3, and the quantitative resources that could separate them were not retrievable.
  • Comparative genomics gives a definite answer. Every filarial benzimidazole-genotyping reference sequence maps to this locus and to no other.

The three most important caveats. First, no functional test exists: no B. malayi knockout, RNAi or allele swap has tested any isotype against flubendazole or oxfendazole, so the step from "closest to BEN-1" to "is the drug target" is inference by analogy. Second, the orthology is a distance statement, not a phylogenetic result: two tree methods both fail at the deep nodes, and NJ produces a demonstrable artifact. Third, the provenance evidence may be circular: bma-ben-1 is the only locus with an independently cloned cDNA and the only one to which field "isotype 1" amplicons map, but the primers behind those amplicons were designed from the H. contortus isotype-1 orthologue in the first place, so the apparent selectivity may reflect primer design rather than biology.

Bm9698 (A0A0J9XTX5) is the standing alternative and is not excluded. It has a perfect benzimidazole pocket, is transcribed, and its only disqualification is that it ranks last on identity to BEN-1. Its long branch in the NJ tree (0.0924, three to nine times the other terminal branches) makes its phylogenetic position the least reliably placed of the four, so arguments that exclude it on tree grounds are weak.

F002 The canonical resistance codons cannot discriminate the Brugia isotypes

Each isotype was aligned globally to H. contortus isotype-1 beta-tubulin Q25022 (GenBank M76491), used as the numbering reference and independently verified to carry F167, E198 and F200 at those exact indices in its own 448-residue sequence. Every one of the four Brugia proteins carries F167, E198 and F200, and in each the alignment-mapped positions are also positions 167, 198 and 200 in native numbering, so the assignment does not rest on an indel model. Q134 and M257 are likewise invariant across all four. The only variation among the Brugia isotypes at the positions examined is at 165 (S in bma-ben-1 and Bm9698, N in Bm12766, T in Bm8661), which is not among the established resistance codons.

The positive control establishes that the method has power. In the same alignment, C. elegans TBB-1 (O17921) and TBB-2 (P52275) carry Y200, MEC-7 (P12456) carries S198, TBB-6 (G5EF01) carries C167 and T257, and human TUBB (P07437) carries Y200, while BEN-1 (Q18817), TBB-4 (P41937) and H. contortus isotype-1 and isotype-2 carry F167/E198/F200. The other filarial sequences (O61358, O61357, LOAG_00602) are also F167/E198/F200.

This reproduces independently the 1998 prediction of Geary and colleagues: "All 4 filarial beta-tubulins are predicted to encode phe at this position, suggesting that filarial beta-tubulin is not inherently insensitive to the benzimidazoles" (PMID: 9576511).

F003 The filarial gene genotyped for benzimidazole resistance is the orthologue of this locus

UniProt Q66T51 is a B. malayi beta-tubulin (GenBank AY705382) whose sole cited reference is Schwab, Boakye, Kyelem and Prichard 2005, the study that developed F167Y and F200Y assays for Wuchereria bancrofti (PMID: 16103581: "We have developed assays capable of detecting these single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in Wuchereria bancrofti, and have applied them to microfilaria obtained from patients in Ghana and Burkina Faso."). Global alignment gives Q66T51 99.8% identity to bma-ben-1, against 87.9%, 87.8% and 85.5% to the other three isotypes. The reference sequence behind that genotyping work is the bma-ben-1 locus.

The O. volvulus and D. immitis cDNAs O61358 and O61357 from PMID: 9576511, which also form the backbone for O. volvulus beta-tubulin SNP genotyping under ivermectin pressure (PMID: 16197589, PMID: 22677339: "Four single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) occurring in the β-tubulin gene of these parasites were investigated. We found changes in genotype frequencies in O. volvulus β-tubulin gene associated with IVM treatments."), are 98.4% identical to bma-ben-1 and only 85.9 to 89.2% to the other Brugia isotypes.

Independent 1991 corroboration comes from Guenette and colleagues, who reported for the single-copy B. pahangi beta-tubulin gene: "The predicted beta-tubulin amino acid sequence is 89%, 94%, 90% and 88% identical to the chicken beta 2, and the Caenorhabditis elegans ben-1, tub-1 and mec-7 gene products, respectively" (PMID: 2052017). That ranking (94 / 90 / 88 to ben-1 / tub-1 / mec-7) reproduces the one computed here for bma-ben-1 (93.7 / 91.3 / 88.0 to BEN-1 / TBB-1 / MEC-7).

This is circumstantial. Those studies genotyped the gene they had cloned, so the choice reflects historical cloning and primer design, not a demonstration that this isotype carries the drug response.

F004 Expression excludes no isotype, and only bma-ben-1 has independent transcript-level evidence

All four reference-proteome beta-tubulins are PE=3 (inferred from homology). The only B. malayi beta-tubulin at PE=2 (evidence at transcript level) is Q66T51, the independently cloned cDNA AY705382, which is the bma-ben-1 locus.

Isotype assignment of 70 NCBI "Brugia malayi tubulin" nucleotide and EST records by six-frame translation and shared 8-mer set overlap (K=8, requiring a top score of at least 8 shared 8-mers and reporting the margin over the runner-up) assigned 10 records, all with margin at least 20: bma-ben-1 4, Bm9698 2, Bm8661 2, Bm12766 2. All four loci therefore have transcript or genomic support and none is silent. The three field-isolate records annotated "Brugia malayi beta-tubulin isotype 1 (tub-1)" (MT674273.1, MT674274.1, MT674275.1) and the partial gene AJ551180.1 all assign to bma-ben-1 (margins 20, 20, 20 and 37).

Resource availability, stated plainly. EBI Expression Atlas holds 0 Brugia malayi experiments (verified programmatically; a later repeat call returned a parse error). WormBase ParaSite REST timed out on three separate attempts and returned no isotype-resolved expression table. No stage-resolved or tissue-resolved per-isotype expression matrix was obtained. Published datasets that would answer this were not queried programmatically: the Choi 2011 lifecycle transcriptome (PMID: 22180794), the Chung 2019 multispecies life-cycle RNA-seq ("we conducted multispecies transcriptome sequencing (RNA-Seq) on the filarial nematode Brugia malayi, its Wolbachia endosymbiont wBm, and its laboratory vector Aedes aegypti across the entire B. malayi life cycle", PMID: 31796568) and the O'Neill 2016 flubendazole RNA-seq on adult female B. malayi (PMID: 27733308).

The practical curation point concerns the gene symbol. bma-ben-1 carries an implication the evidence does not license. The protein is the Brugia beta-tubulin most similar to C. elegans BEN-1 and the 1:1 orthologue of the O. volvulus and D. immitis genes used in filarial benzimidazole work. It is not demonstrably the 1:1 orthologue of C. elegans ben-1, because neither UPGMA nor neighbour joining nests it inside that clade. Record the clade-level statement, and do not let the symbol stand in for a drug-response function.

A second symbol problem sits on a different gene. A0A0J9XTX5 is named Bma-tbb-6 but is only 55.3% identical to C. elegans TBB-6 and has no C. elegans match above 87.61%. That name should be flagged or corrected, because any downstream argument that routes through it is unreliable.

Mechanistic scope

The immediate molecular function being tested is direct binding of a benzimidazole to the colchicine-adjacent site of a specific beta-tubulin isotype, and the consequent block on microtubule polymerisation. Everything downstream of that is a separate layer of evidence.

Benzimidazole resistance in parasitic nematodes maps to three codons in the predominant drug-engaged beta-tubulin: F167Y, E198A/L/G and F200Y in H. contortus isotype-1 numbering. Those substitutions are reported as modifying the beta-tubulin so as to prevent drug binding (PMID: 42302569, review-level). In C. elegans the same role falls to BEN-1, where loss of function gives resistance because the remaining isotypes are drug-insensitive enough to support viability (PMID: 2592410, and confirmed again in competitive fitness and development assays by PMID: 38370666).

For B. malayi the mechanistic claim that survives is narrow. The organism encodes four beta-tubulins. All four carry the benzimidazole-susceptible residues at 167, 198 and 200, and two of the four reproduce the H. contortus isotype-1 drug pocket at all 27 positions examined. All four are transcribed. The drug therefore has at least two, and plausibly four, chemically competent binding partners in the worm, and the question of which carries the phenotype becomes a question about isotype abundance and functional redundancy in the drug-sensitive tissue. That reading is reinforced by the Ascaris docking work, which predicted benzimidazole binding to all seven Ascaris isotypes and concluded that selectivity "is likely the result of isotype expression levels affecting the frequency of interaction" (PMID: 35961997).

The drug-sensitive tissue is better localised than the isotype. Flubendazole exposure of adult female B. malayi produces morphological damage prominently in developing embryos with a decrease in microfilarial output (PMID: 27145083), and the transcriptional response is dominated by genes involved in embryo development and cuticle components (PMID: 27733308). That narrows the relevant tissue to the female reproductive tract and the embryos within it, which is where an isotype-resolved abundance measurement would be most informative.

Separating the layers:

Mechanistic model and interpretation

The four evidence legs can be read as a single filter that starts with four candidates and ends with one favoured gene and one unexcluded alternative.

The structure of the answer matters for curation. Two of the four legs are genuinely informative and two are not. The informative legs, identity ranking and filarial provenance, both point at bma-ben-1 and are partly correlated with each other, since the filarial reference sequences were chosen because they were the ben-1-like gene people had cloned. The uninformative legs, resistance-codon sequence and expression, are uninformative for different reasons: the codons are genuinely identical, whereas the expression data simply could not be retrieved. Treating the second as a biological negative would be an error.

The pocket result is the only new discrimination the sequence can supply. It removes Bm12766 and Bm8661 from contention on grounds of two substitutions each, and it leaves bma-ben-1 and Bm9698 tied. Bm9698 then survives only because expression cannot exclude it, and it is disfavoured only by the identity ranking, which the tree analyses show is a distance measurement and not a resolved phylogeny. That is the precise shape of the remaining uncertainty.

Against this sits a direct warning from the Caenorhabditis comparative work: "prediction of variants in beta-tubulin genes alone is not sufficient to predict resistance, especially across diverse Caenorhabditis species", with C. tropicalis showing no resistance in any strain carrying beta-tubulin variants (PMID: 42247472). If sequence does not predict phenotype reliably even between C. elegans and C. briggsae, the inference from C. elegans ben-1 across to a clade-III filarial nematode is weaker still.

Conflicts and alternatives

The two informative legs do not independently converge. Orthology singles out bma-ben-1 at a 3.83 pp margin. The pocket inventory admits bma-ben-1 and Bm9698 equally, both at zero mismatches. The intersection is bma-ben-1, which is why the verdict leans that way, but the two lines do not separately arrive at the same gene.

Bm9698 (A0A0J9XTX5) is the standing alternative. Perfect drug pocket, transcribed, and disfavoured only by the identity ranking. Its NJ branch length of 0.0924 makes it the least reliably placed of the four, so phylogenetic arguments against it are the weakest available.

The clade assignment is softer than the pairwise ranking, and two methods say so. UPGMA places the filarial beta-tubulins beside the ben-1 / isotype-1 / tbb-1 / tbb-2 clade. NJ groups C. elegans BEN-1 with H. contortus isotype-1 to the exclusion of bma-ben-1, while nesting human TUBB inside a nematode paralog clade. A defensible alternative reading is that the filarial sequences descend from a single pre-duplication ancestor and no Brugia gene is the strict orthologue of C. elegans ben-1.

Database name carry-over. Bma-tbb-6 on A0A0J9XTX5 is unsupported at 55.3% identity to C. elegans TBB-6. This is a concrete instance of paralog mislabelling in the source database for this very gene family in this very organism.

The provenance evidence may be circular. bma-ben-1 is the only locus with an independently cloned cDNA and the only one to which field "isotype 1" amplicons map. That could reflect biological prominence, or it could reflect primers designed from the H. contortus isotype-1 orthologue, which would make the selectivity an artifact of reagent history.

Docking work predicts the opposite of sequence-based selectivity. The Ascaris study found benzimidazoles predicted to bind all seven isotypes and attributed selectivity to expression level (PMID: 35961997). On that model, the identity ranking is the wrong instrument entirely, and the answer lies in the abundance measurement that could not be retrieved here.

Genotype and phenotype dissociate in the field. In Argentine cattle nematodes, Cooperia punctata survivors of benzimidazole treatment "frequently carried predominantly susceptible alleles, revealing a marked discordance between genotype and phenotype" (PMID: 42547829). Beta-tubulin genotype is not a complete account of benzimidazole response even in species where the resistance codons do vary.

Target-independent explanations are not excluded. Effects of benzimidazoles on Wolbachia or on host cells were not addressed here.

Method-level limitations. The alignments used a from-scratch Needleman-Wunsch implementation (BLOSUM62, gap open -11, gap extend -1), verified against a hand-checked 9/10 control that returned 90.0%. All distances are global pairwise identities with no model correction, which is adequate for ranking but not for deep phylogeny. The 27 pocket positions were taken from the colchicine-site contact set used in nematode benzimidazole docking work and were not derived from a Brugia structure, since none exists. The 8-mer transcript assignment is a provenance proxy: 60 of 70 records fell below the assignment threshold or were genomic scaffolds, and record counts are not an expression measurement. No figure in this report summarises an analysis that was not executed.

Discriminating tests

In order of decisiveness.

  1. Allele swap in C. elegans. Replace the endogenous ben-1 coding sequence with each of the four B. malayi isotypes in a ben-1 deletion background and score benzimidazole dose response. This tests each Brugia protein's capacity to carry the drug phenotype directly, in a tractable organism. A positive result for bma-ben-1 with negatives for the other three would settle the question. The C. elegans ben-1 deletion is well characterised as fully resistant (PMID: 2592410), and the resistance phenotype has been re-confirmed in competitive fitness and development assays (PMID: 38370666), so the assay background is solid.
  2. Stage-specific and isotype-specific RNAi in adult female B. malayi, scoring microfilarial output and embryogram under sub-lethal flubendazole. The long-term in vitro culture system for adult female B. malayi on lymphatic endothelial feeder cells (PMID: 35671324) and the drug-exposure model of PMID: 27145083 provide the platform. If knocking down one isotype phenocopies or sensitises to the drug and the others do not, that isotype is the target.
  3. Isotype-resolved quantitative expression in adult female reproductive tissue. The cheapest and most likely to be answerable from existing data: remap the Choi 2011, Chung 2019 or O'Neill 2016 reads to the four loci separately and report per-isotype TPM by life-cycle stage. If one isotype supplies the great majority of beta-tubulin in the tissue where flubendazole acts, mass action makes it the dominant drug-engaged species, which is precisely the model PMID: 35961997 proposes.
  4. Recombinant tubulin polymerisation assays with each Brugia isotype against flubendazole and oxfendazole, to test whether the shared F167/E198/F200 really does give equal affinity and whether S165X plus M316I changes anything.
  5. Model-based phylogeny over clade-III and clade-V nematode beta-tubulins with branch support, to establish whether "orthologue of ben-1" is a meaningful designation for this gene at all.

Test 3 is the realistic next step. Test 1 is the decisive one.
