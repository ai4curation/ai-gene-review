BT_4612 (Q89YW9, Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron VPI-5482): can amine supply constrain the substrate?

Hypothesis slug: kgap-bt4612-amine-supply. Single analysis, run over three iterations: amine supply and gene count first, then HMM family membership and the divergence of the single ureohydrolase, then genomic context. Pocket residues were not used to reach any substrate conclusion, as the seed requires.

Summary

The seed asked whether the physiological amine substrate of BT_4612 can be constrained by working through amine supply and the measured behaviour of the pathway in this organism. The answer is that it can be constrained only by exclusion, and the exclusion removes exactly one of the three candidates.

B. thetaiotaomicron VPI-5482 can make agmatine: biosynthetic arginine decarboxylase BT_3394 (Q8A2B1, EC 4.1.1.19, 630 aa) is present. It cannot make 1,3-diaminopropane. A Pfam domain census over all 4,782 entries of reference proteome UP000001414 returns zero members for PF01276, PF03709 and PF03711, so there is no fold-III ornithine decarboxylase and no lysine decarboxylase of the SpeC/SpeF/CadA type anywhere in the genome, and no protein is annotated to the diaminobutyrate aminotransferase (EC 2.6.1.76) or diaminobutyrate decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.86) activities that would supply 1,3-diaminopropane. Carboxynorspermidine is therefore excluded as a physiological product of BT_4612. The same census returns zero for PF01564 (spermidine synthase, SpeE) and PF01536 (S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase, SpeD), so the classical spermidine route is absent at family level and whatever BT_4612 does is this organism's only path to spermidine.

Between the two remaining candidates, carboxyspermidine and carboxyaminopropylagmatine, the amine supply is silent, and the reason is a single protein. The genome contains exactly one arginase/agmatinase/ureohydrolase-superfamily protein, BT_1099 (Q8A8R8, PF00491, 269 aa, annotated only as "Arginase" with no EC assigned), and that one protein is required by either route: as an agmatinase it makes putrescine upstream of BT_4612, giving carboxyspermidine; as an N1-aminopropylagmatine ureohydrolase it acts downstream of BT_4612 on the aminopropylagmatine product, giving carboxyaminopropylagmatine. Its specificity is not recoverable. Unfiltered Smith-Waterman alignment scores BT_1099 at 39 against human arginase ARG1, 38 against B. caldovelox arginase, 48 against E. coli SpeB agmatinase and 96 against T. kodakarensis N1-aminopropylagmatine ureohydrolase, while those four characterised proteins span 172 to 257 against each other. BT_1099's best match to any characterised member falls below the lowest distance the family's own characterised members show. Genomic context does not rescue it either: the four genes of the route sit at loci BT_0674, BT_1099, BT_3394 and BT_4612, maximally dispersed, with no shared operon and no polyamine-related gene within four loci of any of them.

Two results stand independently of the substrate question. BT_4612 has no paralogue: it is the only PF03435, PF16653 and PTHR43796 protein in the proteome, confirmed by an annotation-independent proteome-wide alignment (self-score 2167, best non-self 54). The seed asked for the count before interpretation, and the count is one. The cofactor is NADP, assignable from sequence and from the structure of a 96.7%-identical ortholog: all 33 NADP-contacting residues of PDB 8DEB are identical in BT_4612, the 2'-phosphate is held by S34, R35, T36, K39 and K286, and Ala33 sits at the C-terminal end of beta-2 where an NAD-preferring enzyme would carry an acidic residue.

Executive judgment

Partially supported, with the supported part much narrower than the seed allows for.

The amine-supply approach works, and it is the right approach for this question, but it delivers one exclusion and nothing else. Three separate methods were tried and all three stop at the same place: amine supply excludes 1,3-diaminopropane and therefore carboxynorspermidine; pairwise sequence comparison cannot assign the ureohydrolase that decides between the other two candidates; genomic context is empty because the route's genes are dispersed. The substrate is constrained only by exclusion of an amine the organism cannot synthesise. Saying exactly that and no more is what the evidence permits.

The most important caveat concerns what would resolve it. The closest characterised relative of BT_4612, the B. fragilis enzyme I9VY56 at 96.7% identity, was published as a carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase in 2023 (PMID: 37437886) and reassigned to a carboxyaminopropylagmatine dehydrogenase in 2026 by the same laboratory (PMID: 42754158). At 96.7% identity, orthology transfer would normally carry an activity. Here it carries an activity that has been named two different ways within three years. Carboxyaminopropylagmatine is now the better-supported guess, backed by three independent lines in three organisms, but it remains a guess for BT_4612 because no assay, deletion or fitness measurement of BT_4612 has been published.

A second caveat is procedural. The seed's carried-forward premise that no amine-contacting position in this family is testable is now out of date. PMID: 43093060 reports a 1.19 Å structure with agmatine and NADPH bound and names a conserved Asn-Glu-Phe-Glu motif that coordinates agmatine. That premise was honoured in this run, and no pocket residue was used to reach a substrate conclusion, but a curator should know it has been overtaken.

Key findings

F1. BT_4612 is the only candidate dehydrogenase in the genome

The seed required the count before any interpretation, because a second candidate would change the question. There is no second candidate.

Reference proteome UP000001414 (4,782 entries) was searched two independent ways. The domain census found exactly one protein carrying Pfam PF03435 (saccharopine dehydrogenase NADP-binding domain), exactly one carrying PF16653 (saccharopine dehydrogenase C-terminal domain), and exactly one assigned to PANTHER PTHR43796 and its subfamily PTHR43796:SF2. In all three cases that protein is Q89YW9 / BT_4612, 397 aa. The sequence census, which does not depend on annotation, ranked all 4,782 proteome sequences against Q89YW9 and realigned the top candidates by Smith-Waterman (BLOSUM62, gaps -11/-1): self-score 2167, best non-self score 54 against Q89ZD7, a 2,183-aa protein, which is 2.5% of self and is background for a sequence of that length.

The adjacent slots are also single-copy. The partner decarboxylase is Q8A9Z1 / BT_0674, the only EC 4.1.1.96 protein in the genome. The aspartate-semialdehyde supply is Q8A1M5 / BT_3636, the only asd (EC 1.2.1.11).

Since B. thetaiotaomicron makes spermidine de novo in polyamine-free medium (PMID: 27118128) and has no classical route, the aspartate-semialdehyde-dependent condensation must run through BT_4612. That makes BT_4612 a well-supported participant in spermidine biosynthesis while fixing nothing about which amine it condenses.

F2. Amine supply excludes carboxynorspermidine and nothing further

The iteration-1 census by protein name and EC number found biosynthetic arginine decarboxylase Q8A2B1 / BT_3394 present (EC 4.1.1.19, PF02784/PF17810/PF17944), alongside BT_3636, BT_0674 and a PotABCD-type spermidine/putrescine importer at BT_1288-BT_1291. It found zero hits for ornithine decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.17), diaminobutyrate--2-oxoglutarate aminotransferase (EC 2.6.1.76), diaminobutyrate decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.86), lysine decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.18), agmatine deiminase (EC 3.5.3.12), N-carbamoylputrescine amidase (EC 3.5.1.53), S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.50) and spermidine synthase (EC 2.5.1.16).

A methodological negative came out of the same iteration and is reported because it bounds what the absence calls can rest on. An exact-4-mer prefilter used to speed a proteome-wide Smith-Waterman screen is too insensitive near 30% identity. It failed its positive control, ranking a histidine kinase above the true BT_3636 match for E. coli Asd P0A9Q9, where direct unfiltered alignment of the same pair scores 291 at 28.9% identity. No prefiltered screen is used as evidence of absence anywhere in this report. The absence calls come from HMM membership; the pairwise scores come from unfiltered Smith-Waterman.

F3. The whole substrate question reduces to BT_1099, and sequence cannot read it

BT_1099 is the pivot. If it is an agmatine ureohydrolase of the SpeB type, it converts agmatine to putrescine upstream of BT_4612 and BT_4612's amine substrate is putrescine. If it is an N1-aminopropylagmatine ureohydrolase, it acts downstream on the aminopropylagmatine product and BT_4612's amine substrate is agmatine. One gene, two mutually exclusive readings, and the same gene is required by either route.

BT_1099's best match to any characterised member, 96, falls below the lowest between-subfamily control pair, 172. It sits outside the measured inter-subfamily spread of the family it belongs to.

It is not a decayed gene. Mapping the binuclear Mn(II) cluster ligands (human ARG1 numbering H101, D124, H126, D128, D232, D234) onto BT_1099 transitively through Q5JI38 recovers D105, H107, D109, D190 and D192: five of six ligands conserved. The sixth, the His of the LVLGGDHS motif (B. caldovelox H99, SpeB H126, T. kodakarensis H114 in ILLGGEHS), falls outside every alignment of BT_1099, which carries His only at positions 48, 77 and 85 in its first 100 residues and has no GGDHS or GGEHS motif there. PROSITE pattern PS01053 scores a miss on BT_1099, but it also misses E. coli SpeB, a characterised agmatinase, so the pattern fails on 1 of 3 characterised controls and a miss alone carries little weight.

BT_1099's single best match is to the aminopropylagmatine ureohydrolase rather than to agmatinase or arginase, which points the same way as the 2026 reassignment of the BT_4612 ortholog. At a score of 96 against controls of 172 to 257, that is a directional hint. It must not be used to name a substrate.

F4. Genomic context transmits nothing

Operon structure was the last genome-level way to link BT_1099 to this pathway and to decide whether it acts upstream or downstream of BT_4612. In Thermus thermophilus and other clustered systems these genes travel together, and co-localisation would have carried real information. Here they do not.

F5. The cofactor is NADP, and that claim is separate from the substrate claim

PDB 8DEB is the B. fragilis CL05T12C13 enzyme (UniProt I9VY56, 397 aa) solved with NADP bound (ligand NAP). Global Needleman-Wunsch alignment (BLOSUM62, -11/-1) gives 96.7% identity to BT_4612 over the full length; the B. fragilis NCTC 9343 copy Q5LG37 is 97.0% identical.

In 8DEB chain A, 33 residues lie within 4.0 Å of NADP: I7, G8, A9, G10, G11, V12, G13, S34, R35, T36, K39, V57, D58, A59, D60, V80, A81, L82, P83, Q85, T103, A104, G138, F139, D140, P141, F180, N181, I184, K286, Q293, V340, T344. All 33 are identical in BT_4612 at the same aligned positions. The 2'-phosphate, the group that distinguishes NADP from NAD, is contacted by R35 at 2.03 Å, K39 at 2.21 Å, T36 at 2.49 Å, S34 at 4.04 Å and K286 at 4.47 Å, and all five are identical in BT_4612.

The sequence logic agrees independently. BT_4612 residues 1-40 read MGRVLIIGAGGVGTVVAHKVAQNADVFTDIMIASRTKEKC: a canonical G8-x-G10-G11-x-G13 dinucleotide fingerprint, with Ala33 at the C-terminal end of beta-2 where an NAD-preferring Rossmann enzyme carries an acidic residue, and a basic S34-R35-T36 ... K39 cluster in its place that salt-bridges the 2'-phosphate in the ortholog structure.

This also matches biochemistry across the family: NADPH for the B. fragilis enzyme (PMID: 37437886) and for Vibrio alginolyticus carboxynorspermidine synthase, where the Km for NADPH is 1.51 mM and NADH is a much poorer cofactor (PMID: 1955861).

F6. The closest characterised ortholog changed its published substrate in 2026

I9VY56 was characterised in 2023 with steady-state kinetics on putrescine, diaminopropane, aspartate β-semialdehyde, NADH and NADPH, concluding that "Putrescine is the likely polyamine substrate and NADPH is the coenzyme used to complete the reaction, forming carboxyspermidine as a product" (PMID: 37437886).

The same group re-examined the B. fragilis and Clostridium leptum enzymes in 2026 with steady-state and transient-state kinetics, mass spectrometry, thermal shift assays, X-ray crystallography and SAXS, and concluded that both "function as carboxyaminopropylagmatine dehydrogenases rather than carboxyspermidine dehydrogenases", with agmatine raising NADPH binding affinity 34-fold against a modest putrescine effect at concentrations judged less physiologically relevant (PMID: 42754158).

A parallel 2026 study of the Streptococcus pneumoniae enzyme, 56.4% identical to BT_4612, measured a kcat/KM of 1.0 × 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹ for agmatine, approximately 3,200-fold greater than putrescine, and solved a 1.19 Å structure with agmatine and NADPH bound (PMID: 43093060).

The route itself was established independently, and earlier, by isotope tracing. PMID: 37878710 used ¹³C and ¹⁵N tracers with metabolomics plus genetic and biochemical characterisation in Synechocystis sp. PCC 6803 to define the carboxyaminopropylagmatine route, which "starts with reductive condensation of agmatine and l-aspartate-β-semialdehyde into CAPA by a previously unknown CAPA dehydrogenase, followed by decarboxylation of CAPA to form aminopropylagmatine, and ends with conversion of aminopropylagmatine to spermidine by an aminopropylagmatine ureohydrolase", and states that "the pathway does not involve putrescine". The same paper reports the route's genes in 15 bacterial phyla.

That three-gene signature is matched slot for slot in B. thetaiotaomicron: BT_4612 (sole PF03435/PF16653), BT_0674 (casdc), BT_1099 (sole PF00491), with no spare copy of any slot and the classical alternative absent at family level. The match is suggestive and not probative, because a carboxyspermidine route uses exactly the same three slots with BT_1099 moved upstream.

Mechanistic model

The immediate molecular activity under test is one NADPH-dependent reductive condensation: aspartate β-semialdehyde plus a primary amine gives a carboxypolyamine, through a Schiff base reduced at the enzyme. Everything else in the published record is downstream of that step or upstream of it.

BT_1099 appears twice in that diagram, once on each branch, and only one of the two can be true. That is the entire difficulty, drawn.

Biological process

GO:0008295, spermidine biosynthetic process, is defensible for BT_4612 at IC or ISS strength. The organism makes spermidine de novo in polyamine-free medium and loses it when the single downstream decarboxylase is deleted (PMID: 27118128); BT_4612 is the only protein in the genome that can perform the aspartate-semialdehyde-dependent condensation that route requires; no classical AdoMetDC and spermidine synthase alternative exists. This is a stronger and safer annotation than any MF substrate term. The review should note that the BP term is reached by pathway necessity plus gene-count uniqueness, not by assay of BT_4612.

Cellular component

Nothing was tested. No CC recommendation. Cytoplasm would be an inference from pathway context only.

Qualifiers

If a CASDH-named term is retained anywhere in the record for provenance, it should carry a note that the compound name is unverified for this protein and is contradicted by the current assignment of its closest characterised ortholog.

Conflicts and alternatives

  1. The same ortholog, two published substrates. PMID: 37437886 (putrescine) against PMID: 42754158 (agmatine) on the identical B. fragilis protein. This is the most important conflict in the record and it argues for annotating above the compound level rather than for picking a winner.
  2. Subfamily name carry-over. PANTHER PTHR43796 is titled "carboxynorspermidine synthase". That name is the one option the B. thetaiotaomicron genome positively excludes. Any annotation inherited from the PANTHER name is database carry-over.
  3. Pathway-diagram carry-over, visible inside this genome's own record. UniProt names BT_0674 "Carboxynorspermidine/carboxyspermidine decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.96)". EC 4.1.1.96 is carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase, the one product excluded here. The dual name also reproduces, at the level of the database record, the carried-forward finding that the partner decarboxylase accepts both candidate substrates and transmits no constraint upstream.
  4. BT_1099 ambiguity cuts both ways. If BT_1099 is a true arginase (arginine to ornithine plus urea), the cell would make ornithine but has no ODC, so putrescine would be unreachable and the carboxyspermidine route would be dead. If it is an agmatinase, the carboxyspermidine route opens. If it is an aminopropylagmatine ureohydrolase, the agmatine route closes its loop. One assay on one 269-aa protein would resolve a large part of the question.
  5. Import as a confounder, bounded. The genome carries a PotABCD-type importer at BT_1288-BT_1291, so in a gut setting polyamine pools can be exogenous. The Δcasdc work used polyamine-free minimal medium, which removes this confounder for the de novo claim.
  6. The seed's structural premise is superseded. "No amine-contacting position is testable" and "no structure in the family holds a carboxypolyamine" were true when the earlier campaign runs were done. An agmatine-bound 1.19 Å structure now exists (PMID: 43093060). The compound bound is a substrate amine and not a carboxypolyamine, so the second clause survives; the first does not.

Discriminating tests

Ranked by information per unit effort.

  1. LC-MS of the published Δcasdc strain. The strain already exists (PMID: 27118128) and blocks the pathway immediately downstream of BT_4612, so the BT_4612 product should pool. Detecting carboxyaminopropylagmatine against carboxyspermidine answers the substrate question directly, in this organism, with no in vitro extrapolation. This is the single highest-value experiment.
  2. Recombinant BT_4612 kinetics: agmatine against putrescine against 1,3-diaminopropane against cadaverine, with ASA and NADPH, reporting kcat/KM. Reproduces the design that reassigned the B. fragilis and S. pneumoniae enzymes, on the actual protein.
  3. Recombinant BT_1099 assay on agmatine, N1-aminopropylagmatine and arginine. Cheap, 269 aa, and it determines which routes the genome can physically close.
  4. ΔBT_4612 in polyamine-free medium, with and without exogenous spermidine, measuring polyamine pools. Tests whether the step is obligatory, as the gene count predicts.
  5. NADPH against NADH kinetics on BT_4612, converting the structural cofactor inference into a measurement.
  6. Comparative check of the Asn-Glu-Phe-Glu motif of PMID: 43093060 across the subfamily, including BT_4612. Deliberately not done here, because the seed forbids reading substrate off pocket residues. Flagged as the obvious next computational step now that the premise has changed.

Direct answer to the hypothesis as posed

Three approaches were tried and all three converge on the same limit. Amine supply excludes one candidate. Sequence similarity cannot assign the ureohydrolase that decides between the other two. Genomic context is empty because the route's genes are dispersed. Pocket residues were not used, as the seed requires.

The physiological amine substrate of BT_4612 is constrained only by exclusion: the organism cannot synthesise 1,3-diaminopropane or cadaverine, so carboxynorspermidine is ruled out. Between carboxyspermidine and carboxyaminopropylagmatine the amine supply is silent, because the genome's single arginase-family ureohydrolase BT_1099 is required by either route and its specificity is not recoverable from sequence, its best Smith-Waterman score against any characterised ureohydrolase being 96, below the 172-257 range those characterised proteins span against each other. No assay or deletion of BT_4612 or of any upstream dehydrogenase has been published in this organism; the only measured genetics is downstream, the Δcasdc strain. BT_4612 is the sole candidate dehydrogenase in the genome. The cofactor is NADP, assignable from sequence and from a complete, fully conserved NADP pocket in a 96.7%-identical ortholog structure, and that claim stands independently of the substrate question. No specific-activity annotation naming a carboxypolyamine should be made for this protein from the present evidence.
