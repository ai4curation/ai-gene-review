Summary

The verdict is split. The part of the seed hypothesis that asks for a family and reaction assignment is supported with high confidence. The part that proposes substrate selectivity "may be constrained by its active-site architecture" is refuted as stated.

BT_0674 is the decarboxylase step of the aspartate-semialdehyde-dependent spermidine route, EC 4.1.1.96, PLP-dependent, group IV (fold type III) alanine-racemase fold. It is not the dehydrogenase. The two steps of this pathway are catalysed by non-homologous folds, which is what distinguishes the families: the dehydrogenase (CASDH) is an NADPH-dependent Rossmann/saccharopine-dehydrogenase-fold condensing enzyme (PF03435 + PF16653), and in this genome that enzyme is a separate protein, BT_4612 (Q89YW9). Q8A9Z1 carries PF00278 (Orn_DAP_Arg_deC), NCBIfam TIGR01047 (nspC), CDD cd06829 (PLPDE_III_CANSDC), PIRSF038941, PANTHER PTHR43727:SF1, and Gene3D 3.20.20.10 plus 2.40.37.10.

Every catalytic and cofactor-binding residue is present. Mapping the PLP contacts (within 4.0 Å) and bound norspermidine contacts (within 4.5 Å) of PDB 3N29, the Campylobacter jejuni carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase product complex at 1.9 Å, onto BT_0674 by Needleman-Wunsch alignment gives 13 of 14 positions identical: Lys42 (Schiff base), His171, Cys174, Gly206-Gly207 (phosphate loop), Glu239 (pyridine N1 anchor), Asp279, Met283, Cys309, Leu310, Tyr338, Lys342 and Phe346. The CANSDC-diagnostic Leu310 is present where both authentic diaminopimelate decarboxylases of the comparison set carry Glu.

The pocket cannot discriminate among carboxyspermidine, carboxynorspermidine and carboxyaminopropylagmatine. The decisive evidence is experimental, not computational: the C. jejuni enzyme carrying exactly this residue set was shown by gene deletion to make spermidine (a 4-3 polyamine) in vivo despite being named for carboxynorspermidine (3-3) (PMID: 22025614). An identical pocket therefore turns over both chain lengths. The single Bacteroidota-specific substitution, C. jejuni Glu236 to BT_0674 Ser242, withdraws one hydrogen bond from the product amine (4.10 Å modelled versus 2.54 Å observed) and changes the computed buried cavity volume by 0 Å³ under in-silico side-chain truncation. Selectivity in this pathway is imposed upstream, at the partner dehydrogenase BT_4612 (PMID: 40074085; PMID: 42754158).

For curation, the consequence is concrete. All five GO annotation lines currently on Q8A9Z1 are IEA or IBA, none experimental. GO:0008836 (diaminopimelate decarboxylase activity) and GO:0009085 (L-lysine biosynthetic process) arrive by IBA from PANTHER node PTN000159906, the same node and the same withFrom set that the genome's authentic lysA (BT_1374, Q8A800) carries, so they are paralog carry-over and should be removed. GO:0045312 (nor-spermidine biosynthetic process) is a name-based IEA transfer from TIGR01047 and should give way to GO:0008295 (spermidine biosynthetic process), which the deletion phenotype in the target organism supports directly (PMID: 27118128). No GO molecular function term exists for EC 4.1.1.96, so GO:0016831 (carboxy-lyase activity) is the most specific term available and a new-term request is warranted.

Executive judgment

Partially supported, with the selectivity claim refuted.

The seed hypothesis bundles four questions. Three are answered affirmatively and one is answered in the negative.

Family and reaction: supported, high confidence. BT_0674 is the carboxyspermidine/carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase (CASDC/CANSDC), the terminal PLP-dependent step, EC 4.1.1.96. The distinguishing feature between the two pathway families is fold, not sequence divergence within a family: CASDH is an NADPH-dependent saccharopine-dehydrogenase-like enzyme and CASDC is a group IV β/α-barrel PLP decarboxylase. BT_0674 is 44.9% identical over 372 aligned positions to the C. jejuni CANSDC in PDB 3N29 and 91.3% identical over 379 positions to the B. fragilis enzyme in PDB 36GY.

Catalytic completeness: supported, high confidence. All 13 shared active-site positions plus the catalytic Lys42 are present and unmodified. There is no basis for calling BT_0674 degenerate or a pseudo-enzyme. UniProt independently annotates a binding site at residue 279 of Q8A9Z1, matching the Asp272 to Asp279 mapping computed here.

Pathway context: supported, high confidence. The VPI-5482 proteome contains zero proteins matching PF01564 (spermidine/spermine synthase, speE) and zero matching PF02675 (S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase, speD). With no canonical spermidine synthase, the aspartate-semialdehyde route carries all spermidine flux. The partner step is present as exactly one CASDH-family protein, BT_4612.

Substrate selectivity constrained by architecture: refuted as stated. Every residue lining the polyamine channel is identical to a characterised enzyme known to accept both 3-3 and 4-3 substrates in vivo. The one clade-specific difference costs a hydrogen bond and no volume.

Most important caveats. The Bacteroides structure PDB 36GY is apo, has no publication, no PMID and no DOI, and its deposited title asserts a substrate (carboxyaminopropylagmatine) that the coordinates do not demonstrate. No CASDC structure with any carboxylated substrate bound exists in the PDB. No kinetic comparison of putrescine-, agmatine- or cadaverine-derived substrates has been published for any CASDC. The permissiveness conclusion therefore rests on an in vivo deletion result in a 44.9%-identical orthologue plus structure-based modelling, not on direct measurement in B. thetaiotaomicron.

Key findings

F001: BT_0674 is the decarboxylase step, not the dehydrogenase

UniProt Q8A9Z1 is 379 aa, 43.6 kDa, and carries PF00278 (Orn_DAP_Arg_deC), NCBIfam TIGR01047 (nspC), CDD cd06829 (PLPDE_III_CANSDC), PIRSF038941 (NspC), PANTHER PTHR43727:SF1 (carboxynorspermidine/carboxyspermidine decarboxylase), Gene3D 3.20.20.10 (alanine racemase TIM barrel) plus 2.40.37.10 (β-barrel), SUPFAM SSF51419 plus SSF50621, with PLP as cofactor and Rhea reactions for both carboxynorspermidine to norspermidine (RHEA:34099) and carboxyspermidine to spermidine (RHEA:34095).

Pairwise global alignment (BLOSUM62, affine gaps, Needleman-Wunsch implemented in numpy) gives 44.9% identity over 372 aligned positions to C. jejuni carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase (PDB 3N29, chain A) and 91.3% identity over 379 positions to the B. fragilis enzyme in PDB 36GY.

The partner step belongs to an unrelated fold. BT_4612 (Q89YW9, 397 aa, PF03435 + PF16653, saccharopine-dehydrogenase fold, NADPH-dependent) is 96.7% identical to the B. fragilis CASDH in PDB 8DEB and to the B. fragilis carboxyaminopropylagmatine dehydrogenase in PDB 36YL. BT_4612 is the only saccharopine-dehydrogenase-family protein in the VPI-5482 proteome.

The genomic neighbourhood gives no support either way. BT_0670 to BT_0673 encode efflux, TolC and an NADP(H) oxidoreductase; BT_0675 to BT_0678 encode GlcNAc-6-P deacetylases, thioredoxin and czcC. There is no operon linking BT_0674 to BT_4612, so the assignment rests on family membership and structural comparison.

F003: One pocket position differs, and it is a Bacteroidota-wide substitution

The only non-identical active-site position between BT_0674 and C. jejuni CANSDC is Glu236 to Ser242, within a conserved Glu-Pro-Gly-X motif: BT_0674 E239-P240-G241-S242, C. leptum CASDC E237-P238-G239-E240, C. jejuni E233-P234-G235-E236. In 3N29, Glu236 OE1 makes the closest polar contact to the bound norspermidine product (2.54 Å to amine N1) and also contacts the PLP phosphate at 2.86 Å.

A survey of 3346 CANSDC/CASDC homologs drawn from UniProt (330 to 430 aa, exactly one [EDQ]PG motif per sequence) gives Glu 68.6%, Ser 15.8% and Val 10.8% globally. The Ser variant is clade-restricted:

The depositor naming of the 91.3%-identical B. fragilis enzyme in PDB 36GY as "carboxyaminopropylagmatine decarboxylase" points the same way, since carboxyaminopropylagmatine carries a bulky guanidinium where carboxyspermidine and carboxynorspermidine carry a primary amine. That is database-level evidence from an unpublished 2026 deposition, not a measured preference.

F004: No canonical spermidine synthase, so the ASA route carries all flux

UniProt proteome queries for taxon 226186 return zero proteins matching PF01564 (spermidine/spermine synthase) or PF02675 (S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase, speD), and zero hits for the protein names spermidine synthase or adenosylmethionine decarboxylase.

The 1.56 Å withdrawal at the Glu236 subsite removes the hydrogen bond or salt bridge at product amine N1. In 9EAF the transferred product clashes with protein (closest atom 0.55 Å), which shows 9EAF is in a closed or apo loop conformation and not a valid frame for ligand transfer.

Grid-based buried-cavity volumes within 11 Å of the product site, computed with a 0.5 Å grid, a 1.4 Å probe, a 1.7 Å van der Waals radius and a 55-atom burial criterion within 8 Å, are 246 Å³ (3N29, holo), 305 Å³ (9EAF, apo, Glu240) and 397 Å³ (36GY, apo, Ser242).

The control rules out a re-sized pocket. Truncating Glu236 to Ser in silico in 3N29 (deleting CG, CD, OE1 and OE2) changes the computed volume by 0 Å³. Since 9EAF is apo yet carries the Glu and sits at an intermediate 305 Å³, the volume differences track apo-versus-holo loop conformation and not the Glu-to-Ser identity.

The AlphaFold model AF-Q8A9Z1-F1 (v6) is high confidence: mean pLDDT 96.1, 92.1% of residues above 90, 100% above 70, minimum 73.6, and a mean of 93.9 across the fifteen mapped active-site positions (Lys42 95.4, His169 98.8, His171 94.6, Cys174 88.8, Gly206 94.9, Gly207 91.9, Glu239 97.2, Ser242 95.2, Asp279 92.4, Met283 81.2, Cys309 95.1, Leu310 94.4, Tyr338 95.1, Lys342 95.7, Phe346 97.2). The AFDB entry is a monomer, and Cys309, Leu310 and Phe346 are contributed by the partner subunit, so no cavity was computed from it.

F006: The pocket cannot discriminate among the candidate carboxylated amines

Every residue that lines the polyamine channel in the C. jejuni product complex is identical in BT_0674: Cys174, Asp279, Met283, Cys309, Leu310, Tyr338, Lys342, Phe346, plus the catalytic Lys42, Glu239, Gly206-Gly207 and His169/His171.

The decisive point is an in vivo result. The C. jejuni enzyme carrying exactly this residue set was shown by gene deletion to make spermidine, not sym-norspermidine (PMID: 22025614): "By deleting these genes in the food-borne pathogen Campylobacter jejuni, we found that the carboxynorspermidine decarboxylase orthologue is responsible for synthesizing spermidine and not sym-norspermidine in vivo." The same pocket therefore turns over both the 3-3 (carboxynorspermidine) and the 4-3 (carboxyspermidine) substrate. The diamine-derived arm is anchored by Asp279 and Lys342, invariant across CANSDC, CASDC and the B. fragilis enzyme.

The confident prediction is a reaction, not a preference. BT_0674 is a PLP-dependent decarboxylase acting on carboxylated triamines of the carboxyspermidine type, and it most likely accepts carboxyspermidine, carboxynorspermidine and carboxyaminopropylagmatine with limited discrimination. Nothing in the architecture distinguishes putrescine-derived from agmatine-derived from cadaverine-derived substrates at the level of a testable structural prediction.

Mechanistic model

The pathway and the point at which selectivity is exercised:

The selectivity logic, stated as a causal chain:

The immediate molecular function is a decarboxylation. BT_0674 forms an internal aldimine between PLP and Lys42, binds the carboxylate and amines of a carboxylated triamine through Asp279, Lys342, Cys174, Tyr338 and the hydrophobic channel lined by Leu310, Met283 and Phe346, anchors the pyridine N1 with Glu239 and the phosphate with the Gly206-Gly207 loop, and removes the α-carboxyl as CO₂. The catalytic unit is the homodimer: the β/α-barrel of one subunit plus the β-barrel of the partner, which contributes Cys309, Leu310 and Phe346.

One step removed from this activity is the spermidine pool. The casdc deletion phenotype is a pathway consequence, which makes it good evidence for the biological process term and no evidence at all for which substrate is preferred. Two steps removed, and not attributable to BT_0674, is anything downstream of the spermidine pool, including growth and biofilm effects reported in other organisms.

How each paper bears on the findings

PMID: 22025614 is the single most informative paper for the seed hypothesis. It supplies the verbatim result that the enzyme with BT_0674's pocket residue set operates on the carboxyspermidine route in vivo, which is what makes "the pocket cannot discriminate" a conclusion from experiment rather than from modelling alone. It also retires GO:0045312 by showing the nspC-derived term names the wrong product.

PMID: 27118128 is the only mutant-phenotype paper in the target organism. Its snippet, "Deletion of the gene encoding a putative carboxyspermidine decarboxylase (casdc), which converts carboxyspermidine to spermidine, resulted in the depletion of spermidine and loss of decarboxylase activity in B. thetaiotaomicron", supports GO:0008295 with IMP evidence and corroborates the genomic finding that no canonical spermidine synthase exists.

PMID: 20534592 supplies the family signature, Leu314, that separates CANSDC from DAPDC, ODC and ADC. It is the literature anchor for removing GO:0008836.

PMID: 39887002 contributes twice. It defines CASDC as the decarboxylase step ("carboxyspermidine decarboxylase (CASDC) performs the terminal step in the production of spermidine"), and it names the structural elements proposed to set chain-length selection: "This analysis reveals structural differences occurring in the β6/β7 loop that acts as a `flap' covering the active site and in the α9/β12 loop that is connected to the α9 helix which is thought to select substrates by their chain length." This is a qualifier on the present conclusion. The selection model is proposed from apo structures and has not been tested kinetically, and the loops in question were not individually compared here beyond the residue mapping.

PMID: 40074085 is the direct competing evidence against a decarboxylase-centred selectivity model: "Production of Spm/Tspm from spermidine is controlled primarily by carboxyspermidine dehydrogenase, not carboxyspermidine decarboxylase."

PMID: 42754158 locates the measured preference at the dehydrogenase: "Both enzymes exhibit dramatically enhanced catalytic efficiency with agmatine, demonstrating that these homologs function as carboxyaminopropylagmatine dehydrogenases rather than carboxyspermidine dehydrogenases." Since BT_4612 is 96.7% identical to those enzymes, this is the strongest available evidence for which route carries flux in B. thetaiotaomicron, and it is evidence about the partner step.

PMID: 37437886 describes the dehydrogenase chemistry: "Putrescine is the likely polyamine substrate and NADPH is the coenzyme used to complete the reaction, forming carboxyspermidine as a product." It supports the fold and reaction distinction between the two pathway families and sits in tension with 42754158 on which diamine initiates the route.

PDB 36GY, 36YL and 8DEB are database-level records. 36GY supplies the Bacteroides CASDC coordinates used for superposition and the Ser242 confirmation. Its citation carries no PMID and no DOI, so it should be cited for fold and residue identity only, with a note that the coordinates are apo and that the deposited title asserts a substrate the structure does not demonstrate.

Conflicts and alternatives

The seed hypothesis conflicts with PMID: 40074085, which states that product identity in this pathway is controlled by the dehydrogenase rather than the decarboxylase. It also conflicts with the gene-deletion result in PMID: 22025614, where an enzyme named for the 3-3 substrate makes the 4-3 product in vivo.

Paralog confusion runs in two directions. GO:0008836 and GO:0009085 reached Q8A9Z1 from a PANTHER node shared with real diaminopimelate decarboxylases, and the genome contains an authentic lysA (BT_1374) that carries the same IBA terms with additional UniProt IEA support. In the other direction, InterPro IPR005730 (nspC) imposes "nor-spermidine" on an enzyme whose characterised relatives make spermidine.

There is a live organism-specific alternative. PMID: 42754158 reports that B. fragilis dehydrogenase homologues prefer agmatine, which would make BT_0674's physiological substrate carboxyaminopropylagmatine rather than carboxyspermidine, and PDB 36GY is deposited under exactly that name. This changes what BT_0674 is handed, not what it can accept, and the deposition is unpublished with no ligand bound. PMID: 37437886 reports putrescine as the dehydrogenase substrate in a different system, so the initiating diamine is organism-dependent and unresolved for B. thetaiotaomicron itself.

One technical alternative was tested and rejected during this run. The larger computed cavity in 36GY (397 Å³ versus 246 Å³ in 3N29) might have indicated a re-sized pocket that accommodates a bulkier guanidinium. It does not. 9EAF is apo and carries the Glu yet is intermediate at 305 Å³, and deleting the Glu236 side chain in silico in 3N29 changes the volume by 0 Å³. The volume difference tracks apo-versus-holo loop conformation.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

No CASDC structure with a carboxylated substrate exists. Checked: PDB searches returned 3N29 (product norspermidine bound), 3MT1 (S. meliloti, 36.3% identity), 9EAF (apo) and 36GY (apo). This matters because every statement about substrate discrimination is extrapolated from a product complex, and the product lacks the α-carboxyl that the enzyme acts on. A ternary complex or a Michaelis mimic would settle it.

No kinetics for any CASDC against alternative substrates. Checked: literature search found no paper comparing carboxyspermidine, carboxynorspermidine and carboxyaminopropylagmatine for one enzyme. "Cannot discriminate" is currently an inference from residue identity plus one in vivo deletion, not a measured kcat/Km ratio.

The functional consequence of Ser242 is untested. Checked: computed that it withdraws one hydrogen bond (4.10 Å versus 2.54 Å) and costs 0 Å³ of volume. What it does to binding affinity or turnover rate is unknown. The substitution is Bacteroidota-restricted across 3346 homologs, which argues it is maintained by selection for something that this analysis did not identify.

Which diamine BT_4612 prefers in B. thetaiotaomicron is not measured. Checked: BT_4612 is 96.7% identical to the B. fragilis enzymes in PDB 8DEB and 36YL, and 42754158 reports agmatine preference for those. Transfer at 96.7% identity is strong but remains inference. This matters for whether the biological process annotation should mention the agmatine-initiated variant route.

PDB 36GY has no publication. Checked: the entry's citation carries no PMID and no DOI. Its title asserts a substrate that the deposited apo coordinates do not demonstrate.

The β6/β7 flap and α9 helix were not compared in detail. Checked: the residue mapping covers positions within 4.0 Å of PLP and 4.5 Å of product. PMID: 39887002 proposes that chain-length selection sits in these loop and helix elements, some of which lie outside the contact shell analysed here. Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Measure kcat/Km of recombinant BT_0674 against carboxyspermidine, carboxynorspermidine and carboxyaminopropylagmatine side by side. This is the single most informative experiment and needs no new structure. A ratio within roughly tenfold across all three confirms a permissive pocket. A sharp preference would revive the seed hypothesis.

Build the reciprocal swap, BT_0674 S242E and C. jejuni E236S, and assay both against the same three substrates. This isolates the one Bacteroidota-specific change and tells a curator whether it is a selectivity determinant or a neutral clade marker.

Co-crystallise or soak a 36GY-type Bacteroides CASDC with a non-hydrolysable carboxylated amine analogue, or trap the external aldimine. This supplies the missing ligand complex and tests whether the β6/β7 flap closes differently around a 4-3 versus a 3-3 substrate, as proposed in 39887002.

Assay BT_4612 directly against putrescine and agmatine, and measure the polyamine pool of a BT_0674 deletion strain by LC-MS for spermidine, norspermidine and N1-aminopropylagmatine. This settles which route carries flux in B. thetaiotaomicron without requiring any new structure.

Complement the B. theta casdc deletion with BT_0674 and separately with the C. jejuni orthologue. If the C. jejuni enzyme restores the wild-type polyamine pool, the pocket difference is functionally irrelevant in vivo.

Run a loop-level structural comparison of the β6/β7 flap and the α9/β12 loop across 3N29, 9EAF, 36GY and the AlphaFold model, which this run did not do, to check whether a chain-length determinant sits outside the 4.5 Å contact shell.
