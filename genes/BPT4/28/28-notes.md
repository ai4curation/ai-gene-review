# Bacteriophage T4 Gene 28 (gp28) — Curation Notes

## Summary of Gene Function
Bacteriophage T4 gene `28` encodes the baseplate hub assembly protein gp28 (UniProt `P13336`, 177 amino acids, ~20.2 kDa; reported historically as ~24 kDa by SDS-PAGE). Classical T4 genetics and in vitro complementation studies classify gene 28 among the "group II" baseplate hub genes (along with genes 5, 26, 27, 29, and 51) required for assembling the central hub/plug of the T4 tail baseplate.

## Role in Baseplate Hub Morphogenesis
- **Mutant Phenotype**: Cells infected with gene 28 amber mutants accumulate normal 15S peripheral wedge complexes (`gp10–gp7–gp8–gp6–gp53–gp25`) and small amounts of unstable 70S hexagonal baseplate-like rings that lack central hub density by electron microscopy, demonstrating that gp28 is specifically required to form or incorporate the central hub plug.
- **Hub Precursor Formation and Chaperone Role**: Biochemical analyses of hub assembly intermediates indicate that gp28 associates with gp27 to form an early hub precursor during morphogenesis. Because gp28 is synthesized in very low copy numbers due to unfavorable initiation structure around its translational start site and is not resolved as a stoichiometric subunit in high-resolution cryo-EM structures of the mature ~6-MDa T4 baseplate (PubMed 27193680), modern reviews and UniProt annotate gp28 as a non-structural baseplate hub assembly chaperone.

## Annotation Review Notes
- `GO:0098003` (`viral tail assembly`, IEA from `GO_REF:0000043`): Directly supported by genetic and morphogenesis evidence establishing that gp28 is essential for formation of the central baseplate hub during T4 tail assembly (`ACCEPT`).
