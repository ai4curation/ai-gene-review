Gene Description

Tetrathionate hydrolase (4THase, Af-Tth; gene tth/tetH, locus AFE_0029) of the acidophilic iron- and sulfur-oxidizing chemolithoautotroph Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans. The precursor carries a 32-residue Sec signal peptide; the mature 467-residue chain folds as an eight-bladed beta-propeller (PQQ-repeat fold, but with no PQQ or other cofactor) and forms a homodimer. The enzyme hydrolyses tetrathionate at an optimum of pH 3, producing sulfate and a reactive sulfane intermediate that yields elemental sulfur and thiosulfate; Asp325 acts as the catalytic acid and the single cysteine is dispensable. It is the sole tetrathionate-hydrolysing activity of the cell (a deletion mutant cannot grow on tetrathionate) and is the hydrolytic step of the S4-intermediate thiosulfate oxidation pathway, strongly induced on tetrathionate and sulfur. The protein is membrane-associated and has been described as a peripheral protein of the cell outer membrane, although no inner/outer membrane separation has been reported; it is also released into the culture medium, where its activity drives extracellular sulfur globule formation.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

tetrathionate hydrolase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the reaction: tetrathionate + H2O = sulfur + thiosulfate + sulfate + H+.

Justification: Tetrathionate hydrolase (EC 3.12.1.-, RHEA:13541) is a well-characterised enzyme of acidophilic sulfur-oxidizing bacteria and archaea (Acidithiobacillus, Acidiphilium, Acidianus, Metallosphaera), with a solved structure and genetic evidence in A. ferrooxidans, but GO has no corresponding activity term; annotation currently stops at GO:0016828.

Parent term: hydrolase activity, acting on sulfur-sulfur bonds

Identity and activity

  • Tetrathionate hydrolase (4THase), purified from tetrathionate-grown ATCC 23270 cells and
    linked to AFE_0029 by N-terminal sequencing PMID:17904676.
  • Products: elemental sulfur, thiosulfate and sulfate PMID:25144400. Mechanistically
    sulfate plus the unstable disulfane monosulfonic acid are thought to form first, with
    sulfur/thiosulfate arising partly abiotically PMID:23722856.
  • UniProt reaction: tetrathionate + H2O = sulfur + thiosulfate + sulfate + H(+) (RHEA:13541), EC 3.12.1.-.
    No GO term exists for tetrathionate hydrolase activity (OLS search for "tetrathionate" in GO
    returns only thiosulfate dehydrogenase terms and CHEBI entities). The nearest parent is
    GO:0016828 hydrolase activity, acting on sulfur-sulfur bonds. Proposed a new MF term.
  • Structure: eight-bladed beta-propeller homodimer; Asp325 is the catalytic acid
    PMID:33103311
    PMID:33103311.
    The sole Cys301 is dispensable PMID:25144400.
  • No PQQ cofactor, despite the PQQ-repeat (beta-propeller) Pfam/InterPro signatures
    PMID:20546308.
    The PANTHER subfamily label (PTHR34512:SF30 "OUTER MEMBRANE PROTEIN ASSEMBLY FACTOR BAMB") is
    a family naming artefact; no GO propagation rests on it here.
  • Recombinant enzyme needs acid exposure to fold PMID:20546308.

Physiological role (genetic evidence)

  • Knockout: ΔtetH cannot grow on tetrathionate, still grows on sulfur and Fe2+ PMID:24727223.
    Strain is ATCC 23270 (full text: "ferrooxidans ATCC 23270 genome (GenBank accession number NC_011761 )").
  • Expression strongly induced on tetrathionate and sulfur relative to iron PMID:36635106.
  • Pathway context: S4-intermediate (S4I) thiosulfate oxidation pathway; TQO/TSD make tetrathionate,
    TetH hydrolyses it PMID:38348185.
  • Ferric reductase activity attributed to the same protein (tetrathionate-dependent Fe3+ reduction)
    PMID:19502725.
    Single-lab report, abstract only; not used for annotation.

Notes on CMMMech's claims (do not edit CMMMech)

CMMMech records (36+ protein examples for B7J3C9) describe it as a "Periplasmic tetrathionate
hydrolase ... generating sulfuric acid protons" and some causal-graph nodes as a "Periplasmic
pyrroloquinoline-domain hydrolase disproportionating tetrathionate into sulfate and protons".
Points where this exceeds the evidence:
1. Location: for A. ferrooxidans the evidence is membrane-associated, described as outer membrane
(peripheral), plus secreted; the exact membrane is not settled, and none of the sources we could read demonstrates a
periplasmic location for ATCC 23270 (earlier soluble/periplasmic reports are cited in
PMID:21833324). Periplasmic localization was shown for A. caldus TetH PMID:14717695 and a
different A. ferrooxidans strain (ATCC 19859, Table 1 of PMID:38348185), not for ATCC 23270.
2. "Pyrroloquinoline-domain": the PQQ-repeat signature describes the beta-propeller fold; the
enzyme does not use PQQ PMID:20546308.
3. "Disproportionating tetrathionate into sulfate and protons" omits the thiosulfate and elemental
sulfur products; one H+ per tetrathionate is released by the hydrolysis itself, while the bulk
of biogenic sulfuric acid comes from downstream oxidation of thiosulfate/sulfur by other enzymes.
4. Leaching-record evidence links are inferential (CMMMech itself notes genes were not mutated);
laboratory enzymology does not establish a role in any particular industrial leach.

Key results

  1. Identity to Af-Tth (B7J3C9): F9ZNI0 58.9%, A0A060URH6 57.9%, A0A5P9XS05 58.6%, ABP38225 57.4%,
    over 97-98% of B7J3C9 in each case. These are full-length family members.
  2. Orthology to characterised genes:
  3. F9ZNI0 vs ABP38225 (A. caldus KU TetH): 99.2% over 503 aligned. F9ZNI0 is the SM-1 ortholog of the characterised A. caldus TetH.
  4. A0A060URH6 vs AEM46280 (A. ferrivorans SS3 tetH1): 98.8% over 503 aligned; vs tetH2 (AEM48684) only 35.4%. A0A060URH6 is the tetH1 ortholog, the copy whose transcripts dominate on tetrathionate (Christel et al. 2016).
  5. Catalytic acid: the residue aligned to Af-Tth Asp325 (essential, D325N inactive) is Asp in every sequence that covers it (F9ZNI0 D323, A0A060URH6 D323, A0A5P9XPU3 D212, A0A5P9XS05 D323, ABP38225 D337). Af-Tth Cys301 aligns to Ala in all of the others.
  6. A0A5P9XPU3 is a fragment. The 235-aa product aligns only to the middle of Af-Tth: A0A5P9XPU3 (A. thiooxidans ATCC 19377, GCD22_00683): 140/235 = 59.6% identity; covers 47.1% of B7J3C9.
    Its N-terminus (MDGLKEALPVQTQFYGNAL...) begins inside the propeller; there is no signal peptide.
    The upstream ORF GCD22_00682 (QFX95165, 107 aa) carries a TetH-like signal-peptide N-terminus
    in another reading frame (82.2% identical to the SS3 tetH1 N-terminus). Translation of the
    locus CP045571.1:618911-622560 finds the N-terminal region in frame +2 (B7J3C9 13-105) and the
    central region in frame +0 (B7J3C9 111-349), at 53% and 59% identity. For the C-terminal
    ~150 residues (B7J3C9 350-499) the only hit within 2 kb downstream is a marginal frame +1
    segment (11/31 identical, 35%, to B7J3C9 341-375) that just meets the script's reporting
    threshold (≥35% over ≥30 residues); it is noise-level, like the other frame +1 hits
    (35-38%), and nothing covers B7J3C9 376-499.
    The locus therefore encodes a frame-shifted, C-terminally truncated tth copy (either a
    genuine pseudogene or an indel error in the PacBio-only assembly; this analysis cannot
    distinguish them). The same genome carries an intact 503-aa tth (A0A5P9XS05, GCD22_02315;
    83.8% identical to A0A5P9XPU3 over its 234 aligned residues), which is the appropriate
    representative of A. thiooxidans ATCC 19377 TetH.

Limitations

Pairwise alignments only; no phylogeny. Frame analysis uses stop-free segments and a simple
identity threshold, so short or divergent C-terminal remnants could be missed. The assembly
was not re-examined at read level.

  intermediate that yields elemental sulfur and thiosulfate; Asp325 acts as the catalytic acid and
  the single cysteine is dispensable. It is the sole tetrathionate-hydrolysing activity of the
  cell (a deletion mutant cannot grow on tetrathionate) and is the hydrolytic step of the
  S4-intermediate thiosulfate oxidation pathway, strongly induced on tetrathionate and sulfur.
  The protein is membrane-associated and has been described as a peripheral protein of the cell
  outer membrane, although no inner/outer membrane separation has been reported; it is also
  released into the culture medium, where its activity drives extracellular sulfur globule
  formation.
