Gene Description

PBP2x is a membrane-anchored class-B penicillin-binding protein of Streptococcus thermophilus LMG18311. Its serine transpeptidase domain crosslinks peptidoglycan peptide stems, while its transmembrane segment activates the cognate FtsW glycan polymerase. These functions are separable: a catalytically inactive PBP2x can activate FtsW, and a soluble PBP2x domain can crosslink polymer synthesized by other glycosyltransferases.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

serine-type peptidoglycan D,D-transpeptidase activity

Definition: Catalysis of peptide crosslink formation in peptidoglycan by a serine-dependent D,D-transpeptidase: transfer of the donor D-Ala4 acyl group from a pentapeptide stem to an amino acceptor on another stem or its peptide bridge, with release of terminal D-Ala5. This forms a D,D (4-to-3) crosslink rather than an L,D (3-to-3) crosslink; the acceptor amino group and bridge depend on the organism.

Justification: The reviewed enzyme performs the class-B PBP crosslink reaction; neither a carboxypeptidase hydrolysis term nor an L,D-transpeptidase term accurately represents this endpoint. Reaction and physiological-scope limits are stated in the core description. Round-1 QuickGO search found no exact D,D-specific peptidoglycan transpeptidase term; GO:0016755 is the verified aminoacyl-transfer parent. The shared definition does not standardize the organism-specific acceptor chemistry or upgrade inferential linkage evidence.

Core Functions

Catalyzes serine-dependent D,D transpeptidation of peptidoglycan peptide stems. LC-MS detected a crosslinked dimer that disappeared with PBP2x catalytic inactivation. Activity also occurs with polymer produced by SgtB or transpeptidase-dead PBP1a and survives deletion of the PBP2x transmembrane segment; therefore crosslink catalysis is not an activity supplied only by an inseparable FtsW–PBP2x complex. The assay used E. faecalis substrate and does not fix the exact native S. thermophilus bridge chemistry.

pbp2X literature and annotation review

2026-11-06

Q5M2U8, official pbp2X/stu1701, 755 residues, LMG18311. PMID30692671 cloned M1–D755 with a C-terminal His6 tag and S343A catalytic mutant; the soluble G53–D755 construct lacked the transmembrane segment. Full-length constructs were expressed in E. coli C43(DE3), soluble protein in BL21. Direct LC-MS crosslinking used E. faecalis lipid II-derived polymer, not isolated native S. thermophilus lipid II.

Catalyzes serine-dependent D,D transpeptidation of peptidoglycan peptide stems. LC-MS detected a crosslinked dimer that disappeared with PBP2x catalytic inactivation. Activity also occurs with polymer produced by SgtB or transpeptidase-dead PBP1a and survives deletion of the PBP2x transmembrane segment; therefore crosslink catalysis is not an activity supplied only by an inseparable FtsW–PBP2x complex. The assay used E. faecalis substrate and does not fix the exact native S. thermophilus bridge chemistry.

Physical activation of FtsW requires the PBP2x transmembrane interface; peptide crosslinking does not require that interface once a suitable polymer is available. Native bridge specificity and physiological kinetic preference are not established by the heterologous-lipid II assay.
