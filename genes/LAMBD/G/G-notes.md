# Curation Notes for Escherichia phage lambda Gene G (gpG, P03734)

## Summary of Gene Product Function
Gene `G` encodes the tail assembly chaperone gpG (UniProt: `P03734`, 140 aa, ~15.5 kDa), a non-structural cytoplasmic chaperone essential for bacteriophage lambda tail morphogenesis (`PMID:23851014`, `PMID:23911548`). Translation of the `G`-`T` region of the late morphogenetic transcript produces two proteins in a ~30:1 molar ratio: abundant 140-aa gpG and the 285-aa frameshift-extended fusion protein gpGT, which arises when ~3.5% of ribosomes translating `G` undergo a programmed `-1` ribosomal frameshift at the slippery heptanucleotide (`GGGAAAG`, codons 135–137 of `G`) into the overlapping `T` open reading frame (`PMID:23851014`). During tail assembly in the *Escherichia coli* cytoplasm, multiple copies of gpG and the N-terminal G domain of gpGT bind along the length of the 853-aa tail length tape measure protein (gpH), maintaining gpH in a soluble, assembly-competent state and converting the 15S tail tip precursor (`gpJ`-`gpI`-`gpL`-`gpK`) together with `gpM` into the 25S initiator complex (`PMID:23851014`, `PMID:23911548`). While the C-terminal T domain of gpGT recruits major tail tube subunits (gpV) to polymerize around gpH, both gpG and gpGT are displaced as the gpV hexameric rings stack and neither chaperone is present in the mature virion (`PMID:23911548`).

## Literature & Structural Evidence
- **Essentiality of both gpG and gpGT in balanced ratio (`PMID:23851014`)**: Demonstrates using a plasmid-based tail complementation system that both gpG and gpGT are strictly required in their natural ~30:1 ratio for assembly of biologically active tails and formation of the 25S tail shaft polymerization initiator [PMID:23851014, "A plasmid system that can direct the efficient synthesis of tails was created and used to show that gpG and gpGT are both essential for correct tail assembly."].
- **Chaperone-protein interactions with gpH and absence from mature virions (`PMID:23911548`)**: Shows by co-purification and Ni-affinity pull-down assays that the G domain (present in both gpG and gpGT) binds along the tape measure protein gpH, whereas the T domain of gpGT binds major tail protein gpV, and neither gpG nor gpGT is incorporated into mature tails [PMID:23911548, "We show that the \"G\" domains of gpG and gpGT bind to all or parts of tail length tape measure protein gpH and that the \"T\" domain of gpGT binds to major tail shaft subunit gpV, and present a model for how gpG and gpGT chaperone gpH and direct the polymerization of gpV to form a tail of the correct length."; "Although both proteins are required and must be present in the correct ratio for assembly of functional tails, neither is present in mature tails."].

## Review of Existing GO Annotations
- `GO:0098003` (`viral tail assembly`, `IDA` from `PMID:23911548`, `IMP` from `PMID:23851014`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000120`): **ACCEPT**. gpG is an essential dedicated chaperone for viral tail assembly.
- `GO:0030430` (`host cell cytoplasm`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000120`): **ACCEPT**. Because gpG is a non-structural assembly chaperone that acts entirely within the bacterial host cytoplasm and is released prior to virion completion (`PMID:23911548`), `GO:0030430` (`host cell cytoplasm`) is its true site of function.
- `GO:0075523` (`viral translational frameshifting`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000043`): **REMOVE**. The `G`-`T` mRNA contains a slippery sequence that directs programmed `-1` ribosomal frameshifting by the host ribosome (`PMID:23851014`), but the encoded gpG protein does not catalyze, regulate, or participate in translational frameshifting. This annotation is a keyword-mapping artifact from `UniProtKB-KW:KW-0688` (`Ribosomal frameshifting`).
