# Curation Notes for Escherichia phage lambda Gene T (gpGT, P03735)

## Summary of Gene Product Function
Gene `T` (UniProt: `P03735`, 279 aa in the frameshift-extended `gpGT` entry, ~31.1 kDa) encodes the C-terminal `T` domain of the frameshift-extended tail assembly chaperone `gpGT`. Open reading frame `T` lacks its own translational initiation site and is expressed exclusively via a programmed `-1` ribosomal frameshift near the 3' end of the upstream `G` gene (`GGGAAAG` slippery heptanucleotide at codons 135–137 of `G`), which occurs in ~3.5% of ribosomes translating `G` (`PMID:23851014`). The resulting transframe fusion protein `gpGT` consists of an N-terminal `G` domain (residues 1–135, identical to `gpG`) fused to a C-terminal `T` domain (residues 136–279). During tail morphogenesis in the *Escherichia coli* cytoplasm, the N-terminal `G` domain of `gpGT` associates with `gpG` molecules coating the tail length tape measure protein (`gpH`), while the C-terminal `T` domain binds directly to the major tail tube subunit (`gpV`) and recruits or catalyzes the polymerization of `gpV` hexameric rings around the `gpH` ruler (`PMID:23911548`). Like `gpG`, `gpGT` is displaced as the tail tube polymerizes and is absent from mature virions (`PMID:23911548`).

## Literature & Structural Evidence
- **Programmed -1 ribosomal frameshifting and requirement for balanced gpG:gpGT ratio (`PMID:23851014`)**: Shows that `gpGT` is produced via `-1` ribosomal frameshifting at the `G`-`T` junction and that both `gpG` and `gpGT` are strictly required in their natural ~30:1 ratio for tail assembly [PMID:23851014, "A plasmid system that can direct the efficient synthesis of tails was created and used to show that gpG and gpGT are both essential for correct tail assembly."; "The second protein gpGT is made when about 3-4% of the ribosomes reading the G orf shift back one base on the mRNA at a slippery sequence at the G-T junction and continue in the -1 frame to the end of gene T."].
- **Domain-specific chaperone interactions of gpGT (`PMID:23911548`)**: Demonstrates that the N-terminal `G` domain of `gpGT` binds both `gpG` and the tape measure protein `gpH`, whereas the C-terminal `T` domain binds directly to the major tail shaft subunit `gpV` to coordinate `gpV` polymerization along the `gpH` ruler, after which both `gpG` and `gpGT` are released [PMID:23911548, "We show that the \"G\" domains of gpG and gpGT bind to all or parts of tail length tape measure protein gpH and that the \"T\" domain of gpGT binds to major tail shaft subunit gpV, and present a model for how gpG and gpGT chaperone gpH and direct the polymerization of gpV to form a tail of the correct length."; "Although both proteins are required and must be present in the correct ratio for assembly of functional tails, neither is present in mature tails."].

## Review of Existing GO Annotations
- `GO:0098003` (`viral tail assembly`, `IDA` from `PMID:23851014`, `IDA` from `PMID:23911548`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000120`): **ACCEPT**. `gpGT` is an essential chaperone that bridges the `gpH`-`gpG` tape measure scaffold and incoming `gpV` subunits during viral tail assembly.
- `GO:0030430` (`host cell cytoplasm`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000120`): **ACCEPT**. `gpGT` is a non-structural chaperone that acts in the bacterial host cytoplasm and is absent from the mature phage virion (`PMID:23911548`).
- `GO:0075523` (`viral translational frameshifting`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000043`): **REMOVE**. `gpGT` is the *product* of programmed `-1` ribosomal frameshifting on the `G`-`T` mRNA (`PMID:23851014`); the `gpGT` protein itself does not perform or regulate translational frameshifting.
