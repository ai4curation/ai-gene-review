CC   -!- FUNCTION: Serves as a ruler that controls the length of tail by
CC       stopping the tail tube polymerization and is probably released from the
CC       tail shaft during infection to facilitate DNA translocation into the
CC       host cell (PubMed:2150582). Assembles into a multimeric linear form
CC       possibly stabilized by the covering tail assembly proteins G and GT
CC       (PubMed:23911548). Its C-terminus fixes the tail tip complex (J, I, L,
CC       K), thereby forming the tail assembly initiator complex
CC       (PubMed:23911548). Tail tube proteins polymerize around tape measure
CC       protein, displacing the tail assembly protein G and GT
CC       (PubMed:23911548). When the tail reaches the length specified by the
CC       tape measure protein, it stops and becomes capped by the tail
CC       terminator protein (Probable). Upon tail assembly, tape measure protein
CC       is cleaved into a form called H*, that plays a role later during virion
CC       entry in a new cell (Probable). Once assembled, the virion is released
CC       and can infect new cells by binding to the entry receptor LambB
CC       (Probable). After opening of a pore on the external membrane, the entry
CC       protein H* protein is probably released in the periplasmic space for
CC       successful DNA injection (PubMed:23911548). {ECO:0000255|HAMAP-
CC       Rule:MF_04138, ECO:0000269|PubMed:2150582, ECO:0000269|PubMed:23911548,
CC       ECO:0000303|PubMed:23911548, ECO:0000305|PubMed:1108413,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:23911548, ECO:0000305|PubMed:6220513,
CC       ECO:0000305|PubMed:6228546}.
CC   -!- SUBUNIT: Interacts with the tail initiator complex presumably through
CC       its C-terminus domain (Probable). Interacts with the tail assembly
CC       protein G (PubMed:23911548). Interacts with the tail assembly protein
