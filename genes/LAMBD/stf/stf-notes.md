# Curation Notes for Escherichia phage lambda Gene stf (gpStf, P03764)

## Summary of Gene Product Function
Gene `stf` encodes the side tail fiber protein gpStf (UniProt: `P03764`, 774 aa, ~80.8 kDa) of ancestral wild-type bacteriophage lambda (`Ur-lambda`). In the common laboratory strain `lambda PaPa` (often referred to as laboratory "wild-type" lambda, sequenced by Sanger et al., 1982), the `stf` open reading frame carries a single base-pair deletion (`frameshift mutation`) that splits it into two pseudogene fragments (`orf-401` and `orf-314`), rendering `lambda PaPa` devoid of side tail fibers (`PMID:1439823`). In authentic `Ur-lambda`, full-length `stf` is co-expressed with the downstream tail fiber assembly chaperone `tfa` (`gpTfa`) to assemble four long, thin, jointed side tail fibers anchored at the top of the tail tip complex (`PMID:1439823`, `PMID:41237242`). High-resolution cryo-EM of `Ur-lambda` (`PMID:41237242`) demonstrates that twelve copies of `gpStf` (organized as four trimeric side fibers, each composed of a dimer-of-trimers collar interface at `gpStfN`) form a collar enveloping the junction where the distal hexameric ring of major tail tube protein `gpV` abuts the hexameric ring of distal tail protein `gpM` in the tail tip complex. Functionally, the C-terminal receptor-binding tips of the side tail fibers reversibly recognize the *Escherichia coli* outer membrane porin OmpC (and/or surface lipopolysaccharide/outer membrane components) as a primary adhesion receptor, significantly increasing the rate of phage adsorption and holding the virion in an upright orientation conducive to central tail fiber (`gpJ`) engagement with the entry receptor LamB (`PMID:1439823`, `PMID:30463036`, `PMID:41237242`).

## Literature & Structural Evidence
- **Discovery of `stf` frameshift in `lambda PaPa` vs functional side tail fibers in `Ur-lambda` (`PMID:1439823`)**: Hendrix & Duda (1992) showed that `Ur-lambda` possesses four thin, jointed side tail fibers composed of `gpStf` and assisted by `gpTfa`, which are absent in laboratory `lambda PaPa` due to a frameshift mutation in `stf`, and that these side fibers expand receptor specificity and accelerate adsorption to *E. coli* [PMID:1439823, "The Ur-lambda virions have thin, jointed tail fibers that are absent from lambda wild type. Two novel proteins of Ur-lambda constitute the fibers: the product of stf, the gene that is disrupted in lambda wild type by the frameshift mutation, and the product of gene tfa, a protein that is implicated in facilitating tail fiber assembly. Relative to lambda wild type, Ur-lambda has expanded receptor specificity and adsorbs to Escherichia coli cells more rapidly."].
- **Single-cell infection kinetics (`PMID:30463036`)**: Quantitative live-cell imaging confirms that side tail fibers significantly increase host cell adsorption of `Ur-lambda` compared to `stf`-deficient lambda [PMID:30463036, "84.0 ± 2.2% (6746 attached phages out of 8034 total phages in 11 experiments) of Ur-λ phages were adsorbed on E. coli host cells, significantly more than 77.3 ± 2.6% (6365 out of 8239 phages in 9 experiments) of λWT phages (Mann-Whitney U-test, p < 0.05) (Fig. 1B). This confirms that the side tail fibers help Ur-λ to encounter the host for infection."].
- **Cryo-EM structure of the gpStf collar and OmpC interaction (`PMID:41237242`)**: Resolves twelve copies of the N-terminal domain `gpStfN` forming a collar around the `gpV`-`gpM` junction at the top of the tail tip complex and shows that the side tail fibers interact with *E. coli* OmpC to accelerate adsorption and poise the virion on the cell surface [PMID:41237242, "Twelve copies of gpStfN (gpStf-a, -b, etc.) form a collar that envelops the junction where a ring of six copies of gpV abuts a ring of six copies of gpM in the tail tip complex"; "These side fibers, encoded by Ur-λ stf, interact with E. coli OmpC and increase the adsorption rate"].

## Review of Existing GO Annotations
- `GO:0098024` (`virus tail, fiber`, `EXP` from `PMID:41237242`, `IDA` from `PMID:1439823`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000043`): **ACCEPT**. Directly supported by cryo-EM and electron microscopy showing that gpStf forms the four jointed side tail fibers of Ur-lambda.
- `GO:0098015` (`virus tail`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000043`): **MODIFY** to `GO:0098024` (`virus tail, fiber`), the more specific child term already supported by experimental annotations on this gene.
- `GO:0098671` (`adhesion receptor-mediated virion attachment to host cell`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000043`): **ACCEPT**. Unlike `gpJ` (which binds the entry receptor LamB to trigger DNA ejection), `gpStf` binds the primary adhesion receptor OmpC to accelerate reversible cell-surface capture and orient the phage for subsequent LamB engagement (`PMID:1439823`, `PMID:30463036`, `PMID:41237242`).
- `GO:0019062` (`virion attachment to host cell`, `IDA` from `PMID:1439823` and `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000120`), `GO:0046813` (`receptor-mediated virion attachment to host cell`, `IDA` from `PMID:30463036`), and `GO:0046718` (`symbiont entry into host cell`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000120`): **MODIFY** all four to `GO:0098671` (`adhesion receptor-mediated virion attachment to host cell`), which is the specific descendant term already present on this gene.
- `GO:0005198` (`structural molecule activity`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000002`): **MODIFY** to `GO:0039660` (`structural constituent of virion`), the more specific descendant term for a viral structural protein.
