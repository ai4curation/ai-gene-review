# Curation Notes for Escherichia phage lambda Gene Z (gpZ, P03731)

## Summary of Gene Product Function
Gene `Z` encodes the tail completion protein gpZ (UniProt: `P03731`, 192 aa, ~21.6 kDa), an essential minor tail structural protein located at the proximal end of the bacteriophage lambda tail. Following polymerization of the hexameric major tail protein (gpV) tube and capping by the tail terminator protein (gpU), gpZ binds to the gpU-capped tail to complete tail morphogenesis and render the tail competent to join the DNA-filled capsid at the portal-neck vertex (`gpB`-`gpW`-`gpFII`-`gpU`-`gpZ` complex) [PMID:41237242, "One of the capsid vertices harbors the portal-neck complex formed by gpB, gpW, gpFII, gpU, and gpZ."]. Without gpZ action, tails still attach to heads morphologically in some conditions or form non-infectious particles incapable of productive DNA injection into *Escherichia coli*.

## Literature & Structural Evidence
- **Cryo-EM structure of Ur-lambda (`PMID:41237242`)**: Identifies gpZ as a component of the portal-neck complex at the head-tail interface (`gpB`, `gpW`, `gpFII`, `gpU`, and `gpZ`), connecting the capsid portal vertex to the proximal end of the tail tube [PMID:41237242, "One of the capsid vertices harbors the portal-neck complex formed by gpB, gpW, gpFII, gpU, and gpZ."].
- **Classical tail morphogenesis (`PMID:1003470`, UniProt `P03731`)**: Katsura (1976) characterized the assembly pathway and polymorphism of the lambda tail, establishing gpZ as a virion tail component synthesized in the host cytoplasm that stabilizes the completed tail structure and connects the tail to the capsid portal vertex [file:LAMBD/Z/Z-uniprot.txt, "CC   -!- FUNCTION: Stabilizes the tail structure and acts as a connector between\nCC       the end of tail and the portal vertex of the capsid. {ECO:0000305}."].

## Review of Existing GO Annotations
- `GO:0098015` (`virus tail`, `IDA` from `PMID:1003470` and `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000043`): **ACCEPT**. gpZ is a structural component at the proximal neck/tail junction of the mature bacteriophage lambda tail.
- `GO:0030430` (`host cell cytoplasm`, `IEA` from `GO_REF:0000044`): **KEEP_AS_NON_CORE**. Newly synthesized gpZ polypeptides reside transiently in the *E. coli* cytoplasm during lytic tail morphogenesis before incorporation into virions, which is a general biosynthetic location rather than a defining core function.
