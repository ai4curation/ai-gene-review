# Bacteriophage lambda gene S (Holin S-105 / Antiholin S-107; UniProt P03705)

## Overview
Gene `S` of *Escherichia phage lambda* (`NCBITaxon:2681611`) is the first gene of the late lysis cassette (`pR' -> S-R-Rz-Rz1`) and encodes the prototype class I phage holin and its dual-start antiholin regulator [PMID:2137120, PMID:11029427, PMID:21187415]. Translation of the `S` reading frame from two adjacent start codons (Met-1 and Met-3), controlled by the `sdi` (structure-directed initiation) RNA stem-loop, produces two protein isoforms with opposing activities in host lysis timing:
- **S-105 (`P03705-2`, 105 AA, initiates at Met-3)**: The lethal effector holin, which accumulates harmlessly in the *Escherichia coli* inner (cytoplasmic) membrane during the late phase of the infective cycle until an allele-specific critical time ("triggering"), when it suddenly coalesces into two-dimensional rafts and forms micron-scale (>300 nm) lesions ("S-holes") that depolarize the inner membrane and release the cytoplasmic endolysin `R` into the periplasm [PMID:9573208, PMID:21187415].
- **S-107 (`P03705-1`, 107 AA, initiates at Met-1)**: The antiholin (lysis inhibitor), which differs from S-105 solely by an N-terminal Met1-Lys2 extension. In an energized inner membrane, the positively charged Lys-2 residue prevents translocation of transmembrane domain 1 (TMD1) into the periplasm, locking S-107 in an inactive 2-TMD topology that heterodimerizes with S-105 and inhibits raft and hole formation until the proton motive force (PMF) collapses [PMID:11029427, PMID:21187415].

## Membrane Topology, Dimerization, and Triggering Mechanism
1. **Inner Membrane Localization and Topology**: Immunoblotting and membrane fractionation demonstrated that S protein appears ~10 min after prophage induction and localizes exclusively to the host inner membrane throughout the lytic cycle [PMID:6213604, PMID:2137120]. Active S-105 adopts a 3-TMD topology (N-out, C-in), whereas S-107 adopts a 2-TMD topology with TMD1 retained in the cytoplasm until membrane depolarization allows TMD1 to flip across the bilayer and convert S-107 into an active holin [PMID:11029427, PMID:21187415].
2. **Homodimerization and Heterodimerization**: Cysteine-scanning disulfide cross-linking showed that both S-105 and S-107 form homodimers in the membrane and also form S-105/S-107 heterodimers [PMID:11029427]. Because S-107 is normally synthesized at ~1:2 to 1:3 stoichiometry relative to S-105, heterodimerization titrates a substantial fraction of S-105 into non-lethal complexes, delaying triggering and sharpening the lysis clock [PMID:11029427, PMID:21187415].
3. **Real-Time Triggering and Micron-Scale Pore Formation**: Purified S-105 permeabilizes liposomes *in vitro* without auxiliary host proteins, whereas lysis-defective `S` missense mutants fail to release trapped dye [PMID:9573208]. Real-time fluorescence imaging of S105-GFP revealed that S-105 distributes uniformly in the inner membrane until less than a minute before lysis, when it rapidly nucleates into large immobile rafts that open irregular >300 nm holes, allowing folded cytoplasmic proteins (including the 17.5 kDa endolysin R and even ~500 kDa R-beta-galactosidase fusions) to escape into the periplasm [PMID:11361346, PMID:21187415].

## GO Annotation Review Summary
- **Accepted Core Terms**:
  - `GO:0140911` (`pore-forming activity`, IMP `PMID:11361346`): Core molecular function of the S-105 holin isoform.
  - `GO:0044659` (`viral release from host cell by cytolysis`, IDA `PMID:9573208`): Core biological process mediated by S-105 hole formation and endolysin release.
  - `GO:0045918` (`negative regulation of cytolysis`, IDA `PMID:21187415`): Core biological process of the S-107 antiholin isoform, which inhibits premature S-105 raft formation and triggering.
  - `GO:0020002` (`host cell plasma membrane`, IDA `PMID:11029427`, `PMID:2137120`, `PMID:6213604`; IEA `GO_REF:0000044`): Site of S-105 and S-107 accumulation, dimerization, and hole formation in the *E. coli* inner membrane.
- **Modified Terms**:
  - `GO:0031640` (`killing of cells of another organism`, IEA `GO_REF:0000043` from `UniProtKB-KW:KW-0204`): Replaced (`MODIFY`) with the more specific viral process term `GO:0044659` (`viral release from host cell by cytolysis`), which is already experimentally annotated to `S`.
