## Introduction and summary

It was derived automatically from the Ensembl translation ENSECAP00000077192.1 of the horse gene ENSECAG00000023918 on chromosome 1, which in turn rests on the Thoroughbred reference genome [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry; wade-2009-horse-genome-abstract]. The symbol DUOX1 matches the description "Dual oxidase 1", the PANTHER subfamily (PTHR11972:SF75, DUAL OXIDASE 1) and the domain set (DUOX peroxidase-like domain, ferric-reductase transmembrane domain, FAD- and NAD(P)-binding domains, and a DUOX-type pleckstrin-homology region) [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry]. This symbol does not seem to be ambiguous. I found no published experimental study of horse DUOX1: PubMed searches combining dual oxidase or DUOX with horse or equine returned no relevant primary work. Everything below about function is therefore inferred from orthology, mainly with human and mouse DUOX1, plus my own sequence comparison of the horse gene products with reviewed human DUOX1 (Q9NRD9) [uniprot-Q9NRD9-human-DUOX1-entry].

Main conclusion. Horse DUOX1 is the orthologue of mammalian DUOX1. DUOX1 is a calcium-regulated, flavin- and heme-containing NADPH oxidase located in the apical plasma membrane of epithelial cells, where it works with its maturation partner DUOXA1. It moves electrons from cytosolic NAD(P)H across the membrane to O2 and releases hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) rather than superoxide into the extracellular or luminal space (EC 1.6.3.1; NAD(P)H + O2 + H+ = H2O2 + NAD(P)+) [amezianeelhassani-2005-duox1-h2o2-abstract; sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-fulltext; wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. The H2O2 is used as a substrate by heme peroxidases. Thyroid peroxidase (TPO) uses it to iodinate thyroglobulin during thyroid hormone synthesis [dedeken-2000-cloning-thox-abstract; rigutto-2009-duox-pka-pkc-abstract]. Lactoperoxidase (LPO) uses it on airway and other mucosal surfaces to produce the antimicrobial and antiviral oxidant hypothiocyanite (OSCN-) [geiszt-2003-duox-mucosal-host-defense-abstract; sarr-2021-duox1-influenza-abstract]. DUOX1-derived H2O2 also acts as a signal, both as a paracrine wound cue and within EGFR/ERK signalling in airway cells [niethammer-2009-zebrafish-wound-h2o2-abstract; boots-2009-atp-duox1-egfr-abstract].

An important caveat about this particular accession: my alignment shows that A0A9L0SQG9 is not the canonical horse DUOX1. It is an alternative transcript model that lacks the segment matching human residues 775–873. That segment contains all of the calcium ligands of EF-hand 1 and most of those of EF-hand 2, the motifs that mediate calcium activation of the enzyme (see the section on structure) [NOTES-sequence-analysis; wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. Every heme ligand and the main structural features are kept. At the gene level, the deletion comes from skipping one exon and using an alternative splice site in the next exon of the same one-to-one DUOX1 orthologue [NOTES-sequence-analysis; ensembl-horse-DUOX1-locus-summary]. If this isoform is expressed, the prediction is that it would be poorly activated by calcium, or not at all. The other horse model from the same gene (F6YEX2) contains the complete EF-hand module.

## Gene and protein identity in the horse genome

The horse gene ENSECAG00000023918 encodes at least four Ensembl translations that UniProt imports as separate TrEMBL entries: F6YEX2 (1,516 aa), A0A9L0SA70 (1,508 aa), A0A9L0SBC0 (1,505 aa) and the target A0A9L0SQG9 (1,452 aa). All are named Dual oxidase 1 and all are "inferred from homology" [NOTES-sequence-analysis]. In pairwise global alignments with human DUOX1 (1,551 aa), each horse sequence is colinear and identical at about 86–90% of human positions, as expected for a mammalian orthologue. Each lacks a different internal or terminal block. F6YEX2 lacks only part of the C-terminal tail. A0A9L0SA70 lacks human residues 964–1005. A0A9L0SBC0 lacks residues 561–606, the region of the first transmembrane segment. A0A9L0SQG9 lacks residues 775–873 [NOTES-sequence-analysis]. The simplest reading is that these are alternative splice products, or gene-model variants, of one locus. Human DUOX1 also has annotated alternative isoforms [uniprot-Q9NRD9-human-DUOX1-entry]. UniProt's own automatic annotation treats A0A9L0SQG9 differently from its siblings: the three other horse entries carry the recommended name and EC 1.6.3.1, while A0A9L0SQG9 has only a submitted name and no EC number [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry]. The entry also notes that it "lacks conserved residue(s) required for the propagation of feature annotation" for the peroxidase domain. This fits the experimental finding that the mammalian DUOX1 peroxidase-like domain has lost its heme-binding residues (discussed below) [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry; meitzler-2009-peroxidase-domain-abstract].

Ensembl's gene-level records add more evidence on identity. Ensembl Compara classifies the horse gene (VGNC symbol DUOX1, chromosome 1:145,730,383–145,761,897, reverse strand) as a one-to-one orthologue of human DUOX1, with about 90% reciprocal identity. The gene encodes four protein-coding transcripts of 33–35 exons [ensembl-horse-DUOX1-locus-summary; NOTES-sequence-analysis]. Comparing exon coordinates explains where the target isoform comes from. Its transcript, ENSECAT00000123284, skips a 226-nucleotide exon that the full-length transcript (ENSECAT00000026134, encoding F6YEX2) contains. It also uses an internal acceptor site in the next exon, removing a further 71 nucleotides. Together that is 297 nucleotides, or 99 codons in frame, exactly the 99-residue gap seen in the protein alignment [NOTES-sequence-analysis]. A0A9L0SQG9 is therefore an alternative-splicing model of the canonical locus, not a gap in the assembly or a paralogue. How much transcript evidence supports this splice form in horse tissues is not documented. The neighbouring genes support orthology too. Horse DUOXA1 (VGNC:17341) starts on the forward strand 11 bp from the 5′ end of DUOX1, and DUOXA2 lies nearby, so the head-to-head DUOX–DUOXA arrangement described in other species is preserved [ensembl-horse-DUOX1-locus-summary; grasberger-2006-duoxa-maturation-abstract]. Horse gene models for the downstream peroxidases TPO and LPO also exist [ensembl-horse-DUOX1-locus-summary]. This means every component of the DUOX1-driven peroxidase systems described below is encoded in the horse genome.

In human, DUOX1 and DUOX2 were first cloned from thyroid as ThOX1 and ThOX2, using low-stringency hybridization with a gp91phox (NOX2) probe. Human DUOX1 is a 1,551-residue polypeptide, and the two genes lie close together on chromosome 15q15.3 [dedeken-2000-cloning-thox-abstract]. Each DUOX gene is arranged head-to-head with a gene for its maturation factor (DUOXA1 next to DUOX1, DUOXA2 next to DUOX2), an operon-like arrangement that dates back before echinoderms diverged [grasberger-2006-duoxa-maturation-abstract]. Phylogenetic surveys put DUOX in one of the early-diverging, calcium-regulated branches of the NOX/DUOX family. DUOX is also among the most strongly conserved family members in vertebrates [kawahara-2007-nox-evolution-abstract]. That conservation is what justifies transferring function from human and mouse to horse.

## Domain architecture and catalytic mechanism

DUOX1 is a multi-domain membrane protein whose catalytic core is shared with the other NOX enzymes. The C-terminal cytosolic dehydrogenase region resembles ferredoxin-NADP+ reductase: it binds NADPH and FAD. It sits beneath a six-helix transmembrane ferric-reductase domain that holds two b-type hemes stacked across the bilayer [sumimoto-2008-nox-structure-evolution-abstract]. Cryo-EM structures of mouse and human DUOX1–DUOXA1 complexes have shown this arrangement directly. The transmembrane domain has seven segments (S0/M0 plus the canonical S1–S6). The outer heme is coordinated by His1144 and His1238 and the inner heme by His1130 and His1225. FAD binds at the interface between the transmembrane and cytosolic domains, and NADPH binds in a lipid-lined pocket of the NADPH-binding domain. The proposed electron path runs NADPH → FAD → inner heme → outer heme → O2 [sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-fulltext; wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. Horse A0A9L0SQG9 conserves all four heme-ligating histidines (horse H1031, H1045, H1126 and H1139). It also conserves the putative oxygen-site residues R1087 and H1148 and the PHD–TMD disulfide pair C118/C1165 (horse C118/C1066) [NOTES-sequence-analysis]. So the electron-transfer machinery is intact in this isoform, and the transmembrane-domain InterPro signatures in the entry agree [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry].

Unlike NOX1–3 and NOX5, mature DUOX releases H2O2 rather than superoxide. Membrane fractions of transfected cells show NADPH- and Ca2+-dependent H2O2 production, and EPR spin trapping shows that mature thyroid oxidase does not release O2•−. Partially glycosylated DUOX retained in the ER, by contrast, does release superoxide, which implies that intramolecular dismutation depends on correct maturation [amezianeelhassani-2005-duox1-h2o2-abstract]. The structures offer an explanation. In DUOX1 the outer heme and oxygen-reacting centre are enclosed by extracellular linkers (S1–S2, S3–S4, S5–S6), which form a positively charged internal cavity. That cavity would hinder O2•− escape and favour dismutation to H2O2. The S3–S4 linker is held in place by a disulfide bond to the peroxidase homology domain [sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-fulltext]. Human DUOX1 has been reported to use both NADPH and NADH as electron donor [uniprot-Q9NRD9-human-DUOX1-entry]. More recently, NOX and DUOX enzymes were found to oxidize NAADPH to the Ca2+-mobilizing second messenger NAADP. This activity matters in T cells, although there DUOX2 contributes more than DUOX1 to the early Ca2+ microdomains [gu-2021-duox-naadp-tcell-abstract].

The N-terminal extracellular peroxidase homology domain (PHD) gives "dual oxidase" its name [edens-2001-ef-hand-duox-abstract]. In *C. elegans*, Duox is needed for di- and trityrosine cross-linking of the cuticle, and its PHD binds heme covalently and has modest peroxidase activity [edens-2001-ef-hand-duox-abstract; meitzler-2009-peroxidase-domain-abstract]. An early report credited the human DUOX1 PHD with peroxidase activity [edens-2001-ef-hand-duox-abstract]. Later work using purified protein, however, showed that human DUOX1(1–593) neither binds heme nor has peroxidase or superoxide-dismutase activity [meitzler-2009-peroxidase-domain-abstract]. The cryo-EM maps agree: they show no heme density, and the positions of the heme-coordinating histidines are occupied by serines (Ser108 and Ser326 in mouse) [sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-fulltext; wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. In mammals, therefore, the PHD acts as a structural and regulatory module, not an enzyme. It packs onto the transmembrane domain, forms one of the interfaces with DUOXA1 (including Gln30, whose DUOX2 equivalent is mutated in congenital hypothyroidism), and helps form the enclosed cavity where H2O2 is made [sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-fulltext]. The automated GO annotations "peroxidase activity" and "heme binding" on the horse entry, inferred from InterPro signatures, should therefore be treated cautiously when they are applied to the PHD [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry; meitzler-2009-peroxidase-domain-abstract]. The heme-binding term is valid for the transmembrane domain.

## Regulation by calcium, phosphorylation and DUOXA1, and the EF-hand deletion in A0A9L0SQG9

DUOX1 activity is regulated mainly by cytosolic Ca2+. The intracellular loop between the first transmembrane segment and the catalytic transmembrane core carries a cryptic pleckstrin-homology-like domain followed by an EF-hand module. In human DUOX1, EF1 coordinates Ca2+ through D828, D830, N832, the backbone of Y834, and E839. EF2 coordinates Ca2+ through D864, D866, N868, the backbone of L870, and E875 [wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. Basal DUOX1 activity requires calcium and functional EF-hands [rigutto-2009-duox-pka-pkc-abstract]. Mutating any one of the EF-hand calcium ligands abolishes calcium activation, and the DUOX2 mutation equivalent to E875 causes congenital hypothyroidism [wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. Structurally, the Ca2+-loaded EF module wraps around helix α4 of the dehydrogenase domain through a hydrophobic interface (F768, F772, F807, F819, F840 and F847 on the EF side), in a way that resembles calcineurin B binding to calcineurin A. This holds the dehydrogenase domain in a conformation that allows electron transfer. When Ca2+ is removed, the cytosolic domains become mobile and electron transfer becomes less efficient [wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. Biophysical work on the isolated EF domain found a high-affinity EF2 (Ka ≈ 10^7 M−1) and a weaker EF1 (≈10^5 M−1), and showed that Ca2+ binding switches the domain from dimer to monomer and exposes hydrophobic patches [wei-2024-duox-efhand-abstract]. Structural reviews take this domain–domain stabilization as the general logic by which NOX activity is controlled [ogboo-2022-nox-architecture-abstract].

This is where the target isoform differs most. A0A9L0SQG9 lacks a 99-residue block corresponding to human 775–873. The block includes every EF1 ligand, the EF2 ligands D864, D866, N868 and L870, and most of the EF-module phenylalanines that contact the dehydrogenase domain. Only E875 (horse E776) remains [NOTES-sequence-analysis]. The PHLD region and the residues downstream of the EF module are kept. This includes the serine equivalent to human Ser955 (horse S856), which protein kinase A phosphorylates to activate DUOX1 after cAMP elevation (forskolin EC50 ≈ 0.1 µM in reconstituted cells), in contrast to DUOX2, which is regulated by PKC [rigutto-2009-duox-pka-pkc-abstract; NOTES-sequence-analysis]. Given the mutagenesis data, this horse product would be expected to lack Ca2+-dependent activation and to have little or no basal activity. A dominant-negative or regulatory role cannot be excluded, but nobody has tested this. The complete horse model F6YEX2 keeps all EF-hand ligands, so it is probably the main catalytically competent form [NOTES-sequence-analysis].

DUOX1 needs DUOXA1 to work. DUOXA proteins are ER-resident transmembrane proteins. They are required for DUOX to leave the ER, pass through the Golgi, acquire mature glycosylation and reach the plasma membrane, and without them transfected DUOX stays in the ER [grasberger-2006-duoxa-maturation-abstract]. In the structures, DUOXA1 stays bound to DUOX1 in the mature complex through extracellular, transmembrane and lipid-mediated interfaces [sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-fulltext]. Cell-biological work shows that DUOXA is more than a chaperone. Active DUOXA proteins travel to the cell surface together with DUOX; endogenous DUOXA1 is found on apical airway membranes; and only matched pairs (DUOX1 with the DUOXA1α splice variant, DUOX2 with DUOXA2) form stable surface complexes that make the most H2O2. Mismatched pairs make less H2O2 and leak superoxide, so DUOXA helps determine which oxidant is produced [morand-2009-duoxa-cell-surface-complexes-abstract]. Heterodimerization with DUOXA is required for any ROS production, and the DUOXA subunit steers the oxidase to particular membrane compartments [luxen-2009-duox-duoxa-heterodimerization-abstract]. Human DUOX1–DUOXA1 was solved as a symmetric 2:2 heterotetramer [wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-abstract]. Mouse DUOX1–DUOXA1 was seen both as heterodimers and as a "dimer-of-dimers" that seems to be inactive, which suggests that oligomerization is a further regulatory step [sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-abstract]. Other reported partners include the thioredoxin-related protein EFP1 (TXNDC11), found by yeast two-hybrid with the Duox1 EF-hand fragment as bait, as well as TPO and p22phox. None of these alters activity [wang-2005-txndc11-duox-abstract]. Unlike NOX1–4, DUOX activity does not depend on p22phox [amezianeelhassani-2005-duox1-h2o2-abstract]. In airway epithelium, DUOX1 is switched on physiologically when extracellular ATP is released in response to bacterial stimuli and acts through purinergic receptors and Ca2+ [boots-2009-atp-duox1-egfr-abstract]. DUOX1 transcription is induced by the Th2 cytokines IL-4 and IL-13 [harper-2005-duox-il4-il13-abstract].

## Subcellular localization and tissue distribution

DUOX1 works at the apical plasma membrane of polarized epithelia, with the peroxidase homology domain and the H2O2 exit site facing the lumen and the NADPH/FAD dehydrogenase domain facing the cytosol [dedeken-2000-cloning-thox-abstract; edens-2001-ef-hand-duox-abstract; wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. In thyrocytes, DUOX accumulates at the apical membrane together with TPO [dedeken-2000-cloning-thox-abstract]. In tracheal and bronchial surface epithelium, DUOX1 is the main isoform and staining is apical [geiszt-2003-duox-mucosal-host-defense-abstract; schwarzer-2004-duox-airway-abstract]. The protein carries an N-terminal signal peptide (horse residues 1–21), is N-glycosylated, and needs DUOXA1-dependent trafficking to get there. Immature forms remain in the ER, where they release superoxide instead of H2O2 [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry; amezianeelhassani-2005-duox1-h2o2-abstract; grasberger-2006-duoxa-maturation-abstract]. The horse entry's IEA annotation to plasma membrane, and its Phobius-predicted multipass topology, agree with this [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry]. Note that Phobius predicts only six TM helices for the horse target, versus seven annotated for human. This seems to come from the prediction rather than the sequence, since the region containing the first TM segment is present. In human, expression is highest in thyroid, trachea and bronchi, with lower levels in other tissues [uniprot-Q9NRD9-human-DUOX1-entry]. T cells contain small amounts of DUOX1 [gu-2021-duox-naadp-tcell-abstract]. I found no horse expression data in the literature. The UniProt entry links to the Expression Atlas baseline resource [uniprot-A0A9L0SQG9-entry].

## Biological pathways

Thyroid hormone biosynthesis. TPO iodinates thyroglobulin and couples iodotyrosines on the luminal side of the thyrocyte apical membrane, and it needs H2O2 as its oxidant. DUOX enzymes were cloned as the source of that H2O2 [dedeken-2000-cloning-thox-abstract]. Genetic evidence in humans points to DUOX2 as the main thyroid isoform. DUOX1 is activated by the TSH–cAMP–PKA pathway through Ser955 phosphorylation, and is therefore thought to add to hormonogenesis [rigutto-2009-duox-pka-pkc-abstract]. Because cAMP regulation and the PKA site are conserved, horse DUOX1 is predicted to serve the same role. The in vivo importance of the DUOX/DUOXA system for the thyroid is clear from mice lacking both Duoxa genes. In these mice DUOX proteins fail to mature (no Golgi processing of N-glycans), thyroid tissue releases no H2O2, and the animals develop severe goitrous congenital hypothyroidism with undetectable serum T4 [grasberger-2012-duoxa-knockout-hypothyroid-abstract]. This model removes both DUOX isoforms at once, so it does not isolate the contribution of DUOX1. That contribution is thought to be the residual iodide organification seen in patients who lack DUOX2 [grasberger-2012-duoxa-knockout-hypothyroid-abstract].

Mucosal innate defense through the lactoperoxidase system. On airway surfaces, DUOX1-derived H2O2 is the substrate LPO uses to oxidize thiocyanate to hypothiocyanite (OSCN−). OSCN− is antibacterial [geiszt-2003-duox-mucosal-host-defense-abstract]. On human, rat and bovine airway surfaces, epithelial DUOX produces enough H2O2 for LPO-generated OSCN− to kill *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* without harming the host. The system fails in cystic fibrosis epithelia because they do not secrete SCN− [moskwa-2007-airway-host-defense-cf-abstract]. The results in cow tracheal explants are worth noting here because they show the system operates in a large domestic herbivore. OSCN− also inactivates influenza virus by reducing viral binding and entry. Duox1-deficient mice have higher mortality, weaker viral clearance and reduced recruitment of innate immune cells after influenza infection [sarr-2021-duox1-influenza-abstract]. This in vivo result is the best evidence for a non-thyroid physiological role of DUOX1. Horses carry a substantial respiratory pathogen burden (including equine influenza), so the pathway is likely to matter in this species, though no one has studied it there directly. The system does not protect against everything. Duox1-knockout mice infected with *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* have the same bacterial burden and lung pathology as wild-type mice, differing only in some airway cytokines [gupta-2023-duox1-tuberculosis-fulltext]. Reviews of the DUOX–LPO literature list antibacterial, antiviral, antifungal and antiparasitic activities, most of them shown in vitro [sarr-2018-duox-lpo-review-abstract; donko-2005-dual-oxidases-review-abstract].

Redox signalling and wound responses. In airway epithelium, ATP released in response to bacteria activates DUOX1. The resulting intracellular oxidants activate ERK1/2 and NF-κB, ADAM17-dependent shedding of EGFR ligands, and IL-8/CXCL8 production [boots-2009-atp-duox1-egfr-abstract]. In zebrafish larvae, Duox produces a tissue-scale H2O2 gradient spreading 100–200 µm from a wound, which is required for rapid leukocyte recruitment. This shows that DUOX-derived H2O2 acts as a paracrine damage signal [niethammer-2009-zebrafish-wound-h2o2-abstract]. DUOX-dependent H2O2 production also contributes to intracellular acid generation and H+ secretion in airway epithelia [schwarzer-2004-duox-airway-abstract]. DUOX1 is also a central part of the airway epithelium's own repair programme. Epithelial injury releases ATP, which activates DUOX1 through purinergic receptors. DUOX1 is required for the ERK1/2 and MMP-9 activation and the cell migration that follow [wesley-2007-atp-duox1-migration-abstract]. Active DUOX1–DUOXA1 complexes concentrate at the leading edge of migrating lung epithelial cells [luxen-2009-duox-duoxa-heterodimerization-abstract]. In mice, silencing DUOX1 delays bronchiolar re-epithelialization after naphthalene injury, through loss of DUOX1-dependent EGFR and STAT3 activation [gorissen-2013-duox1-reepithelialization-abstract]. The same redox circuit is used in type 2 immunity: allergens such as *Alternaria* and house dust mite trigger DUOX1-dependent oxidation of cysteines in EGFR and Src, which activates calpain-2 and the rapid secretion of IL-33. DUOX1-deficient mice show weaker allergic type 2 responses [hristova-2016-duox1-il33-abstract]. Outside the airway, keratinocyte DUOX1 releases H2O2 when cytosolic Ca2+ rises. That H2O2 modulates neuronal TRPA1 and redox-sensitive K+ channels, and DUOX1-knockout mice are more sensitive to certain noxious stimuli, which points to a paracrine role in nociception [pato-2023-epidermal-duox1-nociception-fulltext]. In all of these signalling roles the active element is regulated, Ca2+-triggered H2O2 release, so the calcium-sensing EF-hand module that A0A9L0SQG9 lacks would be expected to matter. And through NAADP synthesis from NAADPH, DUOX enzymes take part in Ca2+ signalling during T-cell activation [gu-2021-duox-naadp-tcell-abstract].

## Evidence assessment for the horse protein

The evidence behind each statement about horse DUOX1 varies in strength. The catalytic activity (NAD(P)H-dependent, Ca2+-stimulated H2O2 production), the requirement for DUOXA1 and the apical plasma-membrane localization are well established by biochemistry and structural biology in human and mouse [amezianeelhassani-2005-duox1-h2o2-abstract; grasberger-2006-duoxa-maturation-abstract; sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-fulltext; wu-2021-human-duox1-structure-fulltext]. The need for DUOXA in vivo is also shown by the hypothyroid Duoxa-knockout mouse [grasberger-2012-duoxa-knockout-hypothyroid-abstract]. These properties transfer to horse by orthology, given one-to-one Ensembl orthology, a conserved head-to-head DUOX1–DUOXA1 locus [ensembl-horse-DUOX1-locus-summary], about 86–90% sequence identity, strong vertebrate conservation of DUOX, and conservation of every catalytic residue I checked [kawahara-2007-nox-evolution-abstract; NOTES-sequence-analysis]. The physiological roles in thyroid hormone synthesis, LPO-mediated host defense and wound and EGFR signalling come from human cells, mice and zebrafish [rigutto-2009-duox-pka-pkc-abstract; sarr-2021-duox1-influenza-abstract; niethammer-2009-zebrafish-wound-h2o2-abstract; boots-2009-atp-duox1-egfr-abstract], so for horse they are reasonable inferences rather than demonstrated facts. The specific accession A0A9L0SQG9 is an Ensembl prediction lacking the EF-hand calcium-sensing region. Its existence as a real transcript, whether it is translated, and whether it has any activity are all unverified. For GO curation, I suggest attaching the canonical DUOX1 functions (NAD(P)H oxidase H2O2-forming activity, apical plasma membrane, hormone biosynthesis/defense response) to the gene. For this isoform, "calcium ion binding" and Ca2+-regulated activity should not be inferred, and "peroxidase activity" should not be asserted for the ectodomain at all, since the mammalian PHD is catalytically inert [meitzler-2009-peroxidase-domain-abstract; sun-2020-mouse-duox1-structure-fulltext].

## Open Questions

Nothing about DUOX1 has been tested in the horse. The most useful first step would be to confirm the horse transcript structure with RNA-seq or long-read data from thyroid and airway epithelium. That would show whether the transcript lacking the EF-hand exons (encoding A0A9L0SQG9) actually exists, how abundant it is relative to the full-length F6YEX2 form, and whether it is tissue-specific. If it is real, co-expressing it with horse DUOXA1 in HEK293 cells, alongside the full-length isoform, would show whether it reaches the plasma membrane, whether it makes H2O2 without Ca2+ regulation or not at all, and whether it can interfere with the full-length enzyme in the 2:2 heterotetramer, for example as a dominant negative. Because the exon skipping that defines A0A9L0SQG9 is precise, RT-PCR across the skipped 226-nt exon and the neighbouring alternative acceptor site would be a cheap way to test whether this splice form exists. It would also be worth checking whether equivalent EF-hand-skipping transcripts occur in human or mouse, which would suggest a conserved regulatory isoform. The other truncated horse models (A0A9L0SA70 and A0A9L0SBC0) need the same check, because the A0A9L0SBC0 deletion overlaps the first transmembrane segment and could change topology. More broadly, the relative contributions of DUOX1 and DUOX2 to equine thyroid hormonogenesis, and the importance of the DUOX1–LPO–thiocyanate system in equine airway defense against equine influenza and bacterial pathogens such as *Streptococcus equi*, have not been studied. Questions that remain open for the enzyme in general include the exact mechanism of superoxide dismutation inside the enclosed extracellular cavity, the physiological trigger for the switch between the dimer-of-dimers and active complex, and the in vivo relevance of NADH and NAAD(P)H as alternative electron donors.
