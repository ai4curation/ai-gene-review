Gene Description

HSPD1 (Hsp60, mitochondrial chaperonin 60) is the mitochondrial member of the type I (GroEL-like) chaperonin family. In mammals it is imported into the mitochondrial matrix via an N-terminal transit peptide and assembles into heptameric rings that, with the HSPE1/Hsp10 co-chaperonin, form ATP-driven folding chambers that fold newly imported and stress-denatured matrix proteins. Each subunit has an equatorial ATP-binding domain, an intermediate hinge domain that couples nucleotide state to movement, and an apical substrate- and Hsp10-binding domain. The horse HSPD1 gene on chromosome 18 encodes this chaperonin; this UniProt entry (F6Z587, 541 aa) is an alternative gene model that skips one 96-bp coding exon and so lacks 32 residues of the intermediate hinge domain (human P10809 residues 171-202), while keeping the transit peptide, the equatorial ATP-binding residues and the apical domain. Whether a product of this model can cycle as a chaperonin is untested. The full-length horse product is represented by A0A9L0S8L8.

Core Functions

Gene-level function of horse HSPD1, inherited from the PTHR45633 PAINT nodes PTN000143677 (protein folding) and PTN000143509 (mitochondrial matrix): ATP-dependent protein folding chaperone that, with the Hsp10 co-chaperonin, folds imported and stress-denatured proteins in the mitochondrial matrix. The full-length horse product is A0A9L0S8L8; this F6Z587 model lacks 32 residues of the intermediate hinge domain, and whether its product is competent for the folding cycle is unresolved.

HSPD1 evidence notes

PMID:1346131(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1346131/) reconstitutes mammalian Hsp60/Hsp10 substrate folding with MgATP; PMID:25918392(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25918392/), DOI 10.1073/pnas.1411718112, solves the human type I chaperonin complex. These establish the distinction from the cytosolic type II TRiC/CCT system, independently of an AI review or ARBA.

Horse F6Z587 has 541 residues versus 573 for human P10809, with an internal deletion of human 171–202. The N-terminal mitochondrial precursor and annotated ATP-binding positions are conserved. Broad ATP binding and mitochondrial targeting remain reasonable inferences, but full ATP-dependent folding competence is uncertain for this model. The existing horse ARBA GO:0005832 TRiC-complex annotation is wrong at family/complex level regardless of whether this deletion preserves Hsp60 activity.

PMID:17823127(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17823127/) shows that cytosolic Hsp60 can promote death or survival in different apoptotic systems, with different caspase-3 interactions. Do not remove opposing apoptotic-direction annotations solely because they share a reference. Immune responses to extracellular preparations require their own preparation/contamination and receptor-dependence controls; those rows remain UNDECIDED rather than being accepted as a universal Hsp60 cytokine activity or rejected from localization alone.

A completed human Falcon report has been inspected. The selected horse record is unreviewed; the initial literature search did not identify a targeted horse HSPD1 mechanistic experiment warranting a separate horse Edison report.

Human structural research

The completed Falcon report identifies PMID:32317635(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32317635/), DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-15698-8, which establishes active single- and double-ring human Hsp60–Hsp10 assemblies. It also identifies PMID:31444388(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31444388/), DOI:10.1038/s41598-019-48762-5, on impaired client refolding by disease variants. These primary identities were independently verified. ATP turnover and productive folding are distinct readouts, strengthening the caution about the selected horse deletion. The earlier manual synthesis is retained as separately labeled primary-source analysis.

Identity of the benchmark accession (alternative gene model)

  • F6Z587 (541 aa; ENSECAT00000009419 / ENSECAP00000007167) is an exon-skipping alternative model of horse HSPD1. It omits the 96-bp coding exon at chr18:72882811-72882906 that is present in the Ensembl canonical transcript ENSECAT00000137290 [file:HORSE/HSPD1/HSPD1-bioinformatics/RESULTS-rereview.md]. The skip removes human P10809 residues 171-202 (VATISANGDKEIGNIISDAMKKVGRKGVITVK) in frame.
  • In mature numbering the gap is 145-176, entirely within the first segment of the intermediate hinge domain PMID:32317635. Transit peptide, equatorial ATP-binding residues and the apical domain are retained; 527/541 paired residues are identical to human.
  • Human P10809 has no equivalent isoform (its isoform 2 is a different C-terminal truncation).
  • The canonical horse product is A0A9L0S8L8 (587 aa; ENSECAP00000072491): every human residue (559/573 identical) plus a 14-aa N-terminal extension MKAHLTKRNSASSE before the MLRLP start. UniProt gives it the PAINT IBA rows (GO_Central) that F6Z587 lacks. A third model, A0A9L0TMN1 (474 aa), is C-terminally truncated.

Evolutionary grounding

  • PANTHER PTHR45633 (Cpn60/GroEL; includes the divergent yeast Tcm62 in SF33). Horse F6Z587 has no subfamily in the local member index. Human P10809 is in SF17 in the PANTHER 19.0 human file, whereas mouse, rat, cow, chicken, frog, zebrafish, fly, worm and yeast HSP60 orthologs are all in SF3. This looks like a subfamily-HMM boundary quirk rather than a real split, since P10809 and MGI:96242 seed the same PAINT nodes.
  • PAINT: protein folding (GO:0006457) IBD at PTN000143677 (GroEL, M. tuberculosis GroEL1/2, yeast HSP60, human); mitochondrial matrix and protein-folding chaperone binding at PTN000143509; UPRmt and IMS import at PTN000143510 (Opisthokonta); immune-regulation terms at PTN002900230 (Euteleostomi, seeded by human and mouse). Horse HSPD1 is inside all of these clades (the canonical model receives them as IBA).
  • The CCT/TRiC complex is never propagated to PTHR45633. Type II chaperonins are a different family (TCP-1).

Decisions

  • GO:0005524 ATP binding: ACCEPT. The equatorial site is intact and mapped residues are identical.
  • GO:0005759 mitochondrial matrix: ACCEPT. The transit peptide is retained, and the location is an IBD at PTN000143509.
  • GO:0005832 chaperonin-containing T-complex (ARBA): REMOVE. This is the wrong chaperonin type.
  • GO:0006457, GO:0042026, GO:0140662 (folding/refolding/ATP-dependent chaperone): UNDECIDED. These are the gene's core function, but this product lacks 32/54 residues of the first hinge-domain segment. Even point variants that destabilise the nucleotide-triggered cycle abolish folding PMID:31444388. I have no expression evidence for the skip transcript. The earlier review had the same actions; the reasoning now rests on the exon structure and domain mapping.
  • GO:0009409 response to cold (ISS from chicken Q5ZL72): changed UNDECIDED -> KEEP_AS_NON_CORE. The donor experiment is now resolved: PMID:23636703 reports Hsp60 mRNA induction in cold-stressed chicken heart PMID:23636703. This is a gene-level stress-induction observation and matches the human review.
  • No NEW rows. The IBA-supported terms carried by the canonical model (UPRmt, immune-regulation terms) belong to A0A9L0S8L8 and depend on a functional chaperonin.
  • Core function: written at the gene level, with an explicit caveat that the competence of the F6Z587 product is unresolved.

HSPD1: ProtNLM function-text review

PLI (score 0): Hsp60 type I chaperonin is assigned the cytosolic TRiC/CCT type II complex and its clients.

Original prediction

ProtNLM F6Z587, frozen API snapshot 2026-09-08.

Component of the chaperonin-containing T-complex (TRiC), a molecular chaperone complex that assists the folding of proteins upon ATP hydrolysis. The TRiC complex mediates the folding of WRAP53/TCAB1, thereby regulating telomere maintenance. As part of the TRiC complex may play a role in the assembly of BBSome, a complex involved in ciliogenesis regulating transports vesicles to the cilia. The TRiC complex plays a role in the folding of actin and tubulin

The horse sequence comparison preserves the precursor region and 97.4% identity across 541 paired residues. It identifies an internal 32 aa deletion, so active Hsp60 folding is not simply presumed for this exact model.

The existing horse GOA TRiC annotation comes from ARBA and repeats the same wrong complex identity. Its agreement with ProtNLM is an error-agreement observation, not validation. PARALOG_OVERANNOTATION captures over-transfer between related chaperonin branches. The model's exact training donor is unknown. The review distinguishes shared general chaperone chemistry from specific complex/client claims and from unresolved effects of the selected protein model.
