Gene Description

OMA1 is a stress-activated, ATP-independent zinc metalloendopeptidase of the mitochondrial inner membrane (peptidase family M48), whose catalytic HExxH...E centre faces the intermembrane space. Horse OMA1 sits in the same PANTHER subfamily (PTHR22726:SF1) as the experimentally studied human, mouse and yeast OMA1 proteins, and retains their transmembrane segment and all three zinc ligands and catalytic glutamate. In mammals OMA1 is held largely inactive under basal conditions and is switched on by membrane depolarization, ATP depletion, oxidative stress or BAX/BAK activation. It then cleaves the dynamin-like GTPase OPA1 at the S1 site, converting long OPA1 into soluble short forms; this suppresses inner-membrane fusion and remodels cristae, which helps release cytochrome c during apoptosis. OMA1 also cleaves DELE1, and the released DELE1 fragment activates the eIF2alpha kinase HRI in the cytosol, so OMA1 relays mitochondrial stress into the integrated stress response. The yeast founding member degrades misfolded inner-membrane proteins, which places OMA1 in the inner-membrane protein quality-control system. The accession reviewed here is the UniProt gene-centric canonical product of horse OMA1 (ENSECAG00000009131). Its C-terminal region, from the residue that corresponds to human 401, does not match OMA1 from other species and lacks the segment that carries the conserved Cys407 and the C-terminal autocatalytic processing region, so the product of this particular gene model may not be fully functional.

  zinc ligands and catalytic glutamate. In mammals OMA1 is held largely
  inactive under basal conditions and is switched on by membrane
  depolarization, ATP depletion, oxidative stress or BAX/BAK activation. It
  then cleaves the dynamin-like GTPase OPA1 at the S1 site, converting long
  OPA1 into soluble short forms; this suppresses inner-membrane fusion and
  remodels cristae, which helps release cytochrome c during apoptosis. OMA1
  also cleaves DELE1, and the released DELE1 fragment activates the eIF2alpha
  kinase HRI in the cytosol, so OMA1 relays mitochondrial stress into the
  integrated stress response. The yeast founding member degrades misfolded
  inner-membrane proteins, which places OMA1 in the inner-membrane protein
  quality-control system. The accession reviewed here is the UniProt
  gene-centric canonical product of horse OMA1 (ENSECAG00000009131). Its
  C-terminal region, from the residue that corresponds to human 401, does not
  match OMA1 from other species and lacks the segment that carries the
  conserved Cys407 and the C-terminal autocatalytic processing region, so the
  product of this particular gene model may not be fully functional.
