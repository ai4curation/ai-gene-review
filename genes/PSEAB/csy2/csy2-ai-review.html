Gene Description

Csy2, also called Cas5f, is the Cas5-family subunit of the Csy crRNA-guided surveillance complex of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa UCBPP-PA14 type I-F (Yersinia-subtype) CRISPR-Cas system. The complex comprises one Cas8f/Csy1, one Cas5f/Csy2, six Cas7f/Csy3 and one Cas6f/Csy4 wrapped around a single 60-nucleotide crRNA in a helically twisted G shape; Csy2 sits with Cas8f in the PAM-proximal tail. Its defining job is to cap and read the 5' handle of the crRNA: together with Cas8f it makes base-specific contacts that bend the first eight nucleotides into an S shape and fix the register of the guide within the complex, and its thumb domain also contributes to the pocket that grips the protospacer-adjacent-motif-containing target duplex. Csy2 is one of the two subunits strictly required for mature crRNA to accumulate in the cell, since an unincorporated guide is degraded, and loss of it therefore abolishes both target interference and the acquisition of new spacers.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex

Definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction. The Pseudomonas aeruginosa type I-F complex, Csy, has the composition Cas8f(1)/Cas5f(1)/Cas7f(6)/Cas6f(1) with one 60-nucleotide crRNA; the type I-E equivalent is Cascade.

Justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic child GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models for the type I-E case (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and the Cas7f backbone in particular is the surface targeted by several families of phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible. This is the same request made from the Escherichia coli type I-E Cascade subunit reviews, so that the two systems ask for one term rather than two.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Does Cas5f protect the mature crRNA directly, by occluding its 5' end from exonucleases, or indirectly, by being required for the complex to assemble at all? The two predict different fates for a crRNA bound by a Cas7f-Cas6f subcomplex in a csy2 null.

Q: Cas5f reads the crRNA 5' handle sequence-specifically. Does that make the repeat-derived handle a second specificity determinant alongside the protospacer-adjacent motif, and would handle mutations be tolerated by engineered guides in this system?

Q: Type I-E Cas5 (casD) is annotated with DNA/RNA hybrid binding. Does PA14 Cas5f also contact the crRNA-target heteroduplex, or is that role carried entirely by the Cas7f thumbs in type I-F?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Compare small-RNA northern blots and 5'-end sequencing of a PA14 csy2 deletion with a csy4 H29A catalytic mutant, in strains with and without major cytoplasmic ribonucleases depleted. Processing-competent but unprotected intermediates in the csy2 strain would separate the two models.

Hypothesis: Cas5f is required for crRNA stability rather than for processing, so a csy2 null accumulates cleaved but unprotected crRNA that is degraded.

Type: RNA stability and processing analysis

Experiment: Mutate the handle nucleotides that contact Cas5f and, reciprocally, the Cas5f residues that read them, and measure Csy complex assembly, crRNA loading and plasmid-targeting efficiency for each pair. Compensatory double mutants would establish the contacts as a direct specificity code.

Hypothesis: Base-specific Cas5f contacts to the crRNA 5' handle are required for correct guide register and for interference.

Type: reciprocal mutagenesis with targeting assay

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: Whether Cas5f contributes to target DNA engagement in its own right, beyond donating one thumb residue to a pocket dominated by Cas8f, is undetermined. No Cas5f DNA-contact mutant has been tested, and no term is asserted for a Cas5f DNA-binding activity.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: Established: the Cas5f thumb is part of the structural sandwich around the PAM-containing duplex, and the type I-E Cas5 orthologue casD carries DNA/RNA hybrid binding rather than DNA binding. Not established: whether isolated Cas5f binds DNA, or whether its thumb contacts are load-bearing for target engagement.

Significance: Distinguishing structural proximity from functional contribution is exactly what separates a defensible subunit annotation from an over-annotation, and the answer determines whether Cas5f should carry a DNA-binding term at all.

What would resolve it: Test Cas5f thumb substitutions (for example R90A) for target binding and interference in an otherwise wild-type complex, and assay isolated Cas5f for DNA binding.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "Numerous positively charged residues line this DNA binding pocket, including residues from Cas7.6f (K299), the Cas5f thumb (R90), and especially the Cas8f hook (R24, K28, K31, R59, K71 and R78)" — PMID:28985564

Gap: GO has no molecular-function term for crRNA-guided, PAM-licensed target recognition, the activity the assembled Csy complex has, so Cas5f can only be annotated with generic nucleic-acid-binding terms.

OPEN ONTOLOGY

What is known: The nuclease chemistry of the system is covered by existing terms and the structural steps are resolved to near-atomic detail; what is missing is a term for the guide dependence that defines the system.

Significance: Without such a term the defining property of every Cas effector is invisible to GO-based reasoning.

What would resolve it: Request a molecular-function term for guide-RNA-dependent nucleic acid recognition, as proposed in modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml. No existing id has been substituted for the missing concept here.

  • "the Csy proteins (Csy1-4) assemble into a 350 kDa ribonucleoprotein complex that facilitates target recognition by enhancing sequence-specific hybridization between the CRISPR RNA and complementary target sequences" — PMID:21536913

What Cas5f does

PMID:32170016, and critically the contacts are base-specific, not backbone-only:
PMID:32170016.

Contributes a thumb to the target-duplex pocket.

PMID:28985564. Note the "especially": the DNA contacts are

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex
  proposed_definition: >-
    A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins
    assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as
    a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type
    I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif,
    forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for
    target destruction. The Pseudomonas aeruginosa type I-F complex, Csy, has the
    composition Cas8f(1)/Cas5f(1)/Cas7f(6)/Cas6f(1) with one 60-nucleotide crRNA;
    the type I-E equivalent is Cascade.

  justification: >-
    GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048,

    cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance
    complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its
    generic child GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined,
