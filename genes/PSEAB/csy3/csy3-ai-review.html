Gene Description

Csy3, also called Cas7f, is the backbone subunit of the Csy crRNA-guided surveillance complex of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa UCBPP-PA14 type I-F (Yersinia-subtype) CRISPR-Cas system. Six copies oligomerise head to tail along the spacer portion of a single 60-nucleotide crRNA, forming the helical spiral between the Cas6f head and the Cas8f-Cas5f tail. Each copy grips the guide through an arginine-rich surface that contacts the sugar-phosphate backbone rather than the bases, so recognition is sequence-independent, and a thumb from each subunit kinks the crRNA at six-nucleotide intervals, dividing the guide into the short segments that are presented for base pairing. When a complementary target strand invades, a beta-hairpin from the adjacent subunit threads through each kink and pins the crRNA-DNA heteroduplex to the backbone, so Cas7f holds the hybrid while target verification proceeds along the guide. A lysine-rich patch on the same surface is critical for the complex to bind target DNA, and that patch is the principal target surface of the phage anti-CRISPR proteins AcrIF8 and AcrIF9.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex

Definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction. The Pseudomonas aeruginosa type I-F complex, Csy, has the composition Cas8f(1)/Cas5f(1)/Cas7f(6)/Cas6f(1) with one 60-nucleotide crRNA; the type I-E equivalent is Cascade.

Justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic child GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models for the type I-E case (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and the Cas7f backbone in particular is the surface targeted by several families of phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible. This is the same request made from the Escherichia coli type I-E Cascade subunit reviews, so that the two systems ask for one term rather than two.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: Cas7f recognises the crRNA sequence-independently but pins the heteroduplex sequence-dependently through the kinks. Should GO distinguish these as separate functions of the same surface, given that one is a scaffolding contact and the other is the verification contact?

Q: AcrIF8 contacts both the Cas7f backbone and the crRNA at a kink, while AcrIF9 competes for the lysine patch. Do the six Cas7f copies differ functionally by position, such that occluding one specific subunit is enough to block verification?

Q: Csy3 is not strictly required for crRNA accumulation yet a csy3 deletion abolishes spacer acquisition. Is the acquisition defect due to loss of target engagement by the complex, or does the Cas7f backbone have a further role in generating the adaptation substrate?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Build Cas7f K76A, K78A, K84A and K256A variants, confirm Csy complex assembly and crRNA loading, then measure target DNA binding by electrophoretic mobility shift and Cas3-dependent cleavage in the reconstituted system. Compare the loss of function with that caused by saturating AcrIF9.

Hypothesis: The lysine-rich Cas7f patch is required for target DNA binding and not merely for complex assembly, so patch mutants give an assembled but targeting-dead complex.

Type: structure-guided mutagenesis with reconstituted binding and cleavage assays

Experiment: Delete or shorten the threading beta-hairpin in individual Cas7f positions using a mosaic complex assembled from differently tagged subunits, and use single-molecule DNA unzipping or magnetic-tweezer R-loop assays to ask whether verification stalls at the modified segment.

Hypothesis: Heteroduplex pinning by the Cas7f beta-hairpins enforces directional target verification, so hairpin mutants permit binding but not licensing of Cas3.

Type: single-molecule R-loop assay with mosaic complexes

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: GO has no molecular-function term for crRNA-guided, PAM-licensed target recognition, the activity the assembled Csy complex has and in which the Cas7f backbone does the segment-by-segment base-pairing work. The terms that can be asserted (RNA binding, DNA/RNA hybrid binding) do not distinguish this protein from an unguided nucleic-acid-binding protein.

OPEN ONTOLOGY

What is known: The structural mechanism is resolved to near-atomic detail, including the kink spacing, the hairpin threading, the helical pitch change on target binding, and the lysine patch required for DNA engagement. What is missing is a term for the guide dependence that defines the system.

Significance: The Cas7f backbone is the host surface that most type I-F anti-CRISPR proteins attack, so the absence of a term for what that surface does leaves both sides of a well-characterised arms race unstatable in GO.

What would resolve it: Request a molecular-function term for guide-RNA-dependent nucleic acid recognition, as proposed in modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml and modules/anti_crispr_suppression.yaml. No existing id has been substituted for the missing concept here.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "a lysine-rich region of the Cas7f subunit (K76, K78, K84, and K256) has been reported to be critical for DNA binding by the Csy complex" — PMID:32170016
  • "the Csy proteins (Csy1-4) assemble into a 350 kDa ribonucleoprotein complex that facilitates target recognition by enhancing sequence-specific hybridization between the CRISPR RNA and complementary target sequences" — PMID:21536913

Gap: Whether the lysine-rich Cas7f patch binds target DNA directly, or only as part of the complex-level DNA-binding surface, has not been separated experimentally; isolated Cas7f has not been assayed for DNA binding.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: Established: the patch is required for DNA binding by the complex, and it is the surface AcrIF8 and AcrIF9 occupy. Not established: whether a Cas7f monomer binds DNA, and whether the requirement is for affinity or for correct heteroduplex geometry.

Significance: This determines whether Cas7f should eventually carry a DNA-binding term in its own right or only contributes_to one, and it is the same distinction that separates competitive from allosteric anti-CRISPR mechanisms.

What would resolve it: Assay purified Cas7f and the Cas7f-Cas6f-crRNA subcomplex for DNA binding, and test lysine-patch substitutions for target affinity versus R-loop geometry in the complete complex.

Not proposed, and why

  • GO:0003677 DNA binding. The lysine-rich patch is described as critical for DNA
    binding by the Csy complex, which is a contributes-to statement about the complex,
    not a demonstration that isolated Cas7f binds DNA. Comparator: E. coli casC does

    contributes_to_molecular_function instead.
  • GO:0051607 defense response to virus. No csy3 allele has been assayed for phage
    resistance, and the PA14 locus-level evidence is contested (see cas3-notes.md).
  • No molecular-function term exists for crRNA-guided target recognition; recorded as an

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex
  proposed_definition: >-
    A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins
    assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as
    a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type
    I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif,
    forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for
    target destruction. The Pseudomonas aeruginosa type I-F complex, Csy, has the
    composition Cas8f(1)/Cas5f(1)/Cas7f(6)/Cas6f(1) with one 60-nucleotide crRNA;
    the type I-E equivalent is Cascade.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:1990904
    label: ribonucleoprotein complex
  justification: >-
    GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048,
    GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no
    cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance
    complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its
    generic child GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined,
    stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal
    already models for the type I-E case (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and the
    Cas7f backbone in particular is the surface targeted by several families of
    phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large
    body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible. This is the same
    request made from the Escherichia coli type I-E Cascade subunit reviews, so
    that the two systems ask for one term rather than two.
