Gene Description

Csy1, also called Cas8f, is the large subunit of the Csy crRNA-guided surveillance complex of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa UCBPP-PA14 type I-F (Yersinia-subtype) CRISPR-Cas system. The complex comprises one Cas8f/Csy1, one Cas5f/Csy2, six Cas7f/Csy3 and one Cas6f/Csy4 wrapped around a single 60-nucleotide crRNA in a helically twisted G shape; Csy1 occupies the PAM-proximal tail together with Cas5f. It has three assignable jobs there. Its N-terminal hook domain, lined with basic residues, forms the pocket that grips the protospacer-adjacent-motif-containing target DNA duplex; a central loop known as the lysine wedge inserts into the duplex fork, reads the motif through minor-groove contacts and splits the strands so the crRNA can base pair with the target strand; and together with Cas5f it clamps the sequence-specific 5' handle of the crRNA that sets the guide register. Completion of base pairing along the whole guide then licenses recruitment of the Cas3 helicase-nuclease to the Csy1 C-terminal helical bundle, the step at which the phage anti-CRISPR protein AcrIF3 intervenes from the Cas3 side.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex

Definition: A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif, forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for target destruction. The Pseudomonas aeruginosa type I-F complex, Csy, has the composition Cas8f(1)/Cas5f(1)/Cas7f(6)/Cas6f(1) with one 60-nucleotide crRNA; the type I-E equivalent is Cascade.

Justification: GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048, GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its generic child GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined, stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal already models for the type I-E case (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and the Cas7f backbone in particular is the surface targeted by several families of phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible. This is the same request made from the Escherichia coli type I-E Cascade subunit reviews, so that the two systems ask for one term rather than two.

Suggested Questions for Experts

Q: GO has no term for crRNA-guided target recognition. Should such a term be placed on the surveillance complex as a whole, with subunits as part_of, or should individual subunits carry contributes_to? Cas8f is the hardest case, because PAM reading is crRNA-independent while the rest of recognition is not.

Q: Which Cas8f surface does Cas3 engage, and is the recruitment signal a specific conformational change in the C-terminal bundle or the global helical elongation of the complex on target binding?

Q: PA14 disruption of csy1 lowers but does not abolish crRNA, while disruption of csy2 abolishes it. Does Cas8f protect the 5' handle from degradation only redundantly with Cas5f, or does it act later, at loading rather than at stabilisation?

Suggested Experiments

Experiment: Build PA14 strains carrying Cas8f K247A, Q249A and N250A substitutions, confirm Csy complex assembly and crRNA loading by pull-down and northern blot, then measure plasmid-targeting and phage-restriction efficiency. This separates the PAM-reading function from complex integrity.

Hypothesis: The Cas8f lysine wedge is required for target binding in vivo as well as for duplex splitting in vitro, and a wedge mutant produces a complex that is assembly competent but interference dead.

Type: structure-guided mutagenesis with in vivo targeting assay

Experiment: Crosslink and mass-spectrometry map the Cas8f-Cas3 interface on the target-bound complex, then test candidate interface substitutions for loss of Cas3-dependent DNA degradation in the reconstituted assay while retaining target binding.

Hypothesis: Cas3 is recruited through the Cas8f C-terminal helical bundle, and masking that bundle phenocopies AcrF3.

Type: crosslinking mass spectrometry with reconstituted cleavage assay

Knowledge Gaps

What is not known — curated, literature-grounded statements of the open unknowns (the inverse of core functions).

Gap: GO has no molecular-function term for crRNA-guided, PAM-licensed target recognition, which is the activity the assembled Csy complex has and which Cas8f licenses. The component activities that can be annotated (DNA binding, RNA binding) do not distinguish this protein from any unguided nucleic-acid-binding protein.

OPEN ONTOLOGY

What is known: The chemistry on either side is covered: the nuclease activities of Cas3 and Cas6f have terms, and the structural steps (PAM reading, duplex splitting, R-loop formation, directional target verification) are resolved to near-atomic detail. What is missing is a term for the guide dependence that defines the system.

Significance: Without such a term the defining property of every Cas effector is invisible to GO-based reasoning, and the host target of a whole class of phage anti-CRISPR proteins cannot be stated.

What would resolve it: Request a molecular-function term for guide-RNA-dependent nucleic acid recognition, with children for RNA-guided DNA endonuclease activity and RNA-guided RNA endonuclease activity, as proposed in modules/crispr_cas_adaptive_immunity.yaml. No existing id has been substituted for the missing concept here.

Provenance (the field's own admissions):

  • "the Csy proteins (Csy1-4) assemble into a 350 kDa ribonucleoprotein complex that facilitates target recognition by enhancing sequence-specific hybridization between the CRISPR RNA and complementary target sequences" — PMID:21536913
  • "The placement of this loop, which we refer to as the lysine-containing wedge (or K-wedge), would sterically block a 1TS-1NTS base pair" — PMID:28985564

Gap: How Cas8f signals a verified target to Cas3 is undetermined. The docking site is placed on the Cas8f C-terminal helical bundle only by inference, and no structure of a Cas8f-Cas3 interface exists for this system.

OPEN BIOLOGY

What is known: Established: R-loop completion is the trigger, Cas3 is the recruited nuclease, and the AcrF3 structure shows which Cas3 surfaces are masked to prevent recruitment. Not established: the reciprocal Cas8f surface, and whether the large helical elongation the complex undergoes on DNA binding is itself the recruitment signal.

Significance: This is the committed step of type I-F interference and the step one of the best-characterised anti-CRISPR proteins blocks; the host side of that inhibition cannot be modelled without it.

What would resolve it: Determine a structure of the target-bound Csy complex with Cas3 engaged, and map the interface genetically with Cas8f C-terminal bundle substitutions.

What this protein is

PA14_33330, 434 aa, "CRISPR-associated protein Csy1" = Cas8f, the large subunit of
the type I-F surveillance complex. It occupies the PAM-proximal tail:
PMID:32170016, in a complex of defined composition
PMID:32170016. The original biochemistry on the PA14 proteins:
PMID:21536913. GOA's two
GO:0005515 rows are the IntAct Csy1–Csy2 pair (Q02ML9–Q02MM0, NbExp=6) from that work
and from PMID:26416740.

The structures behind the claims below are of the P. aeruginosa complex:
PMID:28985564.

What Cas8f actually does

This is the structural-subunit problem, but Cas8f is not a passive scaffold — it has
three assignable jobs, all resolved in cryo-EM of the PA14 complex.

1. Binds the PAM-containing target DNA duplex.
PMID:28985564, with the pocket dominated by Cas8f side chains:
PMID:28985564.

2. Splits the duplex and reads the PAM. A Cas8f loop wedges into the fork:
PMID:28985564 and
PMID:28985564. Independently
confirmed as functionally required:
PMID:32170016.

3. Anchors the crRNA 5' handle, sequence-specifically.
PMID:32170016, and
PMID:32170016.

4. Recruits Cas3.
PMID:32170016. This is the function AcrIF3 neutralises from the
Cas3 side (PMID:27455460, read with PMID:26416740).

In vivo

Csy1 is required for the phage-dependent phenotype but, unlike Csy2 and Csy4, it is not
strictly required for crRNA accumulation:
PMID:21398535. UniProt records a reduction rather than a loss
("Decreased production of crRNA"). This asymmetry is why I propose GO:0043571 for
csy2 but not for csy1.

The complete Csy complex is required for adaptation as well as interference:
PMID:26586803 — but the gene-level alleles tested in
that study were csy3 and csy4, not csy1, so I do not transfer an IMP to Csy1.

description: >-
  Csy1, also called Cas8f, is the large subunit of the Csy crRNA-guided
  surveillance complex of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa UCBPP-PA14 type I-F
  (Yersinia-subtype) CRISPR-Cas system. The complex comprises one Cas8f/Csy1,
  one Cas5f/Csy2, six Cas7f/Csy3 and one Cas6f/Csy4 wrapped around a single
  60-nucleotide crRNA in a helically twisted G shape; Csy1 occupies the
  PAM-proximal tail together with Cas5f. It has three assignable jobs there.
  Its N-terminal hook domain, lined with basic residues, forms the pocket that
  grips the protospacer-adjacent-motif-containing target DNA duplex; a central
  loop known as the lysine wedge inserts into the duplex fork, reads the motif
  through minor-groove contacts and splits the strands so the crRNA can base
  pair with the target strand; and together with Cas5f it clamps the
  sequence-specific 5' handle of the crRNA that sets the guide register.
  Completion of base pairing along the whole guide then licenses recruitment of
  the Cas3 helicase-nuclease to the Csy1 C-terminal helical bundle, the step at
  which the phage anti-CRISPR protein AcrIF3 intervenes from the Cas3 side.

proposed_new_terms:
- proposed_name: CRISPR RNA-guided surveillance complex
  proposed_definition: >-
    A ribonucleoprotein complex composed of CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins
    assembled around a single mature CRISPR RNA (crRNA), which uses the crRNA as
    a guide to locate nucleic acid complementary to its spacer sequence. In type
    I systems the complex additionally recognises a protospacer-adjacent motif,
    forms an R-loop at the target, and recruits a trans-acting nuclease for
    target destruction. The Pseudomonas aeruginosa type I-F complex, Csy, has the
    composition Cas8f(1)/Cas5f(1)/Cas7f(6)/Cas6f(1) with one 60-nucleotide crRNA;
    the type I-E equivalent is Cascade.
  proposed_parent:
    id: GO:1990904
    label: ribonucleoprotein complex
  justification: >-
    GO's entire CRISPR vocabulary is five biological-process terms (GO:0099048,
    GO:0043571, GO:0098672 and the obsolete GO:0110132 and GO:0110133), with no
    cellular-component term, so every subunit of every CRISPR surveillance
    complex can currently only be placed on the bare GO:0032991 root or on its
    generic child GO:1990904. The complex is a well-defined,
    stoichiometrically determined, structurally solved entity that ComplexPortal
    already models for the type I-E case (CPX-1005, Cascade complex), and the
    Cas7f backbone in particular is the surface targeted by several families of
    phage-encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, so a specific term would make a large
    body of structural and counter-defence curation expressible. This is the same
    request made from the Escherichia coli type I-E Cascade subunit reviews, so
    that the two systems ask for one term rather than two.
  supported_by:
  - reference_id: PMID:32170016
    supporting_text: The Csy complex is comprised of four types of Cas proteins (Cas5f-8f)
      and a single 60-nt crRNA
  - reference_id: PMID:21536913
    supporting_text: the Csy proteins (Csy1-4) assemble into a 350 kDa ribonucleoprotein
      complex that facilitates target recognition by enhancing sequence-specific hybridization
      between the CRISPR RNA and complementary target sequences
suggested_questions:
- question: >-
    GO has no term for crRNA-guided target recognition. Should such a term be
    placed on the surveillance complex as a whole, with subunits as part_of, or
    should individual subunits carry contributes_to? Cas8f is the hardest case,
    because PAM reading is crRNA-independent while the rest of recognition is not.
- question: >-
    Which Cas8f surface does Cas3 engage, and is the recruitment signal a
    specific conformational change in the C-terminal bundle or the global
    helical elongation of the complex on target binding?
- question: >-
    PA14 disruption of csy1 lowers but does not abolish crRNA, while disruption
    of csy2 abolishes it. Does Cas8f protect the 5' handle from degradation only
    redundantly with Cas5f, or does it act later, at loading rather than at
    stabilisation?
suggested_experiments:
- hypothesis: >-
    The Cas8f lysine wedge is required for target binding in vivo as well as for
    duplex splitting in vitro, and a wedge mutant produces a complex that is
    assembly competent but interference dead.
  description: >-
    Build PA14 strains carrying Cas8f K247A, Q249A and N250A substitutions,
    confirm Csy complex assembly and crRNA loading by pull-down and northern
    blot, then measure plasmid-targeting and phage-restriction efficiency. This
    separates the PAM-reading function from complex integrity.
  experiment_type: structure-guided mutagenesis with in vivo targeting assay
- hypothesis: >-
    Cas3 is recruited through the Cas8f C-terminal helical bundle, and masking
    that bundle phenocopies AcrF3.
  description: >-
    Crosslink and mass-spectrometry map the Cas8f-Cas3 interface on the

    target binding.
