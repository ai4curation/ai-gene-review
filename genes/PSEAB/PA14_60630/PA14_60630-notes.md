# PA14_60630 (A0A0H2ZHM6) review notes

## Why this gene

Reviewed after the Whiteley lab preprint [DOI:10.64898/2026.11.20.768214 "Homologs of
the Major Vault Protein defend bacteria against ribotoxins"], which gives the first
function for any vault-like protein in any organism. It was reviewed alongside the
existing vault work in this repository: `genes/human/MVP`, `genes/mouse/Mvp`,
`genes/rat/Mvp`, `genes/DICDI/mvpA`, `genes/DICDI/mvpB`, `genes/TRYB2/Tb927.5.4460`,
`genes/TRYB2/Tb927.10.1990` and `interpro/panther/PTHR14165`.

## Deep research

No deep-research provider was available (no API keys in this environment; falcon and
perplexity both fail). Sources were gathered by hand:

- The preprint PDF, fetched from bioRxiv and cached by the reference validator as
  `publications/DOI_10.64898_2026.11.20.768214.md`.
- UniProt A0A0H2ZHM6 and its InterPro/PANTHER cross-references.
- QuickGO term lookups for candidate process terms (OLS was unavailable).

## Family placement: the central caveat

The preprint calls this an MVP homolog. The databases do not:

| source | assignment |
|---|---|
| PANTHER | PTHR10264 "BAND 7 PROTEIN-RELATED", SF83 — **not** PTHR14165 (Major Vault Protein) |
| InterPro | IPR043202 Band-7 stomatin-like; IPR001107 Band 7; IPR036013 Band 7/SPFH superfamily; IPR001972 Stomatin/HflK — **no** IPR039059 (Major vault protein) |
| UniProt name | "Stomatin/prohibitin" (submitter import, ECO:0000313) |

The authors are aware of this and address it: naive searches from this protein return
non-vault SPFH proteins, so they hand-curated 19 vault-like Band7 proteins verified to
have N-terminal globular repeats, built an HMM, and recovered
[DOI:10.64898/2026.11.20.768214 "identified 2,026 high-confidence homologs"] across most
bacterial phyla. For PA14 Band7 itself the architecture claim is from an AlphaFold model:
N-terminal repeats, Band7/SPFH shoulder, C-terminal cap helix, compared to human MVP
PDB 9BW5. A 12-protomer model gives
[DOI:10.64898/2026.11.20.768214 "Modeling the oligomeric complex of 12 Band7 monomers revealed a vault like"]
complex.

**Size argues for a distant relationship.** This protein is 381 aa with the Band7 domain
at residues 148-307, leaving ~147 aa N-terminal — room for roughly two or three MVP-type
repeats (~50-55 aa each). Characterised vault proteins are much larger: human MVP 893 aa
with nine repeats, phage L05 866 aa with seven. In the companion structural preprint
(Athukoralage et al., bioRxiv 2026.11.04.764334) the four clusters are MVP-1 (prokaryotic,
~7 repeats), MVP-2 (eukaryotic, ~9), MVP-like 1 (*H. gulosus*, 5 repeats, 463 aa) and
MVP-like 2 (*C. maritimus*, 3 repeats, 413 aa). On length and repeat count PA14_60630
sits with the MVP-like groups, which that preprint notes "often retain membrane-anchoring
features ancestral to the SPFH family" — consistent with the stomatin-like database
assignment. This is an inference from size and domain boundaries, not a tree, and is
recorded as a suggested question rather than asserted.

Consequence for curation: this gene is **not** added as a member of the PTHR14165 family
review.

## What the evidence does and does not support

Strong (genetic):

- `PA14_60630` is the most strongly induced gene in the genome after ribotoxin
  intoxication — 86-fold at 5 min, ~590-fold at 15 min
  [DOI:10.64898/2026.11.20.768214 "a gene of unknown function encoding a protein with a predicted Band7 domain"].
- Deletion sensitises; complementation restores
  [DOI:10.64898/2026.11.20.768214 "complementation of the ∆band7 mutant with the same"]
  band7 expression vector.
- A premature stop codon abolishes protection, so the **protein**, not just the
  transcript, is required
  [DOI:10.64898/2026.11.20.768214 "sequence abolished protection, demonstrating that both the Band7 protein and upstream"]
  noncoding RNA are required for defense.
- Specificity control: the Rtc-locus deletion is
  [DOI:10.64898/2026.11.20.768214 "∆rtc-locus mutant was as equivalently sensitive as wild-type"]
  against UV, temperature, redox stress, phage and antibiotics.
- Constitutively active RtcR does not rescue the band7 deletion,
  [DOI:10.64898/2026.11.20.768214 "implying that Band7 directly mediates defense against this toxin"].

Absent:

- No purified protein, no experimental structure, no negative stain or cryo-EM.
- No demonstrated physical interaction between Band7 and the bRNA, or with anything else.
- No subcellular localisation.
- No molecular activity of any kind.

So the review asserts one process row and no molecular function.

## Curation decisions

- **`GO:0071237` cellular response to bacteriocin, IMP, NEW.** Pyocins are bacteriocins;
  the response is cellular (operon induction plus resistance); the premature-stop
  experiment shows the protein does part of the work rather than merely being required.
- **Rejected `GO:0030153` bacteriocin immunity.** Its QuickGO ancestors are GO:0009404
  toxin metabolic process and GO:0019748 secondary metabolic process, so it describes
  metabolising the toxin. In the literature it also denotes a producer's cognate immunity
  protein, which the authors explicitly distinguish from this system.
- **Rejected `GO:0042742` defense response to bacterium.** The experiments used purified
  and engineered pyocins, not a live competitor, so the stimulus actually tested is the
  bacteriocin.
- **No molecular function, no cellular component.** The vault-like particle is predicted
  only; the ribonucleoprotein-complex term would need evidence of RNA association, which
  does not exist.
- **UniProt record marked DISPUTED** in `reference_review`: the ARBA-only "Single-pass
  membrane protein" prediction conflicts with the soluble particle the paper models.
  Neither is established; flagged so the tension is visible.

## Defect noticed in the source

The preprint gives two different locus tags for the bRNA: the Methods say the tRNA-like
region of bRNA is PA14_60630, which is the band7 protein-coding gene, while the Figure 2C
legend says PA14_60620. The figure legend appears to be the correct one. Recorded in the
reference review rather than as a `source_defects` entry, since the defect is in a
preprint rather than in a curation resource.
