Gene Description

PP_3231 is an uncharacterized 455-residue cytoplasmic protein of the erythromycin esterase (Ere) family (Pfam PF05139, CDD cd14728 Ere-like, PIRSF036794, PANTHER PTHR31299, COG2312). No experiment has been reported on it. It is about 24-28% identical to the macrolide-inactivating esterases EreA, EreB, EreC and EreD and to the Bacillus cereus esterase Bcr136, which hydrolyses ester substrates but cannot inactivate macrolides. PP_3231 conserves all five residues of the Ere metal-independent catalytic site mapped from EreC (E60, H63, E91, R268, H297, matching EreC E47, H50, E78, R261, H289). It is therefore predicted to be an active carboxylic-ester hydrolase. Its substrate is unknown, and nothing shows that it hydrolyses macrolides or contributes to antibiotic resistance. In KT2440 RB-TnSeq data (300 experiments, none with macrolides) it has no significant phenotype. It lies next to a phosphoribosyltransferase-domain gene (PP_3230), which STRING links to it, including by gene fusion in other genomes. It has no signal peptide or transmembrane segment and is predicted to be cytoplasmic.

Summary

No published experiment has characterised PP_3231. Its function can only be inferred from its domains. The protein belongs to the erythromycin esterase (Ere) family, a group of enzymes that use a metal-independent, histidine-based catalytic mechanism and include esterases that inactivate macrolide antibiotics. Its putative catalytic residues are conserved, so it is probably an active hydrolase that acts on esters. Nothing shows that its natural substrate is erythromycin or any other macrolide. 2. The erythromycin esterase family (what PP_3231 is related to)

2.1 Biochemistry of the family

  • Founding members. EreA and EreB are plasmid-encoded enzymes that make bacteria resistant to 14- and 15-membered macrolides. They hydrolyse the macrolactone ester bond, which opens the ring and inactivates the antibiotic. The fold is distinct from serine esterases.
  • Mechanism. Morar et al. (2012, Biochemistry 51:1740–1751, PMID:22303981) combined kinetics, mutagenesis, solvent isotope effects, pH profiles and inhibitor studies. They ruled out a Ser or Thr nucleophile and found that one histidine (H46 in EreB) is essential. They proposed that this histidine acts as a general base that activates a water molecule to attack the ester. EreA, EreB and the Bacillus cereus homologue Bcr136 had distinct properties. Only EreA was inhibited by chelators, and the authors suggested it holds a non-catalytic metal. The paper's title, "Mechanism and Diversity of the Erythromycin Esterase Family of Enzymes", reflects its point that this family is widespread in bacteria beyond the known resistance genes. (Wright lab summary; PubMed 22303981; McMaster thesis)
  • Structure. Zieliński et al. (2021, Nat. Commun. 12:1732, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-22016-3) solved the first crystal structures in the family, of EreC (>90% identical to EreA). They captured closed and open forms that differ in a flexible loop at residues ~295–303 (PDB 6XCQ and 6XCS). The active-site residues are His50, Glu78, His289, Glu47, Arg261 and Asp262. Earlier mutagenesis had shown that E78A abolishes activity and H289A lowers k_cat about 1000-fold. A secondary summary describes His50 as the catalytic base, Glu47 as positioning His50, and Glu78 and His289 as substrate-specificity residues. That summary has not been checked against the primary text. (PMC7979712; RCSB 6XCQ; RCSB 6XCS; arXiv 2505.23356)

3. Likely function of PP_3231

  • Molecular function, best supported: a hydrolase acting on carboxylic ester bonds (GO:0016788 level), using the Ere-family general-base mechanism.
  • Substrate: unknown. Three points argue against assigning erythromycin esterase activity (EC 3.1.1.-, macrolide hydrolysis) directly:
  • PP_3231 is classified in the PIRSF036794 "uncharacterised protein" subgroup and in PANTHER PTHR31299, whose name refers to fungal orthologues. It does not cluster with the mobile EreA/EreB/EreC/EreD resistance determinants.
  • Morar et al. (2012) stress that the family is diverse, and that homologues such as Bcr136 differ biochemically from EreA and EreB.
  • The gene is chromosomal and core to P. putida. It does not appear to be a mobile resistance element, and Pseudomonads are intrinsically macrolide-tolerant mainly through efflux and outer-membrane impermeability.

The natural substrate may therefore be an endogenous or environmental ester, for example a plant- or soil-derived macrolactone or polyketide. This is speculation.
- Localisation: no signal peptide or transmembrane segment is annotated in the UniProt entry (no Phobius or SignalP features). Characterised Ere enzymes are cytoplasmic. The cytoplasm is the most likely location, but this is predicted only.
- Pathway: none assigned. KEGG ppu:PP_3231 and BioCyc have no pathway placement for it.

4. Assessment of the existing GO annotation

Term Evidence Assessment
GO:0046677 response to antibiotic (BP) IEA, InterPro2GO from IPR007815 Over-annotation. The mapping assumes every Ere-family member confers macrolide resistance. No data show that PP_3231 hydrolyses antibiotics or responds to them. A conservative action would be MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED, or UNDECIDED.

A defensible new annotation would be an esterase/hydrolase MF term at the GO:0016788 level ("hydrolase activity, acting on ester bonds"). It would rest on family membership and the conserved E-x-x-H/E catalytic residues (an ISS/IC-type inference). Erythromycin esterase activity specifically should not be annotated without biochemical data.

5. Open questions and suggested experiments

  1. Purify recombinant PP_3231 and test it against erythromycin, azithromycin and other macrolides, and against generic esters such as p-nitrophenyl esters. Measure kinetics.
  2. Mutate H63A and E91A to test whether the Ere mechanism is conserved.
  3. Measure the macrolide MIC of a ΔPP_3231 mutant and the wild type, for example using the KT2440 RB-TnSeq fitness data in the Fitness Browser (not consulted here).
  4. Superpose the AlphaFold model on EreC (6XCQ) and compare substrate-pocket volume and the conformation of the flexible lid loop.

description: >-
  PP_3231 is an uncharacterized 455-residue cytoplasmic protein of the erythromycin esterase
  (Ere) family (Pfam PF05139, CDD cd14728 Ere-like, PIRSF036794, PANTHER PTHR31299, COG2312).
  No experiment has been reported on it. It is about 24-28% identical to the macrolide-inactivating
  esterases EreA, EreB, EreC and EreD and to the Bacillus cereus esterase Bcr136, which hydrolyses
  ester substrates but cannot inactivate macrolides. PP_3231 conserves all five residues of the
  Ere metal-independent catalytic site mapped from EreC (E60, H63, E91, R268, H297, matching EreC
  E47, H50, E78, R261, H289). It is therefore predicted to be an active carboxylic-ester hydrolase.
  Its substrate is unknown, and nothing shows that it hydrolyses macrolides or contributes to
  antibiotic resistance. In KT2440 RB-TnSeq data (300 experiments, none with macrolides) it has
  no significant phenotype. It lies next to a phosphoribosyltransferase-domain gene (PP_3230),
  which STRING links to it, including by gene fusion in other genomes. It has no signal peptide
  or transmembrane segment and is predicted to be cytoplasmic.
