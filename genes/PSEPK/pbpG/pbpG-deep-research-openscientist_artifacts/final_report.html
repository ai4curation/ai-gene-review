Functional Annotation Report: pbpG (PP_3794 / Q88GD0)

Penicillin-binding protein 7 (PBP7), a periplasmic D,D-endopeptidase of Pseudomonas putida KT2440

1. Summary (Answer to the Research Question)

pbpG (locus PP_3794, UniProt Q88GD0) of Pseudomonas putida KT2440 encodes penicillin-binding protein 7 (PBP7), a low-molecular-weight, serine-type D,D-peptidase (a penicillin-binding protein) of the peptidase S11 family. Its primary function is the hydrolytic cleavage of peptide cross-links in peptidoglycan (murein) — specifically the D,D-endopeptidase reaction that severs the D‑alanyl–meso-diaminopimelate (D‑Ala–mDAP) bond joining two adjacent stem peptides (EC 3.4.–.–, catalysed via a covalent acyl‑Ser enzyme intermediate). The enzyme carries out this reaction in the periplasm, acting on the polymeric cell-wall sacculus, and it does so as part of peptidoglycan-remodeling multi-enzyme complexes that couple cross-link cleavage to glycan-strand cleavage (lytic transglycosylases) and to new wall synthesis, enabling controlled enlargement of the sacculus during cell elongation and division without lysis. It is a non-essential, functionally redundant component of the cell-wall-remodeling machinery.

Identity verification (performed and passed): The gene symbol pbpG is used consistently for PBP7. A global pairwise alignment shows Q88GD0 shares 61.5% amino-acid identity (over 304 aligned positions) with E. coli K-12 PbpG/PBP7 (UniProt P0AFI5), whose UniProt description is identically "D-alanyl-D-alanine endopeptidase." The UniProt record for Q88GD0 further confirms (i) the correct organism (P. putida KT2440), (ii) peptidase S11 family membership with the D-alanyl-D-alanine carboxypeptidase A N-terminal domain (Pfam PF00768; InterPro IPR001967/IPR018044/IPR012338), and (iii) an intact penicilloyl-serine transferase catalytic apparatus. This is unambiguously the correct protein — a bona fide PBP7 ortholog — and the annotation is internally consistent. One family/activity nuance is discussed in §6.

3. Primary Function: The Catalyzed Reaction and Substrate Specificity

PBP7 is a D,D-endopeptidase. The defining biochemical literature on the ortholog established that:

  • The chromosomal ORF directing expression of PBP7 and PBP8 was identified and the gene renamed pbpG; the predicted protein "contains the consensus motifs associated with other PBPs," and a C-terminal OmpT-cleavage site converts PBP7 into the slightly smaller PBP8 (a proteolytic derivative, not a separate gene product) (Henderson, Templin & Young, 1995; 7721700).
  • PBP7/8 was "shown to be a DD-endopeptidase" (Romeis & Höltje, 1994; 8063800).

Substrate and reaction. The physiological substrate is cross-linked peptidoglycan. The D,D-endopeptidase reaction hydrolyzes the D‑alanyl–meso-diaminopimelate (D‑Ala⁴–mDAP) peptide cross-bridge that covalently links two glycan strands (the 4→3 cross-link typical of Gram-negative A1γ peptidoglycan, as in Pseudomonas). This cleaves inter-strand cross-links, in contrast to the related S11 DD-carboxypeptidases (PBP5/PBP6-type) that merely trim the terminal D‑Ala from an uncrosslinked pentapeptide. Biochemically, PBP7 preferentially attacks cross-links within the intact, high-molecular-weight polymeric sacculus rather than isolated soluble muropeptides — consistent with a role in editing the mature wall in situ.

Functionally, low-molecular-weight PBPs "act in the modification of peptidoglycan to allow expansion of the sacculus" (Gittins, Phoenix & Pratt, 1994; 8117464), and cross-link cleavage by endopeptidases is the rate-limiting "space-making" step that permits insertion of newly polymerized glycan during growth.

Authoritative classification. In the standard reference on peptidoglycan hydrolases (Vollmer, Joris, Charlier & Foster, 2008; 18266855), D,D-endopeptidases are the class that cleaves the peptide cross-link (distinct from glycosidases/lytic transglycosylases that cut the glycan backbone, and from carboxypeptidases that trim terminal D-Ala); the reviewed physiological functions of such hydrolases are "the regulation of cell wall growth, the turnover of peptidoglycan during growth, the separation of daughter cells during cell division and autolysis." The substrate is the cross-linked sacculus — "a net-like sacculus made of glycan strands crosslinked by peptides" with a layered architecture in Gram-negative cells (Vollmer & Seligman, 2010; 20060721). Pseudomonas, like E. coli, has A1γ-type peptidoglycan in which this cross-link is the D‑Ala⁴–meso-DAP³ (4→3) bond — the bond hydrolyzed by PBP7.

4. Subcellular Localization

The enzyme functions in the periplasm, acting on the sacculus that lies between the inner and outer membranes:

  • Q88GD0 possesses an N-terminal signal peptide (1–26) directing Sec export to the periplasm (UniProt; §2).
  • Low-M(r) PBPs "are thought to be periplasmic proteins attached with varying affinities to the inner membrane via C-terminal amphiphilic α-helices" (8117464). Because PBP7's catalytic domain projects into the periplasm, it accesses the murein layer directly.

The description in UniProt ("Periplasmic murein D-alanyl-D-alanine endopeptidase") is therefore consistent with both the sequence evidence and the biochemistry of the ortholog.

5. Pathway and Complex Context (Where It Fits Mechanistically)

PBP7 does not act in isolation; it is embedded in the peptidoglycan biosynthesis/remodeling network:

  • Coupling to lytic transglycosylases. PBP7/8 binds the soluble lytic transglycosylase Slt70 in vitro and "not only stabilize[s] but also stimulate[s] the enzymatic activity of Slt70 by a protein-protein interaction," leading to the conclusion that "Slt70, PBP7/8, and PBP3 may form a multienzyme complex in vivo" (8063800). This links peptide cross-link cleavage (PBP7) to glycan backbone cleavage (Slt70) and to a division-specific synthase (PBP3/FtsI).
  • Adaptor-organized hydrolase complexes. The outer-membrane lipoprotein NlpI is a general adaptor that "can specifically form complexes with various PG endopeptidases," incorporating them into synthase-containing multi-enzyme assemblies that "coordinate the spatiotemporal action of PG synthases and hydrolases" (Banzhaf et al., 2020; 32009249).

Mechanistic model. During elongation and division, the sacculus must be enlarged by inserting new material into a covalently closed, load-bearing network. Endopeptidases "cleave the crosslinks between adjacent PG strands to allow the incorporation of newly synthesized PG" and are "collectively essential for bacterial growth" (Alvarado Obando et al., 2024; 38598601). PBP7 thus provides regulated "space-maker" cleavage of cross-links, temporally and spatially coordinated (via Slt70/NlpI/synthase complexes and functional interplay with DD-carboxypeptidases such as DacA1/PBP5) with new synthesis so the wall expands without catastrophic breakage — the essence of the "make-before-break" / three-for-one growth logic of Gram-negative peptidoglycan.

Direct link to cell division (precise role). A recent cell-biological study of the ortholog sharpens PBP7's specific contribution: "PBP7 localizes in the lateral cell envelope and at midcell," with its C-terminal α-helix crucial for midcell localization (but dispensable for catalysis); midcell recruitment "relies on the assembly of FtsZ up to FtsN in the divisome, and on the activity of PBP3" (Liu et al., 2024; 39344863). Functionally, "the absence of PBP7 slows down the assembly of FtsN at midcell," the ΔpbpG mutant shows weaker HADA (transpeptidase) incorporation, and the authors conclude "endopeptidase-mediated PG cleavage may be a rate-limiting step for septal PG synthesis" (39344863). Thus PBP7 is a division-associated (and lateral-wall) DD-endopeptidase whose cross-link cleavage promotes divisome maturation (FtsN recruitment) and rate-limits septal peptidoglycan synthesis — a precise, non-generic role beyond mere bulk remodeling.

Pleiotropy is limited/redundant, not primary. Disruption of pbpG causes "no obvious growth defects... suggesting that PBP 7 is not essential" (7721700), and cells lacking multiple PBPs including PBP7 remain viable (Denome et al., 1999; 10383966). This reflects functional redundancy among several cell-wall endopeptidases (e.g., MepA/MepS/MepM classes) rather than absence of function; the precise, non-redundant contribution of PBP7 is best captured by its biochemical specificity (cross-link cleavage in the mature sacculus) and its physical partnerships, rather than by knockout phenotypes.

6. Nuance / Caveat on Family vs. Annotated Activity

The InterPro/Pfam domain of Q88GD0 is literally named "D-alanyl-D-alanine carboxypeptidase A N-terminal" (the PBP5/PBP6/DacA — S11 — prototype, whose members are classically DD-carboxypeptidases), whereas the EMBL/UniProt name derived from the genome annotation (AAN69388.1) is "D-alanyl-D-alanine endopeptidase" (pbpG/PBP7). This is a genuine and well-known feature of PBP7: it shares the S11 carboxypeptidase-type fold yet functions as an endopeptidase, biochemically demonstrated for the E. coli ortholog (8063800). Two consequences for interpretation:

  1. The catalytic chemistry is certain (serine DD-peptidase acting on D‑Ala-containing stem-peptide bonds; §2), and is direct evidence.
  2. The endopeptidase (cross-link-cleaving) assignment for the P. putida protein is a strong homology-based inference from the E. coli/P. aeruginosa pbpG/PBP7 orthologs; a residual possibility of DD-carboxypeptidase (or dual) activity cannot be excluded without direct enzymology on the P. putida protein. The most parsimonious, evidence-weighted conclusion is that PP_3794 is the P. putida PBP7 D,D-endopeptidase.

8. Limitations and Future Directions

  • No organism-specific enzymology. Substrate specificity (endo- vs. carboxypeptidase; preference for polymeric vs. soluble murein) is inferred from E. coli/P. aeruginosa orthologs, not measured for PP_3794 directly. Direct assays (muropeptide analysis by LC–MS after incubation with purified enzyme ± sacculi) would confirm the reaction.
  • Localization is inferred from the signal peptide and family topology; direct fractionation/GFP-fusion localization in P. putida would confirm periplasmic residence and any inner-membrane association.
  • Interaction partners (Slt70/MltG homologs, NlpI, PBP3/FtsI) are established in E. coli; the specific P. putida interactome should be verified (e.g., pulldowns, bacterial two-hybrid).
  • Redundancy mapping. Combinatorial deletion with other P. putida endopeptidases would reveal the conditional, non-redundant role (division vs. elongation; stress; biofilm).
  • Structure. A high-confidence AlphaFold model is already available (§2) and confirms the LMW-PBP fold and intact active site; an experimental structure with a bound cross-linked-muropeptide/transition-state analog would still be valuable to define the active-site cleft geometry that distinguishes endopeptidase from carboxypeptidase substrate binding.
