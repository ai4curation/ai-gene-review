Gene Description

PP_3108 encodes Tke2, a 1,385-residue "specialized" type VI secretion system (T6SS) effector of the polymorphic-toxin PAAR-Rhs class. It is encoded in the vgrG1 operon of the KT2440 K1-T6SS cluster, after vgrG1 (PP_3106) and a DUF1795/Eag-family chaperone gene (PP_3107), and upstream of its immunity gene tki2 (PP_3109). The locus-tag assignment rests on this gene order and on UniProt/PaperBLAST; the papers name the gene only as tke2. The architecture is a class I prePAAR region (residues 1-58) with a hydrophobic, putatively transmembrane stretch, a PAAR motif (about 69-140) that caps the VgrG spike, a DUF6531 domain, an Rhs/YD-repeat shell ending in the VTQDPIGL (DPxGL) motif at residue 1249, and a roughly 110-130-residue C-terminal toxin domain. The toxin domain is an HNH/colicin E7/pyocin S1-like DNase of the His-Me finger superfamily (InterPro IPR037146). Bernal et al. 2017 showed that Tke2 is secreted in a K1-T6SS-dependent manner and that its expression in E. coli inhibits growth, which is rescued by co-expression of Tki2. Its toxicity is therefore established, while its nuclease activity is inferred from the domain and has not been assayed. KT2440 kills a broad range of competitors, including phytopathogens, through the K1-T6SS. The dashboard protein NZ_SPUT01000035.1_2 (P. putida KB3, WP_135192713.1) is recorded here as an alias because it is the same locus (89.9% identity overall). It shares the N-terminal delivery module with PP_3108 (prePAAR and PAAR 98.9-100% identical, Rhs shell 95.6%) but carries a different C-terminal toxin, an LHH-family nuclease domain only 21.6% identical to PP_3108, and a different immunity gene. Toxin-specific statements here apply to KT2440 Tke2 only.

The Rhs/YD-repeat shell encapsulates the C-terminal HNH/colicin-like nuclease toxin domain, which is delivered into competing bacteria and neutralised in sister cells by the immunity protein Tki2 (PP_3109).

1. Headline conclusion

PP_3108 is, on strong convergent evidence, Tke2 — the Rhs-type, PAAR-bearing, HNH-nuclease antibacterial effector of the K1-T6SS of P. putida KT2440 characterised experimentally by Bernal et al. (2017). It is a toxin delivered into competitor bacteria, where its C-terminal His-Me finger (colicin/pyocin-like) domain is predicted to act as a nuclease; the producing cell is protected by the cognate immunity protein Tki2. Its site of action is the cytoplasm of the recipient (prey) cell, not the P. putida cytoplasm.

The locus-tag assignment requires an explicit chain of reasoning, given below, because the primary paper names effectors (Tke1–Tke10) without locus tags.

2. Establishing that PP_3108 = Tke2

2.1 Direct database naming. The Pseudomonas Genome Database gene page for PP_3108 carries the gene name tke2 (https://pseudomonas.com/feature/show/?id=38827688&view=functions). The site refuses automated fetches (HTTP 403), so I verified this only through indexed search snippets, which report the page as "Functions: tke2, Pseudomonas putida KT2440" with PAAR and RHS-repeat domain predictions. This is a database curator's assignment, not a statement in a paper.

3. Primary molecular function

3.1 The toxin domain: a His-Me finger (colicin/pyocin DNase-like) nuclease

Bernal et al. (2017) state that the C-terminal ~110 aa of Tke2 belongs to the HNH nuclease superfamily, with similarity to colicin E7 and pyocin S1 — both of which are well-characterised, metal-dependent, non-specific DNA-degrading HNH/His-Me finger endonucleases (Bernal et al. 2017, ISME J 11:972–987, PMID:28045455). This agrees with the independent signatures on Q88I91: SUPFAM SSF54060 "His-Me finger endonucleases", Gene3D 3.90.540.10 "Colicin/pyocin, DNase domain", IPR037146 and IPR044925.

Inspecting the sequence directly, the C-terminal region (res. ~1260–1385) contains a candidate ββα-Me active site:

1321 KTDKISIREYALELHHNDIPQRVGGAGVHDSSNLLALTPWEHEAVDQFRHVGSDLIRVIK
          ...H1335 H1336 N1337...H1349...N1353...

The spacing H1335/H1336 – N1337 – H1349 is the canonical His-Me finger arrangement (a general-base histidine, a structural asparagine, and a metal-coordinating histidine ~12 residues downstream), matching the colicin E7/E9 DNase and pyocin S-type paradigm. This residue assignment is my own bioinformatic inference from the sequence and the SSF54060/Gene3D boundaries; no mutagenesis of Tke2 has been reported, so the catalytic residues are a prediction and not an experimental result.

Substrate specificity is not established. The colicin E7/pyocin S1 relatives are non-specific DNases (double- and single-stranded DNA, with Ni²⁺/Zn²⁺/Mg²⁺ dependence varying by member), but HNH-superfamily T6SS effectors also include tRNases and ssDNases. No nuclease assay on Tke2 has been published. UniProt's GOA carries GO:0004519 endonuclease activity with evidence code ECO:0000256 — an automatic, sequence-model-based assertion, with no experimental backing.

Note also that UniProt's "belongs to the colicin/pyocin nuclease family" line is an ARBA rule (ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00006811) — a prediction, and so is not independent support for the family claim; the independent support is the InterPro/SUPFAM/Gene3D signature set plus the Bernal et al. Phyre2-based structural comparison.

3.2 The experimental evidence that Tke2 is a toxin

Three experiments in Bernal et al. (2017) bear directly on this protein:

  1. Toxicity and immunity. The isolated C-terminal domain (construct pTke2-CT) was expressed in E. coli: "On induction of the effector gene tke2, E. coli growth was significantly impaired but growth could readily be rescued on co-expression of the putative Tki2 immunity protein." This establishes (i) that the C-terminal domain is the toxic moiety, and (ii) that Tke2–Tki2 is a genuine effector–immunity pair. It does not establish the biochemical activity — growth arrest is consistent with, but not proof of, nuclease action.
  2. T6SS-dependent secretion. Chromosomally V5-tagged Tke2 was detectable only weakly, so secretion was assayed in an rpoN background: "Tke2 was produced in both the rpoN strain and the isogenic T6SS mutant (rpoNΔtssA1), but was only found in the supernatant of the strain with an intact T6SS." The authors conclude "Tke2 is an effector of the K1-T6SS." This is the key localisation result: the protein leaves the cell through the K1-T6SS apparatus.
  3. Contribution to interbacterial killing. The authors report that "the Tke2 toxin contributes to the antibacterial activity" of P. putida, within a K1-T6SS that kills a broad range of competitors; killing of E. coli was abolished in K1 structural mutants but retained in K2 and K3 mutants.

3.3 Architecture and delivery mechanism

The non-toxin two-thirds of the protein are delivery and packaging machinery, not catalytic:

  • prePAAR (res. 1–58). Pfam assigns this as the DddA-type prePAAR motif (PF25799). prePAAR marks a large family of TMD-containing T6SS effectors (>6,000 across ~15 proteobacterial genera) that "are unified by their requirement for a member of the Eag family of T6SS chaperones for export"; beyond chaperone binding, "prePAAR functions to facilitate proper folding of the downstream PAAR domain, which is required for effector interaction with the T6SS spike" (Ahmad et al. 2020, eLife 9:e62816). The adjacent PP_3107 DUF1795/Eag chaperone is precisely the partner this model predicts, and Eag chaperones work by mimicking transmembrane helical packing to stabilise effector TMDs (co-crystal structures, e.g. Tse6–EagT6, PDB 6XRF). This is a strong, mechanistically specific prediction for PP_3108 that has not been tested directly in P. putida.
  • PAAR domain (~res. 65–150; CDD cd14742 PAAR_RHS). The PAAR repeat — visible in the sequence as the tandem …SPNVFINSRKAARV… / …STNVFINSVAAARK… / …SDNVIIGGGTYRY… motifs — forms the conical tip that caps the VgrG1 spike (PP_3106), sharpening it and physically coupling the toxin to the puncturing device.
  • RHS / YD-shell cage (res. ~230–1250). DUF6531 plus six YD-repeat/Teneurin-like YD-shell blocks. Bernal et al. summarise the function cautiously: "Although the function of the Rhs domain is still unknown, it has been suggested that it forms a shell structure that encapsulates the C-terminal region of effectors." Subsequent structural work on Rhs effectors generally supports this cocoon model, in which the β-barrel shell sequesters the toxin during transit and releases it by aspartyl autoproteolysis in the prey cytoplasm. For Tke2 specifically, neither the encapsulation nor an autoproteolytic release step has been demonstrated.

So the mechanistic chain is: Eag chaperone (PP_3107) stabilises prePAAR → PAAR caps VgrG1 (PP_3106) → T6SS fires the spike into an adjacent bacterium → the RHS shell releases the HNH domain in the prey cytoplasm → nucleic-acid damage → prey death, with Tki2 (PP_3109) neutralising any Tke2 that re-enters a sibling cell. Steps 3 (secretion) and the toxin/immunity relationship are experimentally supported; the chaperone, spike-capping and shell-release steps are domain-based inference.

5. Pathway and regulatory context

PP_3108 sits within the 44 kb K1-T6SS gene cluster, one of three T6SS clusters (K1, K2, K3) in KT2440; the K1 system is the antibacterial one and belongs to T6SS group 4B, with tagX1 (PP3101.1) as a hallmark (Bernal et al. 2017). Ten effector–immunity pairs (Tke1–Tke10 / Tki1–Tki10) were identified genome-wide; at least three (tke1-tki1, tke2-tki2, tke3-tki3) lie in the K1 cluster. Tke2 and Tke4 are the PAAR-containing "specialised" effectors.

Regulation of the operon containing tke2 (the "V operon") was mapped in Bernal et al. 2023:

  • The V operon is a single transcriptional unit driven by two σ70 promoters, PV1 and PV2, upstream of PP_3104; intergenic gaps within the operon are 0–25 nt. No internal tke2 promoter was found, so tke2 expression is operon-level.
  • The cluster is induced in stationary phase (PV::lacZ ~5,000 → ~9,000 Miller units).
  • Positively regulated by the GacS–GacA two-component system (PV activity down 2–3-fold in a gacS mutant); RetS represses the S operon but did not affect PV.
  • RpoS represses: PS and PV activity rose in its absence.
  • RpoN represses the cluster (indirectly, likely via FleQ — "the effect of RpoN is likely indirect since σ factors usually promote instead of inhibiting transcription"). Consistently: "production of the Tke2 effector was considerably higher in a rpoN mutant". This is why the 2017 secretion assay was performed in an rpoN background.

Biologically, the K1-T6SS confers interbacterial competition and biocontrol: P. putida eradicates diverse bacteria, including the phytopathogen Xanthomonas campestris, in a K1-T6SS-dependent manner, and reduces necrosis in co-infected Nicotiana benthamiana leaves (Bernal et al. 2017). Tke2 is one contributor to that activity, alongside the NAD(P)⁺-degrading Tke1 (a Tse6 orthologue) and the colicin-S4-like pore-formers Tke7/Tke8.

A separate note on genomic context: PP_3108 lies within KT2440 genomic island 59, one of three rhs-family genes placed in GIs (the others being PP_4085 and PP_4093.2) — consistent with horizontal acquisition of the toxin, as is typical for Rhs/T6SS effector repertoires.

6. What is not known

  1. No enzymology. No nuclease assay, no substrate determination (dsDNA vs ssDNA vs tRNA), no metal requirement, no catalytic-residue mutagenesis for Tke2. The GO:0004519 annotation is purely computational.
  2. No structure of Tke2 or of the Tke2–Tki2 complex; the Tki2 inhibition mechanism (active-site occlusion, as for Im7/Im9 against colicin DNases, vs. another mode) is unknown.
  3. The RHS cocoon and its release have not been demonstrated for Tke2; no autoproteolysis site has been mapped.
  4. The EagR1a/EagR1b–Tke2 interaction is predicted from the prePAAR/Eag paradigm but not shown biochemically in P. putida.
  5. Full-length secretion and prey-cell delivery were shown as supernatant accumulation; direct translocation into a recipient cytoplasm was not visualised.
  6. The Tki2 locus tag (PP_3109) is my inference from gene order and size, not a published assignment.

7. Suggested annotation stance

  • Molecular function: the His-Me finger / colicin-pyocin DNase assignment is well supported at the sequence-family level (InterPro, SUPFAM, Gene3D, plus the authors' Phyre2 comparison to colicin E7/pyocin S1) but not experimentally; GO:0004519 endonuclease activity is a reasonable ISS/ISM-grade claim, not an IDA-grade one. GO:0004536 DNA nuclease activity or a deoxyribonuclease child would over-reach without an assay.
  • Process: GO:0033104 (type VI protein secretion system complex) is not appropriate for an effector; the defensible process claims are secretion by the T6SS (experimentally shown for this protein) and interbacterial antagonism / killing of cells of another organism — the latter supported for the K1 system as a whole and, per the authors, contributed to by Tke2.
  • Component: extracellular region, by IDA (supernatant, T6SS-dependent). Do not annotate membrane from the Phobius helices.

The exact-sequence UniProt entries were deleted, so the review is filed on KT2440 PP_3108 (Q88I91, 1385 aa). The two are 89.9% identical over 1381 aligned pairs.
Analysis: PP_3108-bioinformatics/ (BERIL run projects/hackathon/runs/20261030T211644Z-pp3108-tke2-windowed-identity).

Conclusion

PP_3108 is Tke2, the PAAR-Rhs "specialized" effector of the KT2440 K1-T6SS. It is encoded in the vgrG1 operon (PP_3106 VgrG1, PP_3107 DUF1795/Eag chaperone, PP_3108 Tke2, PP_3109 Tki2). Bernal et al. 2017 (PMID:28045455) established three things:
- Tke2 is secreted in a K1-T6SS-dependent manner.
- It is toxic when expressed in E. coli.
- Tki2 neutralises it.

Its C-terminal toxin is an HNH/colicin E7-like nuclease domain. That assignment comes from the domain alone; no nuclease assay has been done.

The dashboard protein is a toxin-swapped allele

Identity along the length is not uniform:

Query region Identity to PP_3108
prePAAR 1-58 100%
PAAR 59-148 98.9%
Rhs/YD shell 149-1253 95.6%
C-terminal toxin after DPIGL (1254-1409) 25%
LHH domain 1313-1409 21.6%, no significant local similarity (z = 3.3)
  • Toxin domains. The query's toxin domain hits Pfam LHH (PF14411) at E = 2.7e-13. The PP_3108 C-terminus does not hit LHH significantly; InterPro places it in the colicin/pyocin DNase superfamily instead.
  • Immunity genes. The gene downstream of the query (WP_223271142.1) sits in the tki2 position but is not detectably related to Tki2 (18% identity, z = 2.4).
  • Delivery partners. The Eag chaperone (100% identical to PP_3107) and VgrG (76.2% identical to PP_3106) are conserved.

Filing on PP_3108 is valid for the delivery and secretion function, but not for the toxin function. The Tke2 toxicity and Tki2 data, and the colicin-like DNase fold, do not transfer to the query. For the query's LHH toxin, only a domain-level nuclease prediction exists. The dashboard ID is recorded as an alias on PP_3108 with this caveat in the description.

PAAR motif. PF05488 is present at residues 69-140 (E = 6e-21), and class I prePAAR (PF25799) at 1-58. The prePAAR region is strongly hydrophobic (residues 13-64). UniProt/Phobius predicts two TM helices there; DeepTMHMM calls the protein globular. This is the class I prePAAR, TMD-containing effector organisation that needs an Eag chaperone, and an Eag gene sits next to it. InterPro labels PF25799 "Double-stranded DNA deaminase toxin A prePAAR motif" after the protein where it was first described. That label implies nothing about deaminase activity, and the dashboard call correctly rejected it.

Dashboard verdict

Supported. The call reads "PAAR-containing Rhs-family type VI secretion system effector protein", with GO:0033103 protein secretion by the T6SS. Both are correct. The GO term is scored COR (2): it is novel, since GOA has only an IEA endonuclease annotation. A more informative annotation would be: "PAAR-Rhs T6SS effector (K1-T6SS Tke2 locus, vgrG1 operon) with a C-terminal LHH-family nuclease toxin domain; toxin differs from KT2440 Tke2; cognate immunity protein WP_223271142.1 (predicted)".

The rationale's statement that the 89.7%-identical homolog is "Tke2" is true but misleading, because the identity is concentrated in the delivery module. The call was right not to assign a nuclease or deaminase activity.

The STRING partners PP_3107/PP_3106/PP_4084 were transferred from PP_3108. PP_3107 and PP_4084 are DUF1795/Eag chaperones and PP_3106 is VgrG1, so they corroborate T6SS delivery and say nothing about the toxin.

Caveats

  • The locus-tag mapping tke2 = PP_3108 is inferred from operon order (vgrG1 operon: PP3104, PP3105, VgrG1, EagR adaptors, Tke2, Tki2; PMID:36748579) and from the UniProt and PaperBLAST mapping. The papers do not state the locus tag in the main text.
  • There is no Tke2-specific competition phenotype. Killing was shown for the T6SS as a whole, so GO:0031640 is IC.
  • Fitness: just fetch-fitness failed, because no FEBA source was reachable. The dashboard evidence holds no fitness data for this protein; its only FB homolog is a P. syringae YD-repeat protein with no measurements.
  • PMID:33693029 (Rsm RAP-seq) lists PP_3108 among Rsm-bound RNAs only in its supplement. It bears on regulation, not function, and is not cited.

The full-length identity hides a swapped C-terminal toxin. Over the whole length the
global alignment gives 1242 identical residues in 1381 aligned pairs (89.9%). Identity is
not uniform along the length:

In 100-residue windows the identity is 0.96-1.00 from residue 1 to 1100 (with one dip to 0.89 at 801-900). It falls to 0.80 at 1151-1250, then 0.53, 0.32 and 0.21 in the last three windows (1201-1300, 1251-1350, 1301-1400).

The C-terminal boundary motif of the Rhs core reads VTQDPIGL at residue 1249 in both proteins. Bernal et al. 2017 write it PxxxxDPxGL. After it, the proteins share only a ~60-residue proximal segment: a local alignment of the two toxin regions covers query toxin residues 2-71 at 45.5% identity (shuffle z = 13.0). The query has a second DPYGL at 1271, which PP_3108 lacks (it reads DPWGW). The LHH segment, residues 1313-1409, has no significant similarity to the PP_3108 C-terminus (best local alignment 27 pairs, shuffle z = 3.3).

Toxin domain HMMs.
- Query: LHH (PF14411) hits the C-terminus at 1313-1397 (i-Evalue 2.7e-13 on the full sequence; 6.2e-15 on the excised toxin region).
- PP_3108: the C-terminus gives only weak LHH fragments (i-Evalue 0.005 and 0.02) and no significant hit to the other nuclease HMMs tested. UniProt/InterPro place it in the colicin/pyocin DNase superfamily (IPR037146, Gene3D 3.90.540.10) and the His-Me finger superfamily. Bernal et al. 2017 describe it as an HNH-superfamily nuclease domain similar to colicin E7 and pyocin S1.
- Both C-termini are therefore HNH/ENDO VII-superfamily (His-Me finger) predicted nucleases, but they are different toxin modules.

The immunity genes differ as well. WP_223271142.1, the gene directly downstream of the query and in the position of tki2, is only 18.0% identical to Tki2 (PP_3109) over 111 aligned pairs in a global alignment. The best local alignment has 19 pairs with shuffle z = 2.4, so the two proteins are not detectably related. By contrast, the N-terminal partners are conserved. The DUF1795 (Eag-family) chaperone WP_003248404.1 is 100% identical over 155 residues to PP_3107; PP_3107 has an 18-residue N-terminal extension, aligned at 19-173. The VgrG WP_103444332.1 is 76.2% identical over 617 pairs to VgrG1 PP_3106.

The PAAR and prePAAR motifs are present.
- PAAR_motif (PF05488) hits residues 69-140 in both proteins (i-Evalue 6e-21 in the query, 1.8e-21 in PP_3108).
- Class I prePAAR (PF25799) hits residues 1-58 in both (1e-23).
- The prePAAR region carries a strongly hydrophobic stretch: Kyte-Doolittle 19-residue windows of ≥1.6 cover residues 13-64. This matches the two Phobius TM segments in UniProt (16-36 and 43-64), though DeepTMHMM called the protein globular.
- An N-terminal hydrophobic prePAAR region, together with an adjacent DUF1795/Eag chaperone gene, is the organization of class I prePAAR Rhs effectors.

Interpretation

The query is the KB3 allele of the KT2440 K1-T6SS Tke2 locus. Its delivery module is
conserved: the prePAAR, PAAR and Rhs shell, the Eag chaperone and VgrG. Its C-terminal
toxin and the cognate immunity gene are a different cassette: an LHH-family predicted
nuclease with an unrelated immunity protein. Such a swap is typical of Rhs polymorphic
toxins. Two kinds of property follow:

  • Properties of the N-terminal part transfer from PP_3108 with high confidence: K1-T6SS-dependent secretion, PAAR/VgrG spike association and the Eag chaperone requirement.
  • The toxin-specific properties of Tke2 do not transfer: the toxic domain itself, its neutralisation by Tki2 and the colicin-like DNase fold. For the query, toxin activity rests only on the LHH domain prediction. No LHH-family toxin from this locus has been assayed.
