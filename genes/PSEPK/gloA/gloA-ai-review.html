Gene Description

Q88GF8 is predicted to be the metal-dependent glyoxalase I of Pseudomonas putida KT2440. It catalyzes the first committed step of glutathione-dependent methylglyoxal detoxification, converting the spontaneously formed methylglyoxal-glutathione hemithioacetal to (R)-S-lactoylglutathione for subsequent hydrolysis by GloB.

gloA curation notes

Functional evidence and locus scope

Historical expression experiments establish an active Pseudomonas putida
glyoxalase I: "Pseudomonas putida glyoxalase I was expressed in the transgenic
E. coli to enhance MG detoxification" PMID:11461144. A second heterologous
study reported an 80-90% reduction in free methylglyoxal after expression of a
P. putida glyoxalase I PMID:22358913. Neither abstract identifies the
KT2440 PP_3766/Q88GF8 locus, so these papers provide same-species functional
corroboration rather than direct locus-level evidence.

Q88GF8 itself is an unreviewed entry assigned to EC 4.4.1.5, Rhea 19069, and
PTHR10374:SF30 [file:PSEPK/gloA/gloA-uniprot.txt]. The reviewed same-species
protein P16635 is also PTHR10374:SF30
[file:interpro/panther/PTHR10374/PTHR10374-entries.csv]. This combination
supports the KT2440 assignment while preserving the locus caveat.

Metal specificity

The Q88GF8 record has both Ni2+ and Zn2+ COFACTOR blocks, whereas its
PIRSR-derived residue features specify Zn2+ binding
[file:PSEPK/gloA/gloA-uniprot.txt]. Bacterial GlxI proteins divide into
Zn2+-activated and Ni2+-activated classes [PMID:25557363, "can be divided into
two classes according to its metal activation profile"]. The generic metal-ion
binding annotation is therefore retained without choosing a physiological
metal for Q88GF8.
