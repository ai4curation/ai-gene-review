Gene Description

TruD (PP_1619) is the 352-residue TruD-family tRNA pseudouridine synthase of Pseudomonas putida KT2440. It has the TruD catalytic and insertion domains over its full length (HAMAP MF_01082, Pfam PF01142, PROSITE TruD signature) and is 37.3% identical over 343 aligned residues to Escherichia coli TruD (Q57261). E. coli TruD is the founding member of the family: it was purified as the activity that forms pseudouridine 13 in tRNA(Glu), its deletion removes that pseudouridine, and the purified enzyme modifies only U13 of an in vitro tRNA(Glu) transcript (PMID:12756329). Its essential Asp80 in the GXKD motif and the proposed general base Glu31 (PMID:19664587) are both conserved in the P. putida protein (GLKD at 78-81, Asp81 and Glu32), as are four other annotated E. coli functional residues. By orthology the protein is a stand-alone, cytoplasmic tRNA uridine(13) to pseudouridine isomerase (EC 5.4.99.27). The protein has not been studied in P. putida, and the set of P. putida tRNAs it modifies is unknown. Thermus TruD also modifies U13 of tRNA(Asp) and tRNA(Gln) and U35 of tRNA(Tyr) (PMID:40138658), so the substrate range may be wider than tRNA(Glu) U13. One miniTn5 insertion in PP_1619 was recovered among phenol-tolerant derivatives of a colR mutant (PMID:20398259). The downstream gene pcm (PP_1621) was hit four times in the same screen, so this phenotype may be a polar effect and says nothing about TruD activity.

The identity checks pass. The symbol truD, the protein name, the family and the InterPro signatures all agree. In bacteria the symbol truD refers only to the TruD-family tRNA Ψ13 synthase; no other gene in P. putida carries it. One unrelated name is worth noting: in eukaryotes "Pus7" is the TruD-family homolog. This report covers only the TruD-family Ψ synthase.

Literature on this exact protein is limited. I found no primary experimental study of P. putida PP_1619 itself. Its functional annotation comes from:
- the UniProt HAMAP rule;
- the biochemistry and genetics of E. coli TruD, the founding member;
- crystal structures of E. coli TruD;
- recent substrate mapping of Thermus thermophilus TruD;
- studies of eukaryotic TruD-family Pus7 homologs.

Every functional statement below about PP_1619 is therefore an inference by homology. The family is conserved and carries strong signatures, so the inference is robust for the core catalytic activity. It is less certain for the exact set of tRNA substrates in Pseudomonas.

2. Primary function: reaction and substrate

2.1 Reaction catalysed

TruD is an RNA-modifying isomerase. It converts uridine at position 13 of the tRNA D-stem into pseudouridine (Ψ):

uridine(13) in tRNA = pseudouridine(13) in tRNA (Rhea:RHEA:42540; EC 5.4.99.27)

UniProt Q88MF2 states: "Responsible for synthesis of pseudouridine from uracil-13 in transfer RNAs" (CC FUNCTION, ECO:0000255 | HAMAP-Rule:MF_01082). The reaction is the standard Ψ-synthase isomerization. The uracil base is detached from C1′ of the ribose, rotated 180°, and reattached through its C5, which forms a C–C glycosidic bond. No cofactor or guide RNA is needed, and the enzyme acts alone on the RNA substrate (Rhea 42540).

2.2 Discovery of the activity: E. coli TruD (YgbO)

  • Kaya & Ofengand (2003, RNA 9:711–721; PMID 12756329). Ten known E. coli Ψ synthases accounted for every Ψ in tRNA and rRNA except Ψ13 in tRNA^Glu. Kaya and Ofengand purified the Ψ13-forming activity and identified it as the uncharacterised protein YgbO, which they renamed TruD (PMC article; EcoliWiki).
  • Genetic test. Deleting ygbO removed Ψ13 from tRNA^Glu, and a plasmid carrying the gene restored it. This establishes TruD as both necessary and sufficient for the modification.
  • A new family. The authors found 58 TruD homologs across all three domains of life. They concluded that TruD forms a new, widespread class of Ψ synthase with no sequence homology to the four previously known families (TruA, TruB, RluA, RsuA).

2.3 Substrate specificity: updated by work in Thermus, 2025

  • The E. coli paradigm. Only tRNA^Glu Ψ13 was clearly assigned to TruD.
  • Yamagami et al., 2025 (RNA 31(6):850–867). The authors used PRAISE, a bisulfite-based Ψ mapping method, on wild-type and truD-disrupted T. thermophilus (RNA journal; abstract). They found that:
  • TruD modifies U13 in tRNA^Asp, tRNA^Glu and tRNA^Gln;
  • TruD also modifies U35 in tRNA^Tyr, a site previously attributed to RluF in E. coli;
  • TruD prefers the sequence UNUAR, where the underlined U is the target, N is any nucleotide and R is a purine;
  • in vitro, recombinant TruD modified an mRNA fragment (CDC8), and the authors noted more than 600 ORF fragments carrying this motif. This suggests possible non-tRNA substrates, which have not been confirmed in vivo.
  • Eukaryotic homologs (Pus7). S. cerevisiae Pus7 is a TruD-family enzyme. It makes Ψ13 in cytoplasmic tRNAs, Ψ35 in U2 snRNA and Ψ35 in pre-tRNA^Tyr, and it also acts on mRNA. Its substrate range is therefore broader, but U13 remains the conserved family target (Behm-Ansmant et al. 2003, RNA; reviewed in PMC8794802 and NAR 2021, 49:11810). I took the Pus7 details from these later reviews and the Rhea entry, not from the 2003 paper itself.

Inference for PP_1619. Its most likely biological substrates are the U13 positions of P. putida tRNAs whose D-stem fits the UNUAR-type context, which in E. coli means at least tRNA^Glu. Which specific P. putida tRNAs are modified is untested. Whether Pseudomonas TruD also modifies U35 of tRNA^Tyr or any mRNAs is also unknown.

3. Structure and catalytic mechanism (from E. coli TruD)

Three crystal structures of E. coli TruD were published in 2004:

  1. Hoang & Ferré-D'Amaré (2004, RNA 10:1026; PMID 15208439), at 2.2 Å, PDB 1SB7 (full text; RCSB 1SB7).
  2. The molecule is U-shaped, and its catalytic domain superimposes closely on those of other Ψ synthases.
  3. The domain's connectivity is a circular permutation of the conserved Ψ-synthase fold.
  4. The catalytic aspartate sits in the β3–β4 loops. It forms the conserved active-site triad with an aromatic and a basic residue: D80, F131 and K21.
  5. The two lobes can flex by about 18°.
  6. Kaya et al. (2004, J Biol Chem; PMID 15135053), an independent crystal form. It describes a catalytic domain that differs in sequence from other Ψ synthases but is structurally very similar to them. It also has a second large domain with a novel α/β fold, the "TruD insertion domain" (PubMed 15135053; PDB 1SI7).
  7. Ericsson et al. (2004, FEBS Lett), at 2.0 Å. The protein is V-shaped, with an RNA-binding cleft between the catalytic domain and the insertion domain. The U-shaped versus V-shaped descriptions appear to reflect different crystal forms and the flexibility between domains.

Mapping to PP_1619.
- UniProt places the nucleophile of Q88MF2 at Asp81, the counterpart of E. coli Asp80.
- The TRUD insertion domain spans residues 157–303.
- The domain architecture matches E. coli TruD (IPR011760/IPR043165 insertion domain; IPR042214 catalytic domain).

This conservation supports the view that PP_1619 is an active enzyme rather than a catalytically dead paralog. I did not directly check that the conserved Lys and Phe/Tyr residues are present in the P. putida sequence; an alignment would be needed to confirm it.

4. Biological process and pathway context

  • Process. tRNA pseudouridine synthesis, which falls under tRNA modification and tRNA processing. The specific GO process is "tRNA pseudouridine synthesis" (GO:0031119). The broader "pseudouridine synthesis" (GO:0001522) is less precise.
  • Role of Ψ13. Ψ13 lies in the D-stem (the 13–22 base pair). Ψ in this region is thought to stiffen the stem through an extra N1-H hydrogen bond, which helps tertiary folding of tRNA. In bacteria TruD is not essential, and E. coli ΔtruD strains grow normally under standard conditions (Kaya & Ofengand 2003). Ψ13 is therefore a fine-tuning modification, not one required for viability.
  • Pathway. TruD acts on its own; it is not part of a multi-enzyme pathway or complex. It is one of about ten site-specific bacterial Ψ synthases, alongside TruA, TruB, TruC, RluA–F and RsuA.

5. Cellular localization

TruD has no signal peptide, transmembrane segment or secretion signals. Like all bacterial tRNA-modifying enzymes it is expected to act in the cytoplasm (cytosol), where tRNA maturation takes place. The existing cytosol annotation (GO:0005829, from ECO:0007826) agrees with this. I found no localization experiment specific to P. putida.

7. Summary of core function

PP_1619 (TruD) from P. putida KT2440 is a stand-alone, cytoplasmic tRNA pseudouridine(13) synthase (EC 5.4.99.27). It converts U13 in the D-stem of a subset of tRNAs into Ψ, using a catalytic Asp nucleophile (Asp81, the counterpart of E. coli Asp80). The protein has a circularly permuted Ψ-synthase catalytic domain and a TruD-specific α/β insertion domain that together form the RNA-binding cleft.

8. Open questions and suggested experiments

  1. Which P. putida tRNAs carry Ψ13? Apply PRAISE or a similar Ψ-mapping method to wild type and a PP_1619 deletion.
  2. Does P. putida TruD also modify U35 of tRNA^Tyr? In T. thermophilus TruD does, while in E. coli the site is attributed to RluF. P. putida has its own Rlu complement, so this is an open question.
  3. Are any mRNA sites modified? The UNUAR motif suggests possible mRNA targets.
  4. Does loss of TruD have a phenotype under stress? For example, a growth defect at temperature extremes.

truD (PP_1619, Q88MF2): curation notes

Dashboard gene: DOAB01000105.1_3 (P. putida, tier A), https://gene-annotate-dashboard.kbase.us/review/DOAB01000105.1_3
Filed on Swiss-Prot Q88MF2 (TRUD_PSEPK, KT2440 PP_1619): 351/352 identical (99.7%), no gaps. The exact UniParc entries were deleted (not in a reference proteome).

Conclusion

PP_1619 is the TruD-family tRNA pseudouridine(13) synthase (EC 5.4.99.27). This is an assignment by orthology; the P. putida protein has never been assayed.

  • It is 37.3% identical over 343 aligned residues to E. coli TruD (Q57261). E. coli TruD was purified as the tRNA(Glu) Psi13 synthase, and deleting it loses Psi13, which complementation restores (PMID:12756329).
  • Catalytic Asp conserved. E. coli Asp80 (in the GXKD motif; D80N/T inactive) corresponds to query Asp81 (GLKD 78-81). Glu31, the proposed general base (PMID:19664587), corresponds to Glu32. The four other annotated E. coli functional residues are also conserved. The analysis is in truD-bioinformatics/ and BERIL run 20261030T211234Z-trud-identity-catalytic-site.
  • Substrate range. Thermus TruD modifies U13 of tRNA(Asp), tRNA(Glu) and tRNA(Gln), and U35 of tRNA(Tyr) (PMID:40138658). Which P. putida tRNAs TruD modifies is unknown.

Dashboard verdict: supported

  • Corrected annotation: none needed. Optionally: "tRNA pseudouridine(13) synthase TruD (EC 5.4.99.27); tRNA substrates in P. putida untested."
  • GO terms:
  • GO:0160150 tRNA pseudouridine(13) synthase activity: CNN. It is already in GOA as an IEA.
  • GO:0006400 tRNA modification: LSP. GOA already has the descendant GO:0031119 tRNA pseudouridine synthesis.
  • GO:0005737 cytoplasm: CNN. GOA has the child term cytosol.
  • The call is consistent with the evidence the annotator saw. It also does not over-claim tRNA specificity, and it correctly ignored the PaperBLAST "xylose receptor" homolog at 37.9%, which is an AI-2/XylF blastx artefact.
  • The evidence shown contained no TruD biochemistry paper. Tier A therefore rests on domain signatures, not on literature the annotator read.

Existing GOA for Q88MF2 (9 IEA, no experimental)

  • ACCEPT: GO:0160150, GO:0031119 and GO:0005829.
  • TOO_GENERAL: GO:0009982, GO:0001522, GO:0009451, GO:0006396 RNA processing and GO:0140098 catalytic activity, acting on RNA. All are true but entailed by the accepted GO:0160150 or GO:0031119. (Changed from KEEP_AS_NON_CORE / MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED after PR #5755 review.)
  • MARK_AS_OVER_ANNOTATED: GO:0003723 RNA binding, which restates the substrate binding of an RNA enzyme.

Caveats

  • The only P. putida paper is not about function. PMID:20398259 names PP_1619 as a "conserved hypothetical protein" with one miniTn5 insertion among phenol-tolerant derivatives of a colR mutant. The downstream gene pcm (PP_1621) had 4 insertions in the same screen, so a polar effect on pcm is plausible. This says nothing about TruD activity.
  • No fitness data for PP_1619. just fetch-fitness failed because no FEBA source was reachable. The dashboard evidence holds only homolog fitness: P. fluorescens 79% (L-malate, -1.4) and Shewanella 40% (various N/C sources, about -3 to -4.6). These are pleiotropic growth defects with no bearing on the molecular function.
  • P33643 is not E. coli TruD. The brief named P33643, but that accession is E. coli RluD (23S rRNA Psi synthase, catalytic Asp139). E. coli TruD is Q57261. Q57261 was used here.

The YAML stub was seeded by calling fetch_gene_data with the label resolved through ubergraph:, as was done for yxcA. The stub first landed in genes/genes/PSEPK/truD/ because of a base_path slip; it was moved into place, and that stray directory was removed.
