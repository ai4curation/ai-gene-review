PutP (PP_4946, UniProt Q88D81) of Pseudomonas putida KT2440: A Sodium/Proline Symporter — Functional Annotation Report

Summary

PutP (PP_4946; UniProt Q88D81) of Pseudomonas putida KT2440 is an integral inner-membrane sodium/proline symporter that catalyzes the electrogenic, Na⁺-coupled uptake of extracellular L-proline into the cytoplasm. It belongs to the solute:sodium symporter family (SSS/SSF; Transporter Classification TC 2.A.21; human SLC5 superfamily), and it harnesses the transmembrane electrochemical Na⁺ gradient as the thermodynamic driving force to accumulate proline against a concentration gradient. Its primary, dedicated biological role is to serve as the import step of the proline-utilization (put) pathway, delivering proline to the cytoplasmic bifunctional enzyme PutA, which oxidizes it to glutamate and thereby enables P. putida to grow on proline as a sole source of both carbon and nitrogen.

The functional assignment rests on multiple converging lines of evidence. Direct experimental work in the exact target strain (P. putida KT2440) demonstrated that proline uptake is mediated by PutP and that the organism uses proline as sole C and N source, with putP and putA transcribed divergently from a proline-inducible intergenic region (PMID: 11097893). The genomic architecture in KT2440 — with PP_4946 (putP) immediately adjacent to PP_4947 (putA, the bifunctional proline dehydrogenase/Δ¹-pyrroline-5-carboxylate dehydrogenase) — confirms an intact, co-regulated proline-utilization module. The mechanistic details of transport, though not solved directly for the P. putida protein, are transferable at high confidence from the intensively studied Escherichia coli ortholog (~80% identical), a canonical model of the SSS family whose LeuT-like 5+5 inverted-repeat fold, Na⁺-coordination chemistry, and gating residues have been mapped by decades of biochemistry.

Critically, a residue-level comparison performed in this investigation showed that every experimentally characterized functional residue of E. coli PutP — the Na⁺-coordinating aspartate, the substrate-pathway serines/glycines, the external-gate glutamate, and the TM IX threonine/serine ion-binding cluster — is 100% conserved in the P. putida protein. This near-perfect conservation of the ion/substrate machinery, combined with direct transport evidence in KT2440 and the preserved putP–putA genomic pairing, provides high-confidence, self-consistent support for annotating PP_4946 as a Na⁺-coupled L-proline symporter operating at the cytoplasmic (inner) membrane. A secondary, family-associated role in osmoadaptation — supplying proline as an osmoprotective compatible solute — is plausible but not directly demonstrated for this ortholog.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — PutP is a Na⁺-coupled L-proline symporter (primary function verified)

The primary function of PP_4946 is the sodium-dependent uptake of extracellular L-proline. UniProt Q88D81 annotates the 542-residue (~57.9 kDa) protein as PutP / OrderedLocusName PP_4946 with the function "catalyzes the sodium-dependent uptake of extracellular L-proline," and places it in the solute/sodium symporter family (SSS/SSF; TC 2.A.21; SLC5).

This is not merely a database inference. In the exact target organism, P. putida KT2440, direct experimental work established that proline uptake is mediated by the PutP protein: "Utilization of this amino acid involves its uptake, which is mediated by the PutP protein, and its conversion into glutamate, mediated by the PutA protein" (PMID: 11097893). At the family level, the H. pylori PutP ortholog was reconstituted into proteoliposomes and shown to be obligately Na⁺-coupled — "L-proline transport is exclusively dependent on Na(+) as coupling ion, i.e., Na(+)/L-proline symport" (PMID: 24358297). A dedicated review formally assigns PutP: "The Na⁺/L-proline transporter PutP is a member of the Na⁺/solute symporter family (TC 2A.21, SLC5)" (PMID: 22201772).

The transport reaction can be written:

L-proline(out) + Na⁺(out) → L-proline(in) + Na⁺(in)

Substrate specificity: the physiological substrate is L-proline, co-transported with Na⁺ (Li⁺ can substitute for Na⁺ in some orthologs but with lower affinity). The transport is electrogenic — charge is displaced during both Na⁺ and proline binding, as demonstrated by solid-supported-membrane electrophysiology on the E. coli carrier (PMID: 15476811; PMID: 19366604).

Finding 2 — PutP is an inner-membrane transporter with a LeuT-like 5+5 inverted-repeat fold; mechanism transferable from E. coli (80% identity)

PutP is an integral protein of the cell inner (cytoplasmic) membrane, where it carries out transport. UniProt annotates 12 transmembrane helices and an inner-membrane location; detailed topology work on the E. coli ortholog established a 13-helix motif with the N-terminus in the periplasm and the C-terminus in the cytoplasm, subsequently refined to a LeuT-like core of 5+5 inverted-repeat transmembrane domains (PMID: 9756872; PMID: 22201772).

The P. putida protein is highly similar to characterized orthologs, licensing mechanistic transfer. A global Needleman–Wunsch alignment computed in this investigation gave 80.3% identity (391/487 aligned residues) between Q88D81 and E. coli PutP (P07117). This is independently corroborated by the observation that P. aeruginosa PutP is "80% identity to the P. putida counterpart" (PMID: 12270821). The review literature defines the fold: "PutP has a core structure of five plus five transmembrane domains forming an inverted repeat similar as originally revealed by the crystal structure of the Na+/leucine transporter LeuT" (PMID: 22201772). Within this core, Asp55 (TM II) is essential for transport and directly coordinates Na⁺: "Asp55 (putative TM II) is essential for transport and proposed to interact directly with Na+" (PMID: 9693722).

Finding 3 — PutP is the uptake step of the proline-utilization (put) pathway, feeding proline catabolism to glutamate

PutP operates at the head of a two-component catabolic module. It imports L-proline; the downstream bifunctional flavoenzyme PutA then oxidizes proline → Δ¹-pyrroline-5-carboxylate (P5C) → glutamate via its proline dehydrogenase and P5C dehydrogenase activities: "Proline utilization A (PutA) is a membrane-associated multifunctional enzyme that catalyzes the oxidation of proline to glutamate in a two-step process" (PMID: 18767154). This enables P. putida KT2440 to use proline as sole carbon and nitrogen source: "Pseudomonas putida KT2440 uses proline as the sole C and N source" (PMID: 11097893).

The pathway is autoregulated. In P. putida, PutA moonlights as an autogenous transcriptional repressor of the put genes: "The PutA protein acts as a repressor of put gene expression in P. putida because expression from the put promoters is constitutive in a host background with a knockout putA gene" (PMID: 11097893). The DNA-binding function is mediated by an N-terminal ribbon-helix-helix domain (PpPutA45) that binds the put operator (PMID: 18767154). A secondary, family-associated role in osmoadaptation is proposed: "the transporter may supply cells with compatible solute during adaptation to osmotic stress" (PMID: 22201772). (In the related P. aeruginosa, the putAP genes form an operon regulated by the AraC/XylS-family activator PruR — PMID: 12270821 — illustrating divergence in regulatory architecture among pseudomonads.)

Finding 5 — Genomic context confirms the divergent putP–putA pair (PP_4946/PP_4947)

Genome/UniProt lookup shows that PP_4946 = putP (Q88D81, Na⁺/proline symporter) is immediately adjacent to PP_4947 = putA (Q88D80, "Bifunctional protein PutA," proline dehydrogenase + Δ¹-pyrroline-5-carboxylate dehydrogenase). The other flanking locus, PP_4945 (Q88D82), is an unrelated rRNA large-subunit methyltransferase and is not part of the module. This adjacency matches the experimentally characterized divergently-transcribed, proline-inducible arrangement in KT2440: "Sequence analysis revealed that the putA and putP genes are transcribed divergently" (PMID: 11097893). The genomic pairing independently corroborates the functional assignment — PutP is co-located and co-regulated with the enzyme that consumes its transported product.

Transport mechanism. PutP is a secondary active transporter that couples the downhill movement of Na⁺ (driven by the membrane electrochemical Na⁺ gradient) to the uphill accumulation of L-proline — a symport mechanism. Structurally it adopts the LeuT-like 5+5 inverted-repeat fold shared across the SSS family, which operates by alternating access: the substrate/ion binding site is alternately exposed to the periplasmic and cytoplasmic sides through coordinated movement of the two inverted-repeat halves and opening/closing of external and internal gates. The conserved residue set defines the machine: Asp103 (≡ E. coli Asp55) and Thr389 (≡ Thr341) form the Na⁺ coordination chemistry; Ser102/Ser105/Gly106 and TM IX residues line the proline translocation pathway; Glu359 (≡ Glu311) at external loop 4 controls external-gate closure; and Tyr62 (≡ Tyr14) in TM I participates in the Na⁺-coupled co-transport network. Electrophysiological studies of the E. coli carrier show that both Na⁺ and the neutral proline substrate generate charge displacements on binding, that either substrate can bind individually but an ordered mechanism dominates under physiological conditions, and that the two binding sites cooperate (PMID: 15476811).

Physiological role. The transporter is the committed uptake step of proline catabolism. Imported proline is handed to PutA, a membrane-associated bifunctional flavoenzyme that converts it to glutamate in two oxidation steps, funneling proline-derived carbon and nitrogen into central metabolism. Because putP and putA are divergently transcribed from a shared, proline-inducible intergenic region and PutA autoregulates the operon, PutP expression is tightly coupled to substrate availability — the transporter is made when proline is present and catabolism is warranted. A secondary osmoadaptive role — importing proline as a compatible solute during osmotic stress — is a recognized function of SSS-family proline transporters and is proposed for PutP, though in many organisms this is handled by dedicated osmoregulated transporters (e.g., OpuE in Bacillus subtilis, PMID: 11902719) rather than the catabolic PutP.

Localization. PutP carries out its function embedded in the inner (cytoplasmic) membrane, with its transport pathway spanning the lipid bilayer; the N-terminus faces the periplasm and the C-terminus the cytoplasm. Its catalytic "output" (delivery of proline to the cytoplasmic face) is spatially matched to the location of PutA at/near the inner membrane.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No direct biochemistry on the P. putida protein. Transport kinetics (Kₘ for proline, K₀.₅ for Na⁺, Vmax, Na⁺:proline stoichiometry) have not been measured for Q88D81 specifically. All quantitative mechanistic parameters are inferred from E. coli/H. pylori orthologs. The inference is strong (80% identity; 100% functional-residue conservation) but remains an inference.

  2. No experimental structure. There is no crystal or cryo-EM structure of P. putida PutP; the LeuT-like 5+5 fold and residue roles are transferred from homology/topology models and the E. coli mutagenesis literature.

  3. N-terminal extension not characterized. The P. putida protein is ~40 residues longer than E. coli PutP (542 vs 502 aa). The function/topology of this N-terminal extension is unknown and could add regulatory or localization features.

  4. Osmoadaptive role unproven for this ortholog. The compatible-solute/osmoprotection role is a family-level proposal (PMID: 22201772); whether P. putida PutP contributes to osmotolerance has not been tested. In several bacteria this role is served by a separate transporter (OpuE-type).

  5. Regulatory details specific to KT2440. While PutA autorepression and divergent transcription are established, the identity of the σ⁵⁴-dependent positive regulator of the P. putida put system remains unresolved (noted as still unidentified in the primary study).

  6. Substrate range not exhaustively mapped. L-proline is the established substrate; whether the P. putida carrier accepts proline analogs (e.g., 3,4-dehydroproline, L-azetidine-2-carboxylate — competitive substrates in orthologs) or uses Li⁺ as an alternative coupling ion has not been directly tested here.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Direct transport assay in a defined background. Express P. putida PP_4946 in an E. coli putP⁻ strain and measure Na⁺-dependent [¹⁴C]-proline uptake to obtain Kₘ(proline), K₀.₅(Na⁺), and confirm obligatory Na⁺ coupling for this specific ortholog.

  2. Proteoliposome reconstitution. Purify Q88D81 and reconstitute into proteoliposomes (as done for H. pylori PutP, PMID: 24358297) to determine Na⁺:proline stoichiometry and test Li⁺ substitution and electrogenicity via SSM electrophysiology.

  3. Targeted mutagenesis validation. Mutate the predicted Na⁺-site (Asp103, Thr389) and gate (Glu359) residues to confirm that the conserved positions perform the same roles as their E. coli counterparts; loss of transport would validate the homology-based mechanistic transfer.

  4. Structural prediction analysis. Retrieve and analyze the AlphaFold model for Q88D81 (pLDDT, PAE), superpose onto vSGLT/LeuT and E. coli PutP models, structurally place the conserved functional residues, and characterize the N-terminal extension.

  5. Genetic phenotyping in KT2440. Construct a clean PP_4946 deletion and test (a) growth on proline as sole C/N source, (b) proline uptake rate, and (c) osmotolerance with proline as sole osmoprotectant — to directly quantify PutP's catabolic vs osmoadaptive contributions in the native host.

  6. Regulation mapping. Use RT-qPCR/reporter fusions to map proline-inducibility of the PP_4946/PP_4947 divergent promoters in KT2440, test PutA-dependent autorepression, and identify the σ⁵⁴-dependent activator.

Conclusion

PutP (PP_4946, UniProt Q88D81) of Pseudomonas putida KT2440 is an integral inner-membrane sodium/proline symporter of the solute:sodium symporter family (TC 2.A.21 / SLC5) that catalyzes electrogenic, Na⁺-coupled uptake of extracellular L-proline into the cytoplasm. It functions as the dedicated import step of the proline-utilization (put) regulon — physically paired with and divergently transcribed from putA (PP_4947), whose bifunctional enzyme oxidizes the imported proline to glutamate — enabling growth on proline as sole carbon and nitrogen source, with a probable secondary role supplying proline as an osmoprotective compatible solute. The assignment is supported by direct transport evidence in KT2440, obligate Na⁺-coupling in family orthologs, the preserved putP–putA genomic architecture, and 100% conservation of all experimentally-defined ion, substrate, and gating residues from the ~80%-identical, intensively studied E. coli ortholog.
