# cbrB Gene Review Notes

## Identity and naming

- The KT2440 locus reviewed here is PP_4696. Primary literature on this strain consistently treats PP_4696 as `cbrB`, whereas the current UniProt entry Q88DX2 uses the homology-based name `AlgB` and assigns an alginate-related product description [file:PSEPK/cbrB/cbrB-uniprot.txt "GN   OrderedLocusNames=PP_4696 {ECO:0000313|EMBL:AAN70269.1};"] [file:PSEPK/cbrB/cbrB-uniprot.txt "DE   RecName: Full=Alginate biosynthesis transcriptional regulatory protein AlgB {ECO:0000256|ARBA:ARBA00073743};"]

## Core molecular role

- CbrB is the response regulator output of the CbrAB two-component system and directly activates sigma-N-dependent transcription [PMID:23692431 Transcriptional activation of the CrcZ and CrcY regulatory RNAs by the CbrB response regulator in Pseudomonas putida, "We show that the response regulatory protein CbrB, an activator of σ(N) -dependent promoters, directly controls the expression of the small RNAs CrcZ and CrcY in P. putida."]
- CbrA transfers phosphate to CbrB in vitro, supporting the missing MF term `phosphorelay response regulator activity` for cbrB [PMID:32214184 Transport and kinase activities of CbrA of Pseudomonas putida KT2440., "it is shown that the histidine kinase can autophosphorylate and transfer the phosphoryl group to the response regulator CbrB."]
- The 2018 regulon paper reinforces that CbrB is a sigma-N-dependent activator with direct DNA-level control over many loci and unusual phospho-dependence properties [PMID:30557364 The CbrB Regulon: Promoter dissection reveals novel insights into the CbrAB expression network in Pseudomonas putida., "determined that it directly controls the expression of at least 61 genes"] [PMID:30557364 The CbrB Regulon: Promoter dissection reveals novel insights into the CbrAB expression network in Pseudomonas putida., "CbrB is a quite peculiar σN-dependent activator since it is barely dependent on phosphorylation for transcriptional activation."]

## Biological role

- CbrAB/CbrB is a high-level regulator of carbon/nitrogen balance and amino-acid assimilation in P. putida rather than a pathway-specific metabolic enzyme [PMID:20553554 Lack of CbrB in Pseudomonas putida affects not only amino acids metabolism but also different stress responses and biofilm development., "CbrB is involved in coordination with the nitrogen control system activator, NtrC, in the uptake and assimilation of several amino acids."] [PMID:32214184 Transport and kinase activities of CbrA of Pseudomonas putida KT2440., "CbrA/CbrB regulates carbon utilization and together with NtrB/NtrC ensures a balanced carbon/nitrogen relationship"]
- A central direct output is activation of the regulatory RNAs CrcZ and CrcY, linking CbrB to carbon catabolite repression control [PMID:23692431 Transcriptional activation of the CrcZ and CrcY regulatory RNAs by the CbrB response regulator in Pseudomonas putida, "Thus, CbrAB constitute a global signal transduction pathway integrated in a higher regulatory network that also controls catabolite repression through the expression of the two regulatory RNAs CrcZ and CrcY."] [PMID:22053874 Two small RNAs, CrcY and CrcZ, act in concert to sequester the Crc global regulator in Pseudomonas putida, modulating catabolite repression., "The CbrA/CbrB two-component system activated crcZ transcription, but had little effect on crcY."]
- Broader phenotypes including chemotaxis, stress tolerance and biofilm behaviour are real, but they are likely a mixture of direct and indirect effects and should not all be elevated to core GO claims [PMID:20553554 Lack of CbrB in Pseudomonas putida affects not only amino acids metabolism but also different stress responses and biofilm development., "CbrB affects other carbon utilization pathways and a number of apparently unrelated functions, such as chemotaxis, stress tolerance and biofilm development."] [PMID:27085034 A Pseudomonas putida cbrB transposon insertion mutant displays a biofilm hyperproducing phenotype that is resistant to dispersal., "Also, two additional point mutations in lapG and lapD have been detected in MPO406 by whole genome sequencing. Combination of these effects provides a robust biofilm overproducing phenotype."]

## Curation implications

- Keep `GO:0000160 phosphorelay signal transduction system` as a core BP term.
- Add `GO:0000156 phosphorelay response regulator activity` as a missing core MF term.
- Replace the generic transcription BP annotation with activator-specific language centered on `GO:0045893 positive regulation of DNA-templated transcription`.
- Add `GO:0001216 DNA-binding transcription activator activity` as a core MF term for direct activation of sigma-N-dependent targets.
- Add `GO:0061985 carbon catabolite repression` as a process term only because there is direct evidence that CbrB controls catabolite repression through `crcZ` and `crcY`; keep stress/biofilm terms in the narrative unless a direct target-level GO mapping is justified.

## 2026-11-08 full re-review and target evidence audit

This audit supersedes the broad “61-gene direct regulon” interpretation above. It reviews all six existing/proposed GO rows and records target-level evidence separately in `cbrB-regulatory-evidence.yaml`. No new metabolic enzyme, transporter, amino-acid-catabolic or biofilm process annotation was inferred from a regulator knockout phenotype.

### Identity, experimental strain and evidence access

- Q88DX2 is the local UniProt protein at PP_4696, taxon NCBITaxon:160488. The accessible KT2440 primary full text explicitly says “To individually delete cbrA (PP_4695) and cbrB (PP_4696) from the genome of P. putida KT2440” [PMID:32214184, Methods; `publications/PMID_32214184.md`; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-62337-9]. This resolves the literature-to-locus identity used here. The automated AlgB product label is not evidence that the alginate-specific regulatory role has been demonstrated at this locus; neither is its presence proof of a mistaken experimental annotation.
- The 2018 promoter-dissection experiments use KT2442 and its MPO401 ΔcbrB derivative. Its Table 1 coordinates and ChIP mapping use the KT2440 reference GCF_000007565.1_ASM756v1. These are retained as separate experimental and reference contexts, rather than silently relabeling the experiments KT2440 [PMID:30557364, Methods/Table 1; `publications/PMID_30557364.md`; https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0209191].
- Full cached primary texts were inspected for PMID:30557364 and PMID:32214184. PMC web requests encountered a browser challenge; the local cached full texts remained available. The caches for PMID:23692431, PMID:20553554, PMID:22053874 and PMID:27085034 are abstract-only. No experimental annotation was rejected because its full text could not be accessed. The 2013 direct-activation claim is supported independently by the accessible 2018 promoter assays.
- `just deep-research-falcon PSEPK cbrB --fallback perplexity-lite` completed using provider output; provider reports are retained as sources for finding papers, not independent corroboration. `just fetch-gene-pmids PSEPK cbrB` cached/confirmed all six PMIDs. The MOD command reports no configured PSEPK provider. `just fetch-fitness PSEPK cbrB` failed because neither local FEBA data nor the remote source was available; no fitness inference is made. `just paint-losses PSEPK cbrB` reports Q88DX2 is not a leaf of the available PTHR32071 tree, so lineage losses could not be checked. There are no IBA rows in this review; the final generic “no loss” output is not interpreted as complete phylogenetic coverage.

### Directness, regulatory sign and target scope

- The full text states “a total of 61 individual DNA fragments following these premises were selected.” These are ChIP-derived candidate regions after filtering, not 61 individually functionally validated activation relationships. Of 12 expression-tested targets, seven were activated, three repressed, and two showed no significant expression difference [PMID:30557364, Results/Table 2]. ChIP enrichment and transcript response remain different evidence types.
- Strong promoter-mechanism evidence supports activation of crcZ, crcY and PP2810-PP2813: purified CbrB binding, promoter-subsite substitutions, and lacZ reporters; PP2810 also has DNase I footprinting. See Figures 2-5 and S3 [PMID:30557364]. RNA loci are intentionally omitted from the sidecar because no stable RNA locus identifier was reconciled in this pass.
- PP3420 has ChIP enrichment, purified-protein EMSA and increased wild-type expression (WT/ΔcbrB ratio 11.7 in Table 2). Its direct activating relationship is supported by their combination; a causal binding-site mutation was not shown for this promoter [PMID:30557364].
- oprD/PP_1206 has ChIP enrichment, purified-protein EMSA and repression (WT/ΔcbrB transcript ratio 0.3). This combination supports a direct repressive relationship, with the explicit limitation that the causal repressive site and sigma-factor mechanism were not established. The authors discuss possible sigma-54-independent repression; it would be wrong to make every CbrB edge sigma-54-dependent activation [PMID:30557364, Figure 1/Table 2/Discussion, “three of the target genes validated by quantitative RT-PCR resulted to be repressed (oprD, rpoN and glgA).”].
- rpoN/PP_0952 and glgA/PP_4050 have negative expression responses and ChIP evidence, but no equally resolved promoter mechanism in the inspected study. glgA EMSA was negative under the tested conditions; this does not prove absence of physiological binding. These remain follow-up leads, as do activated PP3074, flgB/PP4391, argT/PP4486 and PP4643. No direct edge was manufactured from these weaker combinations or from ICA module membership. PP3009 and PP3656 had no significant expression response under the tested condition.
- Oxaloacetate is an inducing growth condition, not a demonstrated CbrB ligand. RNA methods specify four-hour OAA induction because strains did not grow on OAA under that protocol. The sidecar therefore says induction for transcript comparisons, rather than claiming steady-state growth for both genotypes.

### GO decisions and mechanistic boundaries

- ACCEPT phosphorelay signaling: purified CbrA-to-CbrB phosphotransfer directly supports this process and the retained NEW phosphorelay response regulator activity proposal [PMID:32214184]. CbrA autokinase/transport experiments are not CbrB ATPase or transport assays.
- ACCEPT ATP binding as part of the core AAA+ transcription-activation mechanism, supported by the InterPro domain. The earlier KEEP_AS_NON_CORE rationale confused generality with biological peripherality. No separate CbrB ATP-hydrolysis measurement was verified, so no NEW ATPase term is proposed.
- MODIFY general transcription regulation to both positive and negative regulation, reflecting target-specific evidence instead of the former blanket positive-only claim. The negative term summarizes the reported expression-regulation direction, without asserting that repression uses the sigma-54 activation mechanism. Retain the NEW DNA-binding transcription activator activity proposal, supported by direct promoter experiments.
- Retain the proposed carbon catabolite repression term as regulatory participation through direct crcZ/crcY activation, not participation in the regulated metabolic reactions. QuickGO's definition for GO:0061985 is “A process in which the presence of one carbon source leads to the modulation of the frequency, rate, or extent of the metabolism of other carbon sources.” Thus the term includes modulation rather than requiring CbrB to be the downstream repressor. Its core-function entry describes the regulatory RNA step that CbrB performs [PMID:23692431; PMID:30557364].
- Comparator check: QuickGO queries of B. subtilis CcpA (P25144) and E. coli CRP (P0ACJ8) found activator/repressor and positive/negative transcription-regulation annotations; CRP also carries GO:0045013, a child of GO:0061985. CcpA lacked this particular process term. This does not establish a systematic convention excluding transcription regulators from carbon catabolite repression, and neither comparator is used as evidence for the P. putida claim. Exact GO:0061985 had no direct annotations in the current query; child-term annotations must be included when assessing coverage. API: `https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/services/annotation/search?geneProductId=UniProtKB:P25144&limit=100` and equivalent P0ACJ8 query.
- Author-supplied GO labels/definitions were checked through the official QuickGO ontology API for GO:0000156, GO:0001216, GO:0045893, GO:0045892 and GO:0061985; ATP binding was also checked. The validated review retains two core functions: phosphorelay response regulator and DNA-binding transcription activator. Repression remains supported target-specific behavior without an additional, less-resolved core molecular mechanism.
- No CbrB small-molecule effector is established here. The 2020 study says “The lack of an effect of L-histidine leaves open the possibility that a yet to be identified intracellular metabolite is perceived by CbrA as a signal.” Histidine transport/binding belongs to CbrA; it cannot be copied to CbrB [PMID:32214184]. Physiological phosphorylation dependence and activation of excess unphosphorylated protein in vitro are different conditions, not contradictory universal claims [PMID:23692431 abstract; PMID:30557364].

Follow-up priorities: obtain the 2013 full text; establish the sigma-factor and site dependence of repression; test functional regulation among the remaining ChIP candidates; reconcile stable identifiers for the regulatory RNAs; resolve the automated AlgB functional label against the experimentally identified PP_4696 locus; and repeat fitness/PAINT queries when those data are available. These are explicit gaps, not reasons to add unsupported GO annotations.

### Follow-up correction: withdraw the authored carbon catabolite repression proposal

The earlier same-session decision to retain NEW GO:0061985 is superseded here after checking its ancestry and the exact regulatory role. This was an authored proposal, not an imported experimental GO annotation, so the proposal is withdrawn from `existing_annotations` rather than converted into a REMOVE judgment on an existing curator assertion. The GO:0061985 `directly_involved_in` entry is also withdrawn from the activator core function. Its primary-source findings remain useful biological context.

The official QuickGO API gives GO:0061985 an `is_a` parent GO:0061984, catabolite repression. That parent's definition is “A process in which the presence of one nutrient source leads to a decrease in the frequency, rate, or extent of processes involved in the metabolism of other nutrient sources.” Its ancestry includes cellular response to nutrient, not a distinct regulation-of-repression process. The child's word “modulation” does not cancel the parent's decrease requirement. CbrB activates crcZ/crcY, whose products antagonize the downstream repressive machinery; this establishes regulation/relief of repression, without establishing that CbrB executes repression itself. The 2013 abstract's “controls catabolite repression” phrase is not sufficient to assert `directly_involved_in` repression. The 2018 full text distinguishes CbrB activation of regulatory RNAs from their sequestration/inactivation of the repressive machinery [PMID:23692431; PMID:30557364].

A closer same-role comparator is P. aeruginosa PAO1 CbrB (Q9HV73/PA4726), whose primary study characterizes activation of crcZ and antagonism of Crc repression [PMID:21478360, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21478360/]. QuickGO returns direct DNA-binding/activator annotations from that paper and positive transcription regulation, but no GO:0061985 or its descendants. The named Pf-5 CbrB homolog Q4K5Y7/PFL_5274 likewise has no carbon-catabolite-repression annotation in the current query; this second comparison is an annotation-pattern check only, not independent experimental establishment of the same mechanism. Neither absence alone proves a prohibition. However, CRP and CcpA in the prior comparison act at a different regulatory layer and cannot justify filling this upstream-regulator process term. Combined with the participation test and ontology parent definition, the conservative decision is withdrawal.

QuickGO searches did not establish a specific regulation-of-carbon-catabolite-repression term to substitute. No new term identifier is invented or broader process substituted merely to fill the gap. The sidecar retains GO:0061985 only as the target of `relationship: regulates`, and the core description explicitly explains relief of Hfq/Crc repression. Sources checked: `https://www.ebi.ac.uk/QuickGO/services/ontology/go/terms/GO:0061984`, `.../GO:0061985/ancestors`, and annotation searches for UniProtKB:Q9HV73 and UniProtKB:Q4K5Y7. This leaves five reviewed GO rows and two core functions.
