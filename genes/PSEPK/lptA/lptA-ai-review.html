Gene Description

LptA is a soluble periplasmic lipopolysaccharide-binding and transfer protein that forms the central bridge of the Lpt trans-envelope machinery. It receives lipopolysaccharide from inner-membrane LptC and transfers it toward the LptDE outer-membrane translocon for outer-membrane assembly.

PMID:17056748
Characterization of lptA and lptB, two essential genes implicated in lipopolysaccharide transport to the outer membrane of Escherichia coli.
  • E. coli LptA/LptB depletion disrupts LPS targeting to the outer membrane. Together with biochemical transfer studies this supports a transport role rather than attributing all envelope phenotypes to direct catalytic activities.
    "Similar defects are observed in cells in which LptA and LptB are individually depleted (Fig. 6 ), indicating that both proteins are required for LPS targeting to the OM."
PMID:18424520
Functional analysis of the protein machinery required for transport of lipopolysaccharide to the outer membrane of Escherichia coli.
  • E. coli localization and depletion distinguish cytoplasmic LptB, membrane-anchored LptC and periplasmic LptA within one LPS-assembly pathway.
    "Thus, we have identified an essential LPS transport component in every cellular compartment: LptB in the cytoplasm, LptC in the IM, LptA in the periplasm, and LptD and LptE in the OM."
PMID:18480051
The LptA protein of Escherichia coli is a periplasmic lipid A-binding protein involved in the lipopolysaccharide export pathway.
  • Purified E. coli LptA binds structurally diverse LPS through the lipid A region, directly supporting substrate binding; KT2440 transfer is inferred from specialized family membership and the conserved bridge role.
    "Here we provide evidence that LptA binds structurally diverse LPS substrates in vitro and demonstrate that it interacts specifically with the lipid A domain of LPS."
PMID:20446753
Proteins required for lipopolysaccharide assembly in Escherichia coli form a transenvelope complex.
  • E. coli membrane fractionation and copurification support a trans-envelope Lpt assembly. LptA cofractionation denotes assembled membrane association, not an integral outer-membrane beta-barrel.
    "Remarkably, LptA was also found to co-fractionate almost exclusively with these OM fractions (Figure 2B)."
PMID:20720015
Structure and functional analysis of LptC, a conserved membrane protein involved in the lipopolysaccharide export pathway in Escherichia coli.
  • E. coli LptC binds LPS and transfers it directionally to LptA; cysteine-accessibility experiments orient its soluble domain toward the periplasm.
    "In vitro, LptA can displace lipopolysaccharide from LptC (but not vice versa), consistent with their locations and their proposed placement in a unidirectional export pathway."
PMID:22668317
Regulated assembly of the transenvelope protein complex required for lipopolysaccharide export.
  • E. coli assembly experiments support domain-to-domain connection of the periplasmic bridge; membrane cofractionation does not make LptA an integral membrane protein.
    "This protein has been proposed to physically link the IM and the OM because it is the only periplasmic Lpt component without a membrane tether and because it fractionates with both membranes."
PMID:29449493
Lipopolysaccharide is transported to the cell surface by a membrane-to-membrane protein bridge.
  • Purified E. coli Lpt components reconstitute ATP-dependent membrane-to-membrane LPS movement. Necessity/sufficiency refers to the tested assembled seven-protein system, not autonomous transport by every component.
    "Thus, all seven Lpt proteins and ATP are necessary and sufficient for membrane-to-membrane transport to occur."
