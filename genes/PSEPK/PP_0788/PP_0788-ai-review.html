Gene Description

PP_0788 encodes a 719-residue soluble, predicted cytoplasmic protein. Its only domain content is the DUF1631 family: Pfam PF07793 N-terminal domain plus the PF26671, PF26672 and PF26670 sub-domains. It has no recognizable catalytic motif and its molecular function is unknown. The UniProt name "Thymidine phosphorylase" is a ProtNLM machine prediction (ECO:0008006) and is wrong. The protein lacks the glycosyltransferase family 3 domains (PF00591, PF02885) that define DeoA-type thymidine phosphorylases. The eggNOG COG1570 (exonuclease VII large subunit) label is also unsupported: KT2440 has a genuine XseA, PP_1027. Transcription of PP_0788 is directly controlled by FleQ and c-di-GMP. FleQ binds two sites in its promoter, and high c-di-GMP represses it in a FleQ-dependent way (PMID:33975969). This links the gene to the motile/sessile switch without assigning it a function. The gene lies 88 bp upstream of, and co-directional with, ampD-ampE. PP_0788 has no fitness phenotype in 300 KT2440 RB-TnSeq experiments, but no motility or nitrate-stress assays were run in KT2440. The P. simiae WCS417 ortholog has reproducibly reduced fitness in soft-agar motility assays and milder defects under nitrate, choline chloride, bacitracin and phosphomycin stress. The stress phenotypes are not consistent across orthologs. Distant DUF1631 proteins in Xanthomonas campestris have pleiotropic mutant phenotypes (motility, EPS, biofilm, virulence) and deregulated c-di-GMP signalling genes (PMID:35706385, PMID:39608507). The family may act in c-di-GMP-linked lifestyle control, but its activity is uncharacterized. This review is filed for the KBase gene-annotate dashboard protein NZ_CAKNDZ010000038.1_57, a P. putida strain whose exact UniProt entries were deleted. That protein is 94.7% identical to PP_0788 over 717 aligned residues and differs only by a longer, non-conserved N-terminal leader from an alternative start-codon call.

The locus tag, organism and domain composition all match. No paper describes a different gene that shares this symbol, because "PP_0788" is a locus tag rather than a gene name. There is no published experimental literature on PP_0788 or on its P. aeruginosa ortholog PA4523. 2. The "Thymidine phosphorylase" name is not supported

  • The name was generated by the ProtNLM machine-learning model (ECO:0008006). Under this project's quoting rules it is an unvetted prediction, not evidence.
  • Thymidine phosphorylase (DeoA, EC 2.4.2.4) belongs to glycosyltransferase family 3. Real members have the Glycos_transf_3 domain (PF00591) and the Glycos_trans_3N domain (PF02885), and are roughly 440 aa long. Q88PR0 has neither. Its four Pfam matches are all DUF1631 sub-domains (local UniProt record).
  • P. putida KT2440 has a separate, conventionally annotated deoA. PP_0788 shares no motif or domain with that enzyme family.
  • The genome annotation (Nelson et al. 2002, PMID:12534463; re-annotation by Belda et al. 2016, PMID:26913973) and KEGG both call it "conserved protein of unknown function".

Conclusion: any thymidine phosphorylase activity, or nucleoside/pyrimidine salvage role, inferred from this name should be treated as a likely ML hallucination. It should not be propagated into GO.

A second label is also doubtful. The record's eggNOG cross-reference is COG1570, which is the XseA family (exodeoxyribonuclease VII large subunit). STRING's link between PP_0788 and xseB (score 0.848) seems to rest mainly on that orthology assignment, through homology and coexpression channels with no neighbourhood support. InterPro detects no OB-fold or ExoVII_L (PF02601) domain in Q88PR0, so I consider the COG1570 assignment unreliable too. That is my inference and has not been tested.

3. Domain and family: DUF1631

  • Pfam/InterPro definition: PF07793 is the "Protein of unknown function (DUF1631) N-terminal domain". InterPro describes it as "the N-terminal helical domain of DUF1631 protein family proteins. The members of this family are sequences derived from a group of hypothetical proteins expressed by certain bacterial species" (InterPro API, PF07793, retrieved 2026-10-30; https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/entry/pfam/PF07793). Pfam has recently split the full-length family into four sub-domain models (2nd, 3rd and C-terminal: PF26671/2/0). This implies a multi-domain protein of about 700 aa, consistent with the 719-aa length of Q88PR0.
  • NCBI CDD (pfam07793) also lists it as a domain of unknown function from hypothetical bacterial proteins, spanning about 440 residues. Seed members include Ramlibacter, Marinobacter, Azoarcus, Thiobacillus and Polaromonas, that is, β- and γ-Proteobacteria (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Structure/cdd/pfam07793).
  • Family size: UniProtKB holds 2,714 entries with PF07793 (UniProt REST query, 2026-10-30). None is Swiss-Prot reviewed, and no member has an experimentally characterized function.
  • Sequence features (my inspection of the sequence): the sequence is rich in Leu, Ala, Arg and Gln and has no obvious signal peptide or hydrophobic transmembrane stretch at the N-terminus. A charged, low-complexity, likely coiled-coil segment sits around residues 440–500 (e.g. "ERRRNELLEQRTRDAEEGRARAMQARQQVQ"). Another family member, A0A8T7DK31, carries a coiled-coil annotation (https://db.cngb.org/data_resources/protein/A0A8T7DK31). Overall this suggests a soluble, largely α-helical, cytoplasmic protein, possibly with scaffold or protein-interaction character. No catalytic motif is recognizable. All of this is inference, not tested.

4. Orthologs and conservation

  • P. aeruginosa PAO1 ortholog: PA4523 (Q9HVQ1, 751 aa, "DUF1631 domain-containing protein"; UniProt REST). It is the only PF07793 protein in PAO1. It is unannotated in UniProt and has no functional annotations in the PAE systems-biology resource, apart from membership in regulatory/coexpression modules (https://networks.systemsbiology.net/pae/gene/PA4523).
  • The family is a single-copy gene conserved across Pseudomonadaceae and other Proteobacteria. Wide conservation suggests a role worth keeping, but there is no direct evidence of what it is.

1. Identity and the 18-residue length difference

  • Query: dashboard protein NZ_CAKNDZ010000038.1_57 (737 aa). Subject: KT2440 PP_0788 (Q88PR0, 719 aa).
  • Global alignment (BLOSUM62, free end gaps): 679 identical residues over 717 aligned pairs, 94.7% identity. The mapping table gives 94.6% over 719 pairs.
  • The proteins differ only at the N-terminus. The query has 36 residues before the conserved motif RTPHPCLP (query 37, subject 19). PP_0788 has 18. Query residues 1-20 (MALTIFSPFGQDTLQGVQDA) have no counterpart, and the next ~16 residues align poorly with the PP_0788 N-terminus (MQKEGKVVPLAATIDRGG). From RTPHPCLP to the C-terminus the two sequences are colinear and end together (...ESLRQGQAQ).
  • This segment lies in the region InterProScan marks as disordered on the query (MobiDBLite 18-40), N-terminal to the conserved DUF1631 core. So the length difference comes from a different start-codon call, or a strain-specific N-terminal sequence, in a non-conserved leader. It does not affect the family assignment.
  • KT2440 genome (NC_002947.4, PP_0788 CDS 903742..905901 +): reading the same frame upstream of the annotated ATG, the open extension after the last in-frame stop is LCDFLPIMATSIWRHSFDDFSALSGKTPCKESR, with one in-frame ATG 26 codons upstream. It does not resemble the query's leader. The KT2440 start call (MQKEG...) is not contradicted.

2. Domain content and the "Thymidine phosphorylase" name

  • The query's InterProScan has one hit, Pfam PF07793 DUF1631, at 40-731 (E = 8.1e-213, covering 99.7% of the HMM). It also has MobiDBLite disorder at 18-40 and 243-265, and a coil at 466-486 (dashboard page).
  • In UniProt, Q88PR0 carries four Pfam DUF1631 sub-domain models: PF07793 25-217, PF26671 285-433, PF26672 465-578 and PF26670 630-696. These are the newer split of the old full-length PF07793.
  • Q88PR0 has no glycosyltransferase family 3 domain (PF00591 Glycos_transf_3 or PF02885 Glycos_trans_3N). Thymidine phosphorylase (DeoA, EC 2.4.2.4) and its paralog TrpD carry these domains. A UniProt query of KT2440 (taxon 160488) for EC 2.4.2.4, "thymidine phosphorylase" or PF00591 returns three entries. One is TrpD PP_0421 (PF02885;PF00591). One is PpnP PP_4248 (PF06865). The third is Q88PR0, which has only DUF1631 models.
  • The UniProt RecName "Thymidine phosphorylase" is evidenced only by ECO:0008006 Google:ProtNLM. Several Fitness Browser orthologs carry the same string (WCS417 GFF773, pseudo1_N1B4 Pf1N1B4_1152, pseudo6_N2E2 Pf6N2E2_3345, pseudo5_N2C3_1 AO356_07365). That points to a propagated legacy gene-description error. The name is unsupported.

  PP_0788 encodes a 719-residue soluble, predicted cytoplasmic protein. Its only
  domain content is the DUF1631 family: Pfam PF07793 N-terminal domain plus the
  PF26671, PF26672 and PF26670 sub-domains. It has no recognizable catalytic motif
  and its molecular function is unknown. The UniProt name "Thymidine phosphorylase"
  is a ProtNLM machine prediction (ECO:0008006) and is wrong. The protein lacks the
  glycosyltransferase family 3 domains (PF00591, PF02885) that define DeoA-type
  thymidine phosphorylases. The eggNOG COG1570 (exonuclease VII large subunit) label
  is also unsupported: KT2440 has a genuine XseA, PP_1027. Transcription of PP_0788
  is directly controlled by FleQ and c-di-GMP. FleQ binds two sites in its promoter,
  and high c-di-GMP represses it in a FleQ-dependent way (PMID:33975969). This links
  the gene to the motile/sessile switch without assigning it a function. The gene
  lies 88 bp upstream of, and co-directional with, ampD-ampE.

  PP_0788 has no fitness phenotype in 300 KT2440 RB-TnSeq experiments, but no
  motility or nitrate-stress assays were run in KT2440. The P. simiae WCS417
  ortholog has reproducibly reduced fitness in soft-agar motility assays and milder
  defects under nitrate, choline chloride, bacitracin and phosphomycin stress. The
  stress phenotypes are not consistent across orthologs. Distant DUF1631 proteins in
  Xanthomonas campestris have pleiotropic mutant phenotypes (motility, EPS, biofilm,
  virulence) and deregulated c-di-GMP signalling genes (PMID:35706385,
  PMID:39608507). The family may act in c-di-GMP-linked lifestyle control, but its
  activity is uncharacterized.

  This review is filed for the KBase gene-annotate dashboard protein
  NZ_CAKNDZ010000038.1_57, a P. putida strain whose exact UniProt entries were
  deleted. That protein is 94.7% identical to PP_0788 over 717 aligned residues and
  differs only by a longer, non-conserved N-terminal leader from an alternative
  start-codon call.
