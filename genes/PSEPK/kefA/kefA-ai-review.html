Gene Description

PP_5067 (kefA, 1102 aa) is the Pseudomonas putida KT2440 member of the MscK subfamily of MscS-type mechanosensitive channels (PANTHER PTHR30347:SF1, NCBIfam PRK11281). It has not been studied experimentally in P. putida. It is 43.1% identical over its full length to E. coli MscK (KefA, P77338) and shares that protein's architecture: an N-terminal signal or anchor segment, a ~465-residue periplasmic region with the MscS porin domain, eleven transmembrane helices that align one-to-one with the eleven of E. coli MscK, and a C-terminal MscS-like pore helix plus cytoplasmic domain. The MscK periplasmic tryptophan gate residue (E. coli W914) and the gating-sensitive glycine (E. coli G922) are conserved. E. coli MscK is a tension-gated heptameric channel that needs high external K+, NH4+, Rb+ or Cs+ to open, with a slight anion preference rather than K+ selectivity; it contributes little to downshock survival. By orthology PP_5067 is therefore a mechanosensitive ion channel of the plasma membrane. Its ion requirements, physiological role and any contribution to antibiotic tolerance in P. putida are untested. The only fitness evidence is from homologs; KT2440 Fitness Browser data could not be retrieved. The review is filed on the KT2440 reference-proteome entry, which is 98.8% identical to the KBase dashboard sequence NZ_LGRH01000055.1_2.

Core Functions

Tension-gated (mechanosensitive) ion channel of the inner membrane, by orthology to E. coli MscK. Gating probably also requires high external K+ or a similar cation, as for E. coli MscK; this is untested in P. putida. The channel is not K+-selective.

1. Key concepts: the MscS family and MscK

Bacteria have mechanosensitive (MS) channels that open in response to tension in the cytoplasmic membrane. During hypo-osmotic shock (a sudden drop in external osmolarity), water floods into the cell. The channels act as emergency release valves, letting solutes out before the cell lyses.

  • MscL and MscS play a vital role in allowing the cell to survive acute hypotonic stress (Li et al. 2002, EMBO J 21:5323–5330, PMID:12374733, PMC129069).
  • MscK is a larger, multidomain member of the MscS family. It appears to be more sensitive to membrane tension than its homologue MscS. Unlike MscS, its activity appears to be regulated by the external ionic environment (Li et al. 2002).
  • MscK gating needs high external K⁺, NH₄⁺, Rb⁺ or Cs⁺. No activity was seen with Na⁺, Li⁺ or NMDG⁺ (Li et al. 2002). This is a mechanical gate with an extracellular ionic co-requirement. It is not a K⁺-selective pore, and "K⁺ efflux" in the UniProt name reflects the history of the gene name.
  • The original genetic description of MscS and MscK as separate activities is Levina et al. 1999 (EMBO J 18:1730–1737). I cite this from memory: it is referenced in Li et al. 2002 but I did not retrieve it here. That work showed that cells lacking both MscS and MscL are sensitive to downshock, and that MscK contributes only marginally under standard conditions.
  • Genetic work on the E. coli gain-of-function allele kefA2, which makes cells sensitive to high external K⁺, supported KefA being a cation-dependent channel involved in osmotic adaptation. This is McLaggan et al. 2002, Mol Microbiol, also cited from memory and discussed in PMC9653487.

2. Structure and gating mechanism (E. coli MscK, 2022)

Mount et al. 2022 (Nat Commun 13:6904, PMID:36371466; PMC9653487) solved cryo-EM structures of E. coli MscK in several states:
- closed (G924S mutant, PDB 7UW5);
- intermediate;
- open (PDB 7UX1).

What the structures show:
- Architecture. MscK is a heptamer. Each subunit has:
- an N-terminal periplasmic domain of about 500 residues;
- a transmembrane domain with eleven helices;
- a cytoplasmic domain that is MscS-like (β-domain plus C-terminal "vestibule").
- Opening. As the channel opens, the transmembrane domain flattens and expands, and the periplasmic ring dilates with it.
- K⁺ sensing. Electrophysiology places the K⁺ sensitivity on the periplasmic side. The authors propose that the periplasmic domain couples external ions to gating. The exact K⁺-sensing residues were not identified in the material I could access.
- Pore and gate. The pore-lining helix (TM-3-equivalent) and the gating mechanism are conserved with MscS. Membrane tension is sensed through lipid–protein interactions at the transmembrane helices.

4. Primary function (inferred)

  • Molecular function. A tension-gated, non-selective, cation-preferring ion channel with mechanosensitive gating, in GO terms mechanosensitive monoatomic ion channel activity (GO:0008381). By orthology it probably requires extracellular K⁺, or another large monovalent cation, to gate. It is not an enzyme, and it is not a substrate-specific transporter. When it opens, ions and small osmolytes flow down their gradients, mainly outward.
  • Biological process.
  • Cellular response to osmotic stress, specifically hypo-osmotic downshock: release of cytoplasmic solutes to limit swelling and lysis. This corresponds to GO:0009992 intracellular water homeostasis and monoatomic ion transmembrane transport.
  • In E. coli MscK is a minor contributor next to MscS and MscL. Its K⁺ dependence suggests a specialized role, active when external K⁺ is high (Li et al. 2002; Mount et al. 2022).
  • Location.
  • The cytoplasmic (inner) membrane, as a multi-pass integral protein (GO:0005886).
  • The large N-terminal domain in the periplasm.
  • The C-terminal domain in the cytoplasm.

5. Context in Pseudomonas

  • Çetiner et al. 2017 ("Tension-activated channels in the mechanism of osmotic fitness in Pseudomonas aeruginosa", J Gen Physiol 149:595–609, doi:10.1085/jgp.201611699; PMC5412531) characterized MS channels in P. aeruginosa. They found MscS-type and MscL-type activities, with MscL at higher density than in E. coli. They concluded that P. aeruginosa relies on MscL as the main osmolyte release valve, while MscS-type channels inactivate under prolonged tension and help terminate the permeability response.
  • The snippets I could see did not show whether the MscK-type homologue was one of the channels characterized.
  • No equivalent electrophysiology or knockout phenotype has been reported for P. putida KT2440 PP_5067. Any role in the downshock survival of this soil saprophyte remains a prediction.

7. Open questions and suggested experiments

  1. Electrophysiology on P. putida giant spheroplasts from wild type and ΔPP_5067 (or heterologous expression in E. coli MJF465/MJF641 lacking its MS channels):
  2. Does PP_5067 form a tension-activated channel?
  3. Does gating require external K⁺, as for E. coli MscK?
  4. Downshock survival assays of ΔPP_5067, alone and combined with deletions of the MscS-family and MscL genes in KT2440, testing the effect of external K⁺.
  5. Whether the periplasmic MscS_porin/coiled-coil domain carries the ion-sensing site; this could be tested by chimeras and point mutants guided by the 7UW5/7UX1 structures.

Summary

kefA (PP_5067, Q88CW3) is not a K⁺ transporter, and despite its name it is unrelated to the KefB/KefC antiporters. It is a large (1102 aa) MscS-family protein in the MscK subfamily, with:
- an N-terminal anchor and ~470-residue periplasmic domain;
- 11 predicted transmembrane helices;
- an MscS-type pore, β-domain and cytoplasmic vestibule.

By orthology to E. coli MscK, it is predicted to be a homoheptameric, membrane-tension-gated, non-selective ion channel whose opening probably requires high extracellular K⁺. Its predicted role is releasing ions and osmolytes during hypo-osmotic shock. No experiments have been reported on the P. putida protein.

The query is a full-length MscK homologue (43.1% identical to E. coli MscK across its whole length), not an MscS-length protein. The MscS-like part is the C-terminal pore helix plus cytoplasmic domain (query 828-1085).

Topology

DeepTMHMM on the query predicts a 1-24 signal peptide, a 25-490 extracytoplasmic region and 11 TM helices, with the C-terminus (919-1102) cytoplasmic.
All 11 predicted query TM segments map, via the global alignment, onto the 11 UniProt TM helices of E. coli MscK in order (TM1 500-520 ... TM11 922-942). The query signal peptide maps onto the MscK UniProt signal region 1-33.
This is the MscK architecture of a ~500-residue N-terminal periplasmic domain, an 11-helix TM domain and a C-terminal MscS-like cytoplasmic domain, as in the cryo-EM structure (PMID:36371466).
DeepTMHMM calls the N-terminal segment a cleavable signal peptide. Whether it is cleaved or acts as an anchor has not been tested for either protein.

Conserved key residues

  • The E. coli MscK W914 periplasmic tryptophan gate ring (PMID:36371466) aligns to query W897, which is conserved.
  • The E. coli MscK G922 (kefA2 mutagenesis site in UniProt) aligns to query G905, which is conserved.
  • The E. coli MscS pore-lining L105/L109 align to query V904/F908, which are hydrophobic.

Genomic context (from dashboard evidence, not re-run)

In KT2440 and several P. fluorescens strains, the gene is flanked upstream by an NhaP2-type K+/H+ antiporter and downstream by SelO. This is conserved neighbourhood, not functional evidence.

Limits

  • No KT2440 fitness data could be retrieved: the Fitness Browser returned HTTP 403.
  • The only fitness phenotypes in the dashboard evidence come from homologs (P. simiae WCS417 76%, polymyxin B; P. stutzeri RCH2 61.6%, nickel; Klebsiella 44%, D-alanine).
  • K+-dependent gating has been shown only for E. coli MscK. Nothing here tests ion selectivity or K+ dependence in P. putida.
