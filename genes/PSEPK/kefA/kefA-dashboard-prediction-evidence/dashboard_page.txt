why
Query InterProScan identifies MscS channel domains and a conserved channel site; topology predicts 11 transmembrane segments. The 98.5%-identity STRING homolog belongs to the MscK orthogroup, consistent with comparative MscK homolog annotations. A 76%-identity Pseudomonas simiae homolog shows repeated polymyxin B fitness defects (−2.768 to −1.339), supporting a role in tolerance. Potassium dependence and ion selectivity remain unestablished.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by specific polymyxin B stress fitness phenotypes, together with convergent query channel domains and homolog annotations. Evidence includes homolog summaries, fitness, InterProScan, conserved neighborhood, STRING, and query membrane topology; no cofitness or direct channel biochemical characterization is provided. The background MscL-like label is outweighed by the query's MscS-family domains and MscK orthogroup assignment; a potassium-specific efflux annotation is unsupported because neither ion selectivity nor potassium-dependent gating was directly demonstrated.

why these terms
The protein is described as a 'small-conductance mechanosensitive ion channel'. This directly supports 'mechanosensitive monoatomic ion channel activity' (MF). Consequently, the biological process of 'monoatomic ion transmembrane transport' (BP) is also supported. Although the trace mentions 'query membrane topology', there is no explicit statement of the protein's subcellular location in the annotation or the trace conclusion; therefore, no CC term is assigned. The trace specifically rejects 'potassium-specific efflux' because ion selectivity was not demonstrated, so more specific ion channel terms (e.g., potassium channel) are excluded.
