enzyme

why
Experimentally characterized EptA homologs from Pseudomonas aeruginosa (46.10% identity) and Pseudomonas syringae (45.10%) add phosphoethanolamine to lipid A. Query phosphoethanolamine-transferase domains and five predicted transmembrane helices support this assignment. Aluminum-sensitive fitness phenotypes provide supporting stress context; weak cofitness correlations do not establish a pathway. Exact lipid A modification position and zinc regulation are not assigned to the query.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by experimentally characterized homologs above 40% identity with explicit lipid A phosphoethanolamine-transfer activity, corroborated by query domain annotations. Available evidence includes homolog summaries, domains, fitness, cofitness, neighborhoods, STRING associations, and query membrane topology. Sulfatase/hydrolase and OpgE glucan-transferase alternatives are less supported: broad domain and orthogroup labels do not outweigh experimentally demonstrated lipid A modification in close homologs.

2 terms
why these terms
The annotation explicitly identifies the protein as 'Lipid A phosphoethanolamine transferase EptA' and describes its activity as 'catalyzing addition of phosphoethanolamine to lipid A'. This directly supports the molecular function 'phosphatidylethanolamine:Kdo2-lipid A phosphoethanolamine transferase activity' (GO:0043838). Since Lipid A is the hydrophobic anchor of lipopolysaccharide (LPS), this modification is part of the 'lipopolysaccharide biosynthetic process' (GO:0009103). The trace mentions 'query membrane topology' and the known nature of EptA, but does not explicitly state a cellular component (e.g., 'inner membrane') in a way that allows for assignment under the strict rules (Rule 5: do not infer location from protein type).
