Gene Description

crc (PP_5292) encodes Crc, the catabolite repression control protein of pseudomonads. Crc belongs to the apurinic/apyrimidinic endonuclease (exonuclease III) fold family but has lost the catalytic residues and shows no nuclease or DNA-binding activity. It does not bind RNA by itself; instead it acts as a co-repressor of the RNA chaperone Hfq. Hfq recognises A-rich catabolite-activity (CA) motifs near the translation initiation sites of catabolic mRNAs through its distal face, and Crc joins Hfq to form stable, higher-order Hfq/Crc/RNA assemblies that block translation initiation and promote decay of the target mRNAs. This sets carbon catabolite repression, the preferential use of favoured carbon sources such as succinate or organic acids. Repression is relieved when the CbrA/CbrB-induced small RNAs CrcZ and CrcY sequester Hfq/Crc. The Crc/Hfq system is functionally conserved across the Pseudomonadaceae, including Azotobacter vinelandii.

Core Functions

Co-repressor of Hfq: joins Hfq on catabolite-activity motifs of catabolic mRNAs to form stable translationally repressive Hfq/Crc/RNA assemblies that mediate carbon catabolite repression; antagonised by the CrcZ/CrcY decoy sRNAs.

Directly Involved In:
negative regulation of translation carbon catabolite repression
Cellular Locations:
cytoplasm
