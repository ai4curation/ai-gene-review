# LexA2 primary-regulon evidence and source limits

Read 2026-11-07 UTC. The original PMID15458417 and PMID17933893 publication caches remain abstract-only and byte-identical. The full primary articles were read through their authoritative online text, separately from those caches.

PMID15458417, DOI10.1111/j.1365-2958.2004.04260.x:
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1365-2958.2004.04260.x
Results, identification of the binding sequence:
> these mutations inhibited the binding of the LexA2 protein to the lexA2 promoter.

Methods specifies P. putida KT2440. Purified LexA2 was tested by EMSA on promoter fragments, with specific and nonspecific competitors; promoter mutations abolished binding. Expression measurements establish repression and mitomycin-C induction. The early SulA/DinP/DnaE nomenclature for PP3117–PP3119 is historical and does not establish those old functional assignments. No target-specific LexA2 cleavage assay was identified in the sections inspected; the autoprotease assignment rests on curated LexA-family evidence.

PMID17933893, DOI10.1128/JB.01213-07:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2168638/
Results, expression analysis:
> only 2 transcriptional units are regulated by LexA2.

The study uses KT2440 lexA1/lexA2 insertion derivatives, microarrays, RT-PCR and promoter-binding experiments. LexA1 has the larger conventional regulon; LexA2 controls its own cassette and PP_3901. This is a difference in regulon scope, not evidence that LexA2 cannot participate in a DNA-damage response. Lack of a growth phenotype in those conditions does not negate its demonstrated regulatory function.
