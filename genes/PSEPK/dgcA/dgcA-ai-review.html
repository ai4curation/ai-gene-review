Gene Description

DgcA (PP_0310) is the soluble flavin-binding subunit of the membrane-associated DgcAB dimethylglycine dehydrogenase system. Together with the iron-sulfur membrane subunit DgcB, it converts dimethylglycine to sarcosine during stepwise glycine-betaine catabolism. Conserved locus organization, genetics, and the proposed electron-transfer architecture of the Chromohalobacter system support a ferredoxin step through DgcB followed by transfer to the adjacent ETF heterodimer, although the KT2440 proteins have not been reconstituted directly.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

dimethylglycine dehydrogenase (iron-sulfur protein acceptor) activity

Definition: Catalysis of the oxidation of N,N-dimethylglycine to sarcosine and a one-carbon product, with electron transfer to an iron-sulfur protein.

Justification: GO:0047865 specifies direct reduction of electron-transfer flavoprotein, whereas the bacterial DgcAB architecture places a membrane-associated DgcB iron-sulfur protein as the immediate acceptor before downstream ETF.

Parent term: oxidoreductase activity, acting on the CH-NH group of donors

Supporting Evidence:

  • PMID:32631860 : the csal_0990, csal_0991, csal_0992, and csal_0993 genes are responsible for DMG degradation to sarcosine.

Notes

(dgcA-notes.md)

dgcA curation notes

  • Q88R24 is the soluble flavoprotein component of the Pseudomonas DgcAB system. The OxRdtase_FMN_N fold name alone does not establish FMN binding.
  • The characterized DgcA-like Q1QYW1 enzyme carries noncovalently bound FAD [PMID:32631860, "a monomer of 79 kDa with a noncovalently bound flavin adenine dinucleotide"].
  • Pseudomonas genetics and isotope tracing support DgcAB-dependent conversion of dimethylglycine to sarcosine. [PMID:17951379, "conversion of DMG to sarcosine."]
  • UniProt's EC 1.5.8.- assignment implies a flavin acceptor, while the
    pathway model uses the more specific ferredoxin-linked EC 1.5.7.3 reaction
    and places DgcB before ETF. GO:0047865 is retained as the best available
    substrate-specific term, with a new ferredoxin-linked child term proposed to
    capture the acceptor distinction.
