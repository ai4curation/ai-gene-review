Functional Annotation of DnaE2 (gene dnaE2 / PP_3119; UniProt Q88I82)

Pseudomonas putida KT2440

1. Summary (Answer to the Research Question)

DnaE2 (Q88I82, locus PP_3119) is a second, non-essential catalytic subunit of DNA polymerase III belonging to the C-family of DNA polymerases (PolC/DnaE; EC 2.7.7.7, DNA-directed DNA polymerase). Its primary function is error-prone, template-directed DNA synthesis across DNA lesions (translesion synthesis, TLS) as the catalytic engine of the bacterial "mutasome." It catalyses the addition of deoxyribonucleotides to a primer 3′-OH using a damaged DNA template as instruction, producing DNA synthesis past bulky/blocking lesions at the cost of introducing point mutations. DnaE2 is induced by DNA damage as part of the RecA/LexA-controlled SOS response and acts together with two accessory proteins encoded in the same cassette — ImuA′ (a RecA paralog) and ImuB (a catalytically dead Y-family polymerase that tethers the complex to the β sliding clamp). The gene product functions in the cytoplasm, at the DNA replication fork, where it is recruited to stalled replisomes. Physiologically it mediates damage-induced (SOS) mutagenesis, contributing to survival of genotoxic stress and to the evolution of adaptive mutations, including antibiotic/drug resistance.

2. Background: what "a second DnaE" means

Most bacteria replicate their chromosome with a single, essential replicative polymerase DnaE (the α catalytic subunit of DNA Pol III). A subset of bacteria — including Pseudomonas, Caulobacter, Mycobacterium, and many other Proteobacteria and Actinobacteria — carry a second, paralogous dnaE gene, dnaE2. The existence of these "extra" replicative-type polymerases was long an "unresolved enigma," resolved by the demonstration that one copy participates in error-prone DNA repair synthesis rather than in bulk genome replication (Boshoff et al. 2003, Cell, 12705867). Importantly, DnaE2 substitutes functionally for the Y-family UmuDC/Pol V mutagenic system, which is absent in these organisms (Galhardo et al. 2005, 15886391). P. putida KT2440 lacks umuDC, so DnaE2 provides the RecA-dependent branch of induced mutagenesis.

3. Primary Molecular Function (the catalyzed reaction and substrate)

  • Reaction: DNA-templated (DNA-directed) DNA polymerization — nucleotidyl transfer: (DNA)ₙ + dNTP → (DNA)ₙ₊₁ + diphosphate (EC 2.7.7.7).
  • Substrate specificity: a primer–template DNA duplex with a free 3′-OH primer terminus, plus the four dNTPs and the catalytic divalent metal ions (Mg²⁺). Unlike the high-fidelity replicative DnaE, DnaE2 is error-prone and, as part of the mutasome, is able to extend primers opposite damaged/distorting template bases (e.g., lesions produced by UV or by cross-linking agents such as mitomycin C). This lesion-bypass capacity is the defining, functionally important activity (Galhardo et al. 2005, 15886391; Boshoff et al. 2003, 12705867).
  • Domain basis: the PolIIIα-type NTPase/polymerase domain (IPR011708) provides the catalytic center; the "polymerase-III finger" (IPR040982) and helix-hairpin-helix (HHH, IPR029460) domains support DNA binding — architecture shared with the replicative DnaE but tuned toward lower fidelity.

DnaE2 is not a general-purpose replicase: knockouts are viable and do not impair normal growth; the phenotype appears specifically after genotoxic stress (reduced survival and greatly reduced induced mutation frequency) (Boshoff 2003, 12705867; Galhardo 2005, 15886391).

4. The Functional Unit: the ImuA′–ImuB–DnaE2 "mutasome"

DnaE2 does not act alone. In P. putida (as in Caulobacter and Mycobacterium) the dnaE2 gene is the third gene of the imuA–imuB–dnaE2 cassette, and all three products are required for error-prone lesion processing (Galhardo et al. 2005, 15886391). Recent biochemical/genetic work defines the roles of the partners:

  • ImuB — a catalytically inactive Y-family DNA polymerase. It cannot itself synthesize DNA but carries a β-clamp-binding motif and "is predicted to enable mutasome function via its interaction with the β clamp" (Santos et al. 2025, 39706264). ImuB thus acts as a non-catalytic scaffold/adaptor that tethers DnaE2 to the DNA Pol III sliding clamp at the replication fork.
  • ImuA′ — a RecA homolog of accessory (non-recombinase) function. ImuB carries a RecA-N-terminus (RecA-NT) homology motif that mediates the ImuB–ImuA′ interaction; disrupting this motif (e.g., hydrophobic residues L378/V383) abolishes the ImuA′–ImuB interaction, and this interaction is essential for DNA damage-induced mutagenesis (Santos et al. 2023, 37034714; Santos et al. 2025, 39706264).

Decisive genetic-biochemical dissection (Warner et al. 2010, 20615954). In M. tuberculosis: (i) imuA′ and imuB are each individually essential for induced mutagenesis and damage tolerance; (ii) yeast two-hybrid shows ImuB interacts simultaneously with ImuA′, with DnaE2, and with the β-clamp — i.e., ImuB is the central adaptor; (iii) disrupting the ImuB–β-clamp interaction reduces induced mutagenesis and damage tolerance, phenocopying the deletion mutants — establishing replisome recruitment as essential; (iv) ImuB retains Y-family fold but lacks the conserved active-site residues needed for catalysis; and, most importantly, (v) replacing DnaE2's own catalytic residues reproduces the dnaE2 deletion phenotype, directly implying that the DnaE2 α-subunit is the catalytic polymerase of mutagenic lesion bypass. Gessner et al. 2023 (37530405) further dissect mutasome composition and recruitment.

Model: ImuB, docked on the β-clamp at a stalled fork, recruits ImuA′ (via RecA-NT) and DnaE2. DnaE2 supplies the catalytic polymerase activity that inserts nucleotides opposite the lesion and/or extends the primer past it. This modular design explains how a C-family (normally replicative) polymerase performs specialized translesion synthesis only when assembled into the mutasome.

5. Subcellular Localization

The gene product functions in the cytoplasm, specifically at the chromosomal DNA / the replication fork. It is a DNA-binding enzyme recruited to stalled replisomes through the ImuB–β-clamp interaction (Santos et al. 2025, 39706264). There is no signal peptide or transmembrane region expected for a C-family polymerase; its site of action is wherever replication is blocked by template damage. Single-molecule/imaging studies of bacterial TLS confirm that lesion-bypass polymerases act at or behind the replication fork, either co-replicationally or on post-replicative gaps (Joseph & Badrinarayanan 2020, 32556198).

6. Pathway / Regulatory Context: the SOS (DNA-damage) response

  • DnaE2/imuAB dnaE2 is a member of the RecA/LexA-controlled SOS regulon. The operon is damage-inducible in a RecA-dependent manner, and induction requires DNA-damaging signals (UV, mitomycin C, and other genotoxins) (Galhardo et al. 2005, 15886391). In M. tuberculosis, dnaE2 is upregulated by several DNA-damaging agents and during infection (Boshoff 2003, 12705867).
  • Mechanistically, DNA damage generates ssDNA, RecA forms a nucleoprotein filament, LexA autocleaves, and SOS genes (including imuA–imuB–dnaE2) are de-repressed — the canonical SOS pathway that operates in P. putida.
  • Functional output of the pathway: conversion of otherwise lethal, replication-blocking lesions into survivable (but mutagenic) events — i.e., DNA damage tolerance via error-prone translesion synthesis. This is a distinct pathway from the RecA-independent, stationary-phase mutagenesis driven by Pol IV/DinB in P. putida (Tegova et al. 2004, 15090515), which underscores that DnaE2 specifically provides the RecA-dependent, SOS-inducible mutagenic branch.

7. Physiological / Evolutionary Significance

  • Survival of genotoxic stress: loss of DnaE2 reduces survival after UV irradiation (Boshoff 2003, 12705867).
  • Induced mutagenesis / adaptive evolution: DnaE2 is responsible for most UV- and mitomycin-C-induced mutations in Caulobacter, including unusual substitution spectra (e.g., G:C→C:G transversions) (Galhardo 2005, 15886391).
  • Drug/antibiotic resistance: by generating point mutations under stress, DnaE2 can directly contribute to the emergence of drug resistance in vivo (Boshoff 2003, 12705867) — a clinically relevant consequence of the same TLS activity.

Genus-level (Pseudomonas) evidence. The closest well-studied relative, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, provides direct functional support: the damage-inducible SOS-response imuBC gene products contribute (together with dinB/Pol IV and RpoS) to the generation of ciprofloxacin-resistance mutations (Fahey et al. 2023, 37625357). This demonstrates that the imuAB dnaE2 module is an active, SOS-inducible mutagenesis system within the Pseudomonas genus and reinforces the annotation-by-orthology of P. putida PP_3119 as the catalytic translesion polymerase of that module.

For P. putida KT2440 specifically, these roles are inferred by strong orthology and conserved operon structure rather than by dedicated single-gene knockout studies; P. putida mutagenesis research has more often focused on Pol IV/DinB (RecA-independent stationary-phase mutagenesis, 15090515), leaving a defined niche for DnaE2 as the RecA/SOS-dependent mutagenic polymerase.

9. Limitations & Future Directions

  • Direct experimental evidence in P. putida KT2440 (PP_3119) is limited; the functional annotation rests primarily on (i) unambiguous C-family/DnaE2 domain and family signatures, (ii) HAMAP orthology rule MF_01902, and (iii) detailed genetics/biochemistry of orthologs in Caulobacter crescentus and Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
  • The precise P. putida mutational spectrum, the exact ImuA/ImuB residues, and in vivo fork localization for PP_3119 have not been directly measured.
  • Future work: clean dnaE2 / imuB deletions in P. putida KT2440 with UV/MMC survival and rifampicin-resistance mutagenesis assays; confirmation of a functional LexA/SOS box upstream of imuA; reconstitution of the P. putida mutasome to measure lesion-bypass fidelity and β-clamp dependence.
