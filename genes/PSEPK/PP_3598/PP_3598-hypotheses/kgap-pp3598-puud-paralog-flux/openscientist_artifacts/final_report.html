PP_3598 (Q88GW9), Pseudomonas putida KT2440: which PuuD-family paralog carries the gamma-glutamyl-gamma-aminobutyrate hydrolase flux

Hypothesis slug: kgap-pp3598-puud-paralog-flux Target: PSEPK, NCBITaxon:160488, gene PP_3598, UniProt Q88GW9 Source: genes/PSEPK/PP_3598/PP_3598-ai-review.yaml, selector free-text Iterations completed: 3. Findings recorded: 8. Papers reviewed: 27.

Summary

Verdict: over-annotated for PP_3598, with the predicted flux assigned to a different paralog. PP_3598 is an active member of the PuuD / class I glutamine amidotransferase family, but every contextual line of evidence places the physiological gamma-glutamyl-gamma-aminobutyrate (gamma-Glu-GABA) hydrolase flux of P. putida KT2440 on PP_2179 (spuA), with PP_5298 a second gamma-glutamylation module. The existing EC 3.5.1.94 / GO:0033969 annotation on PP_3598 is an electronic family-level transfer with no paralog-resolving power, and it should be generalized or qualified pending a direct assay.

The seed hypothesis proposed that catalytic triad integrity would discriminate the paralogs. It does not. A UniProt proteome scan of UP000000556 for Pfam PF07722 and InterPro IPR011697 / IPR044668 returns exactly four KT2440 proteins: PP_2179 (spuA, Q88KW0), PP_5298 (Q88C85), PP_3598 (Q88GW9) and PP_0727 (Q88PX1). Global Needleman-Wunsch alignment (BLOSUM62, gap open -11, extend -1) against E. coli K-12 PuuD (P76038), whose nucleophile Cys114 was established by site-directed mutagenesis, maps an intact Cys-His-Glu triad in all four, together with an intact C-R-G nucleophile elbow that forms the oxyanion hole. The test requested by the hypothesis therefore returns a uniform positive and carries no discriminating information. That is an informative negative result for the curator: no triad-based argument can be used either for or against PP_3598.

What does discriminate is genomic context, orthology to the one structurally and enzymatically characterised pseudomonad family member, and the provenance of the existing annotation. PP_2179 is 69.6% identical to P. aeruginosa SpuA (PA0297) and reproduces 14 of the 14 residues that contact the bound glutamate product in PDB 7D53; it sits in a three-gene same-strand unit with PP_2178 (PuuA-I glutamate-putrescine ligase, 84 bp gap) and PP_2180 (SpuC-I transaminase, 69 bp gap). PP_5298 is 4 bp from PP_5299 (PuuA-II) on the same strand, with a STRING gene-neighbourhood score of 0.781. PP_3598 is 42.8% identical to SpuA, reproduces 11 of 14 pocket residues, and is bounded by intergenic gaps of 425 bp and 347 bp, the two largest of the 20 gaps measured across the three regions (median 80 bp, IQR 26-188 bp), with no polyamine or GABA gene within four loci on either side. All six GO terms on Q88GW9 are IEA, assigned through PANTHER subfamily PTHR43235:SF1, which places E. coli PuuD, P. aeruginosa SpuA and all four KT2440 paralogs in one undifferentiated group.

PP_3598 is predicted to be neither the principal enzyme nor a straightforward condition-specific isozyme of the putrescine gamma-glutamylation route. It reads as a P. putida clade-restricted family member, transcriptionally and genomically decoupled from putrescine catabolism, with an unresolved physiological substrate. The one piece of primary evidence touching it (PMID: 39929973) is a combined spuA / PP_3598 / PP_5298 triple base-edit knockout on hexamethylenediamine, a non-natural C6 diamine, which impaired but did not abolish growth and cannot attribute flux to any single gene.

The seed hypothesis's triad test is uninformative here.

Within +/- 7 loci PP_3598 has no polyamine gene at all: the neighbours are PP_3595 (ArtM arginine ABC permease), PP_3596 (amaD), PP_3597 (amino acid ABC ATPase) and PP_3599-PP_3602 (sugar acid catabolism), with dpkA (PP_3591) and amaC (PP_3590) nearby. PP_0727 lies in an unrelated PEP-utilising enzyme / ATP-grasp cluster.

F006. Structure-anchored pocket mapping: the glutamyl subsite is conserved, the leaving-group subsite is undefined

PDB 7D53 (SpuA H221N with bound glutamate product, 1.6 Å, PMID: 34605433) was downloaded and parsed. Fourteen chain-A residues lie within 4.5 Å of the ligand: G66, S67, P68, S69, N70, C113, R114, Q117, I180, H181, G182, Q183, Q219 and His221 (Asn in the mutant). PP_3598 is the only one of the three KT2440 PuuD-like proteins to alter the 66-70 GSPSN loop that contacts the glutamate; PP_2179, PP_5298 and EcPuuD all retain GSPSN. All four KT2440 paralogs retain the canonical C-R-G nucleophile elbow and the Q/M-W-H-P-E motif, so the oxyanion hole formed by the backbone amides of Cys+1 and the following Gly is intact in every one. Both available SpuA complexes (7D50 thioester, 7D53 product) occupy only the gamma-glutamyl donor subsite. No structure of any family member defines the subsite that binds the GABA leaving group, so specificity cannot be read off structure. An RCSB search on Q88GW9, Q88KW0, Q88C85 and Q88PX1 returns zero entries; only AlphaFold models exist for the KT2440 proteins. PP_3598's two loop substitutions are a weak, non-decisive hint of altered specificity.

F004. The only direct evidence is a triple knockout on a non-natural substrate

de Witt et al. 2025 (PMID: 39929973) report that PP_3598 was transcriptionally upregulated in the evolved strain P. putida NYL growing on C6-polyamide monomers, alongside puuB, spuB, spuC-II and kauB. They then state: "Functional knockout of all glutamyl hydrolase candidates (spuA, PP_3598 or PP_5298) by nCas9-assisted multiplex cytidine base editing led to impaired growth on HDMA, while disruption of the transaminase candidates (spuC-I, spuC-II, PP_0596 or PP_2800), which should affect both pathways, completely abolished growth on HDMA." The methods confirm a single multiplex plasmid, pMBEC2-spuA-PP3598-PP5298, targeting all three hydrolase genes at once. No single-gene hydrolase knockout, no enzyme assay and no putrescine-specific test of PP_3598 is reported.

The phenotype ordering matters. Hydrolase loss impaired growth; transaminase loss abolished it. In P. putida the transamination route carries the main diamine flux and gamma-glutamylation is auxiliary, the reverse of the E. coli Puu hierarchy, where puuA deletion is more limiting than the non-glutamylating route (PMID: 18495664). Bandounas et al. (PMID: 21540064) found independently in P. putida S12 that sequential deletion of up to six oxidative-deamination and transamination genes did not abolish putrescine degradation, and that gamma-glutamylation operates as an alternative route. Within that auxiliary route, a triple knockout cannot attribute activity to one paralog.

Mechanistic model

The reaction at issue, gamma-glutamyl-gamma-aminobutyrate + H2O → GABA + L-glutamate (EC 3.5.1.94), is the fourth step of the E. coli Puu route. PuuA gamma-glutamylates putrescine, PuuB oxidises, PuuC dehydrogenates to gamma-Glu-GABA, PuuD hydrolyses the isopeptide bond, PuuE transaminates GABA. The chemistry is generic class I glutamine amidotransferase chemistry: a Cys nucleophile on a tight elbow attacks the gamma-carbonyl, His and Glu complete the triad, and two backbone amides form the oxyanion hole. That machinery is conserved in all four KT2440 paralogs and in human GGH, which acts on folylpolyglutamate. Triad integrity says the protein is an active amidohydrolase. It says nothing about which gamma-glutamyl amide it acts on.

Substrate choice is set by the leaving-group subsite, and that is exactly the part no experiment has resolved for this family.

The claim this report makes is about flux, not about chemistry. PuuA makes the committed intermediate; a hydrolase co-transcribed with its own PuuA has the substrate delivered to it under shared regulation. Two of the four paralogs have that arrangement and PP_3598 does not. KT2440 also runs the direct transaminase route to GABA, which de Witt 2025 shows is the route whose loss abolishes diamine growth, so the gamma-glutamylation arm is a parallel capability.

Conflicts and alternatives

The one primary paper that mentions PP_3598 points in a different direction from the genomic context. de Witt 2025 includes PP_3598 in a triple knockout that impairs growth on hexamethylenediamine and reports it upregulated in an evolved strain. Two readings are compatible. Either PP_3598 contributes measurable gamma-glutamyl-diamine hydrolase activity in vivo and the context evidence understates it, or the phenotype comes from spuA and PP_5298 while PP_3598 is a passenger included for redundancy. The published experiment cannot separate these, because single knockouts were not reported. The upregulation in the evolved strain is also compatible with a general nitrogen or stress response.

A second alternative is that PP_3598 hydrolyses a different gamma-glutamyl conjugate. Its neighbours are an amino acid ABC transporter and amaD (a FAD-dependent D-lysine oxidase) on one side, and glucarate-galactarate catabolism on the other. Revelles et al. (PMID: 16237033) established that amaA, dkpA and amaB in this region act in the D-lysine / aminoadipate branch of lysine catabolism, a pathway that supplies no gamma-glutamylated intermediate currently known. The restriction of PP_3598's UniRef90 cluster to the P. putida group, while SpuA-type orthologues span 46 to 71 genera, fits a lineage-specific function. Nothing in this report tests that alternative and it remains open.

A third possibility is condition-specific isozyme status under a regulon not sampled here. The 425 bp upstream gap is large enough for an independent promoter with its own regulator site, and nothing rules out induction of PP_3598 under a condition where PP_2179 and PP_5298 are off. The P. putida U data (PMID: 37895246) show that paralog pairs in this pathway do split by inducer: spuC1 responds to putrescine, spuC2 to cadaverine. The evidence needed to test this for the hydrolases is exactly the evidence that could not be retrieved.

A fourth conflict is the organism-level one. The E. coli Puu hierarchy, where gamma-glutamylation is the dominant putrescine route (PMID: 18495664), does not transfer to P. putida, where transamination dominates (PMID: 39929973) and gamma-glutamylation is an alternative (PMID: 21540064). A curator should not read the strength of the E. coli PuuD evidence as support for an equivalently central role for any KT2440 paralog.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Every computational result here is sequence, structure or genome-context inference. None measures enzyme activity and none measures transcription. The verdict of over-annotation is a statement about the strength of the current evidence for PP_3598, not a demonstration that PP_3598 lacks gamma-Glu-GABA hydrolase activity.

No enzyme in the KT2440 family has been assayed. Checked: UniProt, QuickGO, PubMed. All four assignments rest on homology to SpuA and EcPuuD. This matters because GO:0033969 on PP_3598 currently has the same evidence status as GO:0033969 on PP_2179, although the contextual evidence differs sharply. A purified-protein kinetics panel would resolve it.

No structure defines the acyl-acceptor subsite. Checked: RCSB search on all four accessions returns zero entries; the two SpuA complexes occupy only the glutamyl donor site. This matters because the 11/14 pocket score for PP_3598 cannot be converted into a specificity prediction. A complex with a non-hydrolysable gamma-Glu-GABA analogue would resolve it.

The expression arm of the hypothesis is unanswered. Checked: the LBL Fitness Browser (HTTP 403 behind a Cloudflare JavaScript challenge, both myFitShow.cgi and org.cgi), PaxDb (HTTP 404, no JSON API), and the Schmidt 2022 full text in Europe PMC (fullTextXML HTTP 500, BioC abstract-only). This matters because induction of PP_3598 by putrescine would overturn the isolated-transcription-unit argument. A curator should treat a claim of putrescine-specific induction as untested rather than negative. The Schmidt 2022 BarSeq tables across 52 nitrogen sources (PMID: 35285712) would resolve it directly if obtained by hand.

Operon membership is inferred, not measured. Checked: NCBI Gene coordinates and strands only. No RNA-seq transcript boundary, 5' RACE or Rend-seq data for these regions was retrieved. The gap-plus-strand heuristic is strong in bacteria but is a heuristic.

The regulator of PP_3598 is unidentified. Checked: +/- 7 loci in UniProt and STRING. No PuuR-type repressor was found in its neighbourhood. Without a regulator the condition under which PP_3598 is expressed cannot be predicted.

No single-gene knockout of any KT2440 hydrolase paralog exists. Checked: de Witt 2025 methods confirm the triple-target plasmid pMBEC2-spuA-PP3598-PP5298. Redundancy (PMID: 21540064) means a single deletion may give no phenotype even for the principal enzyme, so double and triple combinations are needed alongside singles.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Purified-enzyme kinetics panel. Express and purify PP_2179, PP_5298, PP_3598 and PP_0727 and measure kcat/Km against gamma-Glu-GABA, gamma-glutamylputrescine, gamma-Glu-cadaverine, gamma-Glu-hexamethylenediamine, L-glutamine and folylpolyglutamate. A tenfold or greater kcat/Km gap on gamma-Glu-GABA between PP_2179 and PP_3598 settles the principal-enzyme question directly and converts the whole family from IEA to experimental evidence.

Clean single, double and triple deletions. Build them in KT2440 and measure growth rate on putrescine as sole carbon source and as sole nitrogen source. If deleting PP_3598 alone has no effect while deleting PP_2179 does, the annotation transfer is confirmed as over-annotation. If no single deletion has an effect but the double does, the paralogs are functionally redundant and GO:0033969 is defensible on both. Assay gamma-Glu-GABA accumulation in each mutant, following the design that established PuuD in E. coli (PMID: 16499623).

Condition-resolved expression. Run RT-qPCR or RNA-seq on KT2440 grown on putrescine, cadaverine, GABA, agmatine, succinate and under nitrogen limitation, comparing PP_3598, PP_2179 and PP_5298.
