Gene Description

PP_4572 (Q88E94) is a 357-residue member of the AsmA-like protein family (InterPro IPR052894; PANTHER PTHR30441, subfamily SF4) with a single N-terminal transmembrane helix (DeepTMHMM residues 7-30, N-terminus in the cytoplasm) and a C-terminal region of about 325 residues predicted to lie in the periplasm. Its AlphaFold model of that region is strand-rich and extends about 86 A, consistent with the short tube-like AsmA fold. The UniProt name "DUF748 domain-containing protein" is a ProtNLM prediction: no Pfam DUF748 (PF05359) match is recorded for Q88E94 itself, although its orthologues carry it. PP_4572 is the full-length orthologue of P. aeruginosa PA2708 (Q9I0D4; 71.7% identity over 357 aligned residues), a Pseudomonas-specific AsmA-like protein absent from E. coli. Sposato et al. 2024 found PA2708 dispensable, even in a strain depleted of the redundantly essential AsmA-like phospholipid-transport proteins TamB, YhdP and YdbH, and argued that it is too short to bridge the periplasm unless it has an outer-membrane partner; its role is unknown. PP_4572 aligns only weakly and partially to the E. coli AsmA-like proteins (at best 24-26% identity over 126-195 residues), so it is not an orthologue of AsmA, TamB, YhdP, YdbH, YicH or YhjG. KT2440 RB-TnSeq (300 experiments, BERDL copy of the Fitness Browser) shows no phenotype for PP_4572 mutants (fitness range -0.39 to +0.39) and no informative cofitness (top cofitness 0.27). It is not part of the neighbouring PxpABC 5-oxoprolinase cluster: PP_4575-PP_4579 mutants are defective on L-pyroglutamate, PP_4572 mutants are not. Best description: an inner-membrane-anchored, periplasm-facing AsmA-like protein of unknown function; any role in intermembrane lipid transport or outer-membrane biogenesis is untested.

Core Functions

Inner-membrane-anchored, periplasm-facing AsmA-like protein of the Pseudomonas-specific PA2708 subfamily. Its molecular function and process are unknown. By family membership it may bind lipids in an AsmA-type groove or work with an outer-membrane partner, but it is too short to bridge the periplasm on its own, its orthologue is dispensable, and no phenotype is known in P. putida.

UniProt has not reviewed the entry, and its name, "DUF748 domain-containing protein", is a ProtNLM machine prediction. Its only domain match is to the AsmA-like family (InterPro IPR052894 / PANTHER PTHR30441, subfamily SF4 "PROTEIN ASMA", covering residues 8–244). No paper studies PP_4572 itself.

2. The AsmA-like protein family

  • History of the family name. E. coli AsmA ("assembly of outer membrane proteins") was found because asmA mutations suppress a defective OmpF assembly mutant (OmpF315). AsmA fractionated with the inner membrane, so its effect on OM protein assembly was judged indirect. asmA nulls lowered OM LPS levels, which the authors linked to changes in OM fluidity (Deng & Misra, Mol Microbiol 1996;21:605–612). The GO term that GOA gives PP_4572 by IEA, "regulation of protein targeting to membrane" (GO:0090313), probably reflects this historical OMP-assembly phenotype passed down through the PANTHER family. No PP_4572-specific evidence supports it.
  • Current model: anterograde GPL transport. In E. coli, the large AsmA-like proteins YhdP, TamB and YdbH are redundant, but losing all three is lethal. Cells lacking them show defects in OM lipid homeostasis and GPL transport (Ruiz lab, mBio 2021, PMC8593681; Douglass, McLean & Trent, PLoS Genet 2022, PMC8912898). AlphaFold models show a long β-taco/tube with a hydrophobic groove, resembling the Lpt periplasmic bridge and the eukaryotic chorein/VPS13 bridge-like lipid transporters. This supports a model where lipids slide along the groove between the membranes.
  • The family in P. aeruginosa. There are seven AsmA-like proteins: TamB, YhdP (PA4476), YhjG (PA4879), AsmA (PA5146), YdbH (PA5307), PA4735/EpaF, and PA2708. YhdP, TamB, YdbH and PA4735 are essential but redundant (Sposato et al., mSphere 2024, PMID 38305166, PMC10900882). The 2025 follow-up shows that two C-terminal amphipathic helices of YhdP are needed for OM anchoring and probably for GPL insertion. It also proposes that only YhdP is long enough to span the periplasm (PMID 40970438, PMC12447247).

3. What is known about PA2708, the ortholog of PP_4572

The only experimental data on this subfamily come from Sposato et al. (2024), whose full text I retrieved through Europe PMC:

  • Size and family: PA2708 (361 aa, PF05359) has no counterpart in E. coli. Its AlphaFold model has the family's N-terminal TMH and β-rich tube, but "PA2708 is much smaller than the other AsmA-like proteins of both P. aeruginosa and E. coli… and the length of its periplasmic domain is likely not sufficient to span the entire periplasmic space."
  • Not essential and not part of the redundant essential set: The authors depleted TamB in a ΔyhdP ΔydbH background (a strain that grows poorly without its inducer) and then deleted PA2708. Growth did not get worse. The authors conclude "PA2708… is not required for P. aeruginosa growth". In contrast, deleting PA4735 in the same background abolished growth.
  • Conserved across the genus, function open: The size of PA2708 "is not expected to allow to cross the periplasmic space unless PA2708 can interact with OM partners protruding into the periplasm… this protein is conserved in different Pseudomonas species… Further studies are required to uncover its role."

PP_4572 is 71.7% identical to PA2708 and nearly the same length, so these conclusions very likely apply to it too.

5. Open questions and suggested experiments

  1. Does PP_4572 need an OM-associated partner (for example a lipoprotein, the way YdbH pairs with YnbE) to reach the OM? Pull-down or crosslinking of a tagged PP_4572 would test this.
  2. Does deleting PP_4572 cause OM-integrity phenotypes (SDS/EDTA, vancomycin, NPN uptake) when combined with depletion of YhdP, TamB, YdbH or the PA4735 ortholog? Check the KT2440 RB-TnSeq fitness data first.
  3. Does PP_4572 bind lipids? Purify the periplasmic domain and test GPL co-purification.
