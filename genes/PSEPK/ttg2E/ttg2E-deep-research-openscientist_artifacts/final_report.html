Functional Annotation Report: ttg2E (PP_0962, UniProt Q88P90)

Pseudomonas putida KT2440

1. Summary / Answer

ttg2E (PP_0962; Q88P90) encodes the MlaB ortholog — a small (100-residue) cytoplasmic STAS-domain protein that is the auxiliary regulatory/assembly subunit of the inner-membrane ABC transporter MlaFEDB (the "Ttg2" system) in Pseudomonas putida KT2440. It is not itself an enzyme or a transporter; instead it binds the MlaF ATPase on the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane, where it is required for both the correct assembly and the ATP-hydrolytic activity of the transporter. The MlaFEDB complex powers retrograde transport of mislocalized glycerophospholipids from the outer membrane (OM) back to the inner membrane (IM), thereby maintaining OM lipid asymmetry. This membrane-barrier function is the molecular basis for the historically observed "toluene-tolerance" phenotype and for low-level intrinsic resistance to lipid-permeant antibiotics.

3. Primary Function

3.1 Molecular role — a STAS-domain adapter/regulator, not an enzyme

MlaB is a cytoplasmic STAS (Sulphate Transporter and Anti-Sigma-factor antagonist) domain protein. Direct analysis of the P. putida sequence (Q88P90) shows a 100-residue protein consisting of a single STAS domain (residues 13–100) with no signal peptide and no transmembrane segment (longest hydrophobic stretch = 6 residues, well below the ~15–20 needed for a membrane helix) — i.e. a small, soluble, cytoplasmic protein. In the assembled transporter it clamps onto the nucleotide-binding (ATPase) domain MlaF (typically two MlaB copies per MlaFEDB complex). Functional dissection of the homologous E. coli complex showed that the transporter is built from canonical components MlaF and MlaE plus auxiliary proteins MlaD and MlaB, and that "MlaB plays critical roles in both the assembly and activity of the transporter" (Thong et al. 2016, 27529189). A subsequent explosion of cryo-EM structures of MlaFEDB (apo, phospholipid-, ADP- and AMP-PNP-bound; 3.3–4.1 Å) resolves MlaB as an integral subunit of the complex, and mutagenesis mapped residues that "regulate the activity and structural stability of the MlaFEDB complex" (Tang et al. 2021, 33199922; reviewed in Ekiert et al. 2022, 35981415). Thus the precise role of ttg2E is:

  • Structural/assembly: stabilizes the MlaFEDB complex; without MlaB the complex fails to assemble properly.
  • Regulatory: modulates/couples ATP hydrolysis by MlaF that energizes lipid transport.

It has no substrate specificity of its own (it is not a catalytic or transport subunit); its "substrate" is effectively its protein partner MlaF.

3.2 Pathway — Maintenance of Lipid Asymmetry (Mla)

The MlaFEDB transporter is the inner-membrane engine of the cell-envelope-spanning Mla pathway, which comprises three assemblies: the OM lipoprotein complex MlaA–OmpC/F, the periplasmic phospholipid shuttle MlaC (= Ttg2D here), and the IM ABC transporter MlaFEDB (containing MlaB) (Abellon-Ruiz 2023, 36459067). The pathway removes glycerophospholipids that become mislocalized in the OM outer leaflet and returns them to the inner membrane — i.e. retrograde (OM→IM) phospholipid transport — using energy from ATP hydrolysis to maintain OM lipid asymmetry (Thong et al. 2016, 27529189). MlaB's function is therefore executed within this pathway by enabling the ATPase that drives the shuttle.

3.3 Physiological output — membrane barrier integrity

By preserving OM lipid asymmetry (LPS outer leaflet / phospholipid inner leaflet), the Ttg2/Mla system keeps the OM an effective permeability barrier. This explains the associated phenotypes: - Organic-solvent (toluene) tolerance in P. putida: ttg2 disruption yields toluene-hypersensitive mutants, and membrane integrity/repair — not solvent efflux per se — was identified as the key protective mechanism (Kim et al. 1998, 9658016). - Intrinsic antibiotic resistance: in P. aeruginosa the Ttg2 system contributes to low-level resistance against antibiotics that permeate via a lipid-mediated pathway (Yero et al. 2021, 33837253).

4. Localization

  • Sub-cellular: MlaB/ttg2E is a cytoplasmic protein peripherally associated with the cytoplasmic face of the inner membrane, as part of the MlaFEDB complex (the ATPase MlaF and its MlaB partner face the cytoplasm; MlaE spans the IM; MlaD projects into the periplasm) (36459067; 27529189; visualized in cryo-EM structures 33199922). This is directly supported by the sequence: a soluble single STAS domain (res 13–100) with no signal peptide and no transmembrane helix in the 100-aa protein.

6. Supported and Refuted Hypotheses

  • Supported: ttg2E encodes MlaB, a cytoplasmic STAS-domain auxiliary subunit of the inner-membrane MlaFEDB (Ttg2) ABC transporter that maintains OM lipid asymmetry.
  • Refuted / clarified: The literal annotation "toluene-tolerance protein" does not mean ttg2E is a solvent-efflux transporter or a solvent-sensing/detoxifying enzyme. The solvent-tolerance phenotype is an indirect consequence of maintaining OM barrier integrity. An authoritative review of Gram-negative solvent tolerance (Ramos et al. 2002, 12142492) frames the phenomenon in terms of (a) membrane phospholipid/fatty-acid adaptation, (b) vesiculation, and (c) RND-family active efflux pumps that export solvents. ttg2E/MlaB contributes to the phospholipid-homeostasis arm (category a), whereas the well-known P. putida solvent-efflux pumps are the mechanistically separate RND systems TtgABC / TtgDEF / TtgGHI — a different protein superfamily that must not be conflated with the ttg2/Mla ABC transporter, despite the shared "ttg" (toluene-tolerance genes) label.

7. Limitations and Future Directions

  • Functional data are from orthologs (E. coli, P. aeruginosa); the KT2440 MlaB has not been individually characterized experimentally, though orthology is unambiguous.
  • The direction of net lipid transport (retrograde vs. anterograde) remains debated in the field, though current biochemistry favors retrograde; this affects mechanistic detail but not MlaB's role as an MlaF-regulatory subunit.
  • Useful follow-up: co-purify/crystallize the P. putida MlaFEDB(ttg2E) complex; measure ATPase activity ± MlaB; test a clean PP_0962 deletion for OM permeability, vesiculation, solvent and antibiotic sensitivity.
