Functional Annotation of uvrD (DNA helicase II, Q88C31 / PP_5352) in Pseudomonas putida KT2440

1. Summary / Answer to the Research Question

Q88C31 is UvrD (DNA helicase II), encoded by uvrD/PP_5352 in Pseudomonas putida KT2440. It is an ATP-dependent, superfamily-1A (SF1A) DNA helicase / single-stranded-DNA (ssDNA) translocase that moves along DNA with 3'→5' polarity and hydrolyzes ATP in a DNA-stimulated manner (EC 5.6.2.4). Its physiological substrate is duplex chromosomal DNA bearing a 3' single-stranded loading region, and it functions in the cytoplasm at the nucleoid. Mechanistically, UvrD unwinds/strips DNA at three converging genome-maintenance pathways: (i) nucleotide excision repair (NER) — displacing the lesion-containing strand after UvrABC incision; (ii) methyl-directed mismatch repair (MMR) — unwinding the error-containing strand after activation by MutL; and (iii) recombination control — acting as an anti-recombinase that removes RecA filaments from ssDNA and thereby tunes homologous recombination and the SOS response.

Identity is unambiguous, so function is confidently transferred from the extensively characterized E. coli/model-organism enzyme.

3. Primary Molecular Function — the Catalyzed Reaction and Substrate

UvrD is a DNA-stimulated ATPase that couples ATP hydrolysis to directional 3'→5' translocation on ssDNA, driving separation of duplex DNA (EC 5.6.2.4; GO:0043138 3'-5' DNA helicase, GO:0005524 ATP binding, GO:0016887 ATP hydrolysis).

  • Reaction (UniProt/RHEA): ATP + H₂O → ADP + phosphate + H⁺ (RHEA:13065), and formally "couples ATP hydrolysis with the unwinding of duplex DNA by translocating in the 3'-5' direction." Under the 2021 EC revision, helicases were reclassified as isomerases/translocases (EC 5.6.2.4): UvrD does not chemically modify DNA — it is a molecular motor that alters DNA conformation (duplex → two single strands).
  • Substrate specificity: duplex DNA presenting a 3' single-stranded tail for loading; the enzyme translocates on the tracking strand toward the duplex. Cofactor: ATP + Mg²⁺. Biochemically, the Deinococcus radiodurans ortholog is "a DNA-stimulated ATPase, can translocate along ssDNA in the 3'-5' direction and shows ATP-dependent 3'-5'… helicase activity" (24143224).
  • Activation / regulation: the monomer is a processive 3'→5' translocase but a weak helicase; efficient unwinding requires activation by dimerization (via the 2B regulatory domain) or by an accessory partner protein (42444602; 38677494). This switch makes UvrD a regulatable, pathway-specific motor rather than a constitutively active unwindase.

3.1 Structural basis of the mechanism

UvrD-family helicases have a conserved four-subdomain fold: subdomains 1A and 2A form the tandem RecA-like ATPase motor, while 1B and the mobile 2B domain are accessory/regulatory (conserved in Q88C31: ATP-binding domain res 11–289 with Walker-A AGAGSGKT; UvrD_C domain res 290–567). Two RecA-like lobes hydrolyze ATP to ratchet along ssDNA; a conserved β-hairpin "separation pin" splits the duplex (its mutation abolishes helicase activity), and structures of the D. radiodurans ortholog "trap a DNA helicase undergoing a large-scale spiral movement around duplexed DNA" (24143224). The 2B domain is an allosteric switch: it autoinhibits the monomer and is the general dimerization interface for the family, so activity is turned on by dimerization or by partner proteins (42444602; 29087333). This explains why UvrD is a regulatable, context-specific motor.

4. Biological Processes / Pathways

4.1 Nucleotide excision repair (NER)

After the UvrABC excinuclease dual-incises around a bulky/UV lesion, UvrD unwinds the duplex 3'→5' to displace the lesion-containing oligonucleotide, permitting resynthesis (Pol I) and ligation. "UvrD… plays an essential role in bacterial NER by unwinding the duplex DNA in the 3' to 5' direction to displace the lesion-containing strand" (28120472). Consistent with this, uvrD loss confers UV sensitivity (6355763).

4.2 Methyl-directed mismatch repair (MMR)

UvrD is the helicase that excises the error-containing strand in MMR. MutL activates UvrD to "unwind DNA from the strand incision site up to and beyond the mismatch" (38677494). Because the monomer is a poor helicase, MutL (or dimerization) is the required activator (38677494). Loss of uvrD therefore produces a strong mutator phenotype, and uvrD is grouped genetically with mutH/mutL/mutS in the methyl-directed MMR pathway; the uvrD knockout is "mutator and UV-sensitive" (6355763). This role directly maintains replication fidelity and genome stability.

4.3 Transcription-coupled repair (TCR) — proposed/debated

Beyond global-genome NER, an emerging model proposes that UvrD backtracks RNA polymerase stalled at a lesion, exposing the damage for NER, as an alternative to Mfd-mediated RNAP displacement: damage detection can occur "via transcription-coupled repair using UvrD-mediated backtracking or Mfd-controlled displacement of the RNA polymerase" (35235801). In live E. coli, UvrD recruitment to DNA after UV damage is strongly UvrA-dependent, tying it to the NER machinery (38513450). The relative contributions of the UvrD- vs Mfd-dependent routes remain under active debate.

4.4 Recombination control / SOS (anti-recombinase)

UvrD removes RecA filaments from ssDNA — "The UvrD helicase removes RecA filaments from RecA" (17364684) — thereby limiting inappropriate homologous recombination and modulating the SOS response (e.g., uvrD303 alleles decrease SOS expression; 24084169). uvrD is itself DNA-damage–inducible under lexA-recA (SOS) control (6355763), integrating it into the DNA-damage response network. GO also annotates recombinational repair (GO:0000725).

5. Localization

UvrD is a soluble cytoplasmic enzyme acting on chromosomal DNA at the nucleoid (GO:0005829 cytosol; GO:0033202 DNA helicase complex; GO:0003677 DNA binding). The 728-aa sequence contains only the two intracellular helicase domains and a P-loop, with no signal peptide or transmembrane segment — it is neither secreted nor membrane-associated. It engages DNA at replication forks and repair/recombination intermediates. (Localization is inferred from ortholog annotation and sequence features; no direct P. putida imaging is available.)

7. Limitations and Future Directions

  • Nearly all mechanistic and biochemical evidence derives from orthologs (E. coli, D. radiodurans, Mtb UvrD1, P. falciparum), not from P. putida PP_5352 directly; functional transfer relies on strong sequence/domain conservation.
  • No P. putida-specific knockout, biochemistry, or localization study was located in this search; direct characterization (ATPase/helicase assays, ΔuvrD mutator/UV-sensitivity phenotyping, MutL/UvrABC interaction) would confirm the annotation.
  • P. putida also encodes UvrD-family paralogs (e.g., Rep/PcrA-like helicases); partner specificity and any pathway division of labor in this organism remain to be established experimentally.
