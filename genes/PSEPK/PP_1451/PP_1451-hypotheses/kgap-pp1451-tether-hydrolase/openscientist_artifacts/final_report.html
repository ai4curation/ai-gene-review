PP_1451 (Q88MW7, Pseudomonas putida KT2440) as a peptidoglycan-lipoprotein tether hydrolase

Hypothesis slug: kgap-pp1451-tether-hydrolase. Source: genes/PSEPK/PP_1451/PP_1451-ai-review.yaml, free-text selector. Organism: Pseudomonas putida KT2440 (NCBITaxon:160488). Focus type: free_text, evaluated as a core-function and function-assignment hypothesis.

Summary

Verdict: partially supported. PP_1451 has a competent cysteine hydrolase active site, not a degenerate YkuD fold. Its closest functionally studied relative is a Pseudomonas aeruginosa protein assigned to exactly the proposed reaction, and the substrate lipoprotein exists in KT2440 with its attachment residue intact. What the evidence cannot do is separate hydrolysis of the peptidoglycan-lipoprotein tether from the related L,D-carboxypeptidase and L,D-endopeptidase activities that the same enzyme family performs. On sequence and structure alone those cannot be told apart, and that limit should be recorded in the review rather than resolved by assertion.

Three results carry the case. First, mapping the characterised Escherichia coli DpaA structure (PDB 8IKR, PMID: 37830798) onto the PP_1451 AlphaFold model by sequence-independent structural superposition (126 aligned residue pairs, 1.56 Å RMSD over the 118-residue core) places the catalytic nucleophile Cys143 on Cys159 at 0.97 Å, the inferred proton donor His135 on His137 at 1.04 Å, and the DpaA-diagnostic Ala145 on Ala161 at 0.95 Å. The cysteine-histidine spacing, measured from coordinates, is 4.05 Å (His NE2 to Cys SG) in PP_1451 against 4.32 Å in DpaA. Second, three independent censuses of the KT2440 reference proteome agree that the genome has exactly two YkuD-family proteins, PP_1451 and PP_2320, with no YcbB/LdtD-type 3-to-3 cross-linking enzyme above background and no ortholog of the only cross-linking L,D-transpeptidase of P. aeruginosa. Third, a scan of all 5,527 KT2440 proteins against P. aeruginosa OprI returns a single hit far above background, PP_2322, at 94.0% identity with the lipobox Cys20 and the peptidoglycan attachment residue Lys83 both conserved.

The caveats are equally concrete. Of the six non-catalytic positions that alanine scanning tested in DpaA, the two whose mutation cost the most activity (Tyr109 at 0.7% of wild type and Tyr131 at 4%) are the two PP_1451 does not conserve, replacing them with Phe106 and Met133. The closest characterised ortholog, PA3756/LdtPae3 at 57.1% identity, was never purified in soluble form, its deletion changed no muropeptide, and its hydrolase assignment was made by exclusion (PMID: 37255442). The only measured hydrolase biochemistry in this group sits on E. coli DpaA, at 29.0% identity to PP_1451, and that enzyme also acts as a glycine-specific carboxypeptidase (PMID: 33941679). The tether hydrolase call therefore runs through a chain of two inferences, each reasonable, neither measured in any Pseudomonas protein.

Executive judgment

Partially supported, with one clean positive, one clean negative, and one honest limit.

Tether hydrolysis is the best-supported activity, and the site is competent. The catalytic cysteine of the characterised enzyme is present and correctly positioned at 0.97 Å; the residue that separates hydrolases from transpeptidases in this family is the hydrolase form (Ala at Cys+2); the closest characterised relative is assigned to this reaction; and the substrate exists in the organism. That is enough to call the reaction class (cysteine peptidase acting on a peptidoglycan amide bond) and the compartment (periplasm).

KT2440 lacks a YcbB/LdtD-type 3-to-3 cross-linking enzyme. Three methods agree the genome has exactly two YkuD proteins. The best YcbB-like hit anywhere in the proteome scores 64 against a background of 60, 56, 55, 55, 54 for unrelated proteins. PF20142, the YcbB-specific accessory domain, is absent. There is no ortholog of LdtPae1/PA0732, which gene deletion showed is the only peptidoglycan cross-linking L,D-transpeptidase in P. aeruginosa.

Stated plainly: the site cannot be distinguished from a related hydrolase activity on sequence alone. A competent Cys-His pair with Ala at Cys+2 identifies a YkuD-fold hydrolase. It does not say which amide bond is cut. MEROPS places DpaA-like proteins in subfamily C82.A01 together with the L,D-carboxypeptidases Csd6 of Helicobacter pylori and Pgp2 of Campylobacter jejuni, and E. coli LdtF itself also acts as a glycine-specific carboxypeptidase and as an L,D-endopeptidase on transpeptidase products. Tether hydrolysis is the most likely activity, and it is not the only one the sequence permits.

Two further qualifications matter for curation. The weaker form of the inventory claim, that KT2440 therefore makes no 3-to-3 cross-links at all, is not established, because the single L,D-transpeptidase it does carry (PP_2320) was not assignable against E. coli paralogues alone and would have to cover whichever reactions occur. And the substrate-specificity residues are only partly retained, so the specificity of PP_1451 should be treated as unmeasured rather than assumed identical to DpaA.

Key findings

Finding 1: the catalytic site is competent, and the discriminating residue is the hydrolase form

The characterised reference is E. coli DpaA/LdtF (UniProt P0AA99), crystal structure PDB 8IKR chain A, X-ray at 2.9 Å, residues 36 to 229 fully modelled with wild-type Cys at position 143. Wang et al. 2023 (PMID: 37830798) name exactly one catalytic residue. They write that "the catalytic Cys143 residue is located in a sharp elbow turn of the coils (141-143)" and that "the scissile amide bond's carbonyl oxygen is positioned in the oxyanion hole, formed by the catalytic loop residues 141-143, and the scissile carbonyl group is located in close proximity to the side-chain nucleophile of Cys143". No catalytic histidine, aspartate or triad appears anywhere in the paper: a regex search of the full text for triad|His1[0-9][0-9]|histidine returned zero sentences. The His135 "proton donor/acceptor" entry that UniProt carries for DpaA is a PROSITE ProRule (PRU01373) inference, not an experimental assignment, and should be labelled as such in any downstream review.

Residue correspondences were established by sequence-independent structural superposition of the AlphaFold model AF-Q88MW7-F1-model_v6 (mean pLDDT 86.7, catalytic domain 47-183 mean pLDDT 96.2) onto 8IKR chain A, seeded on the unambiguous HG[D/T] and CxAM motif anchors and refined to convergence by iterated dynamic programming with Kabsch superposition. The result is 126 aligned residue pairs, 2.33 Å RMSD over all pairs and 1.56 Å over the 118 pairs within 4 Å. All mapped PP_1451 residues have per-residue pLDDT between 90.5 and 98.6, so the model is reliable exactly where it is being read.

The cysteine-histidine geometry was measured directly from coordinates rather than assumed. His NE2 to Cys SG is 4.32 Å in DpaA 8IKR (H135/C143), 4.05 Å in the PP_1451 model (H137/C159), and 4.21 Å in the E. coli transpeptidase LdtD/YcbB (PDB 6NTW, H509/C528). PP_1451 reproduces the arrangement found in both a characterised hydrolase and a characterised transpeptidase, so the dyad geometry establishes competence and does not discriminate between the two chemistries.

What does discriminate is the residue two positions after the catalytic cysteine. The DpaA paper notes that "an Arg residue, found in many LD-TPases, is exposed to the elongated groove of the proteins; however, the residue at the equivalent position, Ala145 of DpaA, is buried in the structure". Winkle et al. 2021 state the rule in sequence terms: "most DpaA-like proteins have alanine or asparagine 2 positions after the catalytic cysteine instead of an arginine that is present in LDTs with TPase activity" (PMID: 33947763). Hugonneau-Beaufet et al. 2023 formalise it as SXGChA against SXGChR (PMID: 37255442). Read directly from sequence, with the catalytic Cys at index 0:

Finding 2: KT2440 has exactly two YkuD proteins and no YcbB-type cross-linker

Three independent censuses of the P. putida KT2440 reference proteome (UP000000556, 5,527 proteins, 1,810,253 residues) agree on two YkuD-domain proteins and no more.

  1. UniProt Pfam cross-reference query, (proteome:UP000000556) AND (xref:pfam-PF03734): n = 2, PP_1451 (Q88MW7, 184 aa, signal 1-18) and PP_2320 (Q88KH0, 325 aa, signal 1-25).
  2. InterPro API, IPR005490 (L,D-transpeptidase catalytic domain) restricted to taxon 160488: count = 2, the same two accessions.
  3. A profile-free ungapped maximal-segment-pair scan (BLOSUM62) of all 5,527 KT2440 proteins against each full-length E. coli Ldt. The best KT2440 hit for LdtD/YcbB scored 64, which is background: the next ranks are 60, 56, 55, 55, 54 and the top hits are unrelated proteins PP_2157, fimV and pnp. PP_2320 scored 51 and PP_1451 scored 34 against YcbB. The same scan gave PP_2320 = 282 against LdtB/YbiS and 338 against LdtE/YnhG, against a background near 67, and PP_1451 = 64 as the top hit for DpaA.

Domain-architecture checks agree. PF20142 / IPR045380, the YcbB-specific accessory domain, is absent from KT2440 (n = 0). PF17969, present in all four E. coli ErfK-family proteins, is also absent (n = 0). PP_2320 is LysM (IPR018392, residues 40-86) plus the YkuD catalytic domain (98-233), PANTHER PTHR30582 "Bacterial L,D-transpeptidase". PP_1451 is a bare YkuD domain (47-183), PANTHER PTHR36699:SF1 "L,D-transpeptidase YafK-related".

A comparison against E. coli alone could not subtype PP_2320. By Smith-Waterman (BLOSUM62, gaps -11/-1, full length, score / identity / aligned length) PP_2320 scores LdtB/YbiS 498 / 43.3% / 252, LdtC/YcfS 514 / 38.7% / 297, LdtE/YnhG 509 / 37.3% / 316, LdtA/ErfK 484 / 37.1% / 302, and LdtD/YcbB 94 / 23.2% / 237. It sits clearly outside YcbB and roughly equidistant from the three Lpp-anchoring enzymes and from LdtE/YnhG, which makes 3-to-3 cross-links. That ambiguity is what the P. aeruginosa comparison in Finding 3 resolves.

Finding 3: orthology to functionally assigned P. aeruginosa enzymes resolves both proteins

Hugonneau-Beaufet et al. 2023 assigned a function to each of the three P. aeruginosa L,D-transpeptidase-family enzymes by gene inactivation and heterospecific expression (PMID: 37255442). They report the enzymes under PA14 locus tags, so those were mapped to PAO1 tags programmatically via UniProt (organism 208963): PA14_27180 (A0A0H2ZCT9, 323 aa) is sequence-identical to PA2854 (Q9HZZ0); PA14_15840 (A0A0H2ZF55, 166 aa) is identical to PA3756 (Q9HXN9); PA14_54810 (A0A0H2Z6J6, 347 aa) is 98.8% identical to PA0732 (Q9I5J7) over its full length. So LdtPae1 = PA0732, LdtPae2 = PA2854, LdtPae3 = PA3756.

The functional statements being transferred are specific. Deletion of ldtPae1 "abolished the formation of 3→3 cross-linked dimers, indicating that LdtPae1 is the only peptidoglycan cross-linking l,d-transpeptidase produced by P. aeruginosa", and "deletion of ldtPae2 alone abolished OprI anchoring, indicating that LdtPae2 is the only enzyme responsible for the anchoring of that lipoprotein". El-Araby et al. 2026 then showed directly that "the gene product of PA2854 is the catalyst that performs this transformation between the cell wall and the outer-membrane lipoprotein OprI in live Pseudomonas aeruginosa", reconstituted it in vitro with purified PA2854, OprI and synthetic peptidoglycan, and located the attachment at "the side-chain ε-amino group of Lys83 of OprI" (PMID: 42100858).

The transfer to PP_1451 is weaker than the identity suggests, because of what was demonstrated for its ortholog. Taken verbatim from the full text of the 2023 paper: "The function of LdtPae3 was tentatively assigned to the release of OprI based on both the absence of detectable amide bond-forming activity (formation of 3→3 and tripeptide→OprI bonds) and the close similarity between LdtPae3 and E. coli hydrolase YafK"; "Unlike with the parental strain, deletion of ldtPae3 did not result in any modification of the muropeptides"; and "We could not similarly investigate the catalytic activity of LdtPae2 and LdtPae3, since expression of fragments of the ldtPae2 and ldtPae3 genes under the conditions reported for ldtPae1 did not afford soluble proteins". In their Table 2 the OprI anchoring marker Tri→KR at m/z 1,154.567 is present in wild type, present in the ldtPae3 deletant, and absent only in the ldtPae2 deletant, the triple deletant and the oprI deletant. The abstract confirms the asymmetry of the evidence: function was assigned "by gene inactivation in P. aeruginosa, heterospecific gene expression in Escherichia coli, and, for one of them, direct determination of its enzymatic activity". That one was LdtPae1.

Finding 4: the substrate lipoprotein is present in KT2440 with the attachment lysine intact

An ungapped maximal-segment-pair scan of all 5,527 KT2440 proteins against P. aeruginosa OprI (P11221, 83 aa) returns one hit far above background: Q88KG8 at score 371, with the next seven hits at 71, 69, 65, 62, 60, 57 and 56 (PP_3236, PP_3235, PP_5661, PP_2823, pal, bamB, amgK). Q88KG8 carries the UniProt gene name oprI and the ordered locus name PP_2322, is 83 aa like the P. aeruginosa protein, and aligns ungapped at 94.0% identity (78/83) with five substitutions: H23V, E54D, L56M, G57A, E78D. The lipobox cysteine Cys20 is conserved, and the C-terminal Lys83, the residue whose ε-amino group is joined to the peptidoglycan stem peptide, is conserved as the last residue.

For scale, P. aeruginosa OprI and E. coli Lpp share 7.7% identity at equal offset in this calculation, and the 2023 paper reports 24.8% identity between them, so OprI orthology between the two pseudomonads is of a different order from the Lpp relationship. Finding this protein removes the alternative raised by Winkle et al., who note that "DpaA-like proteins have characteristic sequence motifs and are present in many Gram-negative bacteria, of which some have no Lpp" (PMID: 33947763). KT2440 has the tether-forming machinery and the tether substrate.

The genomic context is informative and asymmetric. PP_2322 lies two locus numbers from PP_2320, the anchoring transpeptidase. PP_1451 is unlinked, at a distant locus. Operon context therefore supports the anchoring assignment of PP_2320 more directly than it supports the hydrolase assignment of PP_1451.

Finding 5: the hydrolase never occurs without a transpeptidase

A co-occurrence census of 22 gammaproteobacterial reference proteomes classified each PF03734 protein by the Cys+2 residue (Arg = L,D-transpeptidase, otherwise DpaA-like) combined with PANTHER PTHR36699 membership. Eighteen genomes carry at least one DpaA-like gene, and all 18 also carry at least one L,D-transpeptidase (18/18). Three genomes carry L,D-transpeptidases with no DpaA-like gene: Haemophilus influenzae Rd (0 + 1), Acinetobacter baumannii (0 + 2) and Moraxella catarrhalis (0 + 2). Francisella tularensis SCHU S4 has neither. No genome in the set carries a DpaA-like hydrolase without a transpeptidase. KT2440 is the minimal co-occurring pair, one of each; E. coli K-12 is 1 + 5, Legionella pneumophila 2 + 7, Coxiella burnetii 2 + 6.

The dependency is directional, which is the pattern a tether-removing enzyme should show: it only appears where the tether can be made. This is a sequence-based classification over 22 genomes, so it is suggestive of the functional pairing and not proof of it.

Mechanistic model

The proposed chemistry is a three-enzyme-step cycle operating on one amide bond in the periplasm, with the hydrolase as the reverse of the anchoring step. The KT2440 assignment, with locus tags, follows from orthology to the P. aeruginosa enzymes whose functions were established by gene deletion.

PP_1451 is a 184-residue protein with a Sec signal peptide (residues 1-18) and a bare YkuD catalytic domain (47-183). The immediate molecular step under test is nucleophilic attack by Cys159 on the amide bond joining the ε-amino group of the C-terminal lysine of an OprI-type lipoprotein to the meso-DAP of a peptidoglycan tripeptide stem, forming a thioester acyl-enzyme that water resolves. His137 is positioned to act as the general base, at 4.05 Å from the cysteine sulphur, though no experiment in this family has tested the equivalent histidine.

The division of labour in KT2440 is cleaner than in E. coli, where five paralogues split anchoring from cross-linking. KT2440 has one transpeptidase and one hydrolase:

The remaining papers retrieved during the search are orientation on OprI biology and do not bear on the catalytic question. PMID: 23123904 shows that OprI affects outer membrane vesicle production in P. aeruginosa, which is a downstream consequence of tether levels. PMID: 36602318 reports that the P. syringae lipoprotein GrlP interacts with OprI and that periplasmic width changes with its expression, which is relevant to what tether turnover controls and says nothing about which enzyme cuts it. PMID: 9163432, PMID: 23246592 and PMID: 15259268 use oprI as a diagnostic marker for Pseudomonas and confirm that the gene is general to fluorescent pseudomonads, consistent with finding it in KT2440.

A molecular function term naming the peptidoglycan-OprI amide bond specifically would be over-reach on current evidence, because no Pseudomonas protein in this family has had its activity measured. GO:0008234 is the most specific term the evidence fully supports, and it remains correct whether the enzyme turns out to be a tether hydrolase, an L,D-endopeptidase or an L,D-carboxypeptidase.

Mechanistic scope

The direct gene-product activity under test is one chemical step in the periplasm: hydrolysis of the amide bond joining the ε-amino group of the C-terminal lysine of an OprI-type lipoprotein to the meso-DAP of a peptidoglycan tripeptide stem, via a Cys159 thioester acyl-enzyme resolved by water. The Sec signal peptide and the periplasmic substrate fix the compartment. The catalytic domain spans residues 47 to 183 of a 184-residue protein, so the enzyme is close to the catalytic domain alone, with no accessory binding modules of the kind PP_2320 carries (LysM, 40-86).

Separate from this step, and not evidence for it:

  • Outer membrane to peptidoglycan attachment levels are a pathway consequence set jointly by the anchoring enzyme and the hydrolase. The 2023 paper reports that biofilm cells show "a greater extent of OprI anchoring to peptidoglycan, which is controlled by LdtPae2 and LdtPae3", a phenotype of the pair, not a measurement of either enzyme.
  • Periplasmic width and outer membrane stiffness follow from attachment levels. PMID: 36602318 shows that changing the expression of an OprI-interacting lipoprotein changes periplasmic size in P. syringae.
  • Outer membrane vesicle production is further downstream again (PMID: 23123904).
  • Antibiotic susceptibility. In P. aeruginosa, deletion of ldtPae1, of ldtPae2 plus ldtPae3, or of all three did not change β-lactam susceptibility, and ldtPae3 deletion alone had no effect on killing. Loss-of-function phenotypes in this family are mild and should not be used to infer the molecular function.

Conflicts and alternatives

L,D-carboxypeptidase or L,D-endopeptidase activity is the main competitor. MEROPS subfamily C82.A01 groups DpaA-like proteins with Csd6 of H. pylori and Pgp2 of C. jejuni, both L,D-carboxypeptidases, and E. coli LdtF itself "also exhibits glycine-specific carboxypeptidase activity on muropeptides containing a terminal glycine residue" (PMID: 33941679). Nothing in the PP_1451 sequence or model distinguishes these from tether hydrolysis, since DpaA is active on both mDAP-L-Lys and mDAP-Gly linkages.

The ortholog's assignment rests on negative evidence. LdtPae3/PA3756 was assigned to OprI release because it did nothing else that could be detected. Its deletion left the muropeptide profile unchanged, including the Tri→KR anchoring marker. A hydrolase of that bond would be expected to raise anchoring when deleted. The published mass table reports presence or absence rather than quantities, so a modest increase could have gone unrecorded, and the paper reports no such increase.

The two most activity-sensitive DpaA positions are not conserved. Tyr109 and Tyr131, whose alanine substitutions left 0.7% and 4% of wild-type activity, become Phe106 and Met133 in PP_1451. The conservative reading is that specificity in PP_1451 has not been measured and should not be assumed identical to DpaA.

Family-name carry-over. Pfam PF03734 and InterPro IPR005490 are both named for L,D-transpeptidase activity. Any automatic transpeptidase annotation on PP_1451 traces to the domain name and conflicts with the Ala at Cys+2.

Paralog confusion with PP_2320. The two KT2440 YkuD proteins catalyse opposite reactions. Anchoring and cross-linking terms belong on PP_2320, hydrolase terms on PP_1451, and the two should not be mixed in the review.

Phylogeny does not predict function in this family. The 2023 paper states that LDTs performing the three functions "cannot be assigned to distinct evolutionary lineages, in contrast to what has been observed with PBPs". Subfamily membership alone is therefore weak; the argument here rests on pairwise identity to a tested protein, which is why the PA14-to-PAO1 mapping was verified rather than assumed.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Ordered by how much each would change the curation call per unit of effort.

  1. Muropeptide LC-MS of KT2440 wild type, ΔPP_1451, ΔPP_2320 and ΔPP_2322(oprI). One experiment settles most of the open questions. Watch the Tri→KR anchoring marker at m/z 1,154.567: it should disappear in ΔPP_2320 and ΔoprI, and rise in ΔPP_1451 if PP_1451 is the tether hydrolase. In the same run, look for Tri→Tri (m/z 1,722.731) and Tri→Tetra (m/z 1,793.768) 3-to-3 dimers to settle whether KT2440 makes 3-to-3 cross-links without a YcbB-type enzyme.
  2. In vitro assay of purified PP_1451 on isolated Tri→KR muropeptide, with the C159A mutant as the inactive control, mirroring the DpaA(C143A) control. Solubility was the obstacle for PA3756, so the 184-residue P. putida protein is worth trying precisely because the P. aeruginosa one failed.
  3. Counter-screen against L,D-carboxypeptidase substrates in the same assay: tetrapeptide stems and muropeptides with a terminal glycine. This is the single test that separates tether hydrolysis from the MEROPS C82.A01 alternative, and it is the reason the verdict is partially supported rather than supported.
  4. Heterospecific expression of PP_1451 in an E. coli ΔdpaA background, scoring restoration of Lpp release. This readout already worked in 37255442 and needs no Pseudomonas genetics.
  5. H137A, plus the specificity swap F106Y / M133Y. H137A tests whether the dyad is real and whether the ProRule proton-donor annotation should be kept. Restoring the two DpaA tyrosines tests whether they account for a specificity difference.
  6. Broader co-occurrence census. The 22-genome sample supports the directional dependency. Extending it to several hundred gammaproteobacterial genomes, with the hydrolase called by Cys+2 and PANTHER membership, would show whether the 18/18 pattern holds at scale.
