Functional Annotation of uppP (Undecaprenyl-diphosphatase, UniProt Q88IY7) in Pseudomonas putida KT2440

1. Summary / Answer to the Research Question

The uppP gene of Pseudomonas putida KT2440 (locus PP_2862; synonyms bacA, upk; UniProt Q88IY7) encodes undecaprenyl pyrophosphate phosphatase (UppP; EC 3.6.1.27), an integral inner (cytoplasmic) membrane enzyme of the UppP/BacA family (InterPro IPR003824; Pfam PF02673). Its primary, specific function is to dephosphorylate undecaprenyl pyrophosphate (C55‑PP) to undecaprenyl phosphate (C55‑P), thereby regenerating the active mono‑phosphorylated form of the universal C55 isoprenoid lipid carrier ("bactoprenol") that is essential for the trans‑membrane assembly of peptidoglycan and other cell‑envelope glycopolymers. It carries out this reaction at the membrane–periplasm interface, with its catalytic site facing the periplasm, and is proposed to help relocate the C55‑P product back to the cytoplasmic face. Loss of this activity depletes the finite C55‑P pool and arrests cell‑wall synthesis; the enzyme's name bacA derives from the fact that maintaining this pool confers resistance to the antibiotic bacitracin.

Note on organism‑specific evidence. The gene identity is unambiguous — the symbol uppP, the UppP/BacA domains, and the "undecaprenyl-diphosphatase / bacitracin resistance protein" description all match a single, well‑defined bacterial enzyme family. However, no primary experimental study of the P. putida KT2440 protein itself was found; its annotation rests on the HAMAP rule (MF_01006) and very strong homology to biochemically characterized orthologs from E. coli, Vibrio vulnificus, and Enterococcus faecalis. The functional description below is therefore transferred from these orthologs by evolutionary/bioinformatic inference, which is highly reliable for this deeply conserved, single‑function enzyme family.

3. Primary Molecular Function — the catalyzed reaction and substrate specificity

UppP catalyzes:

undecaprenyl diphosphate (C55‑PP) + H₂O → undecaprenyl phosphate (C55‑P) + Pᵢ (EC 3.6.1.27)

This activity was first established biochemically for the E. coli BacA/UppP protein and confirmed for multiple orthologs. Chang et al. describe the enzyme directly: "Undecaprenyl pyrophosphate phosphatase (UppP), a cell membrane integral enzyme, catalyzes the dephosphorylation of undecaprenyl pyrophosphate to undecaprenyl phosphate, which is an essential carrier lipid in bacterial cell wall synthesis" (28302513). El Ghachi et al. state that "the enzyme function is to synthesize the carrier lipid undecaprenyl phosphate that is essential for the biosynthesis of peptidoglycan and other cell wall components" (15778224).

Substrate specificity. The physiological substrate is the fully‑unsaturated C55 polyprenyl pyrophosphate. The curated UniProt/Rhea reaction for Q88IY7 specifies the exact stereochemistry — di‑trans,octa‑cis‑undecaprenyl diphosphate + H₂O → di‑trans,octa‑cis‑undecaprenyl phosphate + phosphate + H⁺ (EC 3.6.1.27) — i.e. the bacterial C55 bactoprenol, not the α‑saturated dolichol used by eukaryotes. This isoprenoid selectivity is consistent with the bacterial lipid‑carrier machinery's strict preference for the fully unsaturated C55 species (context from 9134438). The enzyme requires a divalent metal cofactor (Mg²⁺ or Ca²⁺) for activity (24855653).

4. Catalytic Mechanism and Active Site

UppP is a metal‑dependent, membrane‑integral pyrophosphatase. Two conserved sequence regions constitute the catalytic center: a glutamate‑rich (E/Q)XXXE motif together with a PGXSRSXXT motif and a conserved His residue. Mutagenesis in E. coli showed that E17A/E21A, H30A, S173A, R174A and T178A abolish activity, and that catalysis has "an absolute requirement of magnesium or calcium ions" (24855653). Independent work on V. vulnificus UppP confirmed that "the proposed catalytic residues Gln‑13, Glu‑17, His‑26 and Arg‑166 are directly involved in enzyme catalysis", with additional conserved Ser/Gly residues essential for activity (28302513). The 2.0 Å E. coli crystal structure revealed "the mechanistic basis for intramembranal phosphatase action and substrate specificity using an inverted topology repeat" (29559664). These catalytic residues are conserved in the P. putida ortholog under the HAMAP rule, supporting an identical mechanism.

5. Subcellular Localization and Membrane Topology

UppP is a polytopic integral protein of the cytoplasmic (inner) membrane, containing 6–8 transmembrane helices depending on the prediction/structure method (UniProt annotates 6 for Q88IY7; the E. coli crystal structure resolves 8; E. faecalis UppP was "predicted to be a hydrophobic protein with eight transmembrane helices", 23460607). The catalytic site faces the periplasm: topological modeling places the two conserved catalytic motifs "near the aqueous interface of UppP … face the periplasm, implicating that its enzymatic function is on the outer side of the plasma membrane" (24855653). This orientation is logical because the C55‑PP substrate is generated on the periplasmic face after glycan polymers are transferred to their acceptors. The crystal structure further notes "key structural motifs common to a variety of cross‑membrane transporters," hinting at "a potential flippase function in the specific relocalization of the C55‑P product back to the cytosolic space" (29559664) — i.e., the enzyme may couple dephosphorylation to returning the carrier lipid to the cytoplasmic leaflet.

6. Pathway Context — the Bactoprenol (C55‑P) Recycling Cycle

UppP closes the lipid‑carrier recycling loop central to cell‑envelope biogenesis (29559664):

  1. In the cytoplasm, peptidoglycan (and other glycan) subunits are assembled on C55‑P (forming lipid I / lipid II, etc.).
  2. The lipid‑linked intermediate is flipped to the periplasmic face.
  3. The glycan is polymerized/transferred onto the growing sacculus (or O‑antigen, capsule, etc.), releasing C55‑PP as a by‑product.
  4. UppP dephosphorylates C55‑PP → C55‑P, regenerating the active carrier, which returns to the cytoplasmic face to begin another round.

Because the C55‑P pool is small and shared, this recycling step supplies the carrier for multiple competing envelope pathways: "UndP … ferries most glycans and glycopolymers across the cytoplasmic membrane … In addition to peptidoglycan precursors, UndP transports O‑antigen, capsule, wall teichoic acids, and sugar modifications" (39878533). In Gram‑negative P. putida, the principal downstream demands are peptidoglycan synthesis and LPS O‑antigen synthesis.

Two sources of substrate; one committed activation step. The authoritative review by Manat et al. (2014) frames UppP's position precisely: "Undecaprenyl‑phosphate originates from the dephosphorylation of its precursor undecaprenyl‑diphosphate, with itself generated by de novo synthesis or by recycling after the final glycan transfer" (24799078). The Und‑PP substrate therefore arrives from two sources — (i) de novo synthesis by the cytosolic cis-prenyltransferase undecaprenyl‑diphosphate synthase (UppS/IspU), and (ii) recycling of C55‑PP released after glycan transfer — and both converge on the UppP‑catalyzed dephosphorylation that yields the usable Und‑P carrier. This makes UppP the committed activation step of the bactoprenol cycle.

A second, unrelated phosphatase family explains redundancy. The same review notes that "two unrelated families of integral membrane proteins exhibit undecaprenyl‑diphosphate phosphatase activity: BacA and members of the phosphatidic acid phosphatase type 2 super‑family" (24799078). P. putida UppP (Q88IY7) belongs to the BacA family; PAP2‑superfamily paralogs (homologs of E. coli PgpB/YbjG/LpxT) are expected to provide overlapping activity, which is why single bacA/uppP loss is typically sub‑lethal even though the combined activity is essential.

7. Physiological Importance and Redundancy

Blocking C55‑P regeneration is lethal: interruption "leads to the buildup of cell wall intermediates and cell lysis" (29559664). In E. coli, bacA alone is not essential because three additional membrane phosphatases (PAP2‑family YbjG, PgpB, and YeiU/LpxT) provide overlapping Und‑PP‑phosphatase activity; only a conditional bacA ybjG pgpB triple mutant "accumulate[s] soluble peptidoglycan nucleotide precursors and … lyse[s] … due to the depletion of the pool of undecaprenyl phosphate and consequent arrest of cell wall synthesis" (15778224). BacA/UppP contributes the largest single fraction of this activity. The degree of redundancy in P. putida has not been experimentally mapped, but analogous PAP2‑type phosphatases are encoded in its genome, so uppP is expected to be the major but not sole contributor.

8. Secondary Phenotypes that Illuminate the Precise Role

  • Bacitracin resistance (the bacA name). Bacitracin acts by sequestering C55‑PP, starving the cell of recyclable carrier. Elevated UppP activity replenishes C55‑P and confers resistance: overexpression "result[s] in bacitracin resistance and increased levels of undecaprenyl pyrophosphate phosphatase activity" (15778224), and in E. faecalis deletion lowered the bacitracin MIC from 32–48 to 3–6 mg/L while overexpression raised it to ≥256 mg/L, with MICs of unrelated antibiotics unchanged (23460607). This specificity confirms that the phenotype flows directly from C55‑PP → C55‑P turnover, not a general stress effect.
  • Bacteriocin receptor. UppP serves as the membrane receptor for two‑peptide (class IIb) bacteriocins: resistance mutations to lactococcin G all mapped to "the gene uppP (bacA), encoding an undecaprenyl pyrophosphate phosphatase; a membrane protein involved in peptidoglycan synthesis" (24779486). This reflects the enzyme's exposed, essential position in the membrane rather than a distinct biochemical activity.

These are downstream consequences of the single core function and should not be mistaken for the primary role.

10. Limitations and Future Directions

  • No direct experimental data on the P. putida KT2440 protein; all mechanistic detail is transferred from orthologs. Direct enzymatic assay of purified PP_2862, and a ΔuppP phenotype in P. putida, would confirm the transfer and quantify redundancy with PAP2‑type phosphatases.
  • The proposed flippase/product‑relocation activity remains a structural inference and is unproven for any ortholog.
  • The relative contribution of uppP vs. PAP2‑family paralogs to total Und‑PP‑phosphatase activity in Pseudomonas is unknown.
