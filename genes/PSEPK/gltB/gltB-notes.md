

## 2026-10-26 systematic standalone review

GltB (PP_5076) is the large catalytic subunit of the NADPH-dependent glutamate synthase of Pseudomonas putida KT2440. Its conserved N-terminal glutamine amidotransferase and FMN-associated synthase domains couple glutamine hydrolysis to reductive amination of 2-oxoglutarate, producing two glutamate molecules in the complete reaction. GltD supplies the NADPH-linked reducing equivalents. Native KT2440 gltB disruption and nitrogen-source fitness experiments support its metabolic role; the detailed ammonia-channel and redox-cofactor mechanism is inferred from conserved domains and characterized homologs.
