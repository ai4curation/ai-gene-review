# infB curation notes

## Primary evidence

The IF2 GTPase and late-initiation role is supported directly by quench-flow
and stopped-flow measurements. IF2-dependent GTP hydrolysis occurred rapidly
during the transition to the 70S initiation complex, followed by slower
phosphate release [PMID:10790378, "IF2-dependent GTP hydrolysis was fast (30/s)
followed by slow P(i) release from the complex (1.5/s)."]. The paper also
describes IF2 as spanning initiator-tRNA recruitment, subunit association, and
late initiation, supporting its appearance in more than one module phase.
