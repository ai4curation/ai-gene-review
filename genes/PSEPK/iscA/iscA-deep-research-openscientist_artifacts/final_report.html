Functional Annotation Report: IscA (Q88PK6, PP_0844) in Pseudomonas putida KT2440

1. Summary / Answer to the Research Question

IscA (gene iscA, locus PP_0844; UniProt Q88PK6) is an A-type carrier (ATC) protein of the ISC (Iron–Sulfur Cluster) biogenesis machinery. Its primary function is not enzymatic catalysis but molecular carriage: it is a soluble cytoplasmic Fe–S cluster / iron chaperone that binds a pre-assembled iron–sulfur cluster (a labile, solvent-exposed [2Fe–2S], which it can convert/deliver as [4Fe–4S]) and transfers it from the de novo assembly scaffold (IscU) to specific apo-target proteins, thereby maturing them into active Fe–S enzymes. It belongs to the HesB/IscA family and carries the family-defining triad of conserved cysteines that ligate the cluster.

The gene identity is confirmed and unambiguous: the symbol iscA, the "Iron-binding protein IscA / Iron-sulfur cluster assembly protein" description, the HesB/IscA family, and the ATAP/FeS-cluster-insertion domains all coincide with a large, directly applicable body of literature on bacterial (chiefly E. coli) IscA. The 107-aa Q88PK6 sequence retains all three functional cysteines.

2. Molecular Function (Primary Role)

IscA is one of a small set of A-type carrier proteins (ATCs). In the well-studied E. coli paradigm there are four ATCs — IscA, SufA, ErpA and the degenerate ATC* domain of NfuA — which "were shown to partially replace each other in their roles in [4Fe-4S] cluster insertion into specific target proteins" (Hasnat et al. 2021, 33782054).

  • What it does: receives an Fe–S cluster from the biosynthetic scaffold and hands it off to acceptor apoproteins. It does not synthesize sulfide or perform de novo cluster assembly; that is the job of the upstream cysteine desulfurase IscS and scaffold IscU. The ISC system assembles a "transient formation of a 2Fe2S cluster on a scaffold protein from sulfide and iron salts" which is then transferred via carriers to acceptor apoproteins (Bonomi et al. 2022, 35611886).
  • Substrate / ligand specificity: the "substrate" is the iron–sulfur cluster itself (and/or ferrous/ferric iron). UniProt annotates Q88PK6 as an iron-binding protein, and IscA family members are documented to bind iron as well as Fe–S clusters, supporting a dual role as an iron chaperone/donor that recruits iron for cluster assembly and as a cluster carrier for downstream delivery.

Iron-chaperone role and the "scaffold vs. carrier" resolution

Direct biochemical/genetic work (the Ding laboratory) clarified IscA's precise role. In vivo, "IscA binds iron" whereas the scaffold IscU and the frataxin homolog CyaY do not, and IscA "may act as an iron chaperone for the biogenesis of iron-sulfur clusters in E. coli cells under aerobic conditions" (Wang et al. 2010, 20942799). Critically this activity is oxygen-regulated: "the strong iron-binding activity of IscA is greatly diminished in E. coli cells under anaerobic growth conditions," and IscA + its paralogue SufA are essential for Fe–S assembly aerobically but not anaerobically (20942799). In vitro, with IscS/L-cysteine present, "iron-sulfur clusters are assembled in IscU, but not in IscA, suggesting that IscU is a preferred iron-sulfur cluster assembly scaffold protein," while in the absence of cysteine "nearly all iron is bound to IscA," which prevents precipitation of biologically inaccessible ferric hydroxide (Yang, Bitoun & Ding 2006, 16877383). Interpretation for the target: IscA is best described as an iron chaperone / cluster carrier acting downstream of the IscU scaffold, keeping iron soluble and bioavailable under oxidizing conditions. Because P. putida KT2440 is an obligate aerobe, this aerobic iron-chaperone function is expected to be particularly relevant for PP_0844. - Clients: IscA/ATCs deliver clusters to numerous [4Fe–4S] enzymes, including respiratory-chain and metabolic proteins. Concrete examples of IscA-dependent maturation include subunits of the membrane-associated formate hydrogenlyase (FHL) complex — "The A-type carrier (ATC) proteins IscA and ErpA probably deliver [Fe-S] to specific apoprotein components of the FHL complex because mutants lacking either protein exhibited strongly reduced hydrogen production. Neither ATC protein could compensate for the lack of the other" (Pinske et al. 2013, 23558265) — and radical-SAM / molybdenum-cofactor and nitrate-reductase pathway proteins (Hasnat et al. 2021, 33782054).

Structural basis of function

The first Fe–S-cluster-bound structure of an IscA (from Thermosynechococcus elongatus) showed that "one partially exposed [2Fe-2S] cluster is coordinated by two conformationally distinct IscA protomers … with asymmetric cysteinyl ligation by Cys37, Cys101, Cys103" and that the protein "carries three strictly conserved cysteine residues" (Morimoto et al. 2006, 16730357). The solvent-exposed cluster and asymmetric, labile ligation are precisely what enable cluster handoff to acceptor proteins.

Bioinformatic confirmation for the target: Direct analysis of Q88PK6 (107 aa) identifies exactly three cysteines — C35 within the conserved ...RTTG**C**SGL... motif (the InterPro IPR017870 FeS-cluster-insertion conserved site) and C99/C101 in the C-terminal ...RGE**C**G**C**GES... motif. These are positionally homologous to the cluster-ligating Cys37/Cys101/Cys103 of T. elongatus IscA. Retention of this triad distinguishes a functional carrier from the "degenerate' A-type carrier domain (ATC) lacking Fe-S cluster co-ordinating Cys ligands" found in NfuA subfamily II (Py et al. 2012, 22966982) — i.e., P. putida IscA is a bona fide, cluster-competent* ATC.

3. Biological Process / Pathway

IscA operates within the ISC iron–sulfur cluster biogenesis pathway, encoded by the isc operon (iscR-iscS-iscU-iscA-hscB-hscA-fdx-iscX).

Organism-specific genomic context (this study). A UniProt/KEGG query for P. putida KT2440 confirms the ISC cluster is present and canonically ordered around the target: PP_0841 iscR (2Fe–2S IscR transcriptional dual regulator) → PP_0842 iscS (cysteine desulfurase, EC 2.8.1.7) → PP_0843 iscU (scaffold) → PP_0844 iscA (target, Q88PK6) → PP_0845 hscB (co-chaperone) → PP_0846 hscA (Hsp70 chaperone) → PP_0847 fdx (2Fe–2S ferredoxin). A second cysteine desulfurase, iscS-II (PP_2435), lies elsewhere. This exact synteny with the E. coli iscR-iscSUA-hscBA-fdx operon is strong organism-specific evidence that PP_0844 functions as the A-type carrier/iron chaperone of a fully intact ISC machinery, co-regulated with the desulfurase, scaffold, chaperones, ferredoxin, and the IscR master regulator. Genetic dissection of this operon in E. coli placed IscA in the assembler/delivery stage: "the assembly of Fe-S clusters [is executed] by assembler machinery comprising of the iscUA-hscBA-fdx gene products," with IscS providing sulfur via cysteine desulfurase activity (Tokumoto & Takahashi 2001, 11432781). Coexpression of the whole iscS-iscU-iscA-hscB-hscA-fdx cluster dramatically increases holo-ferredoxin production, confirming these products "are involved in the assembly of the Fe-S clusters in a wide variety of Fe-S proteins" (Nakamura et al. 1999, 10393315).

Pathway position (order of events): IscS (Cys → sulfur) → IscU scaffold (+ Fe, + Fdx electrons, HscA/HscB chaperones) builds [2Fe–2S] → IscA/other ATCs receive & traffic the cluster (and supply iron) → apo-target proteins matured to holo-enzymes.

Redundancy and regulation. The cell maintains an interconnected carrier network ("ErpA, IscA, SufA, and NfuA," plus the glutaredoxin GrxD) that bridges the housekeeping ISC and stress-responsive SUF systems (Fisher et al. 2024, 38944118). Among ATCs, "ErpA is the only ATC essential under aerobiosis," while IscA and SufA are partially redundant (Mandin et al. 2016, 27651365). This redundancy explains why iscA single mutants show only a "small but appreciable" defect in Fe-S enzyme activity (Tokumoto & Takahashi 2001, 11432781), even though IscA has non-redundant roles for specific clients (e.g., FHL; Pinske et al. 2013, 23558265). IscA is also reported dispensable under anaerobic conditions in an ISC-only background (Tanaka et al. 2016, 26560204), consistent with condition-dependent redundancy.

Reduced redundancy in P. putida (this study). Database searches of KT2440 reveal a Fe-S biogenesis complement that is notably simpler than E. coli's: the organism has the complete ISC operon plus the ATC carriers ErpA (PP_0433) and NfuA (PP_2378), but no canonical SUF operon and, critically, no SufA paralogue (searches for sufA/sufB/sufD/sufS/sufE return no cognate SUF genes). Consequently IscA is the sole IscA/SufA-type A-type carrier in P. putida, so its aerobic iron-chaperone/cluster-carrier role is less buffered than in E. coli (where SufA can partially substitute). This suggests a comparatively more prominent, less redundant role for PP_0844 in this obligate aerobe — an inference from annotation searches that merits direct experimental verification.

The end products of the pathway — mature Fe–S proteins — are essential for respiration, the TCA cycle (aconitase), isoprenoid biosynthesis (IspG/IspH), DNA repair, and gene regulation, so IscA contributes indirectly but importantly to central metabolism.

4. Subcellular Localization

IscA functions in the bacterial cytoplasm. It is a soluble protein with no signal peptide or transmembrane segment (107 aa, consistent with the compact soluble ATC fold), and it acts on cytoplasmic scaffolds (IscU) and cytoplasmic/inner-membrane-associated apoprotein clients. Its membership in the cytoplasmic Fe-S trafficking network (Fisher et al. 2024, 38944118) situates its activity in the cytosol, where it can also deliver clusters to membrane-embedded complexes (e.g., FHL) from the cytoplasmic face. In P. putida this network comprises the ISC system plus the carriers IscA, ErpA and NfuA (no SUF pathway; see §3).

7. Limitations and Future Directions

  • Nearly all direct functional/structural data derive from E. coli and cyanobacterial orthologs; there is little P. putida-specific experimental characterization of PP_0844. Functional assignment for Q88PK6 is by strong homology and conserved-motif inference rather than direct in-organism assay.
  • The long-debated "scaffold vs. iron-chaperone vs. cluster-carrier" emphasis of IscA is condition- and organism-dependent; the precise balance in P. putida (an obligate aerobe with active oxidative metabolism) is untested.
  • Future work: (i) P. putida ΔiscA / ΔsufA / ΔerpA phenotyping under aerobic vs. iron-limited stress; (ii) in vitro reconstitution to confirm Fe/[2Fe–2S] binding by recombinant Q88PK6; (iii) identify P. putida-specific client apoproteins (e.g., ferredoxins, aconitase, IspG/IspH) that depend on IscA.
