# alr/BSR curation notes

Q88GJ9 is not a conventional cytoplasmic biosynthetic Alr in KT2440. The
reviewed entry assigns the protein to the periplasm and says that activity is
highest with lysine and arginine.
[UniProtKB:Q88GJ9, "SUBCELLULAR LOCATION: Periplasm"; "Among these amino acids,
activity is the highest with lysine and arginine"]

The 2018 deletion/fractionation study directly supports this boundary:
"Alr enzymatic activity is found in the periplasm" and "The stationary phase
peptidoglycan structure did not differ between wild-type and delta-alr
strains." The same abstract identifies the primary role as basic-amino-acid
catabolism.
[PMID:30008699, "Alr enzymatic activity is found in the periplasm";
"The stationary phase peptidoglycan structure did not differ between wild-type
and delta-alr strains, indicating that products resulting from Alr activity are
not incorporated into peptidoglycan under these conditions."]

Although alanine racemase activity is measurable, the 2013 kinetic study found
that Alr's lysine catalytic efficiency is about three orders of magnitude
higher than its alanine efficiency.
[PMID:23995642, "the k cat / K m values with l - and d -lysine were 3 orders of
magnitude greater than the k cat / K m values with l - and d -alanine"]

Therefore Q88GJ9 is excluded from the cytoplasmic D-alanine-supply leaf. This
does not claim it can never affect cell-wall chemistry under any condition; it
records that the available localization and physiological evidence do not
support making it the constitutive supply enzyme.
