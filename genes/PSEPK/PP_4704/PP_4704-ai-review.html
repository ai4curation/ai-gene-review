Gene Description

PP_4704 (Q88DW4) is a 978-residue AsmA-like protein of the PTHR30441 family (subfamily SF8) with a DUF748 (PF05359) region and a single N-terminal transmembrane helix (DeepTMHMM residues 4-32), the rest of the protein predicted to lie outside the cytoplasm. It is the reciprocal-best-hit orthologue of Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA4735 (EpaF, Q9HV64; 64.9% identity over 977 aligned residues, full length of both), a Pseudomonas-specific AsmA-like protein that is absent from E. coli. It is not an orthologue of any E. coli AsmA-like protein (AsmA, TamB, YhdP, YdbH, YicH, YhjG); local alignments to these cover at most about a quarter of PP_4704. Its KT2440 AsmA-like paralogues are TamB (PP_2893), YhdP (PP_0938), YhjG (PP_4857), AsmA (PP_0287) and the short DUF748 protein PP_4572 (orthologue of PA2708); KT2440 has no YdbH orthologue. In P. aeruginosa, EpaF is redundantly essential with YhdP, TamB and YdbH. A quadruple mutant could not be made, and overexpressed EpaF alone restores growth and outer-membrane integrity to a tamB yhdP ydbH triple mutant (PMID:38305166). Because YhdP, TamB and YdbH are the proposed bridge-like inner-to-outer-membrane phospholipid transporters (PMID:34781743, PMID:38748582), PP_4704 is best described by orthology as an inner-membrane-anchored AsmA-like protein that contributes to outer-membrane biogenesis, probably by moving glycerophospholipids across the periplasm. Nothing has been measured for PP_4704 itself. In KT2440 RB-TnSeq (300 experiments, BERDL copy of the Fitness Browser) its transposon mutants have no phenotype (fitness between -0.72 and +0.31), including under polymyxin B. That is expected for a redundant member of this family and says nothing either way. Lipid-transfer activity has not been shown biochemically for EpaF.

Core Functions

Inner-membrane-anchored AsmA-like (bridge-like, repeating beta-groove) protein of the Pseudomonas-specific EpaF subfamily that contributes, redundantly with TamB and YhdP, to outer-membrane biogenesis, probably by moving glycerophospholipids from the inner to the outer membrane. Inferred from its P. aeruginosa orthologue PA4735; untested in P. putida.

The locus tag "PP_4704" is unambiguous. It names one ORF in the KT2440 genome (Nelson et al. 2002, Environ. Microbiol. 4:799, PMID:12534463; re-annotation by Belda et al. 2016, PMID:26913973). I found no publication that studies PP_4704 directly. The genome papers only report its sequence. Everything below about function is inferred from:
- its domains and family membership;
- experimental work on homologs in E. coli and P. aeruginosa.

2. Family placement: an AsmA-like protein in the YicH subfamily

The local PANTHER family file (genes/interpro/panther/PTHR30441/) lists three Swiss-Prot E. coli K-12 members of PTHR30441:
- AsmA (P28249, SF4);
- YicH (P31433, SF8, the same subfamily as PP_4704);
- YhjG (P37645, SF9).

A second P. putida paralog, PP_4572, is in SF4 ("Protein AsmA").

PTHR30441 is one branch of the bacterial AsmA-like superfamily. In E. coli this superfamily has six members: TamB, YhdP, YdbH, YhjG, YicH and AsmA. They are thought to be related to the eukaryotic repeating β-groove ("bridge-like") lipid-transfer proteins (bioRxiv 2025, TamB groove; bioRxiv 2025, polar localization).

The InterPro/PANTHER family description says the family is "implicated in the process of outer membrane protein assembly". That text is LLM-generated and unchecked (llm: true, checked: false in the metadata). It reflects the original genetic finding about E. coli AsmA, not the current mechanistic model.

3. Domain architecture (from the sequence and the signatures above)

  1. N-terminus (about residues 1–35). A hydrophobic stretch: …AGAIVALVALYSLLGFLILPGVALRIA…. This matches the N-terminal transmembrane helix that anchors AsmA-like proteins in the inner membrane. UniProt has no Phobius or TMHMM feature for this segment. This assignment is my reading of the sequence, not an annotated feature.
  2. Central region (about residues 40–800). The DUF748 / AsmA-related region. In characterized family members this corresponds to the long periplasmic domain. AlphaFold predicts that domain as "extended, tube‑like structures consisting of stacked β‑strands, which create a continuous hydrophobic groove along the long axis" (Sposato et al. 2025, Protein Sci. 34:e70227, PMID:40970438). The region also has two disordered, low-complexity segments (333–362 and 556–578; MobiDB-lite).
  3. C-terminus (about residues 830–978). An OmpA-like fold (SSF103088 / Gene3D 3.30.1330.60). In other proteins this fold usually binds peptidoglycan non-covalently. I have not checked whether the canonical peptidoglycan-binding residues are conserved here, so peptidoglycan binding by PP_4704 is not established.

This combination is unusual: an AsmA-like transmembrane anchor and β-groove fused to a C-terminal OmpA-like domain. E. coli YicH (569 aa) has no OmpA module. PP_4704 is therefore a longer, Pseudomonas-type variant of the YicH subfamily.

A comparison suggests one possible role for the OmpA-like domain. In P. aeruginosa YhdP, C-terminal hydrophobic helices are "crucial for its function" and are proposed to anchor the protein to the outer membrane and/or help insert phospholipids (Sposato et al. 2025). By analogy, the OmpA-like domain of PP_4704 could tether the far end of the bridge to the peptidoglycan/outer-membrane side. This is a hypothesis only.

4. What the family does: experimental evidence from homologs

Phospholipid transport to the outer membrane (E. coli).
- YhdP, TamB and YdbH are "redundant but essential". Losing all three is lethal, and any one of them is enough for growth. The authors propose they are the long-sought transporters that move phospholipids from the inner to the outer membrane (Ruiz et al., mBio, Dec 2021; PMC8593681).
- Without YhdP, TamB and YdbH, glycerophospholipid transport and cell morphology are defective. Cells release excess outer-membrane vesicles rich in lipopolysaccharide (Douglass, McLean & Trent, PLoS Genet., Feb 2022; PMC8912898).
- YdbH pairs with the outer-membrane lipoprotein YnbE to form a bridge between the inner and outer membranes (summary; CCNY seminar abstract).
- Lipids have been shown to traverse the hydrophobic groove of TamB. A preprint reports that all six E. coli AsmA-family proteins, including YicH, can hold lipids in their grooves; this is structural and simulation evidence and has not been peer reviewed (bioRxiv 2025).
- Several of these proteins are mostly found at the cell poles (preprint, bioRxiv 2025).

YicH and YhjG specifically. Neither has been shown to transport lipids in vivo. EcoliWiki lists YhjG as "function unknown" (EcoliWiki; PRO P31433). The minor members appear to be accessory or redundant, not essential.

Pseudomonas.
- P. aeruginosa PAO1 encodes seven AsmA-like proteins. Four of them (YhdP, TamB, YdbH and PA4735/EpaF) are "essential yet redundant" for growth and outer-membrane integrity (Sposato et al., mSphere 2024, PMC10900882, as cited in Sposato et al. 2025). I could not retrieve the 2024 full text. As a result, I could not confirm which PAO1 protein is the ortholog of PP_4704, or whether that protein is one of the essential four. Being in the YicH subfamily suggests it is probably one of the non-essential members.
- A ΔtamB ΔyhdP ΔydbH mutant shows "extreme sensitivity" to SDS/EDTA and vancomycin. YhdP has the strongest activity in maintaining outer-membrane integrity (Sposato et al. 2025, PMID:40970438, PMC12447247).

Historical role of AsmA. E. coli asmA was first found as a suppressor of the assembly of defective OmpF (hence "assembly of outer membrane proteins"). asmA mutants also have less lipopolysaccharide (ASU record). The current view is that these effects on outer-membrane proteins are indirect consequences of disturbed outer-membrane lipid homeostasis.

5. Synthesis: likely function of PP_4704

  • Molecular function. Probably a lipid-binding protein with a hydrophobic β-groove that forms an intermembrane bridge for glycerophospholipids. No enzymatic activity is expected. The substrate (glycerophospholipids) is inferred from homologs and has not been tested for PP_4704.
  • Location. Anchored in the inner (cytoplasmic) membrane by its N-terminal helix, with the large groove domain spanning the periplasm. The C-terminal OmpA-like domain may contact peptidoglycan or the outer membrane.
  • Process. Probably contributes, likely redundantly with stronger paralogs, to moving phospholipids from the inner to the outer membrane and to outer-membrane biogenesis and integrity.
  • Confidence. Moderate for family-level lipid transport. Low for any specific role or essentiality of PP_4704 itself.

7. Open questions and suggested experiments

  1. Find the PAO1 ortholog of PP_4704 and check its essentiality and phenotype in the Sposato 2024 dataset. Check KT2440 RB-TnSeq fitness data (Fitness Browser) for PP_4704.
  2. Delete PP_4704 alone, and together with the KT2440 orthologs of yhdP, tamB and ydbH. Test sensitivity to SDS/EDTA and vancomycin, NPN uptake, outer-membrane vesicle release and outer-membrane phospholipid content.
  3. Predict the AlphaFold structure of the full-length protein to see whether the groove is continuous. Align the OmpA-like domain with OmpA and OprF to check the peptidoglycan-binding residues. Test peptidoglycan binding in vitro.
  4. Fractionate membranes or use fluorescent fusions to confirm the inner-membrane anchor and to look for polar localization.
