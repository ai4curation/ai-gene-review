why
The query contains AsmA-related and DUF748 annotations, with an OmpA-like C-terminal domain. Its 99.10%-identity STRING homolog belongs to COG2982, associated with outer membrane biogenesis. A 58.10%-identity P. aeruginosa homolog is discussed in studies of outer membrane integrity and intermembrane phospholipid transport. Query topology predicts one N-terminal inner membrane anchor; no substrate-specific fitness measurements or direct biochemical transport assays are provided.
Why this tier
Tier B: named family assignments converge with homolog studies supporting outer membrane biogenesis, but the supplied experimental descriptions establish physiological involvement rather than direct biochemical activity. Evidence includes homolog summaries, query and homolog domains, neighborhoods, STRING orthogroup membership, and query membrane topology; fitness and cofitness measurements are absent. The Fitness Browser hit supplies only a DUF748 annotation. A specific phospholipid transporter assignment exceeds the demonstrated molecular evidence, while an outer membrane beta-barrel assignment conflicts with the predicted inner membrane anchor.

why these terms
The annotation explicitly states the protein is an 'inner membrane protein' and is 'involved in outer membrane biogenesis'. The reasoning trace confirms that 'outer membrane biogenesis' is supported by homolog studies, but warns against assigning specific molecular functions like 'phospholipid transporter' or 'outer membrane beta-barrel' because the evidence is physiological rather than biochemical and, in the case of the beta-barrel, contradicts the inner membrane localization. For the BP, 'Gram-negative-bacterium-type cell outer membrane assembly' is the most appropriate specific term for outer membrane biogenesis in a bacterial context. For the CC, while 'inner membrane' is mentioned, GO does not have a generic 'inner membrane' term for bacteria; the cytoplasmic membrane (plasma membrane) is the inner membrane of the cell envelope. I will assign 'plasma membrane'.
