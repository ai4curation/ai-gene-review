Functional Annotation Report: flgK (Q88ES2, PP_4381) in Pseudomonas putida KT2440

Summary

flgK (UniProt Q88ES2; ordered locus PP_4381) of Pseudomonas putida strain KT2440 encodes Flagellar Hook-Associated Protein 1 (HAP1), a non-enzymatic structural adapter protein of the bacterial flagellum. FlgK is not an enzyme, transporter, or diffusible signaling molecule — it is a self-assembling axial building block. Its primary function is to form the proximal half of the hook–filament junction: the short, specialized zone that mechanically connects the flexible, curved flagellar hook (built from FlgE) to the long, rigid, helical filament (built from flagellin/FliC). Working in an obligate partnership with FlgL (HAP3, PP_4380, Q88ES3), FlgK bridges the mismatch in curvature and mechanical stiffness between hook and filament, and provides the assembly platform onto which the FliD (HAP2) filament cap and the flagellin subunits are subsequently added. Without FlgK, cells complete the hook but fail to build a filament and are therefore non-motile.

FlgK carries out its function extracytoplasmically, at the distal growing tip of the nascent flagellum, outside the cell body. It reaches that location by a dedicated route: it is exported in an unfolded state through the ~2 nm central channel of the flagellum by the flagellar type III secretion system (fT3SS), escorted by the cytoplasmic export chaperone FlgN, and it polymerizes at the tip only after the hook has reached its mature length. Its export and assembly are temporally gated — FlgK, FlgL, FliD, and FliC are secreted efficiently only after hook completion, reflecting the substrate-specificity switch of the export apparatus. Structurally, FlgK belongs to the flagellar axial-protein superfamily: it shares the flagellin-like architecture of a large elongated α-helical bundle core (the D0/D1 coiled-coil domains that form the tube wall) decorated with smaller, species-variable outer β-domains (D2-like lobes).

In P. putida KT2440 specifically, flgK sits within a single 59-gene flagellar cluster organized into 11 operons and governed by a three-tier transcriptional cascade topped by the master regulator FleQ, feeding through σ⁵⁴ (RpoN) and the flagellar sigma factor σ²⁸ (FliA). As a hook–filament junction gene, flgK is expressed in the late (class 3, σ²⁸/FliA-dependent) tier, co-regulated with the filament and cap genes. In the KT2440 genome it lies immediately adjacent to its junction partner flgL (PP_4380) and downstream of the rod-associated muramidase flgJ (PP_4382), placing it squarely in the basal-body/hook-filament assembly gene set. The flagellum that FlgK completes drives P. putida's swimming motility, surface adhesion, and biofilm/rhizosphere colonization, and the whole system is integrated with c-di-GMP signaling that toggles the bacterium between motile and sessile (adhesive/biofilm) lifestyles.

Key Findings

F001 — FlgK is Hook-Associated Protein 1 (HAP1), the first hook–filament junction protein

The identity of flgK as the gene encoding HAP1 is established through classical genetics and structural studies in the enteric model Salmonella. Three hook-associated proteins cap and extend the flagellar structure: HAP1 = FlgK, HAP3 = FlgL, and HAP2 = FliD. All three are essential for building a functional filament. In a flgK (HAP1) mutant, cells cannot assemble a filament; moreover, when hook protein (FlgE) is overproduced in a flgK background, cells produce abnormally long hooks — direct evidence that FlgK normally caps and terminates the hook, defining where the hook ends and the junction begins (PMID: 10482524). FlgK also belongs to the set of hook-associated proteins whose presence is genetically coupled to flagellin gene expression, underscoring its position as a checkpoint late in assembly (PMID: 1848842). Because Q88ES2 (PP_4381) carries the diagnostic FlgK family signature (IPR002371) and the full flagellar rod/hook domain architecture, this role transfers directly to the P. putida KT2440 protein.

"In a flgK (HAP1, first hook-filament junction protein) mutant or a flgL (HAP3, second hook-filament junction protein) mutant, the overproduction of FlgE also resulted in longer than normal hooks." (PMID: 10482524)

F002 — FlgK replaces the FlgD hook-cap at the hook tip and is required for filament nucleation

FlgK's assembly role is best understood as a cap-replacement event. During hook morphogenesis, the transient scaffolding protein FlgD caps the distal, growing end of the hook and is required for hook polymerization; immunoelectron microscopy localizes FlgD to the distal hook end. Once the hook reaches its mature length, the FlgD cap is discarded and FlgK is added to the hook tip as the first junction protein (PMID: 8157595). The obligatory nature of this hand-off is demonstrated by FlgE Dc-domain deletion mutants: these mutants still build hooks and still export FlgK, FlgL, and FliC, yet they fail to make a filament — precisely because the FlgD-to-FlgK replacement at the hook tip is blocked (PMID: 27493542). This shows that FlgK docking at the hook tip is the committed, non-bypassable step that nucleates filament construction.

"these deletions inhibited the replacement of the FlgD hook cap by FlgK at the hook tip, thereby abolishing filament formation" (PMID: 27493542)

"the hook, having reached its mature length, has the first of the hook-filament junction proteins, FlgK, added to its tip" (PMID: 8157595)

F003 — FlgK has a flagellin-like elongated α-helical bundle fold with accessory β-domains

The only crystal structure of a FlgK protein — from the pathogen Burkholderia pseudomallei, solved at 1.8 Å — reveals a multidomain fold: a large, elongated α-helical bundle core with two small β-domains protruding from it. This architecture is structurally homologous to flagellin, the filament subunit. The authors generalize that flagellar hook-associated proteins share a conserved elongated α-helical bundle scaffold (equivalent to flagellin's D0/D1 coiled-coil domains, which build the tube wall) coupled to a variable number of smaller outer domains (PMID: 25645451). This directly matches the domain annotation of Q88ES2 — Flag_bb_rod_N (IPR001444), Flg_bb/hook_C_dom (IPR010930), FlgK (IPR002371), and FlgK_D2-like (IPR049119) — confirming that the P. putida protein possesses the same conserved axial scaffold plus outer D2-like lobes.

"reveals a multidomain fold, comprising two small β-domains protruding from a large elongated α-helical bundle core. The evident structural similarity to flagellin ... suggests that ... flagellar hook-associated proteins are likely to show a conserved, elongated α-helical bundle scaffold coupled to a variable number of smaller domains." (PMID: 25645451)

F004 — FlgK is a late-class flagellar T3SS export substrate chaperoned by FlgN

FlgK does not fold and travel through the periplasm like a Sec substrate. Instead, it is secreted in an unfolded state through the flagellum's ~2–3 nm central channel by the flagellar type III secretion system and folds only upon reaching the growing distal tip. Its export is temporally ordered: FlgK, FlgL, FliD, and FliC are exported efficiently only after hook assembly is complete, because the export apparatus undergoes a substrate-specificity switch once the hook reaches mature length (PMID: 10478459). Efficient FlgK export additionally requires the dedicated cytoplasmic chaperone FlgN, which binds specifically to the hook-associated proteins FlgK and FlgL (PMID: 10320579). Loss of FlgN specifically reduces FlgK and FlgL export and causes premature leakage of unpolymerized flagellin, showing that FlgN-mediated FlgK delivery must precede and enable ordered filament polymerization (PMID: 10320579).

"the FlgK, FlgL, FliD, and FliC proteins are exported efficiently only after completion of hook assembly" (PMID: 10478459)

"FlgN binds to the flagellar hook-associated proteins (HAPs) FlgK and FlgL" … "export of FlgK and FlgL was specifically reduced, with concomitant increased release of unpolymerized flagellin" (PMID: 10320579)

F005 — flgK is a late (σ²⁸/FliA-dependent, class 3) flagellar gene; the flagellar system drives P. putida motility, adhesion, and biofilm

Transcriptionally, flgK sits at the late tier of the flagellar hierarchy. In Salmonella, the flgK promoter region contains only a class 3 promoter, dependent on the flagellum-specific sigma factor FliA (σ²⁸), whereas earlier genes carry class 2 promoters (PMID: 7770032). This places flgK among the last-expressed structural genes, co-regulated with filament and cap genes. The physiological importance of this late regulon in the target organism is demonstrated directly in P. putida KT2440: a non-polar fliA mutant is non-motile, shows decreased binding to corn seeds and abiotic surfaces, and forms ~3-fold less biofilm (PMID: 23766109). Since FlgK is an essential structural component of the FliA-dependent flagellar apparatus, these organism-level phenotypes — swimming motility, surface attachment, and colonization — are the biological processes to which FlgK contributes.

"the promoter region of fliD contains both class 2 and class 3 promoters, while that of flgK contains only a class 3 promoter" (PMID: 7770032)

"The mutant strain was nonmotile and exhibited decreased capacity to bind to corn seeds" … "its ability to form biofilms decreased by almost threefold" (PMID: 23766109)

F007 — In KT2440, flgK lies in a 59-gene flagellar cluster governed by a FleQ → σ⁵⁴/σ²⁸ three-tier cascade

The P. putida genome contains a single flagellar cluster of 59 genes organized into 11 operons with 22 characterized promoters. Flagellar/chemotaxis synthesis is controlled by a three-tier transcriptional cascade in which the master regulator fleQ is the Class I gene at the top (PMID: 34859548). Upstream regulatory/export-gate genes (flhA-flhF-fleN-fliA) form a σ⁵⁴ (RpoN)-dependent operon activated by FleQ and repressed by FleN, while FliA (σ²⁸) drives late gene expression. Critically, this regulatory network is integrated with the second messenger c-di-GMP: FleN, together with FleQ and c-di-GMP, differentially regulates lapA (a large adhesin) and the bcs cellulose-synthase operon (PMID: 30889223). As a hook–filament junction gene, flgK belongs to the late (σ²⁸/FliA-dependent, class 3) tier of this cascade, meaning its expression is coupled — via the same regulators — to the motile-versus-sessile lifestyle decision.

"A single region of the Pseudomonas putida genome, designated the flagellar cluster, includes 59 genes potentially involved in the biogenesis and function of the flagellar system." … "regulated by a three-tier cascade in which fleQ is a Class I gene, standing at the top of the transcriptional hierarchy" (PMID: 34859548)

"FleN, along with FleQ and the second messenger c-di-GMP differentially regulated transcription of lapA and the bcs operon, encoding a large adhesion protein and cellulose synthase" (PMID: 30889223)

F008 — FlgK (HAP1) with FlgL (HAP3) forms the mechanical hook–filament junction zone completing axial assembly

The flagellar axial structure (rods, hook, hook-associated proteins, cap, filament) shares a common subunit-arrangement architecture, but each segment has distinct mechanical properties tuned to its function — the rod is a rigid straight cylinder while the hook is a flexible curved tube (PMID: 31284631). Within this framework, HAP1 (FlgK) and HAP3 (FlgL) connect the filament to the hook and are required for the last stage of flagellar assembly (PMID: 9168617). FlgK is added first (proximal, on the hook side) and FlgL second (distal, on the filament side); together they bridge the curvature and stiffness mismatch between the flexible hook (which functions as a universal joint) and the rigid, propeller-like filament, and they provide the platform onto which the FliD (HAP2) cap and flagellin subunits assemble. The two proteins are typically encoded together in a conserved flgK operon; in Borrelia burgdorferi, for example, the flgK operon comprises flbF, flgK (HAP1), flgL (HAP3), and orfX (PMID: 9168617).

"the hook-associated proteins HAP1 and HAP3 connect the flagellar filament to the hook and are required for the last stage of flagellar assembly" … "The flgK operon consists of flbF (function unknown), flgK (encoding HAP1), flgL (encoding HAP3) and orfX" (PMID: 9168617)

"They share a common basic architecture of subunit arrangement, but each part shows quite distinct mechanical properties to achieve its specific function." (PMID: 31284631)

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

FlgK is best understood by tracing the flagellum from the cell body outward. The flagellum is built inside-out, subunit by subunit, with each axial protein exported through the central channel and added at the distal tip under a growing cap. FlgK occupies the transition point between two mechanically opposite structures.

   CELL EXTERIOR (distal, growing tip)
        │
   ┌────▼─────────────────────────────┐
   │  FILAMENT  (FliC / flagellin)     │  rigid, helical propeller
   │  + FliD (HAP2) cap at very tip    │

Assembly logic. The hook (FlgE) polymerizes under a transient FlgD cap until it reaches its genetically programmed mature length (~55 nm in Salmonella). At that point the export apparatus switches substrate specificity, the FlgD cap is jettisoned, and FlgK — the first junction protein — docks onto the hook tip (F002, F004). FlgK's proximal D0/D1 helical domains splice seamlessly into the 11-protofilament axial lattice shared by all axial proteins, while its expanded outer D2-like domains sit on the exterior surface (F003, F006). FlgL then adds on top of FlgK, and together the two form a short adapter zone that reconciles the flexible, curved hook with the rigid, straight, helically wound filament (F008). This junction is not merely a passive spacer: it transmits torque from the motor-driven rod/hook into the filament propeller while accommodating the dramatic change in mechanical properties (F001, F008). FlgK/FlgL also establish the correct surface lattice onto which the FliD (HAP2) cap can sit and chaperone flagellin folding at the tip.

Why FlgK is non-enzymatic and where it acts. FlgK has no catalytic residues, no substrate-binding pocket, and no transporter fold — it is a self-polymerizing structural subunit. Its "substrate," in the loosest sense, is itself and its neighbors: it recognizes the hook tip below and templates FlgL above. It performs its function entirely outside the cell, incorporated into the extracellular flagellar axial structure at its growing distal end (F006 subcellular localization: "Secreted," "Bacterial flagellum," "Cell projection").

Regulatory and lifestyle integration in KT2440. Expression of flgK is confined to the late, σ²⁸/FliA-dependent tier of a FleQ-topped, σ⁵⁴-relayed cascade (F005, F007). This ensures FlgK protein appears only when the basal body and hook are already built — a just-in-time logic that prevents wasteful or premature production of junction subunits. Because the same regulators (FleQ, FleN) also respond to c-di-GMP and control adhesin (lapA) and cellulose (bcs) genes, flagellar biogenesis — including flgK — is reciprocally coordinated with the switch to a sessile, biofilm lifestyle (F007). The functional payoff of a completed flagellum is demonstrated by the fliA mutant phenotype: loss of the late regulon abolishes motility and markedly reduces seed/surface binding and biofilm formation (F005). Thus FlgK, though a single structural rivet, is indispensable for the P. putida behaviors of swimming, root/surface colonization, and biofilm initiation.

Supporting / contextual literature. Additional papers reinforce the model: Caulobacter hook-gene organization confirms FlgK/HAP1 assignment by sequence similarity (PMID: 11405627); FliK/FlhB studies show flhB–flgK double mutants give polyhooks, linking FlgK to hook-length control (PMID: 8071222); solution-interaction studies of monomeric axial proteins illuminate HAP heterodimer polarity and the suppression of self-assembly in the cytoplasm (PMID: 12054831); Vibrio parahaemolyticus polar-flagellum genetics show HAP genes under σ²⁸ control with distinct sheathed-filament phenotypes (PMID: 7883718); FlgK is an immunodominant, epitope-mapped antigen in Salmonella (PMID: 29914677); and flgK loss reduces outer-membrane-vesicle production in E. coli, a downstream consequence of impaired flagellation (PMID: 24134841).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No direct experimental study of Q88ES2 itself. The functional assignment rests on strong homology to characterized FlgK proteins in Salmonella, Burkholderia, Caulobacter, Borrelia, and Vibrio, plus the KT2440 genomic context and the KT2440 fliA-mutant phenotype. There is no published P. putida flgK knockout, no purified-protein biochemistry, and no P. putida-specific structure. The mechanistic model is inferred, not directly demonstrated, for PP_4381.

  2. Structure is from a homolog. The 1.8 Å crystal structure is of B. pseudomallei FlgK (PMID: 25645451). P. putida FlgK is ~120 residues larger, with expanded outer domains (~res 420–640) that are unassigned to a single Pfam. The fold and surface features of these species-specific lobes remain unmodeled; whether they contribute additional functions (e.g., surface interactions, sheath contacts, rhizosphere recognition) is unknown.

  3. Stoichiometry and exact junction geometry uncharacterized in P. putida. The number of FlgK subunits in the junction and their precise contacts with FlgE (hook) below and FlgL above have not been resolved for a Pseudomonas flagellum.

  4. Regulatory tier assigned by orthology. The class 3 / σ²⁸-FliA assignment of flgK is anchored in Salmonella promoter mapping (PMID: 7770032) and the KT2440 cascade architecture (PMID: 34859548); the exact flgK promoter and its FliA dependence in KT2440 have not been individually mapped in the primary literature reviewed here.

  5. Polar vs. peritrichous context. Much mechanistic detail derives from peritrichous enterics. P. putida is polarly flagellated (multi-trichous polar tuft), and polar-flagellum systems (cf. Vibrio, PMID: 7883718) can show distinct HAP phenotypes; subtle Pseudomonas-specific features may not be captured by the enteric paradigm.

  6. AlphaFold modeling + comparison of full-length Q88ES2 against the Burkholderia FlgK structure to generate a testable model of the Pseudomonas-specific lobes, guiding the mutagenesis in item 5.
