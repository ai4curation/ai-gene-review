Functional Annotation Report: dacA / PP_4803 (UniProt Q88DM2)

Organism: Pseudomonas putida strain KT2440 (ATCC 47054 / DSM 6125 / NCIMB 11950), taxid 160488 Gene: dacA (ordered locus PP_4803) — EMBL AAN70372.1 Protein: serine-type D-Ala-D-Ala carboxypeptidase, EC 3.4.16.4 Family: Peptidase S11 (Pfam PF00768 + PF07943); penicilloyl-serine transferase / β-lactam-transpeptidase-like fold (InterPro IPR012338, IPR015956, IPR018044, IPR012907, IPR037167) Length: 386 aa (Sec signal peptide 1–24; mature chain 25–386)

1. Summary (answer to the research question)

dacA (PP_4803) encodes a low-molecular-weight penicillin-binding protein (LMW-PBP), specifically a serine-type D-alanyl-D-alanine carboxypeptidase (DD-carboxypeptidase; EC 3.4.16.4) and the P. putida ortholog of Escherichia coli PBP5. Its primary reaction is hydrolytic removal of the terminal D-alanine from the C-terminal D-Ala-D-Ala of muramyl-pentapeptide stems in peptidoglycan, converting pentapeptides to tetrapeptides. By depleting the pentapeptide donor substrate used by transpeptidases, it regulates (limits/tunes) the degree of peptidoglycan cross-linking, which is required for normal cell shape and for buffering the envelope against β-lactam stress. The enzyme acts in the periplasm on the peptidoglycan sacculus, tethered to the outer face of the inner (cytoplasmic) membrane by a C-terminal amphipathic anchor after Sec-dependent export. It functions in the peptidoglycan biosynthesis / cell-wall maturation pathway, and — like its Pseudomonas aeruginosa ortholog — its penicilloyl-serine fold additionally supports intrinsic β-lactam resistance.

Note on evidence base: No experimental study has been published on PP_4803 itself. The functional assignment is high-confidence and rests on (i) unambiguous sequence/domain identity, (ii) conservation of all catalytic motifs, and (iii) an extensive body of experimental work on the orthologous PBP5/DD-carboxypeptidase enzymes of E. coli and P. aeruginosa. Claims are labeled as direct database annotation, bioinformatic inference, or ortholog-based experimental evidence.

2. Identity verification

The gene symbol, EC number, organism, family, and domain architecture from UniProt are internally consistent and match the peptidase-S11 DD-carboxypeptidase (PBP5) group. This is not the ambiguous "dacA" of Firmicutes (the di-adenylate cyclase / c-di-AMP synthase, an unrelated protein); the peptidase-S11 fold, EC 3.4.16.4, and the "dacA/PBP5" nomenclature in Gram-negatives are distinct and correct here.

Bioinformatic confirmation (this work): the 386-aa sequence contains all three canonical penicillin-recognizing active-site motifs of the penicilloyl-serine transferase superfamily:

3. Key findings (with evidence)

3.1 Primary reaction and mechanism

  • Reaction: DD-carboxypeptidase — preferential cleavage of (Ac)₂-L-Lys-D-Ala-|-D-Ala; "removes C-terminal D-alanyl residues from sugar-peptide cell wall precursors" (UniProt catalytic activity / FUNCTION, EC 3.4.16.4). In peptidoglycan terms it removes the terminal D-Ala from the pentapeptide stem (L-Ala–D-Glu–meso-DAP–D-Ala–D-Ala), yielding a tetrapeptide.
  • Mechanism: a two-step acyl-enzyme (ping-pong) mechanism via the Ser64 nucleophile — the same chemistry that makes these enzymes covalent targets ("penicillin-binding proteins") of β-lactams.
  • Ortholog evidence: E. coli PBP5 "cleaves the terminal D-alanine from the muramyl pentapeptide in the peptidoglycan layer… In doing so, it varies the substrates for transpeptidation and plays a key role in maintaining cell shape" (Skoog et al., 21674665). In Gram-negatives, DD-CPases generate the majority tetrapeptide muropeptides (Tehrani et al., 40777447).

3.2 Substrate specificity

  • Specificity is centered on the terminal D-Ala-D-Ala dipeptide: "Beyond the C-terminal d-alanyl-d-alanine moiety, PBP5… has little specificity for other elements of peptidoglycan structure" (Nemmara et al., 27420403).
  • It belongs to the membrane-bound LMMA class of DD-peptidases, which "catalyze DD-carboxypeptidase and endopeptidase reactions in vivo" and — unlike the soluble R39/R61 enzymes — do not require a free stem-peptide N-terminus, consistent with acting on the polymeric sacculus in situ (Nemmara et al., 22029692).

3.3 Subcellular localization / site of action

  • Periplasm, anchored to the outer leaflet of the inner membrane. The Sec signal peptide exports the protein; the C-terminal amphipathic helix anchors it so the catalytic domain projects into the periplasm where peptidoglycan resides (the "periplasm… contains the peptidoglycan layer"; 40777447).
  • E. coli PBP5 was characterized "in its native environment, the inner membrane," where it forms homo-oligomers (likely dimers) that position the catalytic domain to reach pentapeptides at varying distances from the membrane (Skoog et al., 21674665). P. aeruginosa PBP5 was solubilized "by deletion of its C-terminal membrane anchor" (Smith et al., 23629710), confirming the same topology in Pseudomonas.
  • Direct sequence-based corroboration (this work): Kyte–Doolittle hydropathy analysis of Q88DM2 shows a hydrophobic N-terminal Sec signal peptide (residues 1–24, mean KD ≈ +1.27) and a C-terminal amphipathic segment (residues 367–386, …GGFFRRMWDSIRLFFYGLFN) — a basic Arg-Arg doublet (positive-inside) followed by an aromatic/aliphatic run — i.e., a monotopic amphipathic membrane anchor rather than a fully hydrophobic transmembrane helix. This is the diagnostic PBP5 topology and independently rules out a cytoplasmic or polytopic-transporter localization.

3.4 Biological process / pathway

  • Pathway: cell-wall biogenesis; peptidoglycan biosynthesis / maturation (UniProt PATHWAY). GO: serine-type D-Ala-D-Ala carboxypeptidase activity (GO:0009002); regulation of cell shape (GO:0008360); cell-wall organization (GO:0071555); peptidoglycan biosynthetic process (GO:0009252).
  • Mechanistically, by trimming pentapeptides to tetrapeptides the enzyme controls the pool of transpeptidation donor substrate, thereby setting the density/pattern of DD-cross-links. This is a regulatory/maturation role, not a wall-building (synthetic) one.

3.5 Physiological role (precise, not pleiotropic)

  • Cell-shape maintenance and intrinsic β-lactam resistance. In the E. coli ortholog, dacA/PBP5 deletion sensitizes cells 4–8-fold to all β-lactams tested, reversibly (Sarkar et al., 20047819), and the DD-carboxypeptidase catalytic activity is essential for this intrinsic resistance (Sarkar et al., 21719544).
  • Functional redundancy: bacteria encode several DD-CPases that act redundantly for robust cell-shape maintenance across conditions (Peters et al., 27329754); hence dacA is typically individually non-essential, with defects unmasked only when paralogs are also removed.

3.6 Pseudomonas-specific / evolutionary context

  • The closest structurally characterized ortholog, P. aeruginosa PBP5, is "one of the most abundant PBPs"; "its main function is that of a cell wall dd-carboxypeptidase, [but] it possesses sufficient β-lactamase activity to contribute to… resist… β-lactams," and its 2.05-Å structure "shows features closely resembling those of the class A β-lactamases" (Smith et al., 23629710). This documents (i) the DD-CPase/β-lactamase dual activity likely shared by P. putida DacA and (ii) the evolutionary relationship between DD-carboxypeptidases and class A β-lactamases.
  • Ecological relevance in P. putida: this soil bacterium actively edits its peptidoglycan with non-canonical D-amino acids (e.g., via its broad-spectrum racemase acting on plant-derived amino acids such as canavanine; Aliashkevich et al., 33830599). LMW-PBPs including PBP4/PBP5 can process peptidoglycan bearing non-canonical D-amino acids (Miyamoto et al., 32108264), placing DacA within P. putida's cell-wall chemical-editing physiology.

4. Supported and refuted hypotheses

Supported - H1: Q88DM2 is a serine DD-carboxypeptidase of peptidase family S11 (PBP5 ortholog) — supported by domain architecture and full catalytic-motif conservation. - H2: Its reaction is removal of the terminal D-Ala from muramyl-pentapeptide, regulating cross-linking — supported by UniProt catalytic activity + ortholog data. - H3: It localizes to the periplasm/inner-membrane and acts on the sacculus — supported by signal peptide, C-terminal amphipathic anchor, and ortholog membrane studies. - H4: It contributes to cell shape and intrinsic β-lactam resistance — supported by ortholog knockouts.

Refuted / rejected alternatives - Not the Firmicute dacA di-adenylate cyclase (c-di-AMP synthase): different fold, EC, and function — ruled out by family/domain identity. - Not a wall-synthesizing transpeptidase (HMW-PBP): it is a LMW-PBP carboxypeptidase that limits cross-linking substrate, not a primary polymerizing/cross-linking enzyme. - Not a soluble cytoplasmic enzyme: signal peptide + C-terminal membrane anchor place it in the periplasm.

5. Limitations and future directions

  • No direct experimental data on PP_4803; conclusions are inferred from strong orthologs (E. coli PBP5, P. aeruginosa PBP5) and bioinformatics. Direct biochemical kinetics (kcat/Km on pentapeptide and β-lactams), a crystal/cryo-EM or AlphaFold-validated structure, and a P. putida ΔdacA (single and multi-CPase) phenotype would confirm specificity, dual β-lactamase activity, and the precise contribution to morphology.
  • The quantitative relative contribution of DacA vs. other P. putida DD-CPases (redundancy structure) is unknown.
  • Whether DacA preferentially processes non-canonical D-amino-acid-substituted peptidoglycan in P. putida's soil/rhizosphere niche is an open, testable question.
