Gene Description

PP_0312 is the alpha subunit of a heterodimeric electron-transfer flavoprotein (ETF) encoded immediately downstream of dgcA and dgcB, the two subunits of the DgcAB dimethylglycine dehydrogenase system, and beside the beta-subunit gene PP_0313. It belongs to the ETF alpha-subunit/FixB family and carries the ETF alpha C-terminal domain that binds FAD. With PP_0313 it is thought to accept reducing equivalents generated during dimethylglycine conversion to sarcosine and pass them toward the respiratory chain. This assignment rests on the conserved dgcA-dgcB-ETF gene arrangement and on genetics of the orthologous locus in Chromohalobacter salexigens, not on biochemistry of the KT2440 proteins. KT2440 encodes a second ETF pair, PP_4201-PP_4202, from which PP_0312 is clearly divergent in sequence.

Core Functions

FAD-binding alpha subunit of the PP_0312/PP_0313 ETF heterodimer, inferred from conserved synteny and orthology to receive electrons from the DgcAB dimethylglycine dehydrogenase system, probably via the DgcB ferredoxin. The electron-transfer activity belongs to the heterodimer; its terminal acceptor is unresolved. The specific DgcB donor and respiratory acceptor are proposed partners; the cited primary work did not reconstitute their electron transfer.

Interpretation

The dgc-locus alpha subunits are 65.5% identical to each other but only about
33-37% identical to the canonical ETF alpha subunits of the same genomes and to
human ETFA, whereas the canonical bacterial alpha subunits are 58-62% identical
to human ETFA. The beta subunits show the same pattern (dgc-locus pair 60.2%
identical; about 26-34% to canonical ETF beta and human ETFB, against 59-61%
between canonical bacterial ETFB and human ETFB). The dgc-locus ETF is
therefore a distinct paralog group conserved between the two genera, not the
ortholog of the human acyl-CoA-dehydrogenase ETF. PANTHER places both groups in
the same subfamilies, so the subfamily call does not separate them.
Identity values are pairwise and are not a phylogenetic reconstruction.
