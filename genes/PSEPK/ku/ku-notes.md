## 2026-11-07 — Native repair boundaries and conserved-function reassessment

This entry supersedes broader mechanistic wording in earlier notes without deleting it.
All seven original rows, their order, qualifiers, evidence codes and original references

The English full text of Sharaev et al. is a separately acquired source (DOI
10.1134/S0026893322060152), corresponding to the Russian-edition PMID:36475478
(DOI 10.31857/S0026898422060180). Its quotations are cited to the additive English
file, not to the abstract-only canonical PMID cache. In native KT2440 it concludes
“LigD is dispensable for NHEJ for repair of DSBs generated by Cas9 in KT2440, while
the presence of Ku is required at some sites.” The strongest Ku dependence is at
mcb1; pyrF repair persists. LigD overexpression changes mcb1 mutation spectra.
Cas9 survivors, altered mutation spectra and direct protein chemistry are different
evidence types: not every survivor establishes an end-joining event.
[file:analysis/module_audits/bacterial_nonhomologous_end_joining/literature-acquisition-20261107/Sharaev2022-English.txt]

PMID:25942369 examines carbon-starvation mutagenesis in P. putida. Its available
canonical abstract does not identify the strain. An earlier uncached publisher
search snippet suggested a PaW85/KT2440 relationship, but no independent local
source preserves that bridge: it is withdrawn from the curated claims, not
silently treated as verified. Exact strain scope for this 2015 study remains
unresolved here. The abstract says “This indicates that LigD and Ku may participate
also in mutagenic pathways that are independent from each other.” This supports
P. putida physiological context, not purified catalysis or exact KT2440 identity.
The separately accessible 2022 study does identify KT2440 directly.

PMID:20018881 studies recombinant P. aeruginosa proteins. Its Ku end-protection
experiment uses Ku-(1–253), not full-length KT2440 Ku. Its POL dNTP primer-extension
and gap-filling assays must be distinguished from ribonucleotide addition. Neither
native KT2440 reaction rates nor metal preferences are established by these assays.
The conserved molecular functions remain reasonable target assignments, but are
explicitly inferred from target family/domain evidence and ortholog biochemistry.
The actual PAINT ancestral assertions are useful corroboration; neither target is
a leaf in the acquired tree, so no exact target node placement is manufactured.

Official GO definitions and is_a/part_of ancestor responses are frozen with retrieval
provenance in the review artifact directory. NHEJ is a descendant of DNA repair and
DNA damage response, but not DNA recombination. ATP binding is not an ancestor of
ATP-dependent DNA ligase activity. DNA end binding is a descendant of DNA binding,
but is not a descendant of double-stranded DNA binding. No ontology relationship is
inferred merely from label similarity.

Ku: generic DNA binding is refined to DNA end binding; dsDNA binding remains
accepted as complementary substrate specificity. Broad repair and damage-response
rows now refine to the already-supported NHEJ process rather than being called
non-core. The historical NEW end-binding proposal is retired in place because the
original generic row now supplies that assertion. The historical activator proposal
remains: target HAMAP MF_01875 states “Recruits and stimulates the ligase activity of
LigD.” PMID:20018881 reports “PaeKu stimulated ribonucleotide addition to DSB ends by
PaePOL and DSB end sealing by LigD.” This is conserved activation inferred for
Q88HU8, not a native KT2440 activation assay. No new location claim is added.
