why
The 99.6%-identical P. putida homolog is curated as an adhesin-associated sorting protein, supported by conserved adhesin cofitness across several species. InterPro identifies DUF1329; STRING assigns a lipoprotein-sorting orthogroup and links PP_0766 to PP_0765 (score 955). Mutants show strong fitness defects on Tween 20 and butyl stearate. Query topology predicts a cleavable signal peptide without membrane-spanning segments, supporting an exported protein. Sorting remains putative because direct biochemical validation is absent.
Why this tier
Tier A is supported by condition-specific fitness defects and convergent curated homolog, domain, and association evidence. The strongest functional assignment comes from the 99.6%-identical curated homolog and matching reannotations at 50.7–62.5% identity; literature expression and proteomics observations provide no direct molecular characterization. A Tween-hydrolyzing enzyme assignment is unsupported by reaction evidence, and an integral membrane protein assignment conflicts with query topology; the adhesin-sorting role is therefore retained with a putative qualifier.
GO terms
2 terms
why these terms
The protein is described as a 'Putative adhesin-associated sorting protein'. The trace confirms this role is retained. In bacterial contexts, sorting proteins (like Sortases) are involved in the transport and anchoring of surface proteins (adhesins). While 'protein targeting' or 'protein transport' are generic, 'protein targeting' (GO:0006605) describes the process of directing proteins to particular regions (like the cell surface), which fits 'sorting'. However, given the specificity 'adhesin-associated', the protein is linked to the process of cell adhesion (GO:0007155). I considered 'protein transport' but 'protein targeting' is a more precise description of 'sorting' proteins. Since it is 'putative' and no specific molecular function (like protease or ligase) is concluded in the trace (the Tween-hydrolyzing enzyme was rejected), I will assign the broad process of protein targeting and the associated biological process of cell adhesion.
