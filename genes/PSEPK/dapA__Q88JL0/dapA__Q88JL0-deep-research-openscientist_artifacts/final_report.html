Functional Annotation Report: dapA (Q88JL0) — 4-hydroxy-tetrahydrodipicolinate synthase in Pseudomonas putida KT2440

Target: dapA / dapA-II (OrderedLocusName PP_2639), UniProt Q88JL0 Organism: Pseudomonas putida (strain ATCC 47054 / DSM 6125 / KT2440) (PSEPK) Family: DapA family; Class I aldolase; (β/α)8 TIM-barrel (Aldolase_TIM IPR013785; DapA IPR005263; DHDPS PF00701; Schiff-base aldolase signature IPR020625) EC: 4.3.3.7 (formerly 4.2.1.52)

1. Summary (Answer to the Research Question)

dapA (Q88JL0) encodes 4-hydroxy-tetrahydrodipicolinate synthase (HTPA synthase), historically called dihydrodipicolinate synthase (DHDPS). Its primary function is to catalyze the first committed, rate-limiting step of the diaminopimelate (DAP) pathway to L-lysine: the aldol-type condensation of pyruvate with L-aspartate-4-semialdehyde (ASA) to give (2S,4S)-4-hydroxy-2,3,4,5-tetrahydrodipicolinate (HTPA) (Rhea RHEA:34171), a cyclic product that spontaneously dehydrates to dihydrodipicolinate. The enzyme is a soluble, cytoplasmic homotetramer (dimer-of-dimers) that operates through a Class I aldolase, ping-pong mechanism involving a Schiff-base intermediate on a conserved active-site lysine, and is subject to allosteric feedback inhibition by L-lysine. The pathway supplies both L-lysine (protein synthesis) and meso-diaminopimelate (peptidoglycan cross-linking). In P. putida the gene is a paralog ("dapA-II") of a small DHDPS isoenzyme family, paralleling the redundant DHDPS isoforms characterized in P. aeruginosa.

The gene symbol dapA is fully consistent with the UniProt annotation and with the domain architecture: - The protein description (4-hydroxy-tetrahydrodipicolinate synthase, EC 4.3.3.7, DapA family) matches the canonical DHDPS/HTPA synthase enzyme. - Domains PF00701 (DHDPS), IPR005263 (DapA), IPR002220 (DapA-like), IPR013785 (aldolase TIM-barrel) and IPR020625 (Schiff-base-form aldolase active site) are the defining features of DHDPS. - The synonym dapA-II (from EMBL AAN68247.1) indicates this is one of multiple dapA paralogs in P. putida KT2440 — a pattern experimentally documented in the related species P. aeruginosa.

No conflicting identity was found; literature on "DHDPS/DapA" corresponds directly to this protein family. Direct experimental characterization of the specific P. putida KT2440 protein Q88JL0 is limited, so its function is established by (i) strong sequence/domain homology to biochemically characterized bacterial DHDPS enzymes and (ii) direct studies in the closely related Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

3. Primary Function: The Reaction Catalyzed

Reaction (authoritative, UniProt/Rhea RHEA:34171 for Q88JL0 specifically): L-aspartate 4-semialdehyde + pyruvate → (2S,4S)-4-hydroxy-2,3,4,5-tetrahydrodipicolinate (HTPA) + H₂O + H⁺.

  • DapA "converts pyruvate and L-aspartate-4-semialdehyde to 4-hydroxy-2,3,4,5-tetrahydrodipicolinic acid" and "is involved in the production of lysine and precursor molecules for peptidoglycan synthesis" (Schmitz et al., 2020, 32356521).
  • It "catalyzes the first committed step in the lysine biosynthesis pathway" (Gupta et al., 2018, 29744651; Sridharan et al., 2014, 24996798), and this condensation is the rate-limiting step of the DAP pathway (Impey et al., 2020, 31330085).
  • Substrate specificity: the two substrates are pyruvate (donor, forming the Schiff base) and ASA (acceptor aldehyde). Reported Km values across bacterial orthologs are ~0.15–0.57 mM (pyruvate) and ~0.04–0.55 mM (ASA) (Laber et al., 1992, 1463470; Dogovski et al., 2013, 24349508). The enzyme is specific for the (S)-stereochemistry of ASA and for pyruvate as the ketoacid donor.
  • EC reclassification: originally annotated as a dehydratase (EC 4.2.1.52) on the assumption the product was dihydrodipicolinate; once HTPA was recognized as the true, direct enzymatic product (dehydration being a subsequent, largely spontaneous step), the enzyme was reclassified as a lyase, EC 4.3.3.7 (reflected in current annotations, e.g., Gupta et al., 29744651; Gupta et al., 2018 Vibrio, 29337198).

4. Catalytic Mechanism

DHDPS is a Class I (Schiff-base) aldolase operating by a ping-pong bi-bi mechanism: 1. Pyruvate binds first and forms a Schiff base (iminium) with the ε-amino group of the absolutely conserved active-site lysine (Lys161 in E. coli numbering), captured by the IPR020625 Schiff-base aldolase signature present in Q88JL0. 2. ASA then binds; aldol addition, transimination/cyclization and dehydration yield HTPA (Laber et al., 1992, 1463470).

Mutagenesis quantifies the lysine's role: E. coli K161A/K161R retain fold and substrate binding but lose ~280–750-fold in kcat (0.06–0.16 s⁻¹ vs 45 s⁻¹ wild type), with essentially unchanged Km and lysine Ki (Soares da Costa et al., 2010, 20353808). A conserved catalytic Tyr/Thr proton-relay, contributed across the tight dimer, completes the active site.

5. Structure, Oligomeric State, and Localization

  • Fold: each monomer is a (β/α)8 TIM-barrel (IPR013785) with a C-terminal α-helical domain.
  • Quaternary structure: a homotetramer ("dimer of dimers"); two monomers jointly form each active site at the tight-dimer interface, and dimers associate around a solvent-filled central cavity (Soares da Costa et al., 2018, 29204887; Reboul et al., 2012, 22685390). Bacterial DHDPS adopts a "head-to-head" tetramer (contrasting the "back-to-back" plant form).
  • Functional role of oligomerization: tetramerization rigidifies the active-site dynamics; disrupting the tetramer increases flexibility and lowers catalytic efficiency and substrate specificity (Reboul et al., 2012, 22685390; Atkinson et al., 2018, 29804823).
  • Localization: a soluble cytoplasmic enzyme of amino-acid/cell-envelope precursor metabolism — it acts in the cytosol, upstream of membrane-associated peptidoglycan assembly. (No signal peptide/membrane domain is present; consistent with all characterized DHDPS enzymes being cytosolic.)

Interpretation: the complete, conserved catalytic tetrad (Thr, Tyr, Tyr, Lys) plus the ASA-anchoring Arg confirm Q88JL0 is a catalytically competent HTPA synthase performing the canonical pyruvate + ASA condensation. However, the allosteric position 56 — where a His or Glu is required for strong lysine feedback inhibition (Soares da Costa et al., 2018, 29204887) — is a glutamine (Gln57) in Q88JL0. This predicts that the dapA-II isoform is only weakly, or not, lysine-feedback-inhibited, providing a concrete molecular rationale for the reported divergence of allosteric regulation among Pseudomonas DHDPS paralogs (Impey et al., 2020, 31330085).

6. Regulation

DHDPS is the principal feedback-regulated valve of the DAP/lysine pathway. The end-product L-lysine binds allosterically at the tight-dimer interface (~50 Å from the catalytic sites) to inhibit activity (Skovpen et al., 2016, 26836694). Sensitivity is governed largely by a single residue: strong allosteric inhibition requires a His or Glu at position 56 (E. coli numbering) rather than a basic residue; consequently many bacterial DHDPS enzymes are only weakly, or not, lysine-inhibited (Soares da Costa et al., 2018, 29204887; Vauterin et al., 2000, 10758475). In Pseudomonas, distinct DHDPS isoforms differ specifically in their allosteric lysine regulation (Impey et al., 2020, 31330085).

7. Pathway Context and Biological Role

DapA opens the diaminopimelate (DAP) pathway, which branches from aspartate metabolism (aspartate → aspartyl-phosphate → ASA). HTPA is reduced by DHDPR (dapB) and processed through subsequent Dap enzymes (e.g., DapD/DapC/DapE or alternative routes) to meso-2,6-diaminopimelate, which is (i) a direct cross-linking residue in Gram-negative peptidoglycan and (ii) decarboxylated (LysA) to L-lysine for protein synthesis (Fazius et al., 2013, 23504110; Schnell et al., 2012, 22359568). Because animals lack this pathway, DHDPS is a long-standing antibiotic/herbicide target — though target validation must account for isoform redundancy (below).

KEGG pathway assignment (this study): PP_2639 is mapped to KEGG ortholog K01714 (HTPA synthase, EC 4.3.3.7) and to pathway maps ppu00300 (Lysine biosynthesis) and ppu00261 (Monobactam biosynthesis), and to modules M00016 (succinyl-DAP pathway, aspartate ⇒ lysine) and M00527 (DAP-aminotransferase pathway, aspartate ⇒ lysine) — i.e. the enzyme feeds both DAP-pathway variants used by P. putida.

Genomic context (this study): dapA-II (PP_2639; genome coords 3,023,319–3,024,185, 867 bp) is not embedded in a canonical dap/lysine operon. It lies on the same strand immediately (338 bp) downstream of a cellulose-synthase (bcs) operon (PP_2634–PP_2638: cellulose synthase catalytic/translocator subunits, endo-1,4-D-glucanase, cellulose-synthase operon C protein), with a convergent GNAT acetyltransferase (PP_2640), an Fe–S protein (PP_2641) and a GntR-family regulator (PP_2642) downstream. This atypical location (versus the scattered classical dap genes, and versus dapA-I/PP_1237 at a separate locus) raises the hypothesis that dapA-II expression may be linked to the exopolysaccharide/biofilm-matrix context, though co-transcription with the bcs operon is not established.

8. Pseudomonas-Specific Considerations (dapA-II)

Unlike most bacteria, which carry a single dapA, Pseudomonas encode multiple functional DHDPS isoforms: - P. aeruginosa encodes two functional DHDPS enzymes (PaDHDPS1, PaDHDPS2) with similar catalytic activity but different allosteric regulation (Impey et al., 2020, 31330085). - Deletion of the primary dapA in P. aeruginosa is viable (including growth in a mouse lung infection model) because homologues (e.g., PA0223, PA4188) substitute for DapA (Schnell et al., 2012, 22359568).

The UniProt synonym dapA-II for PP_2639 explicitly marks Q88JL0 as a second dapA paralog in P. putida KT2440, consistent with this multi-isoform arrangement. Functionally it performs the canonical HTPA-synthase reaction; regulatory sensitivity and flux contribution may be partitioned across the paralogs, and loss of a single paralog can be buffered by the others.

10. Limitations and Future Directions

  • No direct biochemical study of the specific protein Q88JL0 was found; function is inferred from (i) strong homology and conserved catalytic residues verified here by sequence alignment, and (ii) experimental work on orthologs/paralogs (notably P. aeruginosa and E. coli). Confirmation would benefit from recombinant P. putida dapA-II kinetics, lysine-inhibition assays, and a crystal structure.
  • The predicted lysine-insensitivity of Q88JL0 (Gln57 at the allosteric site, versus His/Glu in feedback-sensitive DHDPS) is a bioinformatic inference that should be tested by direct lysine-inhibition kinetics. The division of labor between P. putida DHDPS paralogs (dapA-I vs dapA-II) also remains to be characterized experimentally.
  • Whether both P. putida paralogs are simultaneously essential, or mutually redundant as in P. aeruginosa, should be tested by single and double knockouts.
