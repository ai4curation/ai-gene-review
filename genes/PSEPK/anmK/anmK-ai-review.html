Gene Description

AnmK is the ATP-dependent anhydro-N-acetylmuramic acid kinase of peptidoglycan recycling. It opens the 1,6-anhydro ring while phosphorylating anhMurNAc to MurNAc-6-phosphate, which enters the Pseudomonas anabolic recycling route to UDP-MurNAc.

Proposed New Ontology Terms

1,6-anhydro-N-acetylmuramic acid kinase activity

Definition: Catalysis of the ATP-dependent phosphorylation and ring opening of 1,6-anhydro-N-acetyl-beta-muramate to form N-acetyl-D-muramate 6-phosphate, ADP, and a proton.

Justification: EC 2.7.1.170 and Rhea 24952 define a substrate-specific reaction that is currently represented only by a broad phosphotransferase term in GOA.

Parent term: phosphotransferase activity, alcohol group as acceptor

anmK curation notes

  • The reviewed Q88QQ4 record defines the EC 2.7.1.170/Rhea 24952 reaction and
    reports a target-strain fosfomycin-sensitive deletion phenotype
    [file:PSEPK/anmK/anmK-uniprot.txt, "Cells lacking this gene accumulate
    anhMurNAc and are fosfomycin sensitive"].
  • The full-text P. putida pathway paper places AnmK upstream of the
    MupP-AmgK-MurU shortcut [PMID:28351914, "the anhMurNAc kinase (AnmK) that
    phosphorylates anhMurNAc, yielding MurNAc 6P, is conserved"].
  • The existing GO molecular-function rows are broad. The exact substrate and
    ring-opening chemistry are retained in prose and proposed as a new term;
    GO:0016773 remains the best available parent and core molecular function.
  • PMID:23831760 is the target-species source cited by reviewed UniProt for the
    AnmK function, pathway, and deletion phenotype; its cached abstract does not
    expose the AnmK-specific result.
