    Proposed ontology changes

    A review can conclude that a GO (or UBERON, CL, CHEBI …) term is itself wrong, not merely that an annotation used the wrong term. proposed_ontology_changes is where that goes.

    What it is for, and what it is not

    Three things in this repository look adjacent and are not interchangeable:

    You want to say Use
    this term should not exist, or should say something different proposed_ontology_changes
    a term that does not exist should be created proposed_new_terms
    an annotation record is wrong (wrong gene, wrong term, wrong evidence code) review.source_defects

    The first two are requests to an ontology; the third is a request to whoever curated an annotation. The distinction that matters most in practice: obsoleting a term does not repoint the annotations that use it. A review that finds both problems records both, and they go to different trackers.

    proposed_new_terms is deliberately left alone — it covers creation, it is used in several hundred reviews, and a ProposedOntologyTerm is a KGCL ClassCreation in disguise that could fold in later if anyone wants it to.

    Where it lives

    Three homes, in increasing scope:

    • a gene review (genes/*/*/*-ai-review.yaml), top level — when the change was noticed while curating one gene;
    • a module review (modules/*.yaml), top level — subtree-level obsoletions are usually noticed while curating a pathway rather than a single gene;
    • a project page's frontmatter (projects/**/*.md) — when the change spans several terms and MOD groups and so belongs to no single gene or module. The projects/*_OBSOLETION.md pages are exactly that case.

    A gene or module review is schema-validated, so linkml-validate checks the block's structure. A project page is markdown and LinkML never sees it, so the structure is checked by validate_change_entries, which reads the required fields, the allowed fields and the permissible change_type values out of the schema file rather than restating them. tests/test_project_frontmatter.py applies it to every project page, and render-projects reports what it rejects as page warnings.

    Structural errors exclude; policy violations do not

    Two severities, and the distinction is deliberate. A structural problem — a missing required field, an unknown field, a change_type outside the enum, a term field with no id — makes the entry unusable: the renderer drops it, the exporter will not ship it, and the frontmatter test fails. A policy violation is advisory: the entry still renders and still exports, and it shows up as a page warning and in check_ontology_change_policy at WARNING severity, exactly as on a gene or module review.

    Getting this wrong is what the first version of the project-page gate did: it excluded an otherwise fine entry because its report_status: FIXED carried no tracker link. A missing link is worth flagging and is not grounds for dropping the record.

    Who wants the change: the distinction everything else hangs off

    origin is required, and it is the first thing to get right. Two cases that look identical in every other field carry completely different work:

    SELF_PROPOSED UPSTREAM_PROPOSED / UPSTREAM_COMPLETED
    What the record is an outbound request us tracking somebody else's plan
    Next action file a ticket, chase it migrate our own affected content
    Load-bearing field justification (the argument) affected_annotations (the blast radius)
    Lifecycle slot request_status adoption_status
    Needs confidence yes, it grades our argument no — their decision is not ours to grade
    Takes a stance no, proposing it is the position yes
    How it goes wrong they never act we never notice it landed
    Appears in <ONT>-requests.md <ONT>-inbound.md

    The first version of this schema had no origin, and the consequence was not hypothetical: all 15 records committed under it were reactive, every one was marked with a report_status whose documented meaning is "the source has corrected the record" — i.e. acted on our report — and every one linked GO's own ticket as though we had filed it. The report called them "requests for GO." Nothing in those 15 rows was a request. just export-ontology-changes now reports 0 open requests and 8 inbound changes to adopt.

    stance exists so that disagreeing with an upstream change is expressible at all. Two live objections in this repository could not previously be recorded: the vesicle page's warning that GO:0099023 is a cellular-component term and must not be reused as the molecular-function replacement, and the amino-acid page's note that batch membership must be confirmed before any in-repo edits. Those are DISAGREE and NEEDS_CONFIRMATION. An objection has to be argued — thin_objection fires on a DISAGREE or PARTIALLY_AGREE carrying fewer than 20 words, because asserting disagreement is not raising it.

    ontology_state_verified records when a curator last looked up the term's state, as distinct from when somebody wrote down a plan. Both backfilled projects had audits claiming a term was live after it had been retired, so an undated status is not evidence of anything.

    The failure this is really for

    unmigrated_landed_change fires when a change has landed and its affected_annotations are still NOT_STARTED or IN_PROGRESS. That is the failure that actually happens: the ontology moves, and the repository keeps citing the retired term. It fires once on AMINO_ACID_ACTIVATION_OBSOLETION today, on the GO:0070143 record, naming three paths: genes/DANRE/gtpbp3, genes/human/AARS2 and modules/mitochondrial_trna_charging_and_initiator_formylation.yaml. Of those, the module is what is genuinely outstanding — it still binds the obsolete term in a processes: slot, and its evidence statement now contradicts AARS2's own review.

    It fired three times, over nine files, when the field was first added. Two of those firings were wrong in the other direction: the affected reviews had already been migrated and the record still said NOT_STARTED. So the check found real unmigrated content and stale bookkeeping, and the count only became honest once the statuses were audited against the files. Read a falling count as either.

    It is KGCL, wrapped

    change_type names a class from KGCL (Knowledge Graph Change Language), and each permissible value carries that class's URI as its meaning. The structured slots carry the KGCL class's own fields, so a proposal can be emitted as KGCL and applied with oaklib apply. kgcl_schema is already an indirect dependency via oaklib, so this costs nothing extra.

    What KGCL does not model is the argument for a change, or which direction the change is travelling in. justification, supported_by, confidence, origin, stance, request_status and adoption_status are this repository's additions, and they are the reason to record a proposal here rather than only in a tracker. confidence does reuse SourceDefectConfidenceEnum, because grading our own case means the same thing in both places. The lifecycle does not reuse the source-defect enum: request_status and adoption_status are separate enums, because a request made of an ontology and the migration it creates for us are two different pieces of state that move independently, and the single report_status this started with could not say which one it meant.

    The DSL is checked by KGCL's own parser

    change_dsl holds the change in KGCL's controlled English (obsolete GO:0099022 with replacement GO:7770062). It is redundant with the structured slots on purpose, so that a disagreement between the two is detectable rather than silent. Validation parses it with KGCL's own lark grammar and reports three things: a statement that does not parse (dsl_unparseable), one that names a different term from the slots (dsl_disagrees), and one that parses as a different KGCL class from the declared change_type (dsl_wrong_kind).

    Leaning on the real grammar rather than a hand-written pattern matters, because the grammar is narrower than it looks. KGCL's DSL has no statement for a merge, a mapping predicate change, or a mapping removal, even though it has classes for the first of those. For those change types leave change_dsl unset; the structured slots and the justification carry the request. kgcl_dsl() renders a statement only when KGCL's parser accepts it, so the exporter never emits a string that looks like KGCL and is not.

    Other grammar details worth knowing before hand-writing a change_dsl:

    • a definition change is change definition of X to '…' — there is no from clause;
    • there is no syntax for a non-direct replacement (consider), so a correct DSL for NODE_OBSOLETION_WITH_NO_DIRECT_REPLACEMENT is a bare obsolete GO:… even when has_nondirect_replacement lists alternatives.

    Direct versus non-direct replacement

    This is the distinction to get right, because it decides whether anything can be fixed mechanically.

    • has_direct_replacement becomes GO's term replaced by. It says the two terms denote the same thing, so annotations can be repointed without re-curation.
    • has_nondirect_replacement becomes consider. It is advice; a curator must choose per annotation.

    A biological process obsoleted in favour of a molecular function is never a direct replacement, however obviously the MF is "the" answer — the five vesicle tethering BPs all point at GO:7770062 vesicle membrane tethering activity through consider, and GO records no term replaced by for any of them.

    Example

    From modules/dmel_exocyst.yaml:

    proposed_ontology_changes:
- change_type: NODE_OBSOLETION_WITH_NO_DIRECT_REPLACEMENT
  ontology: GO
  about_node:
    id: GO:0090522
    label: vesicle tethering involved in exocytosis
  has_nondirect_replacement:
  - id: GO:7770062
    label: vesicle membrane tethering activity
  change_dsl: obsolete GO:0090522
  justification: >-
    Tethering is a binding activity, not a process … recorded as a non-direct
    replacement because a biological process cannot be replaced outright by a
    molecular function.
  origin: UPSTREAM_COMPLETED
  stance: AGREE
  ontology_state_verified: '2026-10-31'
  adoption_status: NOT_APPLICABLE
  upstream_tracker_issue: https://github.com/geneontology/go-annotation/issues/6375

    Read the origin/stance/adoption_status trio together: GO has already made this change, we agree with it, and nothing in this repository cites the obsolete term, so there is no migration to do. An outbound request looks different — origin: SELF_PROPOSED with a request_status and no adoption_status, because there is nothing to adopt until the ontology acts. confidence belongs on an outbound request, where it grades our own case; a landed upstream change needs ontology_state_verified instead, which is a date we checked rather than a judgement.

    Reporting

    just export-ontology-changes

    Writes reports/ontology-changes.tsv plus two Markdown reports per ontology in reports/ontology-changes/:

    • <ONT>-requests.md — the outbound document, holding only SELF_PROPOSED changes with an open request_status. This is what gets sent upstream, so an upstream-originated change must not appear in it: that would hand the ontology's own decision back to them as our request.
    • <ONT>-inbound.md — the worklist for curators here, holding changes the ontology originated (or ours that it accepted) whose adoption_status is not yet COMPLETE or NOT_APPLICABLE. Nothing here is owed upstream; the work is ours.

    Grouping by ontology rather than lumping everything together matters because each ontology has its own tracker. Both paths are gitignored, for the same reason the source-defect report is: they aggregate every review, so committing them makes every curation PR conflict.

    A row is never in both reports — test_the_two_reports_partition_the_work pins that — and a settled request or a fully adopted change drops out of both while staying in the TSV as a record.

    What is checked

    check_ontology_change_policy warns (never blocks) on:

    • an obsoletion-with-replacement or a merge that names no surviving term, and a non-direct obsoletion that names one anyway;
    • a value change (definition, label, synonym, mapping predicate) missing its old or new value, or proposing a value identical to the current one;
    • an edge change missing the other end of the edge or the relation — reparenting a term and adding a part_of link to it are different requests;
    • a term offered as its own replacement;
    • a COMPLEX_CHANGE or OTHER with a justification under 20 words: those carry no KGCL semantics, so the prose is the whole request. KGCL has NodeDirectMerge but no split, so a class split is a COMPLEX_CHANGE whose justification must name the terms that should replace it;
    • a request_status saying the request was filed with no tracker issue linked;
    • a change_dsl that does not parse, disagrees with the slots, or parses as the wrong KGCL class; and one on a change type KGCL's grammar has no statement for (NODE_DIRECT_MERGE, REMOVE_MAPPING, MAPPING_PREDICATE_CHANGE, COMPLEX_CHANGE, OTHER), where a string that parses must be some other kind. Note the distinction: KGCL has classes for four of those five — the enum binds them as kgcl:NodeDirectMerge, kgcl:RemoveMapping, kgcl:MappingPredicateChange and kgcl:ComplexChange — but kgcl.lark gives no controlled-English form for any of them, which is why kgcl_dsl() renders nothing rather than a string that looks like KGCL and does not parse;
    • a reparenting change (NODE_MOVE, NODE_DEEPENING, NODE_SHALLOWING), or a REMOVE_MAPPING, whose old_value is missing or is not an identifier. That one slot carries three different things — the current parent's CURIE for the reparenting changes, the withdrawn mapping's source for REMOVE_MAPPING, and definition, label, synonym or predicate text for the value changes — so its string range cannot tell them apart. A mapping removal without it exports an upstream request naming no target;
    • an adoption_status: NOT_APPLICABLE that nonetheless lists affected_annotations: the value means the change touches no content here, so a file list contradicts it;
    • a term proposed for obsoletion that the same review ACCEPTs on an annotation row — ACCEPT means the term is a core function of the gene, which contradicts asking for its obsoletion. MODIFY with the replacement is the consistent pair.

    What is not checked: whether the ontology should actually make the change, which no rule can settle, and whether about_node exists and carries the stated label, which is the term validator's job.

    Two shapes, from the two projects backfilled so far

    The direct/non-direct distinction is the thing to get right, and the two cases already recorded show both answers to it.

    VESICLE_TETHERING_OBSOLETION — no direct replacement. Five BPs obsoleted in favour of one MF, GO:7770062 vesicle membrane tethering activity. GO records no replaced_by for any of them: a biological process cannot be replaced outright by a molecular function, so the MF is consider and a curator picks the transport process to hang it on. All five are NODE_OBSOLETION_WITH_NO_DIRECT_REPLACEMENT with one has_nondirect_replacement.

    AMINO_ACID_ACTIVATION_OBSOLETION — a direct replacement and advice. 43 substrate-specific tRNA aminoacylation BPs, each obsoleted with a formal replaced_by pointing at its block's generic process (GO:0006418 cytosolic, GO:0070127 mitochondrial) — a BP replaced by a BP, which is legitimate — while GO's obsoletion comment separately names the 1:1 molecular function to annotate instead. So each entry carries has_direct_replacement (the BP) and a has_nondirect_replacement (the MF). Both, on one entry.

    The rule that survives both: what can be repointed mechanically goes in has_direct_replacement; what needs a curator's judgement per annotation goes in has_nondirect_replacement. Whether the replacement happens to be in the same GO aspect is a consequence, not the test.

    What is still open

    A large uniform batch should be generated, not transcribed. Only four of the 43 amino-acid-activation terms are recorded structurally — two cytosolic, two mitochondrial, enough to pin both shapes. The other 39 are mechanically derivable from the markdown tables already on that page, and hand-writing them would duplicate those tables in a second format with no new information. A generator that reads a project's own tables is the right answer; nobody has written one.

    A mapping removal goes to the wrong recipient. REMOVE_MAPPING on an InterPro2GO or UniRule mapping is grouped under ontology: GO, because GO owns the term the mapping points at — but the mapping itself lives in InterPro2GO or UniRule, and that is who would remove it. Five such records are currently filed under GO. SourceDefect has a resource slot for exactly this and ProposedOntologyChange does not; adding one would let the exporter route a mapping removal to the body that maintains the mapping.

    Both pages' audits were stale, and in the same direction. Vesicle tethering recorded two of five terms obsolete as of 2026-09-26; all five are. Amino acid activation recorded all 43 live as of 2026-08-29; the four rechecked are obsolete. In both cases the ontology moved and the page did not. Nothing here detects that: origin and request_status are a curator's assertions, and no check compares either against the live ontology. ontology_state_verified only records the date someone looked, so it ages without ever contradicting itself. A periodic audit that re-queries each about_node and flags a change still recorded as SELF_PROPOSED, or as NOT_FILED, whose term is already obsolete would close the loop, and would have caught both.
