    Receipts for rule reviews

    A rule review ends in a verdict on somebody else's annotation rule. The verdict is only as good as the facts behind it, so every claim in a review must come with a receipt: a fact, in a field a program can test, that names its source. The idea is from Show me the receipts: do not ask the reviewer for a verdict, ask for a verdict plus the receipts, and check the receipts.

    This page says what counts as a receipt in a rule review, which checks run, and why. It exists because an audit of the 78 ARBA reviews in November 2026 found that about 45 of them reached a verdict their own evidence did not support. Nearly all of the errors were detectable from the file.

    The "limit of 12 condition sets" is max_condition_sets=12 in src/ai_gene_review/etl/rule_analysis.py, the number of sets the overlap analysis reads by default. It says nothing about whether a rule is right.

    Two questions, both with receipts

    A rule review answers two questions, and a verdict needs both.

    1. Accuracy. Is the annotation true for the proteins each condition set matches? This is what most of this page is about.
    2. Meaning and parsimony. Is the rule a useful biological statement, made with conditions that are necessary and sufficient? A rule can be accurate set by set and still say nothing: 343 families under one umbrella term, each of which curators annotate with a more specific descendant.

    The audit found the first question answered from memory. The corrections that followed answered it from evidence and dropped the second question, which left reviews that test a rule the way one tests a classifier and say nothing about its biology. Both are required.

    The second question is answered in three fields:

    • biological_units: the families, complexes or pathways the condition sets resolve into. Each unit lists its sets, example proteins, and the most informative GO term that curated annotation puts on its members, with the annotation (accession, GO id, evidence code, reference) in evidence, and term_relation saying how that term relates to the rule's consequent.
    • biological_meaning.assessment: COHERENT (the consequent is the most informative term true of all the units), TRUE_BUT_UNINFORMATIVE (units carry more specific descendants), HETEROGENEOUS (the best term of some units lies outside the consequent), or NOT_ASSESSED with the reason.
    • parsimony, go_specificity, taxonomic_scope: the rule-level judgements, with numbers. The receipts are projects/ARBA_REVIEW/rule_redundancy.tsv (how many descendants the consequent has, and how many of the rule's sets are already matched by a rule asserting a more specific term, so emit nothing), per-set match counts, and rules/arba/_interpro2go.txt (whether one InterPro family already maps to the consequent).

    Size and generality are not evidence that an annotation is false. They are what the second question is about. "343 sets is too many" is not a finding; "320 of 355 sets are covered by rules for more specific terms" is, and so is "each of these six families is curated to a descendant of the consequent". A taxon restriction that follows where the training annotations happen to be (Primates, because the support is human Reactome TAS) rather than where the biology is, is narrow coverage; say so in taxonomic_scope, and do not call the annotation wrong.

    What a receipt is

    Claim Receipt
    "The rule has these conditions" rule.condition_sets copied from the fetched rule, with rule.retrieved_date and rule.source_condition_set_count
    "IPR000999 is X" the official InterPro name as the condition's label; in prose, IPR000999 (Ribonuclease III domain)
    "GO:nnnnnnn means X" / "is more specific than Y" the GO label, and the parent that makes it a descendant
    "This protein is wrongly annotated" a UniProt accession in annotation_assessments[].incorrect_examples, and no experimental annotation to the term or a descendant
    "This set is right" accessions in correct_examples, each with the GO id, evidence code and reference in notes
    "N proteins match" the UniProtKB query, the count and the release
    "The term is too general for this family" a biological_units entry: the family's curated annotation to a descendant of the consequent
    "These proteins match the conditions and should not carry the term, though the rule does not annotate them" accessions in would_match_examples (typically reviewed entries). They show what the rule does to unreviewed relatives and are weaker than incorrect_examples, which lists proteins that carry the rule's annotation
    "N of M sets are covered" the row of projects/ARBA_REVIEW/rule_redundancy.tsv (GO rules) or keyword_redundancy.tsv (keyword rules); prose that names the file must quote its figures
    "These sets do no work" the set numbers with a match count of 0, or sets_covered_by_more_specific_rule from rule_redundancy.tsv
    "The literature says" a PMID or DOI, and a supporting_text that is verbatim in the cached paper

    A note the reviewer wrote, an AI deep-research file, and a file that does not exist are not receipts. Notes and research files are where leads come from; the receipt is the paper or database record the lead points to.

    Rules for verdicts

    • UNDECIDED is the verdict for a rule that was not examined. A rule with hundreds of condition sets that nobody read set by set is UNDECIDED, however general its term. Say which sets were sampled and what they showed.
    • ACCEPT covers the whole rule, on both questions. Every condition set served at retrieved_date is recorded and supported, biological_meaning is COHERENT, and parsimony is PARSIMONIOUS or ACCEPTABLE.
    • SPLIT is the verdict for an umbrella term. At least two biological_units, each with its own more informative term; suggested_modifications says which sets go to which term. A sample is enough to show this for a large rule: state the sample, and let confidence reflect it.
    • MODIFY names the change. A specific set, protein, term or taxon, in suggested_modifications.
    • DEPRECATE is for a rule that is still served and should go: a wrongly annotated protein (accessions in incorrect_examples), an obsolete consequent, or a rule that does no work because every condition set is also matched by a rule asserting a more specific term (sets_covered_by_more_specific_rule equal to the number of sets, as rule_redundancy.tsv gives it). The validator checks only that count. The judgement has a second half it cannot check: nothing in the rule is worth re-homing. A rule that emits nothing but has uncovered sets selecting families worth keeping under their own terms is SPLIT (ARBA00026480, ARBA00027624, ARBA00026572). This ground is for GO rules only: UniProt keeps the most specific ARBA GO term on a protein, but keywords from different rules stack and a parent keyword is not derived from a child, so a covered keyword set still does work and is not a parsimony defect. A true but general term whose sets are not all covered is SPLIT or MODIFY; low recall alone is not a defect.
    • WITHDRAWN records that UniProt no longer serves the rule (rule.withdrawn). It is a fact about the rule, not a verdict, and needs no confidence.
    • Read condition sets as conjunctions. A set is the proteins that match all its conditions, including the taxon. Judge that set, never one of its signatures. Alternative sets are expected to be disjoint.
    • A FunFam label is the name of one member, not the identity of every member. Check what the conjunction matches in UniProtKB.
    • Check the alleged false positive before writing it down. Look up the protein's experimental annotations to the term and its descendants. The rule was mined from such annotations, so a surprising family is more often a curated annotation the reviewer did not know than an error.
    • confidence is confidence in the verdict. Below 0.5, the verdict is UNDECIDED.
    • A wrong claim is withdrawn in corrections, with the evidence that refuted it, and removed from the prose.

    The checks

    just rules-validate runs the schema check and then src/ai_gene_review/validation/rule_review_receipts.py. Errors fail the file.

    Check Severity Fires when
    verdict_without_conditions error action is not UNDECIDED and rule.condition_sets is empty
    missing_rule_snapshot error (warning when UNDECIDED) no rule.retrieved_date, or a verdict with no rule.source_condition_set_count on a rule that is not withdrawn
    verdict_without_confidence error action is not UNDECIDED and no confidence is stated
    accept_on_partial_examination error ACCEPT with fewer sets recorded than source_condition_set_count
    condition_label_mismatch, go_label_mismatch error a recorded label differs from the official one in rules/_labels.json
    prose_identifier_label_mismatch error / warning prose writes ID (name) or name (ID) and the name shares no word with the official label
    condition_set_out_of_range error prose or a reference finding names a condition set number higher than the rule as recorded has (usually text left over from an earlier version of the rule)
    size_as_evidence error a review that faults the rule (DEPRECATE, MODIFY, SPLIT, MERGE) appeals to a limit, threshold or recommended maximum of condition sets
    unresolvable_reference error supported_by cites something that is not a PMID, DOI, database record, URL or file
    missing_cited_file error a cited file: does not exist
    own_notes_as_support warning supported_by cites notes or an AI research file
    literature_verdict_without_literature error literature_support is STRONG, MODERATE or CONTRADICTED and the block itself (its supported_by or notes) names no publication or curated pathway record
    verdict_without_meaning error a verdict (ACCEPT, MODIFY, SPLIT, MERGE, DEPRECATE) with no biological_meaning, or ACCEPT/SPLIT with NOT_ASSESSED
    accept_of_uninformative_rule error ACCEPT with biological_meaning TRUE_BUT_UNINFORMATIVE or HETEROGENEOUS
    accept_without_parsimony error ACCEPT with parsimony missing, or not PARSIMONIOUS/ACCEPTABLE
    split_without_units error SPLIT with fewer than two biological_units that carry a term
    uninformative_without_units, heterogeneous_without_units error the meaning assessment is not backed by units showing it
    unit_term_label_mismatch, unit_term_without_label, unit_term_without_evidence error a unit's term label differs from GO's, is missing, or the term has no curated annotation cited
    redundancy_receipt_mismatch error consequent_descendant_count or sets_covered_by_more_specific_rule differs from rule_redundancy.tsv
    redundancy_prose_mismatch error prose or a correction's evidence names rule_redundancy.tsv or keyword_redundancy.tsv with a figure (N of M sets, covered_sets N, condition_sets N, go_descendants N) the committed table does not contain
    redundancy_prose_unconfirmed warning prose or a correction says "N sets are covered (or matched) by rules", with or without "of M", without citing a redundancy table, and the figures differ from the table's (GO and keyword rules; sentences about a sample are skipped). The same disagreement is the error redundancy_prose_mismatch when the text or the correction's evidence cites the table. corrections[].withdrawn_claim is exempt by design: it quotes the claim that was wrong, so its figures are expected to differ and must not be "fixed". Bounded figures are checked in the direction they bound: "at most N", "no more than N" and "up to N" fail when the table's figure is above N, "at least N" and "no fewer than N" when it is below, "fewer than N" and "more than N" likewise with a strict bound; a total given with "of M" must still match. Approximate figures ("about N", "roughly N") are compared like exact ones
    correct_assessment_with_incorrect_examples error an annotation assessment is CORRECT and lists incorrect_examples
    sample_not_described warning sets_examined_for_meaning is below the rule's set count and the meaning notes do not say how the sets were chosen
    withdrawn_action_mismatch error WITHDRAWN without rule.withdrawn, or DEPRECATE on a withdrawn rule
    deprecate_without_counterexample error DEPRECATE with no incorrect_examples, no consequent_obsolete: true, and not every set covered by a more specific rule
    modify_without_modification error MODIFY, SPLIT or MERGE with no suggested_modifications
    low_confidence_verdict error a verdict other than UNDECIDED at confidence below 0.5
    example_not_an_accession error an example is not a UniProt accession

    just rules-validate --online <file> adds checks that need the network:

    Check Severity Fires when
    false_positive_has_experimental_annotation error an incorrect_examples accession carries the consequent, or a descendant, on experimental evidence in QuickGO
    unit_evidence_not_found error a biological_units[].evidence line of the form ACCESSION GO:id CODE reference names an annotation QuickGO does not hold (that accession, exactly that term, that evidence code and, when given, that reference)
    unit_term_relation_mismatch error a unit's term_relation to the rule's term is not borne out by the ontology
    stale_rule_snapshot, rule_withdrawn_upstream warning UniProt now serves a different number of condition sets, or no longer serves the rule
    withdrawn_but_served error rule.withdrawn is true but UniProt serves the rule
    consequent_not_obsolete error consequent_obsolete is true but QuickGO serves the term as current
    online_check_failed warning a lookup failed, so that check did not run; a clean --online result with this warning is not a pass

    Run --online before marking a review COMPLETE.

    A receipt field left out is not a pass. The checks that test a field also require it: a verdict must state its rule snapshot and its confidence, and the two grounds that excuse a DEPRECATE from naming a protein (withdrawn, consequent_obsolete) are both verified online. FunFam labels are compared with the cache but cannot be refreshed by --refresh-labels, which covers InterPro and GO only; update a renamed FunFam label in rules/_labels.json by hand, from CATH.

    The label checks compare against the committed cache rules/_labels.json. InterPro renames entries, so a label error can mean the cache is stale. Run uv run python -m ai_gene_review.validation.rule_review_receipts --refresh-labels <file>: it re-fetches only the identifiers whose recorded label disagrees with the cache. If the error remains, the review is wrong.

    What ACCEPT means

    In the corrected reviews, ACCEPT means every condition set was examined and its reviewed (Swiss-Prot) matches carry the consequent on evidence other than ARBA itself, usually with an experimental annotation in each set. Swiss-Prot entries can carry a term only as an ARBA IEA (GO_REF:0000117); those are the rule's own output and are excluded. This is still in-sample evidence: the rule was mined from Swiss-Prot, and the unreviewed entries it annotates were counted, not inspected. Read ACCEPT as "no conflict found", and keep the confidence modest.

    What the checks cannot see

    Deterministic checks test that a receipt exists and resolves. They do not test that it supports the claim it is attached to. A real PMID can be cited for something the paper does not say, and a real accession can be a poor example. Two things cover that gap:

    • Spot-checks by a second reader. The November 2026 audit was this: independent readers re-derived the facts each verdict depended on. Sample new reviews the same way, starting with DEPRECATE verdicts and anything a reviewer flags as unusual.
    • Corrections as test cases. Each corrections entry records a claim, its error_type and the refuting evidence. Count them by error_type to see which errors recur, and turn recurring ones into a check here or a line in the reviewer skill.

    Receipts can also be written after the fact to fit a verdict. That is why the online check looks for evidence against the review (experimental annotation on an alleged false positive) and not only for evidence the reviewer chose to cite.
