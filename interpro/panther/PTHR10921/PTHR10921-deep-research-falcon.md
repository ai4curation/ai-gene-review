# PANTHER Family PTHR10921 Research Report

## 1. Executive summary

PTHR10921/IPR033494 is best understood as the **NudE–NDE1–NDEL1 family of non-motor cytoplasmic-dynein regulators**. Fungi and most pre-vertebrate lineages generally have one ancestral NudE-like gene—called *nudE* in *Aspergillus*, *ro-11* in *Neurospora*, and *Ndl1* in budding yeast—whereas vertebrates possess the paralogues **NDE1** and **NDEL1**, probably produced by an early vertebrate duplication. Human NDE1 and NDEL1 are 56% identical and 69% similar, but PANTHER currently defines no subfamilies for this family. The evidence supports introducing at least three annotation groups: pre-vertebrate NudE, vertebrate NDE1, and vertebrate NDEL1. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 1-2, bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 2-5, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)

The ancestral function was almost certainly **scaffolding LIS1/NudF and cytoplasmic dynein to regulate microtubule-based nuclear positioning**. This remains the central function of both vertebrate paralogues, which additionally participate in spindle organization, organelle transport, Golgi positioning, neuronal migration, and cortical development. The family is not primarily enzymatic and requires no known catalytic cofactor or small-molecule substrate. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 2-5, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

Evolutionary divergence is predominantly **subfunctionalization**, not wholesale replacement of the ancestral activity. NDE1 is relatively specialized toward mitosis, neural-progenitor proliferation, interkinetic nuclear migration, and cortical growth; NDEL1 is more prominent in post-mitotic neurons, nucleokinesis, and neuronal migration. NDE1 also acquired an isoform-specific interaction with the 26S proteasome, while NDEL1 has reported regulated serine-oligopeptidase activity. The latter two properties are plausible neo-functionalizations but are not appropriate family-wide annotations. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 12-13, monda2018nde1promotesdiverse pages 1-2, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2)

No evidence establishes different EC numbers or opposite catalytic reactions among family members. Apparent differences in dynein regulation are concentration-, modification-, assay-, and context-dependent rather than evidence that NDE1 and NDEL1 intrinsically catalyze opposite reactions. In particular, NDE1 promotes LIS1-dependent dynein-complex assembly at low concentration but inhibits motility at very high concentration, illustrating a biphasic regulatory mechanism rather than an opposite biochemical activity. (zhao2023nde1promoteslis1mediated pages 1-2)

## 2. Ancestral function and molecular architecture

### Shared fold

The experimentally characterized NDEL1 N-terminus forms a long **parallel homodimeric coiled coil**, approximately residues 1–170, followed by a substantially flexible or disordered C-terminal regulatory region. NDE1 has the same overall organization, and the proteins can form homo- and heterodimers. This is a scaffold architecture, not an enzyme fold. The coiled coil brings LIS1- and dynein-binding surfaces into a functional assembly, while the C-terminal region folds back and regulates access through phosphorylation and other modifications. (zyłkiewicz2011thenterminalcoiledcoil pages 1-2, bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 12-13, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

### Probable ancestral activity

Fungal NudE/RO-11 mutants show defective hyphal growth and abnormal nuclear distribution, with nuclei failing to migrate normally into hyphae and instead accumulating close to the spore body. Together with conserved binding to the LIS1 orthologue NudF and dynein, this indicates that the last common family ancestor regulated **dynein-dependent nuclear migration or positioning**. Vertebrate cell-cycle, organelle-transport, and neuronal functions are elaborations of this ancient microtubule-motor regulatory role. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 1-2, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)

### Partners, substrates, and cofactors

Typical direct or functional partners include:

* **LIS1/PAFAH1B1**—the principal conserved binding partner recruited to dynein;
* **cytoplasmic dynein-1**, including intermediate-, heavy-, and light-chain contacts;
* **dynactin and cargo adaptors**, indirectly, during assembly of activated dynein complexes;
* **DISC1**, especially in vertebrate neural and psychiatric-disease contexts;
* cell-context-specific partners such as CENP-F, NuMA, Mad1, and, for a human NDE1 isoform, the **26S proteasome**. (sotoperez2020roleofnde1 pages 9-11, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7, monda2018nde1promotesdiverse pages 1-2, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 1-2)

These proteins do not use an established enzymatic cofactor. Their functional “substrates” are better described as protein assemblies—LIS1, dynein, dynactin, and cargo-adaptor complexes—rather than metabolites.

### Key functional residues and regions

The best mapped determinants are in NDEL1:

* **Residues 85–169** constitute a minimal LIS1-binding region; E116A and R127A abolish LIS1 binding. LIS1 H149 and S169 contribute to the reciprocal interface. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)
* The first approximately **200 NDEL1/NDE1 residues** bind the dynein intermediate chain; NDEL1 E48A impairs this interaction, and the corresponding binding site lies within the first 40 residues of the dynein intermediate chain. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)
* The disease-associated **NDEL1 R105P** substitution lies in the coiled-coil/LIS1-binding region and markedly reduces LIS1 binding, probably by introducing a local coiled-coil defect. (tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 15-16)
* C-terminal phosphorylation regulates scaffold accessibility. NDEL1 T219A/T245A and S251A mutants fail to interact normally with dynein in the reported assays; Aurora A-dependent phosphorylation at **S285** spatially regulates dynein transport near spindle poles. (zyłkiewicz2011thenterminalcoiledcoil pages 1-2, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 1-2)

There is no established family-wide catalytic residue set. Reported NDEL1 oligopeptidase activity should therefore not be inferred from the coiled-coil scaffold or transferred automatically to NDE1 or fungal NudE.

### A. Pre-vertebrate NudE-like proteins

Fungal NudE, RO-11, and related single-copy proteins retain the clearest ancestral phenotype: LIS1/NudF-associated regulation of dynein-dependent nuclear distribution. No EC number applies, and there is no sound basis for transferring vertebrate neural-development, proteasome, psychiatric-disease, or peptidase annotations into this group. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 1-2, bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 2-5, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)

### B. Vertebrate NDE1

NDE1 retains dynein/LIS1 scaffolding but is particularly important in proliferating neural progenitors, spindle function, kinetochore dynein recruitment, interkinetic nuclear migration, and cortical growth. NDE1 expression is highest early after fertilization and subsequently declines, whereas NDEL1 expression remains comparatively high and stable. NDE1 knockout mice survive but develop cortical abnormalities; human biallelic loss causes severe microcephaly-spectrum malformations. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4, bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 12-13)

A 2023 single-molecule reconstitution study provided a current mechanistic model. In mammalian dynein–dynactin–BicDR1 assays, 10 nM NDE1 alone had little effect and 1000 nM nearly abolished motility. LIS1 alone increased processive-run frequency up to 2.6-fold, whereas 1–10 nM NDE1 together with LIS1 increased run frequency by as much as **16-fold**. NDE1 recruits LIS1 to autoinhibited dynein and promotes active dynein–dynactin–adaptor assembly; after dynactin binds, NDE1 is displaced. Excess NDE1 inhibits because it competes with dynactin for the dynein intermediate chain. Zhao, Oten & Yildiz, *Nature Communications*, accepted 24 October 2023, DOI: [10.1038/s41467-023-42907-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-42907-x). (zhao2023nde1promoteslis1mediated pages 1-2)

Human NDE1 splice isoforms also differ functionally. The comparatively recent NDE1_SSSC isoform has a robust 26S-proteasome interaction, supporting isoform-level neo-functionalization. That property should not be propagated to NDEL1, fungal NudE, or all NDE1 transcripts. Monda & Cheeseman, published online 19 July 2018, DOI: [10.1091/mbc.E18-07-0418](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.E18-07-0418). (monda2018nde1promotesdiverse pages 1-2)

### C. Vertebrate NDEL1

NDEL1 retains the same core scaffold but is more strongly associated with post-mitotic neurons, nucleus–centrosome coupling, and neuronal migration. NDEL1-null mice are nonviable, contrasting with viable NDE1 knockouts. Partial redundancy nevertheless persists: depletion of either paralogue causes mild Golgi dispersal, simultaneous depletion causes a stronger defect, and expression of either can rescue that phenotype. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

A 2024 study identified the same de novo somatic mosaic **NDEL1 p.R105P** variant in two unrelated patients with pachygyria, with or without subcortical-band heterotopia. Single-cell and spatial-transcriptomic analyses showed complementary expression: NDE1 in cycling radial-glial progenitors and NDEL1 in post-mitotic neurons. NDEL1 depletion impaired migration; R105P failed to rescue it, and expression of R105P alone disrupted migration, elongated the leading process, increased nucleus–centrosome separation, and reduced LIS1 binding. This is strong evidence for cortical-development subfunctionalization after duplication. Tsai et al., *Acta Neuropathologica* 147:13, received 23 October 2023 and published 2024, DOI: [10.1007/s00401-023-02665-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-023-02665-y). (tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 15-16)

NDEL1 also has a mitotic spatial-regulation function. Aurora A phosphorylates NDEL1 S285 near spindle poles, reducing pole accumulation of dynein-transported Mad1 and NuMA. A nonphosphorylatable mutant caused their accumulation at poles and weakened spindle-checkpoint behavior; Xenopus rescue/titration experiments quantified more than 100 microtubule structures per condition across three experiments. Janczyk et al., *Molecular Biology of the Cell* 34:br1, 1 January 2023, DOI: [10.1091/mbc.E21-09-0438](https://doi.org/10.1091/mbc.E21-09-0438). (janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 9-10, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 1-2)

Finally, NDEL1 has been reported to act as a DISC1-regulated serine oligopeptidase against unstructured peptides. No authoritative EC number was established in the retrieved evidence, the relationship of this activity to dynein regulation remains unresolved, and equivalent NDE1 activity is considered unlikely. This is therefore a candidate NDEL1 neo-function, but it should remain experimentally scoped rather than family-wide. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 13-15, bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 12-13, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

## 4. Functional divergence and evolutionary interpretation

### Subfunctionalization: strong evidence

The strongest evolutionary conclusion is partitioning of ancestral cellular roles after the vertebrate duplication:

1. **NDE1:** early developmental expression, radial-glial progenitors, interkinetic nuclear migration, mitosis, and cortical progenitor expansion.
2. **NDEL1:** sustained expression, post-mitotic neurons, nucleus–centrosome coupling, nucleokinesis, and neuronal migration.
3. **Shared functions:** LIS1/dynein binding, spindle organization, Golgi maintenance, retrograde trafficking, and aspects of cortical lamination. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 12-13, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2)

This is not complete separation. Redundant rescue experiments and overlapping dynein interactions show that both proteins preserved much of the ancestral molecular activity.

### Neo-functionalization: limited but credible examples

The human NDE1_SSSC–26S proteasome interaction is the strongest isoform-specific gain of function, because the isoform is evolutionarily recent and its proteasome association can be separated experimentally from dynein/LIS1 functions. (monda2018nde1promotesdiverse pages 1-2)

NDEL1 oligopeptidase activity may represent a paralogue-specific gain, but uncertainty about its physiological substrates, catalytic mechanism, EC classification, and conservation makes it unsuitable as a defining family function. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 13-15, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

No subfamily pair has demonstrated different EC numbers, synthesis-versus-degradation activity, or opposite enzymatic reactions. NDE1 and NDEL1 can appear to promote or restrain dynein under different biochemical conditions, but such differences reflect assembly state, concentration, phosphorylation, or cellular localization rather than opposing catalytic functions. (zhao2023nde1promoteslis1mediated pages 1-2, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 1-2)

### Branch-length analysis

A valid branch-length ranking cannot be made from the supplied record because the root node is blank, no PANTHER subfamilies are defined, and no tree with numerical branch lengths was provided. Literature suggests that **NDEL1 is more evolutionarily conserved than NDE1**, but that observation is not a substitute for measuring root-to-tip lengths. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 2-5)

Accordingly:

* No lineage can responsibly be designated the “longest branch” from the available data.
* The NDE1_SSSC terminal-exon innovation is a recent isoform event, not necessarily a long protein-tree branch.
* NDE1 and NDEL1 are ancient vertebrate paralogues, not very recent duplicates.
* Species-specific terminal duplicates, if present in the underlying PANTHER tree, would require direct inspection of that tree before being called short-branch recent duplications.

## 5. GO annotation issues and recommendations

### Safe or relatively safe ancestral propagation

Subject to orthology and taxon constraints, the following broad annotations are consistent with the ancestral family function:

* LIS1/PAFAH1B1 binding;
* cytoplasmic-dynein-complex binding or regulation;
* regulation of microtubule-based movement;
* nuclear migration or nuclear positioning;
* mitotic-spindle organization where supported in the relevant eukaryotic lineage. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 2-5, zyłkiewicz2011thenterminalcoiledcoil pages 1-2, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4, garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 6-7)

Even these should not be expressed as catalytic motor activity: NudE proteins regulate dynein but are not themselves ATPase motors.

### Annotations that should be restricted

1. **Peptidase/endopeptidase activity:** restrict to experimentally tested NDEL1 orthologues; do not propagate to NDE1 or fungal NudE, and do not assign an EC number without authoritative enzyme classification. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 13-15, bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 12-13)
2. **26S-proteasome binding:** restrict to the supported human NDE1 isoform rather than all NDE1 products or the family. (monda2018nde1promotesdiverse pages 1-2)
3. **Neural-progenitor proliferation/interkinetic nuclear migration:** preferentially annotate NDE1, with vertebrate and neural-tissue constraints. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 12-13, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2)
4. **Post-mitotic neuronal migration/nucleokinesis:** preferentially annotate NDEL1; R105P patient and mouse-electroporation experiments provide direct support. (tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 15-16)
5. **Aurora-A-dependent spindle-pole regulation:** NDEL1-specific unless corresponding phosphorylation and function are directly demonstrated for another paralogue. (janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 9-10, janczyk2023auroraaphosphorylates pages 1-2)
6. **Palmitoylation-dependent reduction of dynein binding:** do not infer across the paralogues; palmitoylation inhibits NDEL1–dynein binding but reportedly does not alter NDE1–dynein association. (garrott2022nde1andndel1 pages 2-4)
7. **Disease annotations:** microcephaly-spectrum disease is strongly associated with NDE1 loss, whereas the current direct NDEL1 cortical-malformation evidence concerns mosaic R105P-associated pachygyria/SBH. Disease terms should not be propagated through the ancestral node. (bradshaw2017nde1andndel1 pages 12-13, tsai2024novellissencephalyassociatedndel1 pages 1-2)

### Recommended PANTHER curation

PTHR10921 should be divided at minimum into **NudE ancestral/pre-vertebrate**, **NDE1**, and **NDEL1** subfamilies. A further isoform-level rule is needed for human NDE1 proteasome binding. Ancestral-node GO assertions should emphasize dynein/LIS1 scaffolding and nuclear positioning, while descendant-specific gain/loss rules should prevent transfer of peptidase, proteasome, phosphorylation-site, neural-cell-type, and disease annotations.
