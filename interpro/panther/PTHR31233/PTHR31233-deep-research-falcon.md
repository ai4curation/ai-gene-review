# PANTHER Family PTHR31233 (Bicaudal-D Family): Evolutionary and Functional Assessment

## 1. Executive Summary

PTHR31233/IPR018477 is best interpreted as a family of **non-enzymatic, coiled-coil motor adaptors**, not enzymes. Its conserved role is to couple cargo to cytoplasmic dynein–dynactin and convert dynein into a processive minus-end-directed transport complex. Accordingly, no EC number, catalytic substrate, catalytic cofactor, or catalytic active-site residues apply.

The family includes the classical *Drosophila* BicD/vertebrate BICD1–BICD2 lineage and the more divergent BicD-related proteins, including BICDR1/BICDL1, BICDR2/BICDL2, and fly BicDR. Members retain the same basic polarity: an N-terminal motor-binding region and a C-terminal cargo-binding/autoregulatory region. The strongest evolutionary signal is therefore **subfunctionalization**—different cargoes, tissues, transport distances, motor complements, and activation mechanisms—rather than acquisition of different enzymatic reactions. BICD2 has particularly clear specialization for cell-cycle-regulated nuclear positioning, whereas fly BicD has prominent Egalitarian-dependent RNA-transport functions and BICDR1/BicDR proteins show altered transport kinetics or local developmental functions. Evidence for a qualitatively new biochemical function is weak.

The supplied PANTHER record contains no subfamily nodes, root assignment, topology, or branch-length values. Consequently, numerical longest/shortest-branch claims cannot be made. The absence of PANTHER subfamilies should not, however, be interpreted as functional uniformity: literature supports at least a classical BICD versus BICDR functional partition.

## 2. Ancestral Function and Shared Molecular Architecture

### Structural fold

The shared architecture is an elongated, rod-like **parallel homodimeric coiled coil**, commonly described as three coiled-coil regions, CC1–CC3. CC1 and CC3 are the most conserved; CC2 is less conserved in BICDR proteins, and the classical C-terminal BICD domain is absent or strongly diverged in BICDR proteins. Structural work on *Drosophila* BicD established a parallel homodimeric coiled coil with locally heterotypic registry, while human BICD2-CTD residues 715–804 crystallized as a homodimeric coiled coil at 2.0 Å resolution. Thus “coiled-coil scaffold” is more accurate than a globular enzyme-like fold. (hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 4-5, liu2013bicaudaldusesa pages 14-15, noell2019roleofcoiledcoil pages 1-3)

### Likely ancestral/core activity

The parsimonious ancestral activity is a **cargo-regulated dynein–dynactin activating adaptor**:

1. the N terminus contacts dynein/dynactin, including the dynein light-intermediate-chain interaction region;
2. the extended coiled coil lies along dynactin and promotes assembly of an active motor complex;
3. the C terminus binds cargo or cargo receptors;
4. in classical BICD proteins, intramolecular N–C-terminal association produces a closed, autoinhibited state;
5. cargo engagement shifts the adaptor toward an open state, exposing motor-binding surfaces and enabling processive transport. (baker2023thebicaudaldegalitariancomplex pages 1-2, hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 4-5, zhao2024molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2, gibson2023astructuralmodel pages 1-3)

This mechanism is supported by biochemical, cellular, single-molecule, crystallographic and cryo-EM studies. BICD2 can connect one or two dynein dimers to cargo, and recent structural work indicates that one or two adaptor dimers can occur in dynein–dynactin assemblies. Nup358 and BICD2 predominantly form a 2:2 cargo–adaptor complex. (noell2018aquantitativemodel pages 17-18, gibson2023astructuralmodel pages 1-3)

### Typical partners rather than substrates/cofactors

Because the family is non-catalytic, “substrates” and “cofactors” should be replaced by **binding partners and cargo receptors**:

- motor machinery: cytoplasmic dynein-1, dynactin and LIS1;
- vesicle cargo receptor: GTP-bound Rab6;
- nuclear-envelope cargoes: Nup358/RanBP2 and Nesprin-2;
- RNA-transport machinery in flies: Egalitarian, with RNA cargoes and FMRP-associated complexes;
- other reported partners: clathrin heavy chain, kinesin-1 for BICD2 and KIF1C for BICDR1. (noell2018aquantitativemodel pages 17-18, hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 2-4, hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 7-9, schlager2014bicaudaldfamily pages 1-2)

### Key functional residues and regulatory sites

- The conserved N-terminal alanine region is required for motor-complex association. *Drosophila* BicD A40V and engineered BICD2/BICDR1 alanine-to-valine substitutions markedly reduce dynein/dynactin coprecipitation. The comparative experiment used BICD2 A43V/A44V and BICDR1 A116V/A117V, although one figure-caption rendering transposed the paralog labels; the main result—conserved N-terminal motor binding—is clear. (hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 4-5, schlager2014bicaudaldfamily pages 4-5)
- *Drosophila* BicD K730 is central to cargo-partner interactions; K730M disrupts Rab6, Egalitarian and FMRP binding and causes severe genetic phenotypes. The corresponding conserved lysine is K555 in fly BicDR-B, K461 in BicDR-A, and K512 in zebrafish BicDR. (hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 4-5, hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 7-9, jejina2023roleofbicdr pages 1-4)
- BicD F684I causes cargo-independent or enhanced dynein recruitment and abnormal anterior transport. The corresponding human BICD2 position is F743. F743 and nearby F750 occur in a registry-sensitive interruption of the coiled-coil core. (hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 7-9, noell2019roleofcoiledcoil pages 1-3)
- Human BICD2 R747C is associated with SMALED2 and changes coiled-coil dynamics/cargo recognition. Other disease-linked substitutions occur throughout BICD2, reinforcing that motor activation and cargo selection are separable disease mechanisms. (hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 9-10, noell2019roleofcoiledcoil pages 1-3)
- BICD2 Ser102 lies in a conserved PLK1 polo-box-binding/regulatory region. In G2, CDK1-dependent recruitment of PLK1 and PLK1 phosphorylation activate BICD2, promote dynein/dynactin assembly and favor phosphorylated RanBP2/Nup358 cargo. The 2023 study identifies phosphorylation as a temporal switch governing nuclear-envelope dynein recruitment and centrosome separation. (gallisasune2023bicd2phosphorylationregulates pages 4-4)

## 3. Subfamily Analysis

### A. Classical *Drosophila* BicD

Fly BicD is the founding family member and retains the broad ancestral adaptor mechanism. It connects dynein–dynactin to Rab6-associated membrane cargo, Egalitarian-bound mRNAs, FMRP and clathrin-related cargo. Egalitarian is a selective RNA-binding cargo adaptor, not another BICD-family enzyme; its association allows localization signals in mRNA to enter the dynein pathway. Foundational evidence includes direct RNA-binding and genetic/transport assays (Dienstbier et al., 2009; PMID **19515976**). (noell2018aquantitativemodel pages 17-18, baker2023thebicaudaldegalitariancomplex pages 10-11)

A 2023 bioRxiv study reported that Egalitarian depletion left many cargo–BicD associations intact but reduced BicD–dynein engagement, suggesting that Egl can act as an activation/coupling factor for multiple cargoes in the female germline. This is potentially important but remained a preprint in the retrieved record and should not yet drive broad family-wide annotation. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.09.11.557182; posted 12 September 2023. (baker2023thebicaudaldegalitariancomplex pages 1-2, baker2023thebicaudaldegalitariancomplex pages 8-10)

**Interpretation:** specialized RNA/localization and developmental functions represent subfunctionalization of the ancestral adaptor activity. There is no different EC reaction.

### B. Vertebrate BICD1

BICD1 retains Rab6-dependent Golgi/vesicle transport and the conserved motor-adaptor architecture. Reported contexts include neurotrophin-receptor sorting, neuronal transport, microtubule organization and viral replication. The BICD1 cargo-binding-domain structure adopts a parallel coiled coil with homotypic registry, showing that registry state can differ among homologs or functional states without creating a different fold. Primary structural reference: Terawaki et al., 2015; PMID **25796327**. (noell2018aquantitativemodel pages 17-18, hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 2-4, noell2019roleofcoiledcoil pages 1-3)

Compared with BICD2, evidence for BICD1-specific nuclear-envelope/RanBP2 regulation is weaker. BICD2-specific nuclear-positioning terms should therefore not be automatically transferred to BICD1.

### C. Vertebrate BICD2

BICD2 is the most extensively characterized vertebrate paralog. Its functions include:

- Rab6-GTP-positive secretory and Golgi-derived vesicle transport;
- COPI-independent Golgi-to-ER transport;
- Nup358/RanBP2-dependent nuclear-envelope recruitment of dynein;
- Nesprin-2-dependent nuclear positioning;
- centrosome–nucleus coupling and centrosome separation in G2/M;
- neuronal and radial-glial nuclear migration;
- coordination with kinesin-1 in bidirectional transport. (hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 2-4, zhao2024molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2, gibson2023astructuralmodel pages 1-3)

**Recent structural developments.** Gibson et al. showed that the Nup358 cargo-recognition helix binds BICD2 residues 747–774 in an antiparallel helical bundle, with a secondary Nup358 intrinsically disordered segment contacting BICD2 residues 774–800. Mutagenesis implicated BICD2 C745, F750, D755, E756 and D762 in Nup358 binding; three replicate pulldown experiments gave p<0.0001 for the tested disruptive substitutions. The secondary site overlaps the Rab6/Nesprin-2 region, explaining mutually exclusive cargo selection. Nup358 has approximately 20-fold higher affinity for BICD2-CTD than Rab6-GTP. Gibson et al., published 26 September 2023, DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/biom13101445. (gibson2023astructuralmodel pages 1-3, gibson2023astructuralmodel pages 13-14)

Zhao et al. subsequently modeled and experimentally validated Rab6-GTP recognition. The interface spans Rab6 switch regions and includes an invariant hydrophobic triad, F50/W67/Y82. Nineteen Rab6 mutants reduced BICD2 binding without major CD-detectable misfolding. Rab6 Y42A and F38A reduced cellular BICD2 colocalization and rendered Rab6-positive vesicles nearly immotile; for Y42A, the colocalization reduction was significant across four independent experiments (Mann–Whitney p=0.0286). This is notable because normal Rab6-positive vesicle populations move at approximately 1.20±0.26 and 1.71±0.49 µm/s. Zhao et al., published online 7 May 2024, DOI: https://doi.org/10.26508/lsa.202302430. (zhao2024molecularmechanismfor pages 5-7, zhao2024molecularmechanismfor pages 11-12, zhao2024molecularmechanismfor pages 1-2)

**Interpretation:** BICD2 exemplifies cargo and cell-cycle subfunctionalization, not enzymatic neo-functionalization. Its clinically relevant variants can alter both autoinhibition and cargo choice.

### D. BICDR1/BICDL1

BICDR1 uses the same conserved N-terminal dynein/dynactin-binding region as BICD2 but has distinct localization, partner choice and motor output. It binds Rab6 vesicles, recruits KIF1C and strongly accumulates cargo pericentrosomally. BICDR1 expression increased minus-end-directed Rab6-vesicle speed from about 2.3 to 3.6 µm/s and plus-end speed from 1.6 to 2.2 µm/s. Dynein-heavy-chain depletion reduced average motion in BICDR1-expressing cells from 3.3 to 2.0 µm/s, supporting a dynein-dependent kinetic effect. Excess BICDR1 also reduced axonal outgrowth. Schlager et al., 11 September 2014, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2014.07.052; PMID **25176647**. (schlager2014bicaudaldfamily pages 1-2, schlager2014bicaudaldfamily pages 4-5)

This is a strong case of **subfunctionalization in motor regulation and neuronal context**. It is not a new molecular activity in the EC sense.

### E. BICDR2/BICDL2

BICDR2 belongs to the related BICDR branch and preserves family coiled-coil organization, but direct cargo, biochemical and organismal evidence is much thinner than for BICD2 or BICDR1. The middle coiled-coil and classical BICD cargo-binding region are divergent, making unrestricted cargo/process propagation particularly risky. (hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 4-5)

**Interpretation:** sequence divergence is consistent with specialization, but the available evidence does not establish a unique cargo, opposite activity, or novel molecular function. Conservative annotation is warranted.

### F. *Drosophila* BicDR

Recent fly genetics resolves a clearer BicD/BicDR division. BicDR is required for normal bristle and dorsal-trunk tracheal development and cooperates with BicD to organize actin and localize Rab6 and Spn-F at bristle tips. The authors infer that BicDR acts more locally, whereas BicD provides long-range delivery to the distal tip. Proteomics identified 285 candidate interactors: 82 occurred in both wild-type and K555A samples and 179 only with wild-type BicDR. BicDR71 hemizygotes showed visible bristle phenotypes in 35% of adults; strong genetic interactions were observed with BicD and EF1γ alleles. The peer-reviewed version appeared in *Journal of Cell Science* in January 2024, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1242/jcs.261408. (jejina2023roleofbicdr pages 1-4, jejina2023roleofbicdr pages 26-29)

**Interpretation:** local-versus-long-range transport is a plausible example of subfunctionalization after duplication. It remains uncertain whether every BicDR cargo directly binds the conserved lysine-containing C-terminal site.

## 4. Functional Divergence and Evolutionary Interpretation

### Neo-functionalization

There are no different EC numbers, opposing catalytic reactions, or catalytic/non-catalytic splits because all characterized members are non-catalytic adaptors. The best potential neo-functionalization signals are regulatory or ecological rather than chemical—for example, BICD2’s highly developed phospho-regulated nuclear-envelope pathway or pathogen exploitation of BICD2. The latter includes a 2024 preprint reporting that *Orientia tsutsugamushi* ScaC opens BICD2 and recruits dynein for bacterial motility, but this is host-pathogen co-option of an existing adaptor mechanism, not a newly evolved BICD2 catalytic function.

### Subfunctionalization

Evidence is considerably stronger for:

- **cargo partitioning:** Egl/mRNA in fly BicD; RanBP2/Nup358 and Nesprin-2 in BICD2; Rab6 shared across several members;
- **motor-complement partitioning:** BICD2 associates with kinesin-1, whereas BICDR1 recruits KIF1C;
- **kinetic specialization:** BICDR1 drives faster dynein-dependent Rab6 movement than BICD2;
- **spatiotemporal specialization:** BICD2 switches from Rab6-associated transport in G1/S to phosphorylated Nup358-associated nuclear transport in G2;
- **developmental specialization:** fly BicDR supports local bristle/tracheal transport, whereas BicD contributes more strongly to long-range and RNA transport. (hoogenraad2016bicaudaldfamily pages 2-4, gallisasune2023bicd2phosphorylationregulates pages 4-4, jejina2023roleofbicdr pages 1-4, schlager2014bicaudaldfamily pages 4-5)

### Branch-length analysis

No defensible branch-length ranking is possible from the supplied record: the root node is blank, the number of PANTHER subfamilies is zero, and no tree or edge lengths were provided. Literature establishes greater domain/sequence divergence in the BICDR lineage—especially CC2 and the C-terminal BICD region—but domain divergence is not a substitute for measured phylogenetic branch length. Likewise, no “very short branch” can be identified as a recent duplication without the underlying reconciled tree. A proper follow-up analysis would require the PTHR31233 Newick tree or PANTHER phylogram, taxon sampling, and preferably bootstrap/support values.

## 5. GO Annotation Issues and Recommendations

### Safe family-level propagation

Only broad annotations capturing the shared mechanism should be considered for propagation across well-supported orthologs:

- dynein/dynactin complex binding or cargo-adaptor activity;
- protein binding, preferably replaced by more specific interaction terms where available;
- regulation/activation of cytoplasmic dynein-dependent transport;
- microtubule-based cargo transport;
- coiled-coil adaptor/scaffold localization statements where experimentally supported.

Even these should be qualified for highly divergent or truncated sequences, because preservation of an InterPro domain alone does not prove intact N- and C-terminal functions.

### Terms that should not be propagated to all descendants

1. **mRNA localization, oocyte specification and Egalitarian binding:** appropriate for experimentally supported fly BicD orthologs, not automatically for BICD1/2 or BICDR proteins.
2. **Nuclear-envelope dynein recruitment, centrosome–nucleus attachment and radial-glial nuclear migration:** principally supported for BICD2.
3. **Rab6-GTP binding and Golgi/secretory-vesicle transport:** supported for BicD, BICD1, BICD2 and BICDR1, but insufficiently demonstrated for every family member, especially BICDR2.
4. **Neuritogenesis/pericentrosomal Rab6 targeting:** BICDR1-specific evidence should not become a family-wide biological-process annotation.
5. **Bristle or tracheal development and Spn-F localization:** fly BicDR/BicD lineage-specific.
6. **Pathogen transport or viral/bacterial entry:** context-specific host exploitation, never a family-wide ancestral annotation.
7. **Catalytic activity or EC terms:** exclude entirely unless an individual protein independently acquires and experimentally demonstrates catalysis; none was identified here.

The annotation model should distinguish **molecular function** (motor/cargo adaptor) from **biological process** (RNA localization, secretion, nuclear positioning, neuronal development). The former is comparatively conserved; the latter is where most over-propagation risk lies.

## Overall conclusion

PTHR31233 should not be treated as a functionally homogeneous set merely because PANTHER currently lists zero subfamilies. The conserved ancestral function is a coiled-coil, cargo-regulated dynein–dynactin adaptor. The family shows substantial **subfunctionalization in cargo recognition, transport kinetics, phosphoregulation, cellular location and developmental deployment**, but no convincing enzymatic neo-functionalization. GO propagation should therefore be broad and mechanistic at the family root, while cargo-, tissue- and organism-specific biological-process annotations should be assigned only below appropriately supported lineage nodes.
