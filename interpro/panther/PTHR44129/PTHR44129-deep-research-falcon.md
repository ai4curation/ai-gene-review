# PANTHER Family PTHR44129 Research Report

## 1. Executive summary

PTHR44129/IPR050349 is best interpreted as a family of **LIS1/NudF/Pac1 nonenzymatic regulators of cytoplasmic dynein**, not as a family containing multiple enzyme classes. Its conserved architecture is an N-terminal LisH/coiled-coil dimerization module followed by seven WD40 repeats forming a seven-bladed β-propeller. The propeller is a protein-interaction scaffold rather than a catalytic domain. Accordingly, family members have **no EC number**. In mammals, the gene name *PAFAH1B1* is potentially misleading: LIS1 is a noncatalytic regulatory component of PAFAH1B, whereas acetylhydrolase catalysis resides in the associated α subunits PAFAH1B2/PAFAH1B3. Fungal homologues can occur without recognizable PAFAH catalytic subunits, reinforcing dynein regulation as the ancestral function (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 5-6, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 6-8, tarricone2004couplingpafsignaling pages 12-13).

The strongest evolutionary conclusion is **deep conservation with lineage-specific deployment**, rather than extensive subfamily neo-functionalization. Fungal NudF/Pac1 proteins regulate dynein in nuclear distribution, hyphal transport, spindle-pole organization, and microtubule-plus-end targeting. Metazoan LIS1 retains dynein regulation but is deployed in neuronal migration, mitosis, organelle transport, and the animal-specific PAFAH1B complex. The latter is a partner/context expansion—not acquisition of acetylhydrolase activity by LIS1 (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 18-19, helmstaedt2008thenuclearmigration pages 1-2, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 6-8).

PANTHER reports **zero curated subfamilies**, and no phylogram or branch-length values were provided. Therefore, named “subfamilies,” longest branches, rate acceleration, and recent duplication events cannot be assigned reliably. The fungal and metazoan categories below are literature-supported lineage/context groups, not formal PANTHER subfamilies.

## 2. Ancestral function and molecular mechanism

### Conserved fold and oligomerization

The canonical protein contains an N-terminal LisH/coiled-coil region that mediates homodimerization and a C-terminal seven-WD40-repeat β-propeller. WD40 propellers provide multiple exposed interaction surfaces and do not contain a recognized catalytic center. Mammalian and yeast studies commonly observe a LIS1/Pac1 dimer, although motor complexes can engage one or two LIS1 dimers depending on conformational and assembly state (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 5-6, singh2024molecularmechanismof pages 6-8, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 6-8).

The likely ancestral activity was to bind the cytoplasmic-dynein motor and control its conformational and mechanochemical cycle. The propeller contacts a “ring site” near dynein’s AAA3–AAA4 interface and a second site near the base of the coiled-coil stalk. This interaction helps prevent or reverse the autoinhibited φ conformation, promotes assembly with dynactin and cargo adaptors, and—while LIS1 remains attached—can alter velocity, microtubule affinity, and force-dependent detachment (kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3, singh2024molecularmechanismof pages 6-8, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 11-12).

### Partners and functional surfaces

Typical binding partners are:

- **Cytoplasmic dynein heavy chain:** direct binding at the AAA+ ring and stalk base.
- **Dynactin p150Glued:** the 2024 structure showed that LIS1 also contacts p150 during assembly. Dynein intermediate-chain N-terminus first helps open p150’s autoinhibited CC1A/CC1B hairpin; p150 CC1B residues 420–455 then contact LIS1, while residues 458–478 contact the intermediate chain (singh2024molecularmechanismof pages 6-8).
- **NDE1/NDEL1/NudE/Ndl1:** these coiled-coil proteins recruit or hand LIS1 to dynein. Structural predictions and mass photometry support a defined 1:1 LIS1–NDEL1/Pac1–Ndl1 complex. Their binding surface overlaps the dynein-binding surface on the WD40 propeller, making stable simultaneous binding context-dependent and supporting a regulated hand-off model (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-7).
- **PAFAH1B2/PAFAH1B3:** animal catalytic α subunits bind LIS1, but LIS1 itself is noncatalytic. NDE1 can compete with PAFAH α2 for LIS1, connecting partner availability to dynein activation (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 6-8, tarricone2004couplingpafsignaling pages 12-13).
- **Fungal partners:** NudE/Ndl1, NudC and, in *Aspergillus nidulans*, BnfA at spindle pole bodies (helmstaedt2008thenuclearmigration pages 1-2).

Experimentally investigated LIS1 interaction residues include human **R316 and W340**. Corresponding Pac1 substitutions include **R275A, R301A, R378A, W419A and K437A**. These mutations alter Ndel1/Ndl1 or dynein-associated interactions and demonstrate that function depends on specific exposed propeller surfaces rather than an enzyme active site (okada2023conservedrolesfor pages 6-7).

### Recent mechanistic advances, 2023–2024

A 2023 structural study described two LIS1 dimers wedged between dynein motor domains—the “Chi” intermediate—providing a direct mechanism by which LIS1 destabilizes dynein autoinhibition and favors active-complex assembly. A separate 2023 study showed that microtubule-binding-induced allostery lowers LIS1 affinity, explaining how an assembly factor can be released before sustained cargo transport (Karasmanis et al., published August 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01069-6; Ton et al., published June/September 2023, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41594-023-01010-x) (singh2024molecularmechanismof pages 6-8, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 11-12).

The 2024 *Science* cryo-EM study resolved a dynein–dynactin–JIP3–LIS1 assembly intermediate. Two LIS1 dimers bind the dynein-A motors; each WD40 propeller engages ring and stalk-base sites, stabilizing a bent-linker, pre-powerstroke, low-microtubule-affinity state. LIS1 simultaneously helps dock p150 along dynein, thereby constraining dynein and dynactin into an assembly-competent geometry. This extends the older “LIS1 merely opens dynein” model: LIS1 also organizes dynactin during adaptor capture (Singh et al., published March 2024, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk8544) (singh2024molecularmechanismof pages 6-8).

The 2024 *Nature Chemical Biology* single-molecule/optical-trapping study found that LIS1 slows yeast dynein through its AAA+ ring interaction rather than acting as a steric roadblock on the microtubule. LIS1 did **not reduce stall-force generation**, but prolonged stalls and reduced the normal directional asymmetry of force-induced detachment. Mutating stalk-contact residues partly restored detachment asymmetry but not velocity, separating ring-mediated slowing from stalk-mediated control of detachment (Kusakci et al., volume 20, 521–529; online 2023/issue 2024, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41589-023-01464-6) (kusakci2023lis1bindingregulates pages 7-9, kusakci2024lis1slowsforceinduced pages 1-3).

These findings reconcile apparently opposite historical descriptions of LIS1. It can slow a motor while remaining bound, yet promote faster, stronger transport indirectly by increasing productive assembly and recruitment of two dyneins to dynactin. Published reconstitutions report up to approximately **2.5-fold greater velocity** for appropriate activated complexes, and one-versus-two-LIS1 occupancy produces progressively slower velocity and longer run time in yeast assays (kusakci2023lis1bindingregulates pages 7-9, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 11-12).

### Fungal NudF/Pac1 group

*Aspergillus* NudF and budding-yeast Pac1 retain the core dynein-regulatory mechanism. *A. nidulans* NUDF controls dynein-dependent nuclear distribution, localizes with dynein at microtubule plus ends and spindle poles, interacts through its WD40 region with NudC/NudE-associated machinery, and recruits BnfA to spindle pole bodies. These are noncatalytic functions. Genetic suppression by dynein heavy-chain mutations and cargo-adaptor experiments place NudF upstream of productive dynein activation (helmstaedt2008thenuclearmigration pages 1-2, qiu2019insightsintolis1 pages 13-17).

Fungal specialization is therefore mainly cellular: dynein regulation is used for nuclear migration in elongated hyphae, organelle-transport initiation, and spindle-pole organization. In budding yeast, Pac1-dependent plus-end targeting additionally involves kinesin Kip2; one reviewed estimate attributes roughly **25%** of targeting to the Kip2-dependent route. These distinctions are compatible with subfunctionalization of localization and recruitment pathways, not a new biochemical reaction (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 10-11, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 11-12).

### Metazoan LIS1/PAFAH1B1 group

Animal LIS1 retains direct dynein regulation but operates in expanded contexts: neuronal and nuclear migration, mitotic spindle function, kinetochore transport, retrograde axonal/organelle transport, and assembly of dynein–dynactin–adaptor complexes. Haploinsufficiency of human *PAFAH1B1* causes lissencephaly, demonstrating strong dosage sensitivity of this conserved transport regulator (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 5-6, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 17-18).

Membership in cytosolic PAFAH1B is the clearest animal-specific molecular expansion. Nevertheless, LIS1 is a regulatory/scaffolding subunit; catalytic α subunits execute phospholipid hydrolysis. Therefore, this represents acquisition of an additional interaction network or moonlighting context, not enzymatic neo-functionalization by LIS1 (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 6-8, tarricone2004couplingpafsignaling pages 12-13).

## 4. Functional divergence assessment

### Neo-functionalization

There is **no credible evidence** within the retrieved literature for LIS1/NudF descendants with:

- different EC numbers;
- synthesis-versus-degradation or other opposite catalytic reactions;
- a newly acquired catalytic center; or
- substrate-specific enzyme subfamilies.

All well-characterized members are noncatalytic protein regulators. The strongest candidate for contextual neo-functionalization is metazoan recruitment into PAFAH1B, but this should be described as a new partner/complex association, not platelet-activating-factor acetylhydrolase activity of LIS1 itself (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 18-19, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 6-8).

### Subfunctionalization

Subfunctionalization is better supported at the level of **organism and cellular context**:

- fungal homologues: nuclear distribution, hyphal organelle traffic, spindle-pole bodies and plus-end targeting;
- metazoan homologues: neuronal migration, corticogenesis, mitosis, kinetochores and long-range cargo transport;
- animal LIS1: additional competition between NDE1/NDEL1 and PAFAH1B catalytic α subunits.

Mechanistic differences reported across assays—motor slowing versus transport activation—should not be mistaken for opposite family functions. They reflect stage and occupancy: LIS1 can slow a motor when persistently bound, promote release from φ-autoinhibition during assembly, favor two-dynein recruitment, and then dissociate as the motor enters processive transport (kusakci2023lis1bindingregulates pages 7-9, singh2024molecularmechanismof pages 6-8, markus2020newinsightsinto pages 10-11).

### Branch lengths

No branch-length analysis is possible from the supplied family record. It lacks a root-node assignment, subfamily nodes, sequence tree, branch lengths and taxon labels. Consequently, no claim can be made about the longest branch, correlation between branch length and PAFAH1B association, or recent duplications. A valid analysis would require retrieving all PTHR44129 sequences, checking domain completeness, constructing a maximum-likelihood tree, reconciling it with the species tree, and testing whether rate shifts coincide with changes in interaction residues or domain architecture.

## 5. GO annotation issues and recommendations

### Reasonably safe ancestral annotations

For intact orthologues in organisms possessing cytoplasmic dynein, the best-supported ancestral concepts are:

- cytoplasmic-dynein binding;
- regulation of cytoplasmic-dynein activity;
- positive regulation of dynein–dynactin–adaptor-complex assembly;
- regulation of microtubule-based transport;
- protein homodimerization or protein-complex binding, where the term and evidence code are appropriate.

Even these should not be transferred blindly to highly divergent or truncated sequences lacking the LisH/coiled-coil or complete WD40 propeller.

### Annotations that should not be propagated family-wide

1. **Platelet-activating-factor acetylhydrolase activity:** exclude from every LIS1/NudF protein unless an unexpected catalytic activity is demonstrated directly. LIS1 is the noncatalytic subunit; gene nomenclature does not justify enzyme annotation (markus2020newinsightsinto pages 6-8, tarricone2004couplingpafsignaling pages 12-13).
2. **PAFAH1B complex membership:** restrict to animals with demonstrated PAFAH1B2/3 orthologues and complex formation; do not assign to fungal NudF/Pac1.
3. **Neuronal migration, corticogenesis or lissencephaly-related terms:** restrict to vertebrate/metazoan orthologues with relevant evidence.
4. **Fungal nuclear migration/distribution, hyphal growth and spindle-pole-body localization:** restrict to supported fungal branches.
5. **Kinetochore, nuclear-envelope, centrosome or microtubule-plus-end localization:** these are conditional localizations mediated by species- and cell-state-specific partners, not invariant properties of every family member.
6. **Dynein ATPase activity or microtubule motor activity:** LIS1 regulates the dynein enzyme but is not itself an ATPase or motor.

The most serious likely annotation error is conflation of **PAFAH1B1 complex membership** with **PAF acetylhydrolase catalytic activity**. A second risk is transferring organism-specific biological-process terms from human LIS1 or *Aspergillus* NudF across all eukaryotes.

## Overall conclusion

PTHR44129 is a structurally and mechanistically coherent family of dimeric WD40 dynein regulators. Present evidence favors conservation of an ancestral dynein-binding/activation function, with fungal and animal lineages specializing mainly through cellular deployment and partner networks. There is no support for divergent enzyme subfamilies, opposing reactions, or LIS1 acetylhydrolase catalysis. GO curation should therefore preserve the noncatalytic dynein-regulatory core while sharply restricting PAFAH1B, neuronal, fungal nuclear-migration and localization terms to appropriate lineages. Claims about long branches or recent duplications must await an explicit family phylogeny.
