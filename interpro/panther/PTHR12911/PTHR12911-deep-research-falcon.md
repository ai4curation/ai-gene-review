# PANTHER PTHR12911 / InterPro IPR045119: SAD1/UNC-84-like proteins

## 1. Executive summary

PTHR12911 is best interpreted as the **SUN-domain protein family**, a broad eukaryotic family of membrane-embedded mechanical adaptors rather than enzymes. Its conserved molecular core is a lumenal SUN domain that oligomerizes and binds the short C-terminal KASH region of an outer-nuclear-membrane protein. Together, SUN and KASH proteins form LINC complexes spanning the nuclear envelope and mechanically coupling nuclear contents to cytoskeletal systems. Consequently, there is no EC number, catalytic substrate, or evidence for divergence among enzymatic reactions.

The supplied PANTHER record contains **zero curated subfamilies**, but this does not indicate functional homogeneity. Published phylogenetic and experimental work identifies several biologically meaningful groups: broadly expressed metazoan SUN1/2; germ-cell-enriched SUN3/4/5; nematode UNC-84 and SUN1/MTF1 systems; fungal Mps3/Sad1; and plant C-terminal SUN versus structurally divergent Mid-SUN proteins. Their conserved activity is protein-complex assembly and force transmission, while their biological outputs have strongly subfunctionalized by interaction partner, tissue, membrane topology, and organism.

The clearest divergence involves: (i) partner-dependent nuclear migration versus anchorage in *C. elegans*; (ii) meiotic and sperm-specific specialization in animals; (iii) spindle-pole-body insertion, centromere/telomere organization, and nuclear-envelope homeostasis in fungi; and (iv) the ancient plant split between canonical Cter-SUN and Mid-SUN proteins, followed by reproductive specialization of SUN5. These findings make family-wide propagation of narrowly defined GO biological-process terms unsafe.

## 2. Ancestral function and molecular mechanism

### Shared fold and topology

Canonical SUN proteins are predominantly **type-II inner-nuclear-membrane proteins**: an N-terminal nucleoplasmic region contacts lamins, chromatin, or other nuclear factors; one or more transmembrane helices anchor the protein; and the conserved SUN domain lies in the perinuclear/ER lumen. The canonical SUN domain is approximately 175–200 residues and adopts a **β-sandwich fold with SUN-specific helical and loop additions**. An N-terminal helical extension and adjacent coiled-coil support oligomerization, while the KASH lid and a conserved cysteine-containing loop form the KASH-binding surface. Three SUN domains normally assemble into a trimer that binds three KASH peptides, producing a heterohexameric lumenal junction. A conserved SUN–KASH disulfide can reinforce this interface under load, although some cysteine-lacking KASH systems remain functional. (sosa2013structuralinsightsinto pages 2-3, demircioglu2016purificationandstructural pages 1-2, kim2015makingthelinc pages 1-3, hao2019sunkashinteractionsfacilitate pages 9-12)

Crystal structures of human SUN2 revealed a cloverleaf-like trimer on a three-helix-bundle stem, burying approximately **6,200 Å²** of accessible surface. Deleting the stem disrupted trimerization and KASH binding. Mutagenesis implicated SUN2 D541, G608, F609, A646, and R692 in assembly or partner recognition: D541N and G608D abolished detectable KASH/nesprin binding, F609A reduced it, and A646E slightly enhanced it. These are interaction and structural residues—not catalytic residues. (zhou2012structureofsad1unc84 pages 6-7, zhou2012structureofsad1unc84 pages 5-6)

### Likely ancestral function

The parsimonious ancestral function was a **nuclear-envelope bridge**: oligomeric SUN proteins in the inner membrane bound outer-membrane KASH-like proteins, thereby coupling nuclear structures to cytoskeletal or microtubule-organizing machinery. Conserved outputs include nuclear positioning and anchorage, transmission of mechanical force, centrosome–nucleus coupling, and chromosome movement. The varying nucleoplasmic and cytoplasmic partners determine the particular cellular output. (hieda2017implicationsfordiverse pages 1-3, demircioglu2016purificationandstructural pages 1-2, kim2015makingthelinc pages 3-4)

Typical partners include:

- KASH/nesprin proteins in animals;
- UNC-83, ANC-1, and ZYG-12 in nematodes;
- Mps2, Kms1/Kms2, Ndj1, Csm4, Bqt1/Bqt2, and centromere/SPB proteins in fungi;
- plant KASH-like/WIP proteins;
- lamins, chromatin-associated factors, motors, actin, and microtubule systems indirectly through these assemblies.

No obligate small-molecule cofactor is established. Structural work reports a metal-binding, disulfide-stabilized loop in the SUN domain, but this supports fold/interface integrity rather than catalysis. (sosa2013structuralinsightsinto pages 2-3)

### 3.1 Metazoan SUN1/SUN2

SUN1 and SUN2 are broadly expressed canonical LINC proteins. They retain the strongest evidence for the inferred ancestral function: KASH/nesprin binding, nuclear positioning, centrosome coupling, mechanotransduction, and maintenance of nuclear architecture. SUN1 also works with KASH5 during meiosis to tether and move telomeres and support homolog pairing. SUN1/SUN2 double-knockout fibroblasts accumulate DNA damage and show impaired DNA-damage-response activation, indicating additional specialization through associations with DNA-PK, Ku70, and Ku80. These proteins remain non-enzymatic and have no EC number. (hieda2017implicationsfordiverse pages 1-3, kim2015makingthelinc pages 6-8)

Some reported activities may be partially LINC-independent. Earlier work associated SUN2 with antiviral responses and SUN1 with endosome-related processes, while more recent reviews emphasize that not every SUN phenotype can automatically be reduced to canonical KASH-mediated force transmission. Such functions should therefore be annotated protein-specifically rather than inferred from the family domain. (zhou2012structureofsad1unc84 pages 2-3)

### 3.2 Metazoan germ-cell SUN3/SUN4/SUN5

SUN3, SUN4, and SUN5 are predominantly testis/germ-cell-associated, unlike broadly expressed SUN1/2. This is strong evidence for **tissue-specific subfunctionalization**. Their biological roles center on spermatogenesis, sperm nuclear-envelope remodeling, and attachment or organization of specialized cytoskeletal structures. The available evidence also cautions that their membrane localization and topology need not be identical to canonical SUN1/2. (hieda2017implicationsfordiverse pages 1-3, zhou2012structureofsad1unc84 pages 2-3, demircioglu2016purificationandstructural pages 1-2)

The group should not be treated as functionally interchangeable: “spermatogenesis” is a defensible group-level theme, but specific functions and partners require paralogue-specific evidence. This appears to be specialization of the ancestral mechanical adaptor rather than acquisition of a new catalytic activity.

### 3.3 *C. elegans* partner-specific systems

UNC-84 demonstrates how one SUN protein can produce distinct outputs by selecting different KASH partners. UNC-84 recruits UNC-83 and ANC-1 to the outer nuclear membrane. UNC-83 couples to kinesin and dynein to mediate **nuclear migration**, whereas ANC-1 connects nuclei to actin and mediates **nuclear anchorage**; ANC-1 loss causes unanchored and aggregated nuclei. A separate SUN1/MTF1–ZYG-12 system contributes to pronuclear fusion and meiotic chromosome movement. This is a particularly clear case of subfunctionalization by binding partner and cellular context, not by enzyme chemistry. (kim2015makingthelinc pages 6-8, kim2015makingthelinc pages 4-6)

### 3.4 Fungal Mps3/Sad1

Budding-yeast Mps3 binds Mps2 to anchor and insert the spindle pole body into the nuclear envelope. It also tethers meiotic telomeres through Ndj1/Csm4-associated machinery, anchors DNA-break regions, contributes to sister-chromatid cohesion, and affects nuclear-envelope and lipid homeostasis. Fission-yeast Sad1 binds Kms2 and participates in SPB insertion; through Csi1, Ima1, Ndc80, Bqt proteins, and associated factors it links centromeres or telomeres to the SPB and supports chromosome segregation and meiotic “horsetail” movement. (kim2015makingthelinc pages 3-4, kim2015makingthelinc pages 4-6)

These functions represent strong lineage-specific adaptation to fungal closed mitosis and the spindle pole body. SPB insertion and lipid homeostasis are plausible examples of neo-functionalization at the biological-process level, although the molecular activity remains non-catalytic complex assembly.

### 3.5 Plant Cter-SUN proteins

A survey of **216 SUN proteins** from protists, fungi, and plants recovered two monophyletic groups, Cter-SUN and Mid-SUN. Canonical Cter-SUN proteins are structurally closest to animal/fungal SUN proteins and generally function as inner-nuclear-membrane LINC components. In plants they contribute to nuclear morphology and meiosis. Arabidopsis and rice SUN1/SUN2 double mutants have severe meiotic defects, and rice OsSUN2 appears to make the stronger individual contribution. (yuan2021evolutionandfunctional pages 1-2)

### 3.6 Plant Mid-SUN proteins

Mid-SUN proteins place the SUN domain internally and may possess three transmembrane segments. They occur at the nuclear envelope and ER and therefore should not automatically receive the canonical type-II, C-terminal-lumenal topology assigned to Cter-SUN proteins. Phylogenetic analysis indicates that Mid-SUN split into SUN3 and SUN5 before the angiosperm ancestor; Mid-SUN duplication was more frequent than Cter-SUN duplication, although losses occurred at similar frequencies. (yuan2021evolutionandfunctional pages 1-2, murphy2010structureandexpression pages 1-3, poulet2017exploringtheevolution pages 21-32)

Functional data support substantial reproductive specialization. In maize, ZmSUN1–4 were expressed at low levels across multiple tissues—approximately **50–200 parts per ten million**—whereas ZmSUN5 was pollen-preferred; antibodies localized ZmSUN3/4 to the nuclear periphery. In cotton, SUN3 is expressed more broadly in reproductive and vegetative tissues, while SUN5 expression begins during pollen division and becomes strong in mature pollen. GhSUN5 suppression to approximately **65%** of normal transcript abundance increased seed abortion from **3.12% to 17.14%**. CRISPR knockout caused extensive boll/seed abortion without obvious pollen-viability or general-growth defects, suggesting a function in fertilization, karyogamy, or early development rather than pollen production itself. (yuan2021evolutionandfunctional pages 9-11, murphy2010structureandexpression pages 1-3)

## 4. Functional divergence and branch-length assessment

### Neo-functionalization

There is **no evidence of different EC numbers or opposite catalytic reactions**, because SUN proteins are not enzymes. Neo-functionalization is instead visible at the systems level:

1. fungal acquisition of specialized SPB-insertion and nuclear-envelope-remodeling roles;
2. animal germ-cell SUN proteins specialized for spermiogenesis;
3. plant Mid-SUN proteins with altered topology, ER localization, and reproductive or stress-associated functions;
4. reported LINC-independent activities of particular mammalian SUN proteins.

These conclusions are strongest when supported by knockout, localization, or interaction data. They should not be interpreted as proof that the conserved SUN domain itself acquired catalytic activity.

### Subfunctionalization

Evidence for subfunctionalization is stronger and more pervasive:

- SUN1/2 versus SUN3/4/5: ubiquitous somatic versus germ-cell-biased expression;
- UNC-84–UNC-83 versus UNC-84–ANC-1: nuclear migration versus anchorage;
- SUN1/KASH5 versus somatic SUN/nesprin complexes: meiotic chromosome movement versus general mechanics;
- plant Cter-SUN versus Mid-SUN: different domain positions, transmembrane architectures, localization, and duplication histories;
- plant SUN3 versus SUN5: broad/reproductive-organ expression versus mature-pollen specialization;
- fungal Mps3/Sad1: distinct combinations of SPB, centromere, telomere, repair, and membrane functions.

### Branch lengths

A defensible numerical branch-length ranking cannot be produced from the supplied PANTHER record because it has no root-node or subfamily-tree information, and the retrieved literature does not report branch lengths in a directly comparable table. Published phylogenies establish the Cter-SUN/Mid-SUN split, the SUN3/SUN5 division, and elevated Mid-SUN duplication, but these facts do not identify the longest PTHR12911 branches. Likewise, very short branches or recent duplications cannot be called reliably without the underlying PANTHER tree, species sampling, alignment, and branch-length scale. (yuan2021evolutionandfunctional pages 1-2, poulet2017exploringtheevolution pages 21-32)

Thus, branch length should not be used here to assert functional change. A proper analysis would require exporting the complete PTHR12911 phylogeny, mapping experimentally characterized proteins onto it, and testing whether accelerated branches coincide with changes in topology, KASH-contact residues, expression, or phenotype.

## 5. GO annotation issues and recommendations

### Annotations suitable for cautious ancestral propagation

Where sequence architecture supports a genuine SUN protein, defensible high-level annotations include:

- SUN-domain-containing protein;
- protein binding/complex assembly involving a KASH-like partner, but only if the relevant interface is retained;
- nuclear-envelope organization or nucleocytoskeletal coupling at a broad level;
- LINC-complex component for proteins with established nuclear-envelope topology and KASH interaction.

Even these require caution for divergent Mid-SUN proteins and lineage-specific noncanonical complexes.

### Annotations that should not be propagated to all descendants

The following should be restricted to supported orthologues or experimentally defined clades:

- inner nuclear membrane localization;
- canonical type-II membrane topology;
- binding to a specific nesprin/KASH paralogue;
- nuclear migration or nuclear anchorage;
- meiotic telomere attachment/movement;
- sperm head–tail coupling or spermiogenesis;
- spindle-pole-body insertion;
- centromere/kinetochore clustering;
- DNA-double-strand-break tethering or DNA repair;
- lipid homeostasis;
- antiviral response;
- ER stress/dehydration response;
- pollen function, fertilization, or seed development.

For example, Mid-SUN proteins can occupy both the nuclear envelope and ER, while germ-cell SUN3/4/5 proteins have restricted contexts. Assigning universal “inner nuclear membrane” or “nuclear migration” terms would therefore create false positives. Conversely, fungal SPB terms and plant reproductive terms should never be propagated across kingdom boundaries. (zhou2012structureofsad1unc84 pages 2-3, yuan2021evolutionandfunctional pages 1-2, yuan2021evolutionandfunctional pages 9-11, poulet2017exploringtheevolution pages 21-32, kim2015makingthelinc pages 3-4)

### Recommended annotation strategy

1. Create PANTHER subfamilies corresponding at minimum to canonical metazoan SUN1/2, germ-cell SUN3/4/5, fungal Sad1/Mps3, plant Cter-SUN, and plant Mid-SUN; plant Mid-SUN3 and Mid-SUN5 merit separate nodes.
2. Place only broad, non-enzymatic bridge/complex-assembly terms at the deepest confidently reconstructed node.
3. Add process terms at lineage-specific nodes: fungal SPB insertion; metazoan meiosis; germ-cell spermiogenesis; plant meiosis; and Mid-SUN5 reproductive development.
4. Use NOT or annotation-extension logic where topology is demonstrably incompatible with a canonical inference—for example, ER-localized Mid-SUN proteins should not inherit exclusive inner-nuclear-membrane localization.
5. Preserve evidence codes and distinguish direct experiments from phylogenetic inference. Domain presence alone is insufficient for a specific process or partner.

## 6. Recent developments and real-world relevance

Recent studies reinforce that SUN proteins are regulated mechanical assemblies and disease-relevant systems rather than static tethers. A May 24, 2024 study found SUN2 homodimers in non-tumorigenic MCF10A breast epithelial cells but not invasive MDA-MB-231 cells. Pharmacological inhibition of protein-disulfide isomerases in MDA-MB-231 cells reduced SUN proteins, displaced Nesprin-2, altered perinuclear cytoskeletal and lamin organization, and increased invasion through confined environments in vitro. This links SUN disulfide/assembly biology to cancer-cell mechanics, but it is not yet clinical evidence or a validated therapy. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/cells13110906. (young2024inhibitionofpdis pages 1-2)

A 2024 nervous-system review synthesized evidence that LINC disruption occurs in neurological disease and highlighted reports of altered LINC components in sporadic and familial ALS/FTD and SUN1-facilitated CHMP7 nuclear influx in sporadic ALS. These observations make SUN proteins potential disease-mechanism and biomarker candidates, but do not establish clinical utility. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms252111525; published October 2024. (kuwako2024diverserolesof pages 19-19, kuwako2024diverserolesof pages 15-16)
