# PANTHER Family PTHR14514 — PKA Anchoring Protein Research Report

## 1. Executive summary

### Central conclusion

PTHR14514 is labelled **“PKA anchoring protein,”** but the supplied record has no root annotation, member list, subfamilies, InterPro mapping, alignment, or phylogenetic tree. Direct literature searching did not identify publications referring explicitly to **PTHR14514**. Consequently, the full AKAP functional class must **not** be assumed to constitute one homologous PANTHER family.

This is important because A-kinase anchoring proteins (AKAPs) are defined principally by function: they are structurally diverse, multivalent scaffolds that bind PKA and localize it to particular cellular microdomains. More than 50 human genes/proteins have been described as AKAPs, ranging from approximately 18 to over 500 kDa, with little full-length sequence similarity and widely different domain architectures. Their shared feature is usually a short PKA-binding amphipathic helix rather than a common globular fold. Thus, “AKAP” resembles a **functionally convergent interaction class**, not a single conventional protein family whose complete membership can be placed on one reliable sequence tree. (gotz2016akap18pkariiαstructurereveals pages 1-3, pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 3-4, bucko2021drugsthatregulate pages 2-4, gabrovsek2018akinaseanchoringprotein pages 1-3)

The defensible ancestral/core activity for genuine PTHR14514 descendants is therefore **non-catalytic PKA anchoring or scaffolding**, provided the descendants retain a functional A-kinase-binding region. Functional diversity among the broader AKAP class is dominated by **subfunctionalization**—different targeting signals, organelles, tissues, PKA regulatory-subunit isotypes, splice isoforms, and recruited effectors. A stronger case of added or neo-functionalized activity is AKAP13/AKAP-Lbc, which combines PKA scaffolding with a catalytic Rho guanine-nucleotide-exchange module. (pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 3-4, gabrovsek2018akinaseanchoringprotein pages 1-3, pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 4-5)

There is no evidence for different EC-numbered reactions or opposite catalytic reactions across the shared AKAP anchoring module. AKAPs are generally not enzymes; therefore no EC number, catalytic substrate, catalytic cofactor, or catalytic residues should be assigned to the family merely from the “PKA anchoring protein” label.

## 2. Ancestral function and structural basis

### Core molecular function

The common function of bona fide AKAPs is to bind the regulatory-subunit dimer of PKA and position the holoenzyme near selected activators, substrates, phosphatases, phosphodiesterases, receptors, or ion channels. This creates spatially restricted cAMP/PKA signaling islands and changes which substrates are efficiently phosphorylated. Reviews estimate that typical mammalian cells express roughly 10–15 AKAPs, while the human functional class contains more than 50 genes/proteins. (bucko2021drugsthatregulate pages 2-4, gold2013engineeringakinaseanchoring pages 1-1, bucko2021drugsthatregulate pages 1-2)

### Shared structure—not a common full-length fold

There is **no established common full-length AKAP fold**. Many AKAPs are large, modular, and substantially intrinsically disordered. The shared structural element is usually a **14–18-residue amphipathic α-helix**, also called the AKAP-binding or A-kinase-binding region. Its hydrophobic face docks in a hydrophobic groove formed by the antiparallel helical docking/dimerization domain of a PKA regulatory-subunit dimer. The remainder of each AKAP contains unrelated targeting and partner-binding regions. (gotz2016akap18pkariiαstructurereveals pages 1-3, collins2023phosphorylationcompartmentalizationand pages 3-4, gabrovsek2018akinaseanchoringprotein pages 1-3)

A proposed RII-binding hydrophobic pattern is **HxxHHxxHHxxHHxxHH**, where H denotes a hydrophobic residue; another experimentally derived consensus is **LxxxAxxIVxxAIxxA**. These are physicochemical patterns rather than a rigid sequence motif. Hydrophobic packing is essential, while sequence-specific hydrophilic contacts flanking the helix provide additional affinity and selectivity. AKAP18β structural work identified three hydrophilic anchor points outside the core helix for RIIα recognition and a different anchor-point set in RI-selective AKAPs. (gotz2016akap18pkariiαstructurereveals pages 1-3, gold2013engineeringakinaseanchoring pages 1-1)

### Binding partners and specificity

The direct conserved partner is a PKA regulatory dimer—RIα, RIβ, RIIα, or RIIβ. Most historically characterized AKAPs prefer RII, but RI-selective and dual RI/RII anchors exist. Differences in regulatory-subunit docking-groove topology and AKAP side-chain packing determine selectivity. In addition, individual AKAPs recruit phosphatases, PDEs, other kinases, adenylyl cyclases, receptors, ion channels, GTPases, GAPs, and GEFs. (gotz2016akap18pkariiαstructurereveals pages 1-3, pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 3-4, collins2023phosphorylationcompartmentalizationand pages 3-4)

### Functional residues rather than catalytic residues

The important residues are binding-interface residues on the hydrophobic face of the amphipathic helix and flanking polar anchor points—not catalytic residues. Structure/function studies and naturally occurring variants show that substitutions on the hydrophobic face can weaken PKA anchoring. Accordingly:

- **EC number:** none for the anchoring activity.
- **Catalytic substrate:** none.
- **Catalytic cofactor:** none.
- **Typical ligand/partner:** PKA RI or RII regulatory-subunit dimer.
- **Functional determinant:** amphipathic helix plus flanking isotype-selectivity contacts.

### Experimental evidence and notable examples

**AKAP7/AKAP15/18** provides the clearest example of splice-isoform subfunctionalization. AKAP18α targets plasma-membrane L-type Ca²⁺ channels, whereas long isoforms localize to intracellular compartments; AKAP18δ organizes PKA near SERCA2/phospholamban at the sarcoplasmic reticulum and participates in cardiac Ca²⁺ handling. Other isoforms participate in sodium-channel regulation or vasopressin-dependent AQP2 trafficking. Comparative evolutionary analysis identified AKAP7α as the ancestral short form and AKAP7γ as the ancestral long form, while AKAP7δ arose relatively recently in some rodent and primate lineages. This is evidence for lineage- and isoform-level innovation, although it is not a branch analysis of PTHR14514 itself. (pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 3-4, zaccolo2025nanodomaincampsignaling pages 21-23, welch2010networkingwithakaps pages 3-4)

**AKAP9** illustrates alternative-splicing-based specialization. Yotiao organizes PKA, PP1, PDE4D3, AC9, and KCNQ1 at the plasma membrane and thereby regulates cardiac IKs repolarization; other AKAP9 products such as AKAP350/450 act at centrosomes and Golgi. These functions should be represented as isoform- or complex-specific annotations rather than automatically copied across the gene or family. (zaccolo2025nanodomaincampsignaling pages 21-23, tasken2004localizedeffectsof pages 5-6, welch2010networkingwithakaps pages 3-4)

**AKAP13/AKAP-Lbc** is the strongest example of an added biochemical function. Besides anchoring PKA, it has a Rho-GEF module coupling Gα12 to Rho and organizes PKD, ERK-related, 14-3-3, LC3, and cytoskeletal signaling. The anchoring segment itself remains non-enzymatic, but the full-length protein possesses catalytic nucleotide-exchange activity. This is consistent with domain accretion or neo-functionalization at the protein level, not with a new EC activity of the shared AKAP helix. (welch2010networkingwithakaps pages 3-4, pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 4-5)

**AKAP11—recent development.** An August 2024 preprint used lysosome immunopurification proteomics, AKAP11 knockout/rescue, phosphoproteomics, structural modeling, and kinase assays to identify a Cα–RIα–AKAP11 complex associated with autophagic machinery. AKAP11’s LC3-interacting region connected PKA to autophagosomes; RIα Ser83 was identified as an AKAP11-dependent phosphosite affecting RIα–Cα association and cAMP-dependent activation. AKAP11 loss in induced-pluripotent-stem-cell-derived neurons disrupted multiple homeostatic pathways, suggesting a mechanism connecting AKAP11 loss of function to schizophrenia/bipolar-disorder risk. Because this was initially a 2024 bioRxiv report, autophagy-specific conclusions should be annotated with appropriate evidence status. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.08.06.606738, posted 6 August 2024. (seguraroman2024autophagosomescoordinatean pages 36-41, seguraroman2024autophagosomescoordinatean pages 1-3)

Recent work has also emphasized disease caused by altered interaction selectivity rather than altered AKAP catalysis. A 2024 *Science Advances* study reported a Cushing-syndrome PKA mutant that biases signaling through type-I AKAP complexes (DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adl1258; PMID: **38381834**). (zaccolo2025nanodomaincampsignaling pages 51-52)

## 4. Functional divergence

### Subfunctionalization: strong evidence

The dominant pattern is specialization of a common spatial-organizing function:

1. **Subcellular targeting:** mitochondria/ER (AKAP1), sperm fibrous sheath (AKAP3/4), postsynaptic membrane (AKAP5), nuclear envelope (AKAP6), sarcoplasmic reticulum or plasma membrane (AKAP7 isoforms), nuclear matrix (AKAP8), Golgi/centrosome or plasma membrane (AKAP9), mitochondria/endosomes (AKAP10), autophagosomes (AKAP11), and actin/focal-adhesion compartments (AKAP12/13). (pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 3-4, zadeh2018investigatingtheinteraction pages 23-26, gabrovsek2018akinaseanchoringprotein pages 1-3)
2. **Partner specificity:** different AKAPs recruit distinct combinations of PDEs, phosphatases, ion channels, receptors, GTPase regulators, and cytoskeletal proteins. (pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 3-4, zaccolo2025nanodomaincampsignaling pages 21-23, pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 4-5)
3. **PKA-isotype specificity:** most bind RII preferentially; some bind RI or both RI and RII. Structural differences in the RI/RII docking domains and AKAP contact residues explain this specialization. (gotz2016akap18pkariiαstructurereveals pages 1-3, collins2023phosphorylationcompartmentalizationand pages 3-4)
4. **Alternative splicing:** especially important for AKAP7 and AKAP9, whose products localize to different compartments and engage different effectors. (zaccolo2025nanodomaincampsignaling pages 21-23, welch2010networkingwithakaps pages 3-4)

### Neo-functionalization: qualified evidence

- **AKAP13:** acquisition or retention of a Rho-GEF catalytic module creates a function distinct from PKA anchoring. This is the clearest protein-level neo-functionalization candidate. (pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 4-5)
- **AKAP3/4:** extensive structural specialization in sperm supports a new architectural role in the fibrous sheath in addition to kinase anchoring. (pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 3-4, tasken2004localizedeffectsof pages 5-6)
- **AKAP11:** acquisition of an LC3-interacting/autophagy interface adds a selective-turnover and signaling-checkpoint function. The evidence is strong mechanistically but was emerging in 2024. (seguraroman2024autophagosomescoordinatean pages 36-41, seguraroman2024autophagosomescoordinatean pages 1-3)

### No evidence for opposite enzymatic reactions

No reviewed AKAP anchoring subgroups catalyze synthesis versus degradation or carry different EC numbers as a consequence of the shared AKAP module. Kinases, phosphatases, and PDEs found in AKAP complexes are generally **recruited partners**, not catalytic activities of the AKAP itself. A GO transfer that converts “binds/anchors PKA” into “protein kinase activity” would therefore be incorrect.

Branch-specific functional shifts should be tested only after sequence membership is validated.

## 6. GO annotation issues and recommendations

### Appropriate conserved annotations

At an ancestral node supported by retention of the relevant binding motif and orthology, conservative annotations may include:

- PKA regulatory-subunit binding;
- PKA anchoring/localization;
- protein-kinase-A holoenzyme complex organization; and
- signaling-scaffold or protein-complex scaffold activity.

Even these terms should not be applied solely because a sequence contains a predicted amphipathic helix: short amphipathic helices occur frequently, and direct binding evidence or strong orthology is preferable.

### Annotations that should not be propagated family-wide

The following are member-, isoform-, tissue-, or complex-specific and should be blocked above the relevant clade:

- protein kinase or phosphatase catalytic activity;
- cAMP phosphodiesterase activity;
- mitochondrial, Golgi, centrosomal, nuclear, synaptic, sperm-fibrous-sheath, sarcoplasmic-reticulum, or autophagosomal localization;
- ion-channel regulation;
- sperm motility/capacitation;
- cardiac repolarization, excitation–contraction coupling, or hypertrophic signaling;
- AQP2 trafficking;
- focal-adhesion/cell-migration functions;
- autophagy and LC3 binding; and
- Rho guanyl-nucleotide-exchange activity.

The large differences in targeting, partners, and biological processes across AKAPs directly demonstrate why such transfer would over-annotate descendants. (pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 3-4, zaccolo2025nanodomaincampsignaling pages 21-23, gabrovsek2018akinaseanchoringprotein pages 1-3, pidoux2010specificityandspatial pages 4-5)

### Isoform exclusions

Annotation should occur at isoform level wherever the anchoring helix or targeting sequence differs. An AKAP isoform lacking the functional anchoring helix should not inherit PKA-binding/anchoring merely from its gene-level parent. Naturally occurring substitutions in the anchoring interface can also change affinity and localization, so motif integrity should be checked rather than assumed.

### Recommended PANTHER curation action

1. Recover the actual PTHR14514 member list and tree.
2. Determine whether the family corresponds to one homologous AKAP lineage rather than the entire AKAP functional class.
3. Create subfamilies at well-supported duplication nodes with changed targeting/domain architecture.
4. Place ancestral PKA-binding annotations only above descendants retaining the AKAP-binding region.
5. Add gain/loss events for organelle targeting, RI/RII preference, and accessory catalytic domains.
6. Keep AKAP13-like Rho-GEF activity, AKAP11 autophagy functions, AKAP3/4 sperm functions, and AKAP7/9 isoform functions below their specific nodes.

## Overall assessment

The evidence supports a conserved **PKA-anchoring interaction mechanism**, but not a universal AKAP fold or a single pan-AKAP phylogeny. Functional divergence is primarily subfunctionalization through targeting, partner recruitment, PKA-isotype selectivity, and alternative splicing. Added domain activities—most clearly the Rho-GEF activity of AKAP13—represent plausible neo-functionalization. For PTHR14514 specifically, branch-level and subfamily-level conclusions must remain provisional until its descendant sequences and PANTHER tree are available; GO propagation should therefore be deliberately conservative.
