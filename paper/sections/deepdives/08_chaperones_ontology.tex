\subsection{When the ontology is the error: binding terms, precomposition and obsoletion}\label{sec:dd-ontology}
\textbf{Claim.} Some systematic errors are not in the annotations but in the terms: terms that
describe what a protein sticks to rather than what it does, or pre-compose context into
function, attract inconsistent use across thousands of genes.
\textbf{Data.} \proj{UNFOLDED\_PROTEIN\_BINDING.md}: every experimental annotation to
\go{GO:0051082}{unfolded protein binding} in a 148-gene, 17-species snapshot reassigned to
mechanism-specific MFs (foldase, holdase, co-chaperone, E3); both source terms subsequently
obsoleted. \proj{INVOLVED\_IN\_PRECOMPOSITION.md}: 320 distinct ``X involved in Y'' terms in 1,204
rows. \todo{count of term obsoletions driven by AIGR obsoletion projects (\texttt{projects/*\_OBSOLETION.md}).}
\textbf{Message.} Agentic review doubles as ontology QC: inconsistent usage at scale is a signal
that the term is ill-defined.
