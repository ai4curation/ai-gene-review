\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\note{Themes:}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Systematic, not random.} Errors cluster by mapping, code and ontology region;
        so do fixes---one mapping edit corrects thousands of rows.
  \item \textbf{Evidence codes are a poor proxy for truth.} IBA $\approx$ IDA; IEP and ISO weak;
        IMP/IGI encode necessity. Downstream users filtering ``IEA-only'' remove good rows and
        keep bad ones.
  \item \textbf{Consequences.} Enrichment analysis, CAFA ground truth, and predictors trained on GO
        (\S\ref{sec:dd-predictors}): benchmarks that score against uncorrected GO reward reproducing its errors.
  \item \textbf{Why agents, why now.} Re-reading at the scale of a proteome was never affordable
        for human curators; guardrails make it auditable.
  \item \textbf{Failure modes of the agent, and checks built from them.} The residue-claim validator
        (\S\ref{sec:seqchecks}) exists because an audit of 17 ``lacks the catalytic residue'' arguments
        in early reviews found 4 where the site was fully intact; the real explanations were a truncation (CASP12),
        the activation junction (LPA), lost substrate contacts with the catalytic cysteine kept
        (AZIN1), and a different domain (HSPA13), as recorded in the schema's description of
        \texttt{residue\_claims}. Each conclusion survived for another
        reason, so no reviewer noticed the faulty mechanism. A plausible residue argument is exactly
        the kind of error an LLM agent makes fluently, and the remedy was to make the claim
        structured enough to be contradicted by sequence data. \todo{script the 17-claim audit.}
        Structured claims cover only \nResGenes{} genes so far; most residue arguments remain in prose
        and are not machine-checked.
  \item \textbf{Other failure modes.} Over-confidence on abstract-only papers (policy now
        forbids removing experimental rows without full text); boilerplate generation
        (\proj{REVIEW\_QUALITY\_AUDIT.md}); deference vs over-correction.
  \item \textbf{Limitations.} Non-random gene selection; actions are judgements, not ground
        truth; snapshot in time; the agent shares biases of the literature.
  \item \textbf{Towards continuous review.} Re-run on ontology change, retraction, new paper.
\end{itemize}
